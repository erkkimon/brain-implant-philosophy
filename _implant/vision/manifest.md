# Manifest: the givens of this implant

Every body of knowledge stands on assumptions. Most hide them. This implant
declares them, so that a reader can see exactly what has been granted, what
has been refused, and what follows from which. The list is deliberately
short: a given that is not needed is a bias waiting to happen. Each is marked
**unavoidable** (denying it would use it) or **a choice** (a decision this
implant made and could have made otherwise, recorded as such).

Founded by erkkimon on 2026-09-25 ([journal](../journals/2026-09-25.md)).
The wording is the implant's; the givens are the founder's.

## G1. Logic is trusted — *unavoidable*

Valid inference preserves truth; a proposition and its negation are not both
true. This is assumed, not defended, because any defence of it would already
use it. The working logic of the implant is classical first-order logic;
non-classical logics — intuitionistic, paraconsistent, relevance, fuzzy,
many-valued — are documented in [methods](../knowledge/methods/index.md) as
subject matter, and any page whose argument depends on one says so.

## G2. Logic needs no authority — *unavoidable, with a chosen consequence*

A valid inference is valid whoever made it, and an individual can check it
alone. The chosen consequence: **this implant does not gatekeep thoughts by
peer review.** The admission test for a thought is *checkability* — it is
stated in a citable place, so a reader can verify that the implant reported
it faithfully — not *approval* by a journal, a department, or a consensus.
Peer review is reported as a fact about a source where it exists; it is
never a condition of entry. (Empirical claims are a different case; see G4.)

## G3. Nobody knows what is true on a contested question — including us — *a choice*

On any question that has a page in [problems](../knowledge/problems/index.md)
the implant takes no position, in any voice, on any page. It reports what
has been said and by whom; the reader decides. Opinions, interpretations and
conclusions belong to the reader and the reader's agent — the *cortex*, in
brainpick's terms — never to the implant
([Using the brain implant](../skills/using-the-brain-implant.md)).

This is a choice, not a theorem: someone *may* know. The implant refuses the
role because a repository that adjudicates is one side's repository wearing
a neutral name, and every other side would be right to distrust it.

## G4. Only two kinds of sentence are admissible — *a choice*

A sentence enters the implant only if it is

1. **logic or mathematics** a reader can verify alone — a reconstruction of
   an argument into premises and conclusion, a validity check, a comparison
   of two attributed claims, a computation; or
2. **a report that someone said something, at a location** — with the
   receipt attached, so the reader can check that the report is faithful
   ([Every claim carries its receipt](../conventions/every-claim-carries-its-receipt.md)).

Everything else is an opinion and does not enter. In particular, evaluative
predicates — *fallacious*, *debunked*, *fringe*, *pseudo-*,
*widely regarded*, *rightly*, *fails* — never occur in the implant's voice; they
occur only inside an attributed report.

Two clarifications the line needs:

- **Comparison and structure are ours; evaluation never is.** "Positions A
  and B diverge at premise 3" is an operation of kind 1 on two reports of
  kind 2, and a reader can verify it. "Position A handles the objection
  better" is an evaluation and stays out.
- **Empirical claims are a sub-case of kind 2.** A study's finding is
  reported as *that study's finding*, together with its evidence grade and
  replication record ([How claims are graded](../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md)).
  The implant never asserts an empirical result as simply true; it says who
  found it, how, and whether anyone found it again.

## G5. Selection is part of neutrality — *a choice*

A page can be neutral sentence by sentence and biased as a whole, by what it
leaves out. Wikipedia's neutral-point-of-view policy governs the sentence;
the bias lives in the selection — a position that can be defended is
irrelevant if it is never told. So admission here is a **mechanical rule**,
not an editorial judgement of importance or respectability: a position,
argument, thinker, technique or bias is in scope if it has a named proponent
or source in a citable published place. How widely it is held is reported as
data (surveys, citation counts — cited), never used as a reason to leave it
out. Traditions from every region and period enter under the same rule, not
as an appendix ([Selection is a stated rule](../conventions/selection-is-a-stated-rule.md)).

## G6. Words are contracts — *a choice*

Truth can be carried by any vocabulary, even a clumsy one, provided the
meanings are transparent, definite, and fine-grained enough for the piece of
reality being discussed. So for each term the implant relies on it fixes a
**normative** definition — how the term is used here, and why it is split
from its neighbours the way it is, to keep the whole vocabulary coherent —
and places beside it the **descriptive** definitions: how the term is
actually used, by whom, cited. The normative choice is recorded as a
decision, never as a claim that other uses are wrong
([Words are contracts](../conventions/words-are-contracts.md)).

## G7. Bayesian updating is offered as a tool, not imposed as a truth — *a choice*

This implant does not tell a reader what is true; it gives the reader tools
to decide. The tool it recommends for turning a gut feeling into something
educated and replayable is Bayesian updating — a prior with a named
reference class, factors with likelihood ratios, a range instead of a point,
and a report of whether the evidence or the prior is carrying the result.
The tool lives in the shared cognitive-tools implant and is driven by its
[estimate-a-credence](../skills/index.md) skill; the philosophy implant holds
no probability machinery of its own.

Neutrality applies to the method too: the objections to Bayesianism — the
problem of the priors, frequentist and likelihoodist alternatives, the
Dutch-book debate — are documented in [methods](../knowledge/methods/index.md)
with the same care as the case for it. A worked example of a calculation is
published as a **provocation**: its numbers exist to be overwritten by the
reader's own.

## G8. Evidence before authority — *a choice*

Weight goes to the argument, never to the arguer. Fame, credentials,
institutional standing and consensus are reported as facts about the world
where a source states them; they are never used as reasons in the implant's
voice. Criticism lands on a claim, never on a person
([Evidence before authority](../conventions/evidence-before-authority.md)).

## G9. Written for a stranger — *a choice*

The repository is public from its first commit. Every page is addressed to
someone on the open internet who has none of our context: no personal data,
no infrastructure, no host names; every receipt resolves for anyone; pull
requests are welcome ([Written to be public](../conventions/written-to-be-public.md)).

## G10. The implant is one half of a brain — *a design given*

A brain is a **cortex** — the agent's own memory, holding its and its user's
conclusions — plus any implants mounted beside it. The implant never holds an
opinion; the cortex holds nothing that is not the agent's own. Either half
works alone, slightly lobotomized. Skills are written for the implant and
say so ([Using the brain implant](../skills/using-the-brain-implant.md)).

## What follows

From these, the rest of the implant should be derivable:

| Given | Yields |
| --- | --- |
| G1, G2 | argument reconstruction and validity checking as first-class content; no peer-review filter |
| G3, G4 | [Reporting, not endorsing](../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md); the evaluative-predicate lint; symmetric case-for/case-against on every position page |
| G4 | the two-kind sentence test in every authoring skill; the claim taxonomy |
| G5 | the mechanical admission rule; the coverage ledger measured against Wikipedia's own lists |
| G6 | the [vocabulary](../knowledge/vocabulary/index.md) branch with normative and descriptive entries |
| G7 | the credence skill; the [methods](../knowledge/methods/index.md) pages on Bayesianism and its critics |
| G8 | thinker pages as hubs of links, reception attributed, no ranking |
| G9 | the publication convention; the contract's `no_secrets` rule |
| G10 | every skill says *implant*; opinions are routed to the cortex |

If a page cannot be traced to a row of this table, either the page does not
belong or the manifest has a gap — and the gap is the more interesting
finding. Record it in the journal.

## The benchmark

The implant measures itself against Wikipedia on three axes, each made
checkable:

- **Neutrality** — G3, G4, G5: no evaluation in our voice (lint-checked), a
  case for and against on every position (checklist), admission by rule
  rather than by judgement (stated rule).
- **Quality** — G4, G8: every claim carries a receipt to a *primary* source
  with a canonical locator where one exists, and every empirical claim its
  grade and replication record.
- **Amount** — G5: a coverage ledger in `todo/` counts pages against
  Wikipedia's own lists (*List of philosophers*, *List of unsolved problems
  in philosophy*, *List of fallacies*, *List of cognitive biases*), so
  "more" is a number and not an impression.
