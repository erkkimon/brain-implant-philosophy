> "This article presents the first meta-analysis of experimental research on rejection, sampling 88 studies."  (abstract)

> "Aggressive responses to rejection, considered paradoxical by some, appear to be due to attempts to gain control"  (abstract)

> "Possibly the biggest puzzle in the rejection literature is that rejection makes people act prosocially some of the time and antisocially at other times."  (section "Prosocial and Antisocial Responses to Rejection")

> "The belonging hypothesis is only partially correct. The most important caveat to our needs findings (and the belonging hypothesis) is that people will not always seek to restore belonging following rejection. People will restore both belonging and control if possible but will prioritize restoring control over restoring belonging."  (Discussion)

Relied on for: a later meta-analysis that includes Maner et al. (2007)
among its studies (listed four times in Appendix Table A1 and in the
reference list, "References marked with an asterisk indicate studies
included in the meta-analysis") and qualifies the belonging account.
Context: the authors contrast a "needs account" with a "numbness account";
they do not mention Schopenhauer or porcupines in the text searched.
