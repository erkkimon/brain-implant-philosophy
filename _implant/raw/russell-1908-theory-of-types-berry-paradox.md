# Russell 1908 — the paradox of "the least integer not nameable in fewer than nineteen syllables", its credit to G. G. Berry, and Russell's solution

> "(4) The number of syllables in the English names of finite integers tends to increase as the integers grow larger, and must gradually increase indefinitely, since only a finite number of names can be made with a given finite number of syllables. Hence the names of some integers must consist of at least nineteen syllables, and among these there must be a least. Hence "the least integer not nameable in fewer than nineteen syllables" must denote a definite integer; in fact, it denotes 111,777. But "the least integer not nameable in fewer than nineteen syllables" is itself a name consisting of eighteen syllables; hence the least integer not nameable in fewer than nineteen syllables can be named in eighteen syllables, which is a contradiction."  (§I, p. 223)

> "This contradiction was suggested to me by Mr. G. G. Berry of the Bodleian Library."  (footnote to (4), p. 223)

> "Here we assume, in obtaining the contradiction, that a phrase containing "all names" is itself a name, though it appears from the contradiction that it can not be one of the names which were supposed to be all the names there are. Hence "all names" is an illegitimate notion."  (p. 225)

> "It is to be observed, in the first place, that nameable must mean "nameable by means of such-and-such assigned names," and that the number of assigned names must be finite. For if it is not finite, there is no reason why there should be any integer not nameable in fewer than nineteen syllables, and the paradox collapses."  (p. 240)

> "The solution of this paradox lies, I think, in the simple observation that "nameable in terms of names of the class N" is never itself nameable in terms of names of that class."  (p. 240)

> "Hence we have only to observe that nameability in terms of such functions is non-predicative in order to escape the paradox."  (p. 241)

Relied on for: the 1908 statement of the paradox, the figure 111,777, the
credit to Berry, Russell's diagnosis ("all names" illegitimate) and his
solution (nameability relative to an assigned class of names; non-predicative).
Context: (4) is the fourth of seven contradictions listed in §I (after
Epimenides, Russell's class paradox, the relations version; followed by
the least indefinable ordinal, Richard and Burali-Forti); on p. 224 Russell
says all of them share "self-reference or reflexiveness". The solution
passage, pp. 240–241, follows his solution of the liar ("This solves the
liar.") and precedes "the least indefinable ordinal". OCR normalised only
for line-break hyphenation and spacing; the OCR reads "can not" as two words.
