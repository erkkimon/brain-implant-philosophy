## §119 — how identity depends on when we were conceived

> "The Time-Dependence Claim: If any particular person had not been conceived when he was in fact conceived, it is in fact true that he would never have existed."  (§119)

## §122 — A young girl's child

> "This girl chooses to have a child. Because she is so young, she gives her child a bad start in life. Though this will have bad effects throughout this child’s life, his life will, predictably, be worth living. If this girl had waited for several years, she would have had a different child, to whom she would have given a better start in life."  (§122, "The 14-Year-Old Girl")

> "On both views, this girl’s decision was not worse for her child."  (§122; the two views are that causing to exist can, or cannot, benefit)

> "We cannot claim that this girl’s decision was worse for her child. What is the objection to her decision? This question arises because, in the different outcomes, different people would be born."  (§122; the next sentence in the OCR: "I shall therefore call this the Non-Identity Problem")

> "(A) is not about what is good or bad for any of the particular people who ever live. (A) appeals to a new principle, that must be explained and justified."  (§122; (A) is the claim that the girl's decision "will probably be worse for her child", "her child" covering any child she might have)

> "The Same Number Quality Claim, or Q: If in either of two possible outcomes the same number of people would ever live, it would be worse if those who live are worse off, or have a lower quality of life, than those who would have lived."  (§122)

> "I suggest that, on reflection, we can accept (3)."  (§122; (3) is "that it would have been better if the child who existed had not been her actual child")

> "Though Q is plausible, it does not solve the Non-Identity Problem. Q covers only the cases where, in the different outcomes, the same number of people would ever live."  (§122)

> "Call what we ought to accept Theory X."  (§122)

## §123 — Depletion

> "(It may help to think about this question: how many of us could truly claim, ‘Even if railways and motor cars had never been invented, I would still have been born’ ?)"  (§123)

> "If we choose Depletion, the quality of life over the next two centuries would be slightly higher than it would have been if we had chosen Conservation. But it would later, for many centuries, be much lower than it would have been if we had chosen Conservation."  (§123)

> "We know that, even if it greatly lowers the quality of life for several centuries, our choice will not be worse for anyone who ever lives."  (§123)

> "Some believe that what is bad must be bad for someone. On this view, there is no objection to our choice."  (§123)

> "Certain writers accept this conclusion.* But it is very implausible."  (§123; the asterisk is Parfit's note marker in the OCR text)

> "The great lowering of the quality of life must provide some moral reason not to choose Depletion. This is believed by most of those who consider cases of this kind."  (§123)

> "Our need to answer (1), and other similar questions, I call the Non-Identity Problem."  (§123; question (1): "What is the moral reason not to choose Depletion?")

> "Some people believe that this problem is a mere quibble. This reaction is unjustified. The problem arises because of superficial facts about our reproductive system. But, though it arises in a superficial way, it is a real problem."  (§123)

## §124 — Why an appeal to rights cannot wholly solve the problem

> "But this man’s letter shows that he was glad to be alive."  (§124; a man born when his mother was 14 who wrote to The Times)

> "If we had claimed that her act was wrong, because he has a right that cannot be fulfilled, he could have said. ‘I waive this right’."  (§124; punctuation as in the OCR text)

> "It would have been better if this man’s mother had waited. But this is not because of what she did to her actual child. It is because of what she could have done for any child that she could have had when she was mature."  (§124)

> "We should expect that, as I have claimed, appealing to rights cannot wholly solve the Non-Identity Problem."  (§124)

## §§125, 127 — the No-Difference View; conclusions

> "In considering both cases, I accept the No-Difference View. So do many other people."  (§125)

Relied on for: the statement of the 14-year-old girl and Depletion cases,
  the name "Non-Identity Problem", Q and Theory X, Parfit's rejection of
  "what is bad must be bad for someone" for these cases, his objection to
  the rights-based answer (the waiver), the No-Difference View, and his
  notes crediting Kavka, Adams and Schwartz.
Context: §122 opens with the two questions whether causing someone to
  exist can benefit them; Parfit says both answers are defensible and runs
  the argument on each. §123 presents Depletion in two versions (Lesser
  and Greater Depletion). §124 considers "a right to a good start in life"
  and replies with the waiver argument, then argues that Lesser Depletion
  cannot be explained by rights. §125 compares two medical programmes
  (Conditions J and K) and states the No-Difference View; §127 summarises.
  Part Four goes on (ch. 17 onward) to different-number choices and the
  Repugnant Conclusion.
