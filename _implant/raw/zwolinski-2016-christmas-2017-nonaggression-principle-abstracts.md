# Zwolinski 2016 and Christmas 2017 — the libertarian nonaggression principle criticised and defended (publisher abstracts via DOI)

Source: (1) Matt Zwolinski, "The Libertarian Nonaggression Principle",
  Social Philosophy and Policy 32(2) (2016), pp. 62–90.
  (2) Billy Christmas, "Rescuing the Libertarian Non-Aggression Principle",
  Moral Philosophy and Politics 5(2), pp. 305–325 (online 2017-10-12;

> "The libertarian “Nonaggression Principle” (NAP) prohibits aggression against the persons or property of others, and it is on this basis that the libertarian opposition to redistributive taxation, legal paternalism, and perhaps even the state itself is thought to rest."  (Zwolinski, abstract)

> "First, insofar as libertarians employ a moralized understanding of aggression, their principle is really about the protection of property rights rather than the prohibition of aggression."  (Zwolinski, abstract)

> "Second, the absolutist prohibition on aggression, which libertarians typically endorse and which is necessary to generate strongly libertarian conclusions, is grossly implausible."  (Zwolinski, abstract)

> "It argues that this more moderate and defensible version of the NAP still has important libertarian implications, but that a full defense of libertarianism cannot rely upon appeals to nonaggression alone."  (Zwolinski, abstract)

> "This approach is often criticised insofar as it defines aggression, in part, as the violation of legitimate property rights, and is therefore parasitical upon a prior – and unjustified – theory of property."  (Christmas, abstract)

> "A commitment to property rights and to non-aggression can both be grounded in a commitment to non-interference."  (Christmas, abstract; italics markup removed)

Relied on for: Zwolinski's two objections (moralized aggression;
  absolutism) and his moderate version; Christmas's non-interference reply.
