# SEP "John Buridan" (Zupko) — the insolubles in Summulae 9.8 and Buridan's final solution (§4)

> "Most modern logicians know of his solutions to alethic paradoxes such as the Liar, addressed in the eighth and final chapter of ninth treatise of the Summulae, which belongs to the medieval literature of sophismata or insolubilia."  (§4)

> "A proposition must also signify or assert itself to be true (S 9.8, 7th sophism: 967)."  (§4, the earlier view, held by "some people, including himself")

> "Unfortunately, this looks no less ad hoc than the transcasus and restriction solutions he has just criticized."  (§4)

> "The solution Buridan finally settles on receives the somewhat tepid endorsement of being “closer to the truth” than the previous solution—a reflection, perhaps, of his awareness of the imperfectability of any formal system that tries to stick close to the facts of human language."  (§4)

> "“Every proposition,” he says, “virtually implies another proposition in which the predicate ‘true’ is affirmed of the subject that supposits for [the original proposition]” (S 9.8, 7th sophism: 969)."  (§4)

> "In this way, for the truth of any proposition P, it is required not only (1) that the subject and predicate terms of P stand for the same thing or things, but also (2) that P implies another proposition, ‘P is true’, which must also be true."  (§4)
