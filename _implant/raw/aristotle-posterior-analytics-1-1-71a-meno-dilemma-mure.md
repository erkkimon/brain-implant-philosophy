# Aristotle, Posterior Analytics I.1 (71a1–71b8) — "the dilemma in the Meno": knowing universally but not without qualification (Mure tr.; Bekker Greek)

> "All instruction given or received by way of argument proceeds from pre-existent knowledge."  (I.1, 71a, opening sentence, Mure)

> "Before he was led on to recognition or before he actually drew a conclusion, we should perhaps say that in a manner he knew, in a manner not."  (71a, Mure)

> "If he did not in an unqualified sense of the term know the existence of this triangle, how could he know without qualification that its angles were equal to two right angles? No: clearly he knows not without qualification but only in the sense that he knows universally. If this distinction is not drawn, we are faced with the dilemma in the Meno: either a man will learn nothing or what he already knows; for we cannot accept the solution which some people offer."  (71a, Mure)

Greek (Bekker, 71a): "ἀλλὰ δῆλον ὡς ὡδὶ μὲν ἐπίσταται, ὅτι καθόλου ἐπίσταται, ἁπλῶς δ' οὐκ ἐπίσταται. εἰ δὲ μή, τὸ ἐν τῷ Μένωνι ἀπόρημα συμβήσεται· ἢ γὰρ οὐδὲν μαθήσεται ἢ ἃ οἶδεν."
— ἀπόρημα ("puzzle", "difficulty"), rendered "dilemma" by Mure.

> "The solution which some people offer is to assert that they do not know that every pair is even, but only that everything which they know to be a pair is even"  (71a, Mure)

> "On the other hand, I imagine there is nothing to prevent a man in one sense knowing what he is learning, in another not knowing it. The strange thing would be, not if in some sense he knew what he was learning, but if he were to know it in that precise sense and manner in which he was learning it."  (71b, Mure)

1901 Blackwell translation (translator not named in the scan's text; public domain), same passage:

> "On any other view we shall have the dilemma of the Meno — a man will either learn nothing at all or only what he knows before."  (I.1, p. 3)

Relied on for: Aristotle's explicit reference to the Meno; his distinction
between knowing universally (καθόλου) and knowing without qualification
(ἁπλῶς); his rejection of the rival solution (restricting a universal to
the instances one knows); knowing in one sense and not in another (71b).

Context: the example is a student who knows that every triangle has
angles equal to two right angles and is then shown a figure inscribed in
a semicircle which he did not know to be a triangle. Jowett's Meno
translation prints "(Compare Aristot. Post. Anal.)" at Meno 80e.
