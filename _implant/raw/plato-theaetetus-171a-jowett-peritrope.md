# Plato, Theaetetus 171a–b (Jowett tr.) — the "turning around" of Protagoras' measure doctrine on itself

Source: Plato, Theaetetus, tr. Benjamin Jowett, Project Gutenberg eBook
  #1726, passage at Stephanus 171a–b (the exchange with Theodorus after
  "And how about Protagoras himself?").
Original: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1726 (public domain translation);
  canonical locator Theaetetus 171a–b
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (plain-text file pg1726.txt fetched and read)

> "SOCRATES: And the best of the joke is, that he acknowledges the truth
> of their opinion who believe his own opinion to be false; for he admits
> that the opinions of all men are true."

> "SOCRATES: And does he not allow that his own opinion is false, if he
> admits that the opinion of those who think him false is true?
>
> THEODORUS: Of course."

Relied on for: the primary text of the argument SEP "Relativism" §4.3.1
calls the original self-refutation argument (peritrope).
Context: the Stephanus number is the one SEP "Relativism" gives
("Theaetetus: 171a–c"); the Gutenberg text carries no Stephanus margins, so
the locator rests on that citation and on the passage's content.
