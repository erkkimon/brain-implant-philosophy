# Sharp 1991 — Jung Lexicon: entries quoting Psychological Types with CW 6 paragraph numbers

Source: Daryl Sharp, *Jung Lexicon: A Primer of Terms & Concepts*, Toronto: Inner City Books, 1991 (Copyright ©1991 Daryl Sharp), entries "Attitude", "Auxiliary function", "Extraversion", "Function", "Individuation", "Primary function", "Typology". The quotations in the entries are from C. G. Jung, *Psychological Types*, Collected Works vol. 6, trans. H. G. Baynes rev. R. F. C. Hull, Princeton University Press / Routledge, 1971 (ISBN 9780691018133), cited by paragraph.
Original: http://www.psychceu.com/jung/sharplexicon.html (complete lexicon posted with the author's permission, per the page). Copyrighted; excerpts only.
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (curl, HTML stripped, grepped; bracketed CW locators as printed on the page)

> "From a great number of existing or possible attitudes I have singled out four; those, namely, that are primarily oriented by the four basic psychological functions: thinking, feeling, sensation, intuition. When any of these attitudes is habitual, thus setting a definite stamp on the character of an individual, I speak of a psychological type."  (entry "Attitude", quoting CW 6 ¶835, "Definitions")

> "A further division into two classes is permitted by the predominant trend of the movement of libido, namely introversion and extraversion."  (entry "Attitude", CW 6 ¶835)

> "Absolute sovereignty always belongs, empirically, to one function alone, and can belong only to one function"  (entry "Auxiliary function", quoting "General Description of the Types", CW 6 ¶667)

> "Very frequently, indeed as a general rule, a man identifies more or less completely with the most favoured and hence the most developed function. It is this that gives rise to the various psychological types."  (entry "Primary function", quoting "Definitions", CW 6 ¶763)

> "In general, it is the process by which individual beings are formed and differentiated; in particular, it is the development of the psychological individual as a being distinct from the general, collective psychology."  (entry "Individuation", quoting CW 6 ¶757, "Ibid." after a "Definitions" citation)

> "Sensation establishes what is actually present, thinking enables us to recognize its meaning, feeling tells us its value, and intuition points to possibilities as to whence it came and whither it is going in a given situation."  (entry "Function", quoting "A Psychological Theory of Types", CW 6 ¶958)

> "Jung believed that introversion and extraversion were present in everyone, but that one attitude-type is invariably dominant."  (entry "Extraversion", Sharp's own wording; preceding quotation cited "Psychological Typology," CW 6 ¶972)

> "Jung's model of typology grew out of an extensive historical review of the type question in literature, mythology, aesthetics, philosophy and psychopathology."  (entry "Typology", Sharp's own wording)

Relied on for: CW 6 paragraph locators for the definitions chapter (¶¶ 757, 763, 835), the auxiliary function (¶667) and the later lectures printed in the CW 6 appendix ("A Psychological Theory of Types", ¶958; "Psychological Typology", ¶972); Sharp's summary of the typology.
Context: Sharp is a Jungian analyst and the publisher of Inner City Books (per the page). The lexicon quotes Jung in the Collected Works translation, so its wording differs from the 1923 Baynes translation.
