> "The argument from free will, also called the paradox of free will or theological fatalism, contends that omniscience and free will are incompatible and that any conception of God that incorporates both properties is therefore inconceivable."  (lead; tagged "citation needed", February 2021)

Maimonides, as the article quotes him (Gorfinkle tr., *Eight Chapters*, pp. 99–100):

> "Does God know or does He not know that a certain individual will be good or bad? If thou sayest 'He knows', then it necessarily follows that the man is compelled to act as God knew beforehand how he would act, otherwise, God's knowledge would be imperfect."

> "Norman Swartz, however, contends that the above arguments commit what is known as modal fallacy."

> "Some arguments against the existence of God focus on the supposed incoherence of humankind possessing free will and God's omniscience."  ("Omniscience and free will")

> "Dan Barker suggests that this can lead to a "Free will Argument for the Nonexistence of God""  ("Free will argument for the nonexistence of God"; Barker 1997, FFRF, not read here)
