> "He told me that the most original idea, the guiding practice, was indeed taken from the Theravada Buddhist tradition of Vipassana or ‘Insight’ meditation."

> "Like many post-Vatican II South Asian Christians Tony had done a retreat with S.N.Goenka"

> "But what gave them all a particular life and energy was his adaptation of Buddhist mindfulness."

> "Tony intended the Buddhist mindfulness exercises as no more than an introduction to prayer, a preparation which would build up the right dispositions for listening to the Word of God."

> "When I teach my course on Indian spirituality he joins a distinguished list of Christian teachers who have engaged deeply with Asian forms of meditation, from Thomas Merton and Bede Griffiths to William Johnston and Hugo Enomiya Lassalle."

> "That process was bound up with the detailed scrutiny of the teaching of Indian theologians that issued in the publication of Dominus Iesus in 2001."

> "A number of friends and colleagues rallied to defend Tony’s memory, many making the point that a man who was a retreat director and not a systematic theologian could hardly be judged by the rigorous standards fit for a university professor. Tony was primarily, said one, a ‘wisdom teacher’."

> "Later collections of his writing were not the best of him. He was a speaker not a writer"

> "Yet this was the work of a Catholic bodhisattva – literally a ‘being for enlightenment’ – who had developed what the Buddhists call the ‘skilful means’"

Relied on for: Barnes's report that de Mello told him the guiding practice of Sadhana came from Vipassana; Barnes's description of Sadhana as Ignatian contemplation plus Buddhist mindfulness, with mindfulness as preparation for Christian prayer; his list of Christian teachers engaged with Asian meditation; his linking of the CDF process to Dominus Iesus; the defence that a retreat director is not a systematic theologian; his "Catholic bodhisattva" description.
Context: Memoir and appreciation by a Jesuit scholar of Indian religions who made a retreat under de Mello at Loyola Hall. The article's standfirst calls him "the controversial author of Sadhana".
