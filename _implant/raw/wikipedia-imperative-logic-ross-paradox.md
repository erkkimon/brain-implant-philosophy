> "Ross' paradox: Disjunction introduction poses a problem for imperative inference by seemingly permitting arbitrary imperatives to be inferred."  (List of paradoxes, Logic)

> "Alf Ross observed that applying the classical rule of disjunction introduction under the scope of an imperative operator leads to unintuitive (or apparently absurd) results."  (Imperative logic, Ross's paradox)

> "P1. Clean your room! C1. Therefore, clean your room or burn the house down!"  (same section)

> "The challenge is what we mean by a valid imperative inference. For valid declarative inference, the premises give you a reason to believe the conclusion. One might think that for imperative inference, the premises give you a reason to do as the conclusion says. While Ross's paradox seems to suggest otherwise, its severity has been subject of much debate."  (same section)

> "Some strands of this debate connect it to Hans Kamp's paradox of free choice, in which disjunction introduction leads to absurd conclusions when applied under the scope of a possibility modal."  (same section)
