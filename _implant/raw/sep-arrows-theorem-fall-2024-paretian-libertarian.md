# SEP "Arrow's Theorem" (Morreau) — weak Pareto, Sen 1970 and the "Paretian libertarian"

Source: Michael Morreau, "Arrow's Theorem", The Stanford Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri Nodelman (eds.);
  first published 2014-10-13, substantive revision 2019-11-26. Discussion
  of the weak Pareto condition (WP), and bibliography.
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/arrows-theorem/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (read in a text extraction of the archived page;
  every quotation below checked against it)

> "not debatable except perhaps on a philosophy of systematically denying people whatever they want"  (Arrow 1951 [1963]: 34, as quoted by Morreau)

> "But WP is not as harmless as it might seem, and in combination with U it tightly constrains the possibilities for social choice."

> "This is evident from Sen’s (1970) demonstration that these two conditions conflict with the idea that for each person there is a personal domain of states of affairs, within which his preferences must prevail in case of conflict with others’."

> "This important problem of the “Paretian libertarian” meanwhile has its own extensive literature."

Bibliography entry: Hylland, Aa., 1986, "The Purpose and Significance of
  Social Choice Theory: some general remarks and an application to the
  ‘Lady Chatterley problem’", in Elster and Hylland 1986: 45–73.

Relied on for: Morreau's assessment of WP and his name "Paretian
  libertarian"; the existence of the "Lady Chatterley problem" label in
  Hylland's 1986 title (not read).
Context: Directly before, Morreau quotes Arrow that WP is "not debatable
  except perhaps on a philosophy of systematically denying people whatever
  they want" (Arrow 1951 [1963]: 34); directly after, he introduces Sen's
  welfarism.
