## Fine 2021, chapter abstract (Crossref)

> "Socrates is often thought to have said that he knows that he knows nothing. The main source of evidence for this view is Plato’s Apology. Chapter 2 argues that Socrates does not say, or imply, that he knows that he knows nothing in a way that involves self-contradiction. Rather, he is saying either that he knows that he is not wise (where knowing something falls short of being wise with respect to it); or else that he is aware that he is not wise (where being aware of something falls short of knowing it). Neither claim involves or implies a contradiction."

## Fine 2008, note 1 (Crossref abstract field)

> "Or so we are told. I do not know when this claim was first attributed to Socrates. One early source often thought to do so is Cicero, Acad 1. 16: ‘nihil se scire dicat nisi id ipsum’."

> "For this reading of what Cicero says, see J. Annas, ‘Plato the Skeptic’, in P. Vander Waerdt (ed.), The Socratic Movement (Ithaca, NY, 1994), 309–40 at 310. For a recent challenge to this reading, see M. F. Burnyeat, ‘Antipater and Self-Refutation’, inB. Inwood and J. Mansfeld (eds.), Assent and Argument (Leiden, 1997), 277–310 at 290–300"
