> "These arguments closely resemble the arguments for two positions on the Newcomb Problem , a puzzle popularized among philosophers in Nozick. (The extent of the resemblance is made apparent in Lewis.)"  (§7)

> "(See Hurley (1991) and Bermúdez (2015), however, for arguments that the two puzzles are significantly different.)"  (§7)

> "The rather far-fetched scenario described in Newcomb's Problem initially led some to doubt the importance of the distinction between causal and evidential decision theory. Lewis argues that the link to the PD suggests that situations where the two decisions diverge are not so unusual"  (§7)

> "Despite the increasing sophistication of the discussion, however, there remain people committed to each view."  (§7)

Relied on for: the Prisoner's Dilemma between replicas as a Newcomb
problem (Lewis 1979), and the dissent from that identification (Hurley
1991, Bermúdez 2015); Kuhn's description of the debate as unresolved.
Context: §7 sets out the argument for cooperating with a replica (higher
expected payoff given correlation) against the argument for defecting
(causal independence plus dominance), then introduces Newcomb's problem as
the parallel case. "Lewis" in §7 is Lewis 1979, "Prisoner’s Dilemma is a
Newcomb Problem"; the Hurley and Bermúdez works were not read.
