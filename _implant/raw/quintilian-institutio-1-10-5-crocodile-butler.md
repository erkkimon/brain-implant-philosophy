# Quintilian, Institutio Oratoria I.10.5 — "horn" and "crocodile" problems (Butler tr., with Latin)

> "as for instance the elaborate subtleties of formal logic: not that acquaintance with the so called "horn" or "crocodile" problems can make a man wise, but because it is important that he should never trip even in the smallest trifles."  (I.10.5; the page break p. 163 falls after "acquaintance")

> "A crocodile, having seized a woman's son, said that he would restore him, if she would tell him the truth. She replied, "You will not restore him." Was it the crocodile's duty to give him up?"  (translator's note 98)

> "You have what you have not lost: you have not lost horns: therefore you have horns."  (translator's note 97)

> "sicut exquisitas interim ambiguitates: non quia keratinai aut krokodilinai possint facere sapientem, sed quia illum ne in minimis quidem oporteat falli."  (Latin, I.10.5; the Latin Library prints the Greek terms in transliteration)

Relied on for: Quintilian's mention of "crocodile" problems among the
logical subtleties a sage is taught; the translator's statement of the case
(mother, "You will not restore him", "duty").
Context: I.10 argues that the orator, like the philosophers' ideal sage,
should study subjects beyond literature (geometry, music); the logical
puzzles are an example of "comparatively trivial" subjects in the sage's
course. Quintilian himself does not state the crocodile case; note 98 does.
