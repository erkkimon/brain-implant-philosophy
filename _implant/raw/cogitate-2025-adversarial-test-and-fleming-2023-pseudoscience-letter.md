# Cogitate 2025 & Fleming et al. 2023 — the IIT/GNWT adversarial test and the "pseudoscience" letter

## A. The adversarial collaboration result

Source: Cogitate Consortium (Ferrante, Gorska-Klimowska, Henin, Hirschhorn,
  Khalaf, … Pitts, Mudrik & Melloni) (2025), "Adversarial testing of global
  neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness",
  Nature 642(8066): 133–142 (published online 30 April 2025); the abstract.
Original: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1
  (CC BY 4.0 — "This article is licensed under a Creative Commons
  Attribution 4.0 International License"; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (DOI verified by content negotiation; text read at
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1)

> "Here we present an open science adversarial collaboration directly
>  juxtaposing integrated information theory (IIT) and global neuronal
>  workspace theory (GNWT) via a theory-neutral consortium. The theory
>  proponents and the consortium developed and preregistered the
>  experimental design, divergent predictions, expected outcomes and
>  interpretation thereof. Human participants (n = 256) viewed
>  suprathreshold stimuli for variable durations while neural activity was
>  measured with functional magnetic resonance imaging,
>  magnetoencephalography and intracranial electroencephalography."
>  (Abstract)

> "These results align with some predictions of IIT and GNWT, while
>  substantially challenging key tenets of both theories. For IIT, a lack
>  of sustained synchronization within the posterior cortex contradicts the
>  claim that network connectivity specifies consciousness. GNWT is
>  challenged by the general lack of ignition at stimulus offset and
>  limited representation of certain conscious dimensions in the
>  prefrontal cortex."  (Abstract)

Preregistration (bibliographic only, DOI verified by content negotiation):
  Lucia Melloni, Liad Mudrik, Michael Pitts, Katarina Bendtz et al. (2023),
  "An adversarial collaboration protocol for testing contrasting
  predictions of global neuronal workspace and integrated information
  theory", PLOS ONE 18(2): e0268577, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0268577

## B. The open letter calling IIT pseudoscience

Source: IIT-Concerned, Stephen M. Fleming, Chris D. Frith, Melvyn A.
  Goodale, Hakwan Lau, Joseph LeDoux, Alan L. F. Lee, Matthias Michel,
  Adrian M. Owen, Megan A. K. Peters, Heleen A. Slagter, and c. 110 further
  signatories (2023), "The Integrated Information Theory of Consciousness
  as Pseudoscience", PsyArXiv preprint, posted 16 September 2023; pp. 1–2.
Original: https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/zsr78
  (copyrighted preprint; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (DOI verified by content negotiation; PDF read via
  https://osf.io/download/d4p6y/, the primary file of the OSF preprint)

The version read carries this header note:

> "This short statement was intended as a 'Letter to the Editor' -type of
>  article, with the general public as the intended audience. Following
>  feedback from the community we are currently in the process of revising
>  and expanding it into a longer article [...]; this is not the final
>  version."  (p. 1, header)

The central claim:

> "IIT is an ambitious theory, but some scientists have labeled it as
>  pseudoscience. According to IIT, an inactive grid of connected logic
>  gates that are not performing any useful computation can be
>  conscious—possibly even more so than humans; organoids created out of
>  petri-dishes, as well as human fetuses at very early stages of
>  development, are likely conscious according to the theory; on some
>  interpretations, even plants may be conscious. [...] Given its
>  panpsychist commitments, until the theory as a whole—not just some
>  hand-picked auxiliary components trivially shared by many others or
>  already known to be true—is empirically testable, we feel that the
>  pseudoscience label should indeed apply."  (p. 1; reference
>  superscripts omitted)

> "Our consensus is not that IIT and its variants decidedly lack
>  intellectual merit. But with so much at stake, it is essential to
>  provide a fair and truthful perspective on the status of the theory."
>  (p. 1)

> "by design the studies only tested some idiosyncratic predictions made by
>  certain theorists, which are not really logically related to the core
>  ideas of IIT"  (p. 1, on the adversarial collaboration)

Relied on for: what the 2025 adversarial collaboration actually reported
(mixed results, both theories "substantially" challenged, not a win for
either); the wording and the stated grounds of the 2023 "pseudoscience"
letter (untestability of the theory as a whole plus its counter-intuitive
attributions of consciousness) and its explicit disclaimer that IIT is not
being called meritless.
Context: the letter was written after the June 2023 public announcement of
the Cogitate results (before the preprint) and before the Nature paper. The
Nature abstract lists which brain areas carried information about
conscious content; the body reports the preregistered predictions and
their pass/fail status in detail, which is not reproduced. The popular
telling "IIT and GNWT were tested and both failed" and the telling "IIT was
partially supported" are each partial; the abstract's own summary is the
sentence quoted above.
