> "“White horses are horses” would be interpreted as in effect claiming that white horses and horses are “the same,” and “Oxen are not horses” as claiming that oxen and horses are “different.”"  (§ on same and different)

> "The simplest early Chinese model of language—probably the default starting point for the Mohists’ and other ancient Chinese thinkers’ reflections—is what we might call the one-name-one-thing model (cf. Hansen 1983)."  (§ on compound terms)

> "In classical Chinese, ‘oxen-and-horses’ ( niú mǎ 牛馬) and ‘white horse’ ( bái mǎ 白馬) have a similar formal structure, but the first denotes the sum of two kinds of things, the second a portion of one kind of thing."  (§ on kinds)

> "But whereas a white horse is white all over, a blind horse is not “blind all over”; only its eyes are blind (B3)."  (§ on compound terms)

> "White horses are horses; riding white horses is riding horses. Black horses are horses; riding black horses is riding horses."  (§ on parallelism)


Relied on for: the later Mohist analysis of compounds that the white-horse
readings draw on; Fraser's report of Hansen's one-name-one-thing model.
Context: the canons discussed (B3, B67, the "Lesser Selection") are Mohist,
not Gongsun Long's; Fraser treats them in their own right.
