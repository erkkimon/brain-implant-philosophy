> "Here, the most well-known solution concept is the so-called subgame perfect equilibrium, also known as backward induction in games of perfect information."  (§4.2)

> "Aumann (1995) showed that this epistemic condition implies that the players will play according to the backward induction solution while Stalnaker (1998) argued that this is not necessarily true."  (§4.2; "this epistemic condition" is rationality and common knowledge of rationality)

> "The crucial difference between these two results is the way in which they model the players’ belief change upon (hypothetically) learning that an opponent has deviated from the backward induction path."  (§4.2)

Relied on for: the Aumann/Stalnaker disagreement over whether common
knowledge of rationality yields backward induction, as the entry reports it.
Context: the entry's examples are the centipede game and small trees; it
does not discuss the chain-store game or the surprise examination by name.
