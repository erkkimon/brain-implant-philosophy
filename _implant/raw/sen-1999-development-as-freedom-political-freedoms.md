> "In this approach, expansion of freedom is viewed as both (1) the primary end and (2) the principal means of development."  (p. 36; the scan's OCR reads "(rt)" for "(1)")

> "In particular, I shall consider the following types of instrumental freedoms: (1) political freedoms, (2) economic facilities, (3) social opportunities, (4) transparency guarantees and (5) protective security."  (p. 38; OCR "{2)" for "(2)")

> "Political freedoms, broadly conceived (including what are called civil rights), refer to the opportunities that people have to determine who should govern and on what principles, and also include the possibility to scrutinize and criticize authorities, to have freedom of political expression and an uncensored press, to enjoy the freedom to choose between different political parties, and so on."  (p. 38)

> "Indeed, no substantial famine has ever occurred in a democratic country—no matter how poor."  (p. 51)

> "The significance of democracy lies, I have argued, in three distinct virtues: (1) its intrinsic importance, (2) its instrumental contributions, and (3) its constructive role in the creation of values and norms."  (p. 157)

> "Marx distinguishes between (to use the term used by Jon Elster) the formal freedom of the worker under capitalism and the real unfreedom of workers in pre-capitalist systems:"  (p. 29, Sen quoting V. K. Ramachandran's study of bonded labour)

Relied on for: Sen's classification of political freedoms as both
  constitutive of and instrumental to development; his list of
  instrumental freedoms; the famine claim as his; the "formal freedom"
  term as Ramachandran, via Sen, attributes it to Elster's reading of Marx.
Context: Sen calls the five-item list "by no means an exhaustive list".
  The famine sentence continues "This is because famines are extremely
  easy to prevent if the government tries to prevent them". In the
  Ramachandran passage the "formal freedom" of the wage worker is
  presented as an advance over bonded labour.
