# Roesler 2022 — report to the IAAP on archetype theory: Jung's references to Kant and Plato, inconsistencies, testability, Hillman

> "Jung is clearly influenced by Kant’s philosophy, which also emphasises that time, space, and causality are a priori forms of apperception ahead of any actual experience."  (p. 45)

> "In his theories around the archetype concept, Jung is an outspoken Platonian. He regards the archetypes as analogous to the platonic ideas and as preceding every experience."  (p. 46)

> "Knox previously outlined ‘four models that repeatedly emerge in the debate about archetypes’, identifying these as biological entities, organising mental frameworks of an abstract nature, core meanings containing representational content and eternal metaphysical entities (Knox 2003, p.24)."  (p. 60)

> "a concept that is thought to be transcendental and having no place in this world cannot be at the same time a biological entity and part of the genetic code (see also Knox 2003)."  (p. 60)

> "“In particular, demonstrating – on a case-by-case basis – that a proposed archetype could not have derived from personal or common cultural experience, and therefore must be genetic in origin, is exceedingly difficult, if not impossible” (Neher 1996, p.86)."  (p. 68)

> "“[...] one would have to say that the archetypes of the collective unconscious do not exist, in the sense that they cannot be said to be some place. They are not in the genome. .. one must look at the complete context within which we attribute their existence to a set of interacting phenomena.” (Hogenson 2003, p. 19)"  (p. 87)

> "This viewpoint has been excellently described by James Hillman (1971, 1975, 1983), who never made the mistake to confuse the psychology of the archetype with the natural sciences, but made clear from the beginnings of his archetypal psychology that he was speaking of a world of imaginations, of psyche, of soul."  (p. 258)

> "In this sense, archetype theory could be reformulated as being a theory of cultural symbolization processes of psychological transformations."  (p. 258)

Relied on for: a secondary report of Jung's Kantian and Platonic references; Knox's four models; Roesler's inconsistency and testability criticisms; the Neher 1996 and Hogenson 2003 statements as Roesler quotes them; Roesler's characterisation of Hillman's archetypal psychology (vocabulary/archetype-and-collective-unconscious.md).
Context: Roesler writes as a Jungian analyst and professor of clinical psychology commissioned by the IAAP; p. 45 quotes Jung calling archetypes "similar to the Kantian categories" (CW 10); p. 46 quotes Jolande Jacobi contrasting an "Aristotelian" and a "Platonian" reading; the p. 87 passage reports the 2001 IAAP congress debate between Hogenson and Anthony Stevens; p. 258 continues "we no longer argue that behind these stories, images and cultural products are some innate patterns".
