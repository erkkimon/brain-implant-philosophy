# Locke 1690 — Second Treatise §95 (consent founds political society) and §119 (express and tacit consent)

Source: John Locke, *Second Treatise of Government* (1690), ch. VIII "Of the
  Beginning of Political Societies", Sect. 95 and Sect. 119; Project Gutenberg
  eBook #7370.
Original: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/7370/pg7370.txt
  (public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-10-15 (plain text fetched with curl; quotations checked by
  script against the text)

> "MEN being, as has been said, by nature, all free, equal, and independent, no one can be put out of this estate, and subjected to the political power of another, without his own consent."  (Sect. 95)

> "There is a common distinction of an express and a tacit consent, which will concern our present case."  (Sect. 119)

> "And to this I say, that every man, that hath any possessions, or enjoyment, of any part of the dominions of any government, doth thereby give his tacit consent, and is as far forth obliged to obedience to the laws of that government, during such enjoyment, as any one under it;"  (Sect. 119)

Relied on for: consent as the only way into political subjection (§95); the
  express/tacit distinction and the scope Locke gives tacit consent (§119).
Context: §95 continues that men join "into a community for their
  comfortable, safe, and peaceable living", the majority then having "a right
  to act and conclude the rest"; §119 ends that tacit consent reaches "as far
  as the very being of any one within the territories of that government",
  whether by land, "a lodging only for a week", or "barely travelling freely
  on the highway".
