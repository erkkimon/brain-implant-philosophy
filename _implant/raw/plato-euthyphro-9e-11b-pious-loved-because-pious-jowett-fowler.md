## Euthyphro's amended definition (9e)

> "Well, I should say that what all the gods love is holy and, on the other hand, what they all hate is unholy."  (9e, Fowler)

> "Yes, I should say that what all the gods love is pious and holy, and the opposite which they all hate, impious."  (9e, Jowett)

## The question (10a)

> "Just consider this question:—Is that which is holy loved by the gods because it is holy, or is it holy because it is loved by the gods?"  (10a, Fowler)

> "The point which I should first wish to understand is whether the pious or holy is beloved by the gods because it is holy, or holy because it is beloved of the gods."  (10a, Jowett)

## Euthyphro takes the first alternative (10d)

> "It is loved because it is holy, not holy because it is loved?"  (10d, Fowler; Euthyphro: "I think so.")

> "It is loved because it is holy, not holy because it is loved?"  (10d, Jowett; Euthyphro: "Yes.")

## The conclusion drawn (10e–11a)

> "Then that which is dear to the gods and that which is holy are not identical, but differ one from the other."  (10e, Fowler)

> "For the one becomes lovable from the fact that it is loved, whereas the other is loved because it is in itself lovable."  (11a, Fowler)

> "Thus you appear to me, Euthyphro, when I ask you what is the essence of holiness, to offer an attribute only, and not the essence--the attribute of being loved by all the gods."  (11a, Jowett)

## Euthyphro's reaction (11b)

> "For somehow or other our arguments, on whatever ground we rest them, seem to turn round and walk away from us."  (11b, Jowett)

> "For whatever statement we advance, somehow or other it moves about and won't stay where we put it."  (11b, Fowler)

Relied on for: the wording of the question on the euthyphro-dilemma page;
that Euthyphro accepts "loved because it is holy" and that Socrates infers
that "dear to the gods" and "holy" differ; that the dialogue's own question
is about piety and the gods (plural), not about God's commands.
Context: Euthyphro is prosecuting his father for the death of a labourer;
his earlier definitions of piety (5d, 6e–7a) have been rejected because the
gods quarrel. Socrates then proposes the amendment "what all the gods love"
(9d), which Euthyphro accepts (9e). The carrying/being-carried analogy
(10a–c) precedes 10d. At 11b Socrates asks Euthyphro to "begin over again and
tell me what holiness is, no matter whether it is loved by the gods or
anything else happens it" (Fowler).
