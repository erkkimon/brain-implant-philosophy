## Foot's problem and the bystander (pp. 1395, 1397)

> "Some years ago, Philippa Foot drew attention to an extraordinarily interesting problem."  (p. 1395)

> "I will call her case Trolley Driver; let us now consider a case I will call Bystander at the Switch."  (p. 1397)

> "Nevertheless, my own feeling is that an ordinary person, a mere bystander, may intervene in such a case."  (p. 1397)

## The name (p. 1401)

> "Since I find it particularly puzzling that the bystander may turn his trolley, I am inclined to call this The Trolley Problem."  (p. 1401)

> "Those who find it particularly puzzling that the surgeon may not operate are cordially invited to call it The Transplant Problem instead."  (p. 1401)

> "It should be clear, I think, that "kill" and "let die" are too blunt to be useful tools for the solving of this problem."  (p. 1401)

## Distributive exemption and Fat Man (pp. 1408–1409)

> "We might speak here of a "distributive exemption," which permits arranging that something that will do harm anyway shall be better distributed than it otherwise would be"  (p. 1408)

> "Would it be permissible for you to do this? Everybody to whom I have put this case says it would not be. But why?"  (p. 1409, of Fat Man)
