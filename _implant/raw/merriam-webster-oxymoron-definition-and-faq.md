> "a combination of contradictory or incongruous words (such as cruel
> kindness ) broadly : something (such as a concept) that is made up of
> contradictory or incongruous elements"  (definition)

> "While we are loath to place restrictions on language use, oxymoron
> usually refers to a set of contradictory words (such as bittersweet )
> rather than to a contradictory person."  (FAQ "Can a person be an oxymoron?")

> "An oxymoron is a self-contradicting word or group of words (as in
> Shakespeare’s line from Romeo and Juliet , "Why, then, O brawling love! O
> loving hate!"). A paradox is a statement or argument that seems to be
> contradictory or to go against common sense, but that is yet perhaps
> still true—for example, "less is more.""  (FAQ "What is the difference between oxymoron and paradox?")

> "The phrase "Broadway rock musical" is an oxymoron . Broadway doesn't have
> the nerve to let the really hard stuff in the house. — Mark Coleman ,
> Rolling Stone , 26 Dec. 1996/ 9 Jan. 1997"  (examples in a sentence)
