> "The 20th century Reformed theologian Karl Barth opposed natural theology for much the same reason, and made his opposition to Emil Brunner’s version of the hybridist project clear in a book titled simply “Nein!” (Barth 1934)."

> "contemporary theologian Stanley Hauerwas espouses a fideistic view in the tradition of Pascal and Barth"

Bibliography entry (transcribed, not quoted): Barth, Karl, 1934, *Nein! Antwort an Emil Brunner*, München: C. Kaiser.

Relied on for: Barth's opposition to natural theology and the 1934 Nein! as the encyclopedia authors report it; Hauerwas placed by them in a tradition "of Pascal and Barth".
Context: "much the same reason" refers to the preceding sentence on Pascal, who "ultimately concluded ... that unaided reason is more likely to lead us to the false god “of philosophers and scholars” than to the true “God of Abraham, God of Isaac, God of Jacob”". The paragraph is about thinkers (Hamann, Jacobi, Kierkegaard) who hold theistic beliefs "as articles of faith rather than as teachings of reason".
