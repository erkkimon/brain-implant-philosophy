# Hegel 1820, Philosophy of Right (Dyde 1896) — preface, the three spheres, punishment, the empty-formalism passage on Kant, the ethical system

Source: G. W. F. Hegel, *Hegel's Philosophy of Right*, translated by S. W.
  Dyde (London: George Bell & Sons, 1896); translation of *Grundlinien der
  Philosophie des Rechts* (preface dated "Berlin, June 25th, 1820.").

  §101 (p. 98); §§133–135 with Note and Addition (pp. 127–129); §142 (p. 154).

> "What is rational is real; And what is real is rational."  (Author's Preface; set as two lines in Dyde)

> "As for the individual, every one is a son of his time; so philosophy also is its time apprehended in thoughts."  (Author's Preface)

> "The owl of Minerva takes its flight only when the shades of night are gathering."  (Author's Preface)

> "This is the sphere of abstract or formal right."  (§33 A, p. 39)

> "This is the sphere of morality."  (§33 B, p. 39)

> "The idea here is its absolutely universal existence, viz., ethical observance."  (§33 C, p. 39)

> "In this view of punishment it is much the same as when one raises a cane against a dog; a man is not treated in accordance with his dignity and honour, but as a dog."  (§99 Addition, p. 96, on Feuerbach's theory of punishment as a threat)

> "The doing away with crime is retribution, in so far as retribution is in its conception injury of an injury,"  (§101, p. 98)

> "It is the signal merit of the standpoint of the Kantian philosophy of action that it has made prominent this signification of duty."  (§133 Addition, p. 127)

> "In so far as duty is the universal or essence of the moral consciousness, and merely refers itself to itself within itself, it is only an abstract universality, and has for its characteristic an identity without content, an abstract positive, an absence of definite character."  (§135, p. 128)

> "Yet if this merely moral standpoint does not pass into the conception of the ethical system, this philosophical acquisition is reduced to empty formalism, and moral science is converted into mere rhetoric about duty for duty's sake."  (§135 Note)

> "From such a position can be derived no inherent doctrine of duties."  (§135 Note)

> "The rule that there should be no private property contains of itself no contradiction, nor does the proposition that this or that particular nation or family should not exist, or that no one should live at all."  (§135 Note)

> "Only if it is really fixed and assumed that private property and human life should exist and be respected, is it a contradiction to commit theft or murder."  (§135 Note)

> "Although we exalted the standpoint of the Kantian philosophy, in so far as it nobly insists that duty should accord with reason, yet its weakness is that it lacks all organic filling."  (§135 Addition)

> "But in the Kantian theory the rule is not to hand, and the criterion that there should be no contradiction produces nothing."  (§135 Addition)

> "The ethical system is the idea of freedom."  (§142, p. 154)

> "The ethical system is thus the conception of freedom developed into a present world, and also into the nature of self-consciousness."  (§142, p. 154)

Relied on for: Hegel's own wording of the Doppelsatz, the owl of Minerva and
"its time apprehended in thoughts"; the division of the work into abstract
right, morality and the ethical system (§33); his objection to punishment as
threat and his definition of retribution (§§99, 101); the empty-formalism
objection to Kant and its examples (§135); the ethical system as "the idea of
freedom" (§142, p. 154).
Context: Dyde renders *Sittlichkeit* as "the ethical system" / "ethical
observance" and *Moralität* as "morality"; Nisbet (1991, the edition SEP
cites) uses "ethical life". In §135 Note Hegel credits Kant with grounding
duty in "the infinite autonomy of the will" before the objection; the
Addition opens with praise ("we exalted the standpoint of the Kantian
philosophy"). In §99 Addition the dog image is aimed at Feuerbach's
threat-based theory; Duff and Hoskins (SEP "Legal Punishment" §6) quote a
different translation of the same Addition ("Hegel 1821: 246").
