# PhilPapers Survey 2009 — trolley problem, normative ethics and meta-ethics (target faculty)

Source: David Bourget & David J. Chalmers, The PhilPapers Surveys,
  "Preliminary Survey results", https://philpapers.org/surveys/results.pl
  (default view: population "Target faculty", response grain "coarse").
  The survey was run in November 2009 (per Chalmers' 2020 discussion
  document, https://www.consc.net/ppsurvey.html); the paper reporting it is
  Bourget & Chalmers, "What Do Philosophers Believe?", Philosophical
  Studies 170: 465–500, 2014.
Retrieved: 2026-09-26 (page fetched with curl, HTTP 200). The 2020
  survey's results pages (survey2020.philpeople.org) returned an anti-bot
  challenge / HTTP 403 on this date, so 2020 trolley figures are not
  recorded here.

> "Trolley problem (five straight ahead, one on side track, turn requires
>  switching, what ought one do?): switch or don't switch?
>  Accept or lean toward: switch 635 / 931 (68.2%)
>  Other 225 / 931 (24.2%)
>  Accept or lean toward: don't switch 71 / 931 (7.6%)"

> "Normative ethics: deontology, consequentialism, or virtue ethics?
>  Other 301 / 931 (32.3%)
>  Accept or lean toward: deontology 241 / 931 (25.9%)
>  Accept or lean toward: consequentialism 220 / 931 (23.6%)
>  Accept or lean toward: virtue ethics 169 / 931 (18.2%)"

> "Meta-ethics: moral realism or moral anti-realism?
>  Accept or lean toward: moral realism 525 / 931 (56.4%)
>  Accept or lean toward: moral anti-realism 258 / 931 (27.7%)
>  Other 148 / 931 (15.9%)"

Relied on for: what a 2009 sample of 931 target-faculty philosophers
reported on the switch case and on normative ethics / meta-ethics.
Context: a survey of opinion reports what respondents said, not what is
true; "Other" pools several answers (e.g. "insufficiently clear",
"no fact of the matter"); the question describes only the switch case,
not the footbridge.
