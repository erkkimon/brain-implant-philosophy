# Dewey 1922 — Human Nature and Conduct: habit, means and ends, deliberation as dramatic rehearsal, ends-in-view, experimental moral judgment, blame

Source: John Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct: An Introduction to Social
  Psychology, New York: Henry Holt and Company, 1922 (Project Gutenberg
  eBook #41386, released 2012-11-17). Locators are Part.Section as printed

> "Conduct is always shared; this is the difference between it and a physiological process. It is not an ethical "ought" that conduct _should_ be social. It _is_ social, whether bad or good."  (Part I, §I, "Habits as Social Functions")

> "Causes for an act always exist, but causes are not excuses."  (Part I, §I)

> "Questions of causation are physical, not moral except when they concern future consequences."  (Part I, §I)

> "Our entire tradition regarding punitive justice tends to prevent recognition of social partnership in producing crime; it falls in with a belief in metaphysical free-will."  (Part I, §I)

> "Means and ends are two names for the same reality. The terms denote not a division in reality but a distinction in judgment."  (Part I, §II, "Habits and Will")

> ""End" is a name for a series of acts taken collectively--like the term army. "Means" is a name for the same series taken distributively--like this soldier, that officer."  (Part I, §II)

> "We begin with a summary assertion that deliberation is a dramatic rehearsal (in imagination) of various competing possible lines of action."  (Part III, §III, "The Nature of Deliberation")

> "Deliberation is an experiment in finding out what the various lines of possible action are really like."  (Part III, §III)

> "Consequently ends arise and function within action. They are not, as current theories too often imply, things lying beyond activity at which the latter is directed."  (Part III, §VI, "The Nature of Aims")

> "Ends are foreseen consequences which arise in the course of activity and which are employed to give activity added meaning and to direct its further course."  (Part III, §VI)

> "In a strict sense an end-in-view is a _means_ in present action; present action is not a means to a remote end."  (Part III, §VI)

> "The doctrine of fixed ends not only diverts attention from examination of consequences and the intelligent creation of purpose, but, since means and ends are two ways of regarding the same actuality, it also renders men careless in their inspection of existing conditions."  (Part III, §VI)

> "Because there is no final recipe by which to decide this question all moral judgment is experimental and subject to revision by its issue."  (Part IV, §I, "The Good of Activity")

> "The reference in blame and every unfavorable judgment is prospective, not retrospective."  (Part IV, §III, "What is Freedom?")
