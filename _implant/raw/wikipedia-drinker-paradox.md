> "In any pub, there is a customer such that if that customer is drinking, everybody in the pub is drinking."  (List of paradoxes, "Logic")

> "is a theorem of classical predicate logic that can be stated as"  (article lead)

> "There is someone in the pub such that, if he or she is drinking, then everyone in the pub is drinking."  (lead)

> "It was popularised by the mathematical logician Raymond Smullyan, who called it the "drinking principle" in his 1978 book What Is the Name of this Book?"  (lead)

> "The apparently paradoxical nature of the statement comes from the way it is usually stated in natural language."  (lead)

> "It seems counterintuitive both that there could be a person who is causing the others to drink, or that there could be a person such that all through the night that one person were always the last to drink."  (lead)

> "for logics that demand relevant relationships between premise and consequent, unlike classical logic assumed here"  (lead, on relevance logic)

> "The formal statement of the theorem is timeless, eliminating the second objection because the person the statement holds true for at one instant is not necessarily the same person it holds true for at any other instant."  (lead; tagged "citation needed", July 2017)

> "where D is an arbitrary predicate and P is an arbitrary nonempty set."  (lead, after the formula ∃x∈P [D(x) → ∀y∈P D(y)])

> "What is important to the paradox is that the conditional in classical (and intuitionistic) logic is the material conditional."  (Explanation of paradoxicality)

> "even though their drinking may not have had anything to do with anyone else's drinking."  (Explanation of paradoxicality)

> "As "Smullyan's ‘Drinkers’ principle" or just "Drinkers' principle" it appears in H.P. Barendregt's "The quest for correctness" (1996), accompanied by some machine proofs."  (History and variations)

> "it is sometimes used to contrast the expressiveness of proof assistants."  (History and variations)

> "In the setting with empty domains allowed, the drinker paradox must be formulated as follows:"  (Non-empty domain)

> "If and only if there is someone in the pub, there is someone in the pub such that, if they are drinking, then everyone in the pub is drinking."  (Non-empty domain)
