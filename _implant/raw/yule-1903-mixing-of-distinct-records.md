> "It follows from the preceding work that we cannot infer independence of a pair of attributes within a sub-universe from the fact of independence within the universe at large."  (§5, p. 132)

> "But the converse theorem is also true; a pair of attributes does not necessarily exhibit independence within the universe at large even if it exhibit independence in every sub-universe"  (§5, p. 132)

> "Some given attribute might, for instance, be inherited neither in the male line nor the female line; yet a mixed record might exhibit a considerable apparent inheritance."  (§5, p. 133)

> "there is quite a large but illusory inheritance created simply by the mixture of the two distinct records."  (§5, p. 133; the sentence opens with the figures: 13/30 of the offspring of parents with the attribute have it, against 30 % of offspring in general — the OCR's rendering of the percentage, "434°/,", is garbled and is not quoted)

> "That is to say, there will be apparent association between A and B in the universe at large unless either A or B is independent of C."  (§5, p. 134)

> "Suppose for instance experiments were being made with some new antitoxin on patients of both sexes."  (§5, p. 134)

> "The general expression for (AB)—(AB)₀ shews how it may be avoided; it is only necessary to administer the antitoxin to the same proportion of patients of both sexes."  (§5, p. 134; subscript rendered)

Relied on for: Yule's statement that independence in every sub-universe
does not entail independence in the whole (the "Yule's association
paradox" case), his inheritance example, his condition (association in
the whole unless A or B is independent of C), and his antitoxin remedy.
Context: §5 closes by linking this "fictitious association" to the
"spurious correlation" for continuous variables discussed "by Professor
Pearson in a recent memoir" (footnote: Phil. Trans. A, Vol. 192, p. 277).
The paper speaks of disappearance/creation of association, not sign
reversal.
