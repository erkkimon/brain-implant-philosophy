# Dehaene, Lau & Kouider 2017 — C0/C1/C2 and whether machines could be conscious

Source: Stanislas Dehaene, Hakwan Lau & Sid Kouider (2017), "What is
  consciousness, and could machines have it?", Science 358(6362): 486–492
  (27 October 2017); abstract, section "Multiple meanings of
  consciousness", and the closing section.
Original: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan8871
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (DOI verified by content negotiation; text read in
  the author-deposited copy at
  https://escholarship.org/content/qt56b2q8h9/qt56b2q8h9.pdf)

> "We suggest that the word 'consciousness' conflates two different types
>  of information-processing computations in the brain: the selection of
>  information for global broadcasting, thus making it flexibly available
>  for computation and report (C1, consciousness in the first sense), and
>  the self-monitoring of those computations, leading to a subjective sense
>  of certainty or error (C2, consciousness in the second sense). We argue
>  that despite their recent successes, current machines are still mostly
>  implementing computations that reflect unconscious processing (C0) in
>  the human brain."  (Abstract, p. 486)

> "Our stance is based on a simple hypothesis: What we call 'consciousness'
>  results from specific types of information-processing computations,
>  physically realized by the hardware of the brain."  (closing section,
>  p. 491–492)

> "We contend that a machine endowed with C1 and C2 would behave as though
>  it were conscious [...]. Still, such a purely functional definition of
>  consciousness may leave some readers unsatisfied. [...] Are we leaving
>  aside the experiential component ('what it is like' to be conscious)?
>  Does subjective experience escape a computational definition? Although
>  those philosophical questions lie beyond the scope of the present paper,
>  we close by noting that empirically, in humans the loss of C1 and C2
>  computations covaries with a loss of subjective experience."  (closing
>  section, p. 492)

> "the empirical evidence is compatible with the possibility that
>  consciousness arises from nothing more than specific computations."
>  (final sentence, p. 492)

Relied on for: the C0/C1/C2 taxonomy; the authors' verdict that machines of
2017 mostly implement C0 (unconscious) computations; their explicit
bracketing of the "what it is like" question as beyond the paper's scope.
Context: the paper is a review of unconscious processing, global
availability (global workspace) and metacognition in humans and animals,
with a table of examples per level; the authors' surmise that "mere
information-theoretic quantities" do not suffice is stated in the closing
section in contrast to IIT. Page numbers are inferred from the Science
pagination (486–492); the eScholarship copy is the publisher's PDF.
