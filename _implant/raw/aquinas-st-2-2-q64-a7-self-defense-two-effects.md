# Aquinas, Summa Theologiae II-II q. 64 a. 7 — self-defence and the act with two effects

Source: Thomas Aquinas, Summa Theologiae II-II, q. 64 ("Of murder"), a. 7
  ("Whether it is lawful to kill a man in self-defense?"), respondeo ("I
  answer that") and reply to obj. 3; tr. Fathers of the English Dominican
  Province, Second and Revised Edition, 1920.
Original: https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3064.htm#article7
  (public domain translation, 1920; online edition by Kevin Knight)
Retrieved: 2026-09-27 (page fetched with curl, HTTP 200; article 7 text
  extracted and read in full; the page footer names the 1920 edition and
  the English Dominican translators)

> "Nothing hinders one act from having two effects, only one of which is intended, while the other is beside the intention."  (a. 7, respondeo)

> "Now moral acts take their species according to what is intended, and not according to what is beside the intention, since this is accidental as explained above (II-II:43:3; I-II:12:1)."  (a. 7, respondeo)

> "Accordingly the act of self-defense may have two effects, one is the saving of one's life, the other is the slaying of the aggressor. Therefore this act, since one's intention is to save one's own life, is not unlawful, seeing that it is natural to everything to keep itself in "being," as far as possible."  (a. 7, respondeo)

> "And yet, though proceeding from a good intention, an act may be rendered unlawful, if it be out of proportion to the end. Wherefore if a man, in self-defense, uses more than necessary violence, it will be unlawful: whereas if he repel force with moderation his defense will be lawful"  (a. 7, respondeo)

> "But as it is unlawful to take a man's life, except for the public authority acting for the common good, as stated above (Article 3), it is not lawful for a man to intend killing a man in self-defense, except for such as have public authority"  (a. 7, respondeo)

> "For this reason a cleric, though he kill a man in self-defense, is irregular, albeit he intends not to kill him, but to defend himself."  (a. 7, reply to obj. 3)

Relied on for: the double-effect-conditions argument page's "Earliest
  primary source" — the two-effects sentence, the specification of the act
  by what is intended, the proportion clause, and the restriction of
  intended killing to public authority.
Context: Objection 1 quotes Augustine to Publicola (Ep. xlvii) against
  killing "lest one be killed"; the sed contra cites Exodus 22:2. The
  respondeo ends by noting that soldiers and ministers of the judge "sin if
  they be moved by private animosity". Article 3 (cited in the respondeo)
  reserves the killing of evil-doers to public authority.
