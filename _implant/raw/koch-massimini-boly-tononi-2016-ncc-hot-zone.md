# Koch, Massimini, Boly & Tononi 2016 — the NCC and the "posterior hot zone"

Source: Christof Koch, Marcello Massimini, Melanie Boly & Giulio Tononi
  (2016), "Neural correlates of consciousness: progress and problems",
  Nature Reviews Neuroscience 17(5): 307–321 (May 2016); abstract and the
  glossary entry "Hot zone" (p. 309).
Original: https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2016.22
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (DOI verified by content negotiation; text read in
  the author-deposited copy at
  https://zenodo.org/record/439357/files/KochMassiminiTononi2016.pdf)

> "There have been a number of advances in the search for the neural
>  correlates of consciousness — the minimum neural mechanisms sufficient
>  for any one specific conscious percept. In this Review, we describe
>  recent findings showing that the anatomical neural correlates of
>  consciousness are primarily localized to a posterior cortical hot zone
>  that includes sensory areas, rather than to a fronto-parietal network
>  involved in task monitoring and reporting."  (Abstract, p. 307)

> "Hot zone: A temporo-parietal-occipital zone of the posterior cerebral
>  cortex where the best current anatomical candidates for full and
>  content-specific neural correlates of consciousness in the human brain
>  are located."  (glossary, p. 309)

> "We discuss how two popular candidate physiological markers of
>  consciousness — gamma activity and the P3b wave — have not shown any
>  predictive power"  (introduction, p. 307)

Relied on for: the definition of the NCC ("minimum neural mechanisms
sufficient for any one specific conscious percept"); the "posterior hot
zone" claim that is one side of the front-versus-back dispute later tested
by the Cogitate adversarial collaboration.
Context: the review favours no-report paradigms and sleep/dream studies as
evidence for a posterior locus; the authors include Tononi and Koch, both
IIT proponents, and the review's final sections argue for measures of
differentiation and integration (e.g. the perturbational complexity index)
as quantitative indices of consciousness. Those sections are not
reproduced. Page numbers are from the publisher's PDF as deposited.
