# Kolber 1994 — "Mental statism and the experience machine": the revised (reversed) question and status quo bias

Source: Adam Kolber, "Mental Statism and the Experience Machine", *Bard
  Journal of Social Sciences* 3 (nos. 3–4), 1994/95, pp. 10–17.
Original: SSRN https://ssrn.com/abstract=1322059 (copyrighted; excerpts
  only). Read in a scan (image-only PDF, 8 pp.) at
  https://pdf.stafforini.com/kolber-1994-mental-statism-experience.pdf ;
  the text was read from the page images (no text layer), so the
  quotations below were transcribed by eye from the scan.
Retrieved: 2026-09-27 (PDF fetched with curl, pages rendered with
  pdftoppm and read; no DOI; the journal and pages as printed on the scan
  and as cited by De Brigard 2010 and Buscicchi, IEP)

> "I will argue that it does not."  (p. 10; "it" = Nozick's thought experiment leading us to refute subjective mental state utilitarian theories)

> "Nozick asks us if we would connect ourselves to the experience machine (call this the "experience machine question", or EMQ)."  (p. 11)

> "To reformulate the EMQ, imagine that you are currently hooked up to an experience machine."  (p. 15)

> "The revised-EMQ then is, "Would you get off of an experience machine to which you are already connected?""  (p. 15)

> "Certainly, more people would stay on the machine than would agree to connect in the first place. This fact alone indicates that we may be biased in our response to the EMQ."  (p. 15)

> "In order for Nozick's argument to refute mental statism, it must go beyond affirmation of our status quo bias."  (p. 16)

> "The EMQ has failed to refute the idea that all that matters to us are subjective mental states."  (p. 17)

> "Maybe we should avoid experience machines, and maybe mental statism is false. Nevertheless, the experience machine thought experiment is insufficient to yield these results."  (p. 17)

Relied on for: Kolber's revised question (the source of the "reversed" or
  "reverse" machine), his prediction that more would stay on than connect,
  and his conclusion — reported as his.
Context: Kolber concedes the first of three criteria (that most people
  would not connect, p. 13), lists biases (fear of the unknown, distrust
  of the module's makers, "roller coaster effects", drug analogies,
  pp. 13–14), and then (p. 15) stipulates that life off the machine
  would contain worse mental states. He offers no survey data; De Brigard
  2010 (p. 56, note 10) says he read Kolber after finishing his draft.
