# Stein & Swan 2019 — the MBTI's Jungian basis and an evaluation of MBTI theory

Source: Randy Stein and Alexander B. Swan, "Evaluating the validity of Myers-Briggs Type Indicator theory: A teaching tool and window into intuitive psychology", *Social and Personality Psychology Compass* 13 (2), 2019, e12434.
Original: https://doi.org/10.1111/spc3.12434 (DOI verified by content negotiation 2026-10-01: Wiley, issue 2). Author-posted PDF read at https://swanpsych.com/publications/SteinSwanMBTITheory_2019.pdf. Copyrighted; excerpts only.
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (pdftotext, grepped; line-end hyphen in "under- standing" joined)

> "The MBTI is based on Carl Jung's theory of psychological types (Jung, 1921/1971)."  (§2, p. 2)

> "Jung posited that personalities are primarily either extraverted or introverted and then defined by four paired “functions”: sensing vs. intuition and feeling vs. thinking."  (§2, p. 2)

> "Two decades following Jung, Katharine Cook Briggs, and later her daughter, Isabel Briggs Myers, extended Jung's theory to add a fourth dimension, judging vs. perceiving (Quenk, 2009, p. 2)."  (§2, p. 2)

> "We find that the MBTI theory falters on rigorous theoretical criteria in that it lacks agreement with known facts and data, lacks testability, and possesses internal contradictions."  (Abstract, p. 1)

> "Jungian theory is not grounded in empirical data."  (Table 1, p. 3)

> "Jung's ideas were based on argumentation and anecdotal observation rather than systematic empirical evidence"  (§4.1.1, p. 4)

> "Indeed, he considered the unscientific nature of his theories to be a strength (McGowan, 1994)."  (§4.1.1, p. 4)

> "The Myers‐Briggs Company claims that reports of problems with the assessment's test–retest reliability are fixed in newer versions (Schaubhut, Herk, & Thompson, 2009)."  (§2, p. 2)

Relied on for: the MBTI as a derivative of *Psychological Types* (attributed to Stein & Swan); the Briggs/Myers addition of judging–perceiving; Stein & Swan's criticisms of MBTI theory and of its Jungian roots; the publisher's reliability claim as they report it.
Context: the paper is framed as a teaching reference on what makes a valid psychological theory; it evaluates MBTI theory against Shaw and Costanzo's (1982) criteria. It notes the reliability claim is "difficult (and costly ...) to verify independently".
