# Jaworska & Tannenbaum (SEP) — "The Grounds of Moral Status": definition, Kant's reasons against cruelty, contractualist grounds and the opt-out argument

Source: Agnieszka Jaworska and Julie Tannenbaum, "The Grounds of Moral
  Status", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), ed.
  Edward N. Zalta & Uri Nodelman; first published 14 March 2013,

  intro, §2.4, §6.

> "An entity has moral status if and only if it matters (to some degree) from the moral point of view for its own sake."  (intro)

> "Kant’s remarks about the treatment of animals can be interpreted along these lines. He argued that we have reasons to avoid cruelty to animals, and thus to treat animals better than their (lack of) moral status implies, since otherwise we might develop psychological propensities that could lead us analogously to mistreat humans who have FMS (Kant [LE], pp. 212–13)."  (§2.4)

> "Several contractualists (see section 6 below and the entry on contractualism) have argued that one may reasonably opt out of any agreement that does not afford sufficient moral status to one’s children and others one cares about, including those who are severely cognitively impaired (Morris 2011, pp. 265–267 and Carruthers 2011, pp. 387–394)."  (§2.4)

> "Critics and proponents disagree whether these considerations can establish reasons (e.g., reasons not to interfere) that are for these beings’ sake; hence, it is unclear whether they can establish the moral status of the beings in question."  (§2.4)

> "Contractualist conceptions of morality attempt to derive all of morality, together with full moral status of individuals, from a hypothetical reciprocal agreement (entered into under conditions specified variously by different versions) among rational agents"  (§6)

> "These views work well to explain why the capacity to enter and adhere to such a reciprocal agreement, which includes the capacity to both demand moral status for oneself and to respect the moral status of others by assuming duties and responsibilities, would confer FMS on an individual."  (§6)

Relied on for: a definition of moral status; the contractualist route to
moral status and the claim (Morris 2011; Carruthers 2011) that
contractors could opt out of agreements not protecting the severely
cognitively impaired; the authors' remark that critics and proponents
disagree whether such reasons are for those beings' sake.
Context: FMS is the entry's abbreviation for "full moral status". The
Carruthers item cited is "Animal Mentality: Its Character, Extent, and
Moral Significance", in Beauchamp & Frey (eds.), The Oxford Handbook of
Animal Ethics (2011), pp. 373–406 (not read). §2.4 is about reasons to
treat a being as if it had a moral status it lacks.
