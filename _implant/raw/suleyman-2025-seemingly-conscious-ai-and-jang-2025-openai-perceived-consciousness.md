# Suleyman 2025 & Jang 2025 — "Seemingly Conscious AI" (Microsoft AI) and "ontological vs perceived consciousness" (OpenAI)

## A. Suleyman, "Seemingly Conscious AI is Coming"

Source: Mustafa Suleyman (CEO, Microsoft AI), "Seemingly Conscious AI is
  Coming", personal site essay, page-dated "19 August 2025"; sections
  "Summary" and "Seemingly conscious AI".
Original: https://mustafa-suleyman.ai/seemingly-conscious-ai-is-coming
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (HTML fetched with curl, HTTP 200; the page's
  "Recent Articles" sidebar, dated © 2026, now also lists a later piece
  "A warning about 'model welfare'")

The definition:

> "In this essay I want to discuss what I'll call, 'Seemingly Conscious
>  AI' (SCAI), one that has all the hallmarks of other conscious beings
>  and thus appears to be conscious. It shares certain aspects of the idea
>  of a 'philosophical zombie' (a technical term!), one that simulates all
>  the characteristics of consciousness but internally it is blank. My
>  imagined AI system would not actually be conscious, but it would
>  imitate consciousness in such a convincing way that it would be
>  indistinguishable from a claim that you or I might make to one another
>  about our own consciousness."  (Summary)

The position:

> "The arrival of Seemingly Conscious AI is inevitable and unwelcome."
>  (Summary)

> "It's highly likely that some people will argue that these AIs are not
>  only conscious, but that as a result they may suffer and therefore
>  deserve our moral consideration. To be clear, there is zero evidence of
>  this today and some argue there are strong reasons to believe it will
>  not be the case in the future. Yet the consequences of many people
>  starting to believe an SCAI is actually conscious deserve our immediate
>  attention."  (Summary)

> "The debate about whether AI is actually conscious is, for now at least,
>  a distraction. It will seem conscious and that illusion is what'll
>  matter in the near term."  (Seemingly conscious AI, reason 2 of 3)

> "Simply put, my central worry is that many people will start to believe
>  in the illusion of AIs as conscious entities so strongly that they'll
>  soon advocate for AI rights, model welfare and even AI citizenship.
>  This development will be a dangerous turn in AI progress and deserves
>  our immediate attention. We must build AI for people; not to be a
>  digital person."  (section beginning "In this context, I'm growing
>  more and more concerned")

Sidebar teaser for the later essay (title "A warning about 'model
welfare'"; body not read):

> "AIs are not conscious. They do not feel, experience, or suffer.
>  Training a model to believe it might be a moral patient, as
>  Anthropic's constitution does, makes the alignment and containment
>  challenge far harder."  (Recent Articles teaser)

## B. Jang, "Some thoughts on human-AI relationships"

Source: Joanne Jang (head of model behavior & policy, OpenAI), "Some
  thoughts on human-AI relationships — and how we're approaching them at
  OpenAI", Substack newsletter *Reservoir Samples*, page-dated "Jun 05,
  2025"; section "Untangling 'AI consciousness'".
Original: https://reservoirsamples.substack.com/p/some-thoughts-on-human-ai-relationships
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (HTML fetched with curl, HTTP 200)

> "If users were to ask our models on whether they're conscious, our
>  stance as outlined in the Model Spec is for the model to acknowledge
>  the complexity of consciousness – highlighting the lack of a universal
>  definition or test, and to invite open discussion. (*Currently, our
>  models don't fully align with this guidance, often responding 'no'
>  instead of addressing the nuanced complexity. [...])"

> "1. Ontological consciousness: Is the model actually conscious, in a
>  fundamental or intrinsic sense? Views range from believing AI isn't
>  conscious at all, to fully conscious, to seeing consciousness as a
>  spectrum on which AI sits, along with plants and jellyfish.
>  2. Perceived consciousness: How conscious does the model seem, in an
>  emotional or experiential sense? [...]"

> "Ontological consciousness isn't something we consider scientifically
>  resolvable without clear, falsifiable tests, whereas perceived
>  consciousness can be explored through social science research. [...]
>  For that reason, we prioritize focusing on perceived consciousness:
>  the dimension that most directly impacts people and one we can
>  understand through science."

Relied on for: the SCAI definition and Suleyman's explicit
"zero evidence"/"distraction" stance; OpenAI's two-axis distinction and
its choice to work on the perceived axis while treating the ontological
one as not currently resolvable; both lab positions as contrasts to
Anthropic's model-welfare programme.
Context: Suleyman's essay is a personal blog post by a company CEO, not a
Microsoft policy document; it argues from the "psychosis risk" of users
believing in AI consciousness rather than from a theory of
consciousness. Jang's note is a personal newsletter by an OpenAI
employee describing company stance ("our stance as outlined in the
Model Spec"). Neither cites the scientific theories surveyed elsewhere
in this batch. The Suleyman page has been updated since the stated
August 2025 date (it carries a 2026 copyright and a later article
list), so the exact wording may differ from the original posting.
