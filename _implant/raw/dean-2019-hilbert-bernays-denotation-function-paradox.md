# Dean 2019 — Hilbert & Bernays 1939 on the denotation function, and later readings

Source: Walter Dean, "Incompleteness via Paradox and Completeness", The
  Review of Symbolic Logic 13(3), 2020 (online 2019-05-23), pp. 541–592;
  quoted from the author's accepted manuscript, §2 "Historical setting"
  and footnote 10.

  negotiation: title, Review of Symbolic Logic 13(3): 541–592); accepted

Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (WRAP PDF through pdftotext)

> "The Grundlagen (1939, pp. 262-268/271-277) develops a related paradox, which Hilbert and Bernays liken to that of Richard (1905) based on the assumption that there exists a definable denotation function d(x) which provably satisfies"  (§2; the displayed formula (2.2) is d applied to the code of t equals t, for all closed terms t of the language L_F, "inclusive of those containing the least number operator")

> "Hilbert & Bernays denote this function with the symbol e(x) rather than d(x)."  (footnote 10)

> "Subsequent authors (e.g. Priest, 1997a; Read, 2016) have tended to view the inconsistency resulting from the assumption that such a function is definable as a sui generis antinomy – the so-called denotational paradox."  (footnote 10)

> "But as Hilbert & Bernays themselves observe, the underlying argument is similar to their prior demonstration (1934, p. 330/335) that the denotation function for primitive recursive terms cannot itself be primitive recursive."  (footnote 10)

> "Wang (1955) later showed how this result can be transformed into an incompleteness theorem by observing that the denotation function for first order-arithmetical terms (including the µ-operator) can be formalized in second-order arithmetic and then invoking the Arithmetized Completeness Theorem"  (footnote 10)

> "Although Hilbert & Bernays (1939) ultimately group these paradoxes together as semantische, the reaction which they register to them varies in detail across the sources we have been considering."  (§2)

Relied on for: the locus in Hilbert & Bernays 1939 (pp. 262–268 in the
first edition / 271–277 in the second, per Dean's double pagination);
Hilbert & Bernays' own likening of it to Richard's paradox; the reading of
it as a variant of a diagonal argument; Wang 1955's incompleteness
version (as Dean reports it).
Context: Dean's subject is how paradoxes become incompleteness proofs via
the Arithmetized Completeness Theorem; the passage sits in his account of
the paradoxes in Hilbert & Bernays' Grundlagen, just before their Liar
discussion (1939, §5.1a). "Read, 2016" is the manuscript later published
as Read 2019. The manuscript's "semantische" was rendered with a
hyphenation break; joined here.
