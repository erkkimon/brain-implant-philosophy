> "These premises are inconsistent in SDL. First, they are simply excluded by NC (OB p → ¬OB ¬p)."  (§6.4, on OB j and OB ¬j)

> "Non-aggregative deontic logics, which invalidate OB-C (aka the Aggregation rule for OB)."  (§6.4, the first of three classes of conflict-tolerant deontic logics)

> "However, (DC) in combination with (OB-C) yields triviality as soon as we endorse a minimalistic version of Kant’s law that ought implies can"  (§6.4; DC is "OB p & OB q & ¬◇(p & q)", OiC is "OB p → ◇p")

> "Let us note that a long-ignored and challenging further puzzle for conflicting obligations, sometimes called “van Fraassen’s Puzzle” and inspired by van Fraassen 1973 has deservedly received increased attention: Horty 1994, 2003; van der Torre and Tan 2000; McNamara 2004a; Hansen 2004; and Goble 2005."  (§6.4)

> "Originating in the work on conflict-tolerant deontic logics (van Fraassen 1973, cf. Hansen 2008 and Hansen 2013), this route became most influential under the banner of Input/Output logic"  (§6.1, on a primitive notion of "norms" generating obligations syntactically)

Relied on for: the deontic-logic counterpart of the two consistency
arguments (NC is McConnell's PC; OB-C is agglomeration; OiC is ought
implies can); non-aggregative logics as the formal form of rejecting
agglomeration; van Fraassen 1973 as the origin of "van Fraassen's Puzzle"
and of conflict-tolerant, norm-based deontic logic.
Context: §6.4 goes on to paraconsistent and inheritance-free logics and to
adaptive logics (Goble 2013), and notes that most conflict-tolerant logics
treat only conflicts between logically contradictory obligations.
