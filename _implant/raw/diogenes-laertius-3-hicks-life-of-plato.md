# Diogenes Laertius, Lives III.2, 4, 6–7 (Hicks tr.) — Plato's birth and death dates as reported by ancient chronographers, family, pupil of Socrates, the Academy

Source: Diogenes Laertius, Lives of Eminent Philosophers, Book III
  ("Plato"), §§2, 4, 6, 7, tr. Robert Drew Hicks (Loeb Classical Library,
  1925), as transcribed on English Wikisource; footnotes are Hicks's.

> "Apollodorus in his Chronology fixes the date of Plato's birth in the 88th Olympiad, on the seventh day of the month Thargelion, the same day on which the Delians say that Apollo himself was born."  (3.2)

> "He died, according to Hermippus, at a wedding feast, in the first year of the 108th Olympiad, in his eightyfirst year."  (3.2; Hicks's footnote: "427-347 B.C.")

> "Neanthes, however, makes him die at the age of eighty-four."  (3.3)

> "He had two brothers, Adeimantus and Glaucon, and a sister, Potone, who was the mother of Speusippus."  (3.4)

> "From that time onward, having reached his twentieth year (so it is said), he was the pupil of Socrates."  (3.6)

> "Having returned to Athens, he lived in the Academy, which is a gymnasium outside the walls, in a grove named after a certain hero, Hecademus"  (3.7)

Relied on for: the plato thinker page — the ancient dating (and its
variants), the brothers who are speakers in the Republic, the report that
he was Socrates' pupil from about twenty, the Academy.
Context: 3.1 gives his parents Ariston and Perictione and her descent from
Solon; 3.3 adds a reported birth "in the archonship of Ameinias, the year
of Pericles' death" (Hicks: 429 B.C.), so Diogenes himself reports
discrepant dates; 3.4 reports that his name was originally Aristocles;
3.6 reports travels after Socrates' death (Megara, Cyrene, Italy, Egypt).
Diogenes writes centuries later and cites his sources by name.
