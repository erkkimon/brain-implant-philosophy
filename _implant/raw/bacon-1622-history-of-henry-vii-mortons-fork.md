# Bacon 1622 — the "tradition of a dilemma" of Bishop Morton (the fork) in the History of the Reign of King Henry VII

Source: Francis Bacon, *The Historie of the Raigne of King Henry the Seventh*
  (London, 1622), p. 101 (account of the parliament of 1491 and the
  benevolence for the French war). Modernised spelling quoted from the
  reprint in *The Moral and Historical Works of Lord Bacon*, ed. Joseph Devey
  (London: George Bell, 1877), "History of the Reign of King Henry the
  Seventh", p. 377.
Original: 1622 first edition scan, https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1475-1640_the-historie-of-the-raig_bacon-francis-viscount_1622
  (passage on leaf 106, printed page 101 per the item's page_numbers.json);
  1877 Devey edition, https://archive.org/details/moralhistoricalw00baco_1
  (passage between the printed page numbers 376 and 378 of the OCR text)
  (public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-10-09 (OCR text _djvu.txt of both items via archive.org;
  the 1622 page located through the djvu.xml leaf map and page_numbers.json;
  quotation transcribed from the 1877 OCR, "liis" corrected to "his" and
  "wdth" to "with" against the 1622 text, which reads "his" and "with")

> "There is a tradition of a dilemma, that bishop Morton the chancellor used, to raise up the benevolence to higher rates; and some called it his fork, and some his crotch."  (1877 ed., p. 377)

> "For he had couched an article in the instructions to the commissioners who were to levy the benevolence; That if they met with any that were sparing, they should tell them, that they must needs have, because they laid up: and if they were spenders, they must needs have, because it was seen in their port and manner of living."  (1877 ed., p. 377; the 1877 text closes this sentence with a stray right quotation mark)

> "So neither kind came amiss."  (1877 ed., p. 377)

The 1622 OCR of the same sentence reads, uncorrected: "ſome called it his
  Forke, and ſome his (rorch." (p. 101); the rest of the 1622 OCR of the
  passage differs from the 1877 text in spelling and OCR noise (e.g.
  "Chancelonr", "Benexolence", "ſpenders", "So neither kinde came anulſle").
  Spacing before colons and semicolons, present in the 1877 OCR, is
  normalised in the quotations above.

Context (1877 text, same page): the parliament consents "that commissioners
  should go forth for the gathering and levying of a benevolence from the
  more able sort"; "This tax, called a benevolence" (the next sentence); Bacon says the benevolence was abolished by Richard the
  Third by act of parliament and "now revived by the king, but with consent
  of parliament", and that London "contributed nine thousand pounds and
  better; and that chiefly levied upon the wealthier sort". The paragraph
  then turns to the statutes of "a parliament of war". Bacon names only
  Morton for the dilemma; Fox is not named in the passage.

Relied on for: the earliest report read of the argument and of the names
  "fork" and "crotch"; Bacon's framing as "a tradition"; the two horns
  (sparing / spenders) and the conclusion "neither kind came amiss".
