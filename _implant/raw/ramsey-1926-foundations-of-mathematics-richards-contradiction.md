# Ramsey 1926 — Richard's contradiction in Group B (not purely logical), Peano's dismissal, and Ramsey's solution by orders of meaning

List of contradictions as given: Group A (1)–(3): Russell's class, the
relations version, Burali-Forti; Group B (4)–(8): "I am lying", the
least integer not nameable in fewer than nineteen syllables, the least
indefinable ordinal, "(7) Richard's Contradiction.", Weyl's
"heterologisch".

> "But the contradictions of Group B are not purely logical, and cannot be stated in logical terms alone ; for they all contain some reference to thought, language, or symbolism, which are not formal but empirical terms."  (p. 20)

> "For instance, Peano decided that " Exemplo de Richard non pertine ad Mathematica, sed ad linguistica and therefore dismissed it. But such an attitude is not completely satisfactory."  (p. 21; the OCR shows no closing quotation mark after "linguistica")

> "the mathematician dismisses them by saying that the fault must lie in the linguistic elements, but the linguistician may equally well dismiss them for the opposite reason, and the contradictions will never be solved."  (p. 21)

> "All these result from the obvious ambiguity of ‘ naming ’ and ‘ defining ’."  (p. 48, of the nineteen-syllable integer, the least indefinable ordinal and "Richard's Paradox"; OCR spacing inside the quotes kept)

> "The sense in which it means must be made precise by fixing its order ; the name or definition involving all such names or definitions will be of a higher order, and this removes the contradiction."  (p. 48)

> "My solutions of these contradictions are obviously very similar to those of Whitehead and Russell, the difference between them lying merely in our different conceptions of the order of propositions and fractions."  (p. 48)

Relied on for: Ramsey's classification of Richard's contradiction in
Group B; his report of Peano's verdict and his reply; his own solution.
Context: ch. I ends with Ramsey's request that opponents of the
Principia view locate the fault in "what Peano called linguistics, but what I should prefer to call epistemology"; the p. 48 passage is the last of a list of
Group B solutions in ch. III, followed by the chapter summary ("a solution
of the contradictions of group B which rests on and explains the fact that
they all contain some epistemic element").
