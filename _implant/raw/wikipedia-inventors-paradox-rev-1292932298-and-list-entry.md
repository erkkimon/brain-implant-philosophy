List entry (rev. 1376699902):

> "Inventor's paradox: It is easier to solve a more general problem that covers the specifics of the sought-after solution."

Article (rev. 1292932298):

> "Instead of solving a specific type of problem, which would seem intuitively easier, it can be easier to solve a more general problem, which covers the specifics of the sought-after solution."  (lead)

> "The inventor's paradox has been used to describe phenomena in mathematics, programming, and logic, as well as other areas that involve critical thinking."  (lead)

> "As an example in applying the same logic, it may be harder to solve a 23-case problem than it would be to solve an n-case problem, and then apply it to the case where n=23."  (Examples, Programming; cited to Bentley, Programming Pearls, 2000, p. 29; tagged "Expansion needed")

The article's "See also" lists "Architecture astronaut, related to the converse problem where abstraction is taken too far." Its categories include "Decision-making paradoxes" and "1945 introductions".
