# Moore 1903 — Principia Ethica §§40, 47–48: the naturalistic fallacy charged against Mill; quality of pleasure

Source: G. E. Moore, *Principia Ethica* (Cambridge: Cambridge University
  Press, 1903; reprint 1922), ch. III "Hedonism", §§40 and 48.
Original: Project Gutenberg eBook #53430, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/53430
  (public domain in the USA per Project Gutenberg; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (plain text fetched from
  https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/53430/pg53430.txt; §§39–48 read)

> "There, that is enough. That is my first point. Mill has made as naïve and artless a use of the naturalistic fallacy as anybody could desire."  (§40)

> "The fact is that ‘desirable’ does not mean ‘able to be desired’ as ‘visible’ means ‘able to be seen.’ The desirable means simply what ought to be desired or deserves to be desired; just as the detestable means not what can be but what ought to be detested and the damnable what deserves to be damned."  (§40; "ought" and "deserves" italic in the source)

> "I have pointed out that, if you say, as Mill does, that quality of pleasure is to be taken into account, then you are no longer holding that pleasure alone is good as an end, since you imply that something else, something which is not present in all pleasures, is also good as an end."  (§48; "alone", "not", "also" italic in the source)

Relied on for: Moore's charge that Mill's proof commits the naturalistic
  fallacy and his argument that qualitative hedonism is not hedonism.
Context: §40 quotes Mill's visible/audible/desirable passage at length
  before the first excerpt; §47 quotes Mill's competent-judges test, and
  §48 continues with a colour analogy ending in Bentham's "pushpin is as
  good as poetry".
