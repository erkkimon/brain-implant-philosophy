# Quiggin 2005 — the Y2K scare: remediation as largely wasted

Source: John Quiggin, "The Y2K scare: Causes, Costs and Cures", Australian
  Journal of Public Administration 64(3), September 2005, pp. 46–55.
Original: doi:10.1111/j.1467-8500.2005.00451.x (verified by content
  negotiation 2026-10-09); author PDF read at
  https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/8/1676/files/2017/10/y2kscareanalysis-2e0rs1c.pdf
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-09 (PDF through pdftotext, grepped)

> "Most of this expenditure can be seen, in retrospect, to have been unproductive or, at least, misdirected."  (abstract, p. 46)

> "A smaller minority dissented for the opposite reason, claiming that the whole problem had been grossly overstated, and most of the money spent on remediation had been wasted. Australian Y2K sceptics included Fist (1998a; 1998b and Quiggin (1999a;1999b)."  (p. 46)

> "Most importantly, it became apparent that Y2K-related problems had been insignificant even where little or no remediation effort had been undertaken."  (p. 46)

> "In this paper, it will be argued that, although some relatively minor problems were prevented, and some collateral benefits were realised, most money spent specifically on Y2K compliance exercises was wasted. Moreover, it will be argued, evidence available early in 1999, should have been sufficient to justify the adoption of a less costly strategy of ‘fix on failure’."  (p. 46)

> "The absence of any serious Y2K problems could always be attributed to the success of the remediation program."  (section on incentives)

Relied on for: the case that Y2K remediation was mostly unnecessary; the
low/no-remediation comparison; the incentive-asymmetry argument.
Context: Quiggin reports expenditure "estimated at $A12 billion in Australia
(Campbell 2000) and as much as $US 500 billion for the world as a whole";
he names Italy among countries that did little, and discusses the
precautionary principle; he was himself a Y2K sceptic in 1999.
