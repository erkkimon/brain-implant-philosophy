> "No-show paradox: A situation in some voting systems where voting for one's candidate could cause them to lose, as opposed to not showing up to vote."  (List of paradoxes, "Decision theory")

> "The participation criterion is a voting system criterion that says candidates should never lose an election as a result of receiving too many votes in support."  (Participation criterion, lead; cites Doron & Kronick 1977 and Ray 1986)

> "Voting systems that fail the participation criterion exhibit the no-show paradox, where a voter is effectively disenfranchised by the electoral system because turning out to vote could make the result worse for them; such voters are sometimes referred to as having negative vote weights, particularly in the context of German constitutional law, where courts have ruled such a possibility violates the principle of one man, one vote."  (Participation criterion, lead; cites Moulin 1988, Pukelsheim 2014, Die Zeit 2013 and BVerfG 2 BvC 1/07, 3 July 2008)

> "Studies suggest such failures may be empirically rare, however."  (Participation criterion, "Condorcet methods"; cites Mohsin et al., AAMAS 2023, on 306 election datasets)
