## Preamble (Ballarin's assessments)

> "Ruth Barcan Marcus (1921–2012) was one of the most influential philosophers of the second half of the twentieth century."  (preamble)

> "Her first 1946 publication contains the first published construction of formal systems of quantified modal logic."  (preamble)

> "In the fifties and sixties, in formidable opposition to Quine, she crucially contributed to establishing the legitimacy of modal logic and thus of modal metaphysics."  (preamble)

> "Her philosophical views—about language, essentialism, belief, and moral dilemmas—are deeply naturalistic and eschew rationalistic idealizations."  (preamble)

> "In this entry Ruth Barcan Marcus is sometimes called Barcan, sometimes Marcus, and sometimes Barcan Marcus, mainly according to the temporal context"  (preamble)

## §1 Life

> "The main written source of information on Ruth Barcan Marcus’s personal and professional life is her Dewey Lecture “A Philosopher’s Calling” delivered at the Eastern APA meeting on December 29, 2009 (Marcus 2010)."  (§1)

> "Marcus was born Ruth Charlotte Barcan in New York on August 2, 1921"  (§1)

> "She started Yale’s graduate program in 1942 and received her PhD in 1946."  (§1)

> "In 1973 Marcus agreed to join and help rebuild the Yale philosophy department"  (§1)

> "Marcus retired from Yale in 1992"  (§1)

> "She died in New Haven on February 19, 2012."  (§1)

Also §1 (paraphrase, not quoted on a page without checking): NYU (philosophy
and mathematics), J.C.C. McKinsey as teacher, F.B. Fitch as supervisor at
Yale; dissertation "A Strict Functional Calculus" (1946); post-doctoral
study with Carnap in Chicago from 1947; chair of the new philosophy
department at the University of Illinois Chicago campus from 1964;
Northwestern 1970–1973; president of the Association for Symbolic Logic
(1983–86).

## §2.1 Quantified modal logic

> "the Barcan formula, as Prior will later dub it (1956: 60)."  (§2.1)

> "System S2 is not strong enough to prove BF, which is thus assumed as an axiom."  (§2.1)

Context: Axiom 11 of Barcan's 1946 quantified S2 is "◇(∃α)A ⥽ (∃α)◇A"
(rendered from SEP's LaTeX, ⥽ = strict implication); Ballarin calls it,
with axiom 7, one of "The purely modal additions".

## §2.3 Necessity of identity

> "Burgess (2014) emphasizes the differences between Barcan’s and Kripke’s proofs of the necessity of identity and argues that Kripke’s distinct proof may ultimately derive from Quine’s (1953b, 1953c, 1961) search for a simpler proof than Barcan’s."  (§2.3)

## §3 The dispute with Quine

> "Quine (1946, 1947b and 1947c) had originally reviewed her first three papers, and the friction with Quine was probably triggered by his initial review of her 1947 paper on the identity of individuals in modal systems"  (§3)

## §4.3 Names

> "This tag, a proper name, has no meaning. It simply tags. (1961: 310)"  (§4.3, quoting Marcus 1961)

> "One interesting, debated question is whether back in 1961 Marcus was already proposing, though not fully developing, the referential theory of ordinary proper names which will dominate analytic philosophy in the seventies, thanks to the work of Donnellan (1970), Kripke (1972), and Kaplan on direct reference (1989, delivered 1977)."  (§4.3)

> "The answer to this question seems to be neither an unqualified yes nor a simple no."  (§4.3)

> "Given the bitter controversy that took place on these questions in the nineties (see Humphreys & Fetzer 1998, Holt 1996, and Neale 2001)"  (§4.3)

> "Kripke provided us with a “picture” which is far more coherent than what had been available."  (§4.3, quoting Marcus 1978b: 502–503)

## §6 Actualism and the Barcan formula

> "In contrast to Marcus, Linsky and Zalta 1996, and Williamson 1995 and 1998 interpret BF as committed to the existence of possibilia. Simchen 2013 supports Marcus’s actualist reading; Williamson 2013b rejects both interpretations."  (§6)

> "This is quite a bold claim that is hard to support"  (§6, Ballarin on Marcus 1986b: 113, her criticism of Kripke's variable-domain semantics)

## §8 Moral dilemmas

> "Marcus criticizes the dominant view of moral dilemmas which is based on an excessively demanding notion of consistency. She attributes this view in some form or other to W.D. Ross, John Lemmon, Hare, Rawls and Davidson."  (§8)

> "The circumstances of the world conspire against us. However perfect our will, the contingencies are such that situations arise where, if we are to follow one course of action, we will be unable to follow another. (1980b: 127, fn. 6)"  (§8, quoting Marcus)

> "the consistency of a moral code requires only that there be possible circumstances in which all its demands can be met (1980b: 128)."  (§8)

> "Guilt is thus the appropriate moral response, even when the intractability of the circumstances is not our fault."  (§8)

> "Williams (1973) is a precursor of the view that dilemmas arise from contingent circumstances. Foot (1983) is critical of Marcus on guilt."  (§8)

> "She now criticizes the axiom \(\neg (OA \land O\neg A)\) as false to the facts of moral dilemmas."  (§8, on Marcus 1996)

> "Deontic operators like epistemic ones are not closed under logical consequence (1996: 29–30)."  (§8)

> "Such considerations are particularly appropriate to the question of the inevitability of dirty hands in public life."  (§8, quoting Marcus 1996: 33)
