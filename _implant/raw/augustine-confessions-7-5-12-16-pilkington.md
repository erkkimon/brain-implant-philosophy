# Augustine, Confessions VII.5, 12, 16 (Pilkington tr.) — "whence is evil?", whatever is is good, iniquity a perversion of the will

Source: Augustine, *Confessions*, Book VII, chapters 5, 12 and 16
  (paragraphs 7, 18 and 22), translated by J. G. Pilkington, in Nicene and
  Post-Nicene Fathers, First Series, Vol. 1, ed. Philip Schaff (Buffalo,
  NY: Christian Literature Publishing Co., 1887), revised and edited for
  New Advent by Kevin Knight.
Original: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110107.htm ; canonical locator
  Confessiones VII (public domain: Latin original and 1887 translation)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (New Advent page fetched with curl, HTML stripped;
  each quotation checked by string match against that text)

> "And I sought whence is evil? And sought in an evil way; nor saw I the evil in my very search."  (VII.5.7)

> "So long, therefore, as they are, they are good; therefore whatsoever is, is good. That evil, then, which I sought whence it was, is not any substance; for were it a substance, it would be good."  (VII.12.18)

> "And I inquired what iniquity was, and ascertained it not to be a substance, but a perversion of the will, bent aside from You, O God, the Supreme Substance, towards these lower things"  (VII.16.22)

Relied on for: Augustine's first-person statement of the question of the
  origin of evil, of the argument that whatever exists is good and evil is
  no substance, and of moral evil as a turning of the will
  (thinkers/augustine.md). Tornau (SEP "Augustine of Hippo" §7.5) cites
  Confessiones 7.7 and 7.18 for these points (paragraph numbering).
Context: chapter 5 is headed "Questions Concerning the Origin of Evil in
  Regard to God, Who, Since He is the Chief Good, Cannot Be the Cause of
  Evil"; chapter 12 is headed "Whatever Things the Good God Has Created are Very
  Good";
  chapter 16 is headed "Evil Arises Not from a Substance, But from the
  Perversion of the Will".
