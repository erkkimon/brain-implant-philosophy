# Thomas (EB 1911) — "Taboo": definition, non-moral character, taboo and moral obligation

Source: Northcote Whitridge Thomas, "Taboo", *Encyclopaedia Britannica*,
  11th edition (1911), Vol. 26 (Wikisource scan pages 358–362 of the
  volume's djvu), opening, §2 "Fundamental Ideas", §10 "Taboo and
  Moral Obligation".
Original: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Taboo
  (public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-10-15 (fetched with curl via the Wikisource parse API;
  quotations checked by script)

> "TABOO (also written tapu and tabu), the Polynesian name given to prohibitions enforced by religious or magical sanctions. As a verb it means to “prohibit,” as an adjective “prohibited, sacred, dangerous, unclean.”"  (opening)

> "This definition does not cover the whole connotation of taboo as it is employed at the present day, but it indicates clearly the non-moral character of the idea."  (§2)

> "In proportion as a taboo becomes a custom and its sanctions fall into the background and are forgotten, its obligations thus transformed are one source of the categorical imperative, the distinguishing feature of which is that it is non-rational and instinctive."  (§10)

Relied on for: Thomas's definition; his statement of taboo's
