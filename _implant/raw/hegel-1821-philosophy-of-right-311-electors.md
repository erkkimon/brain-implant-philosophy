# Hegel 1821 — Philosophy of Right §311 note: little desire to vote

Source: G. W. F. Hegel, Grundlinien der Philosophie des Rechts (1821),
  §311, remark; English: Hegel's Philosophy of Right, translated by S. W.
  Dyde, London: George Bell and Sons, 1896, p. 320.
Original: https://archive.org/details/cu31924014578979 (Cornell scan,
  public domain; translation by S. W. Dyde, 1896, public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-10-09 (djvu text fetched with curl; quotation checked by
  script, line-break hyphens removed)

> "Of elections by means of many separate persons it may be observed that there is necessarily little desire to vote, because one vote has so slight an influence."  (§311 note, Dyde p. 320)
> "Even when those who are entitled to vote are told how extremely valuable their privilege is, they do not vote."
> "Hence occurs just the opposite of what is sought."

Relied on for: an early statement of the small-influence observation.
  Wikipedia "Paradox of voting" (oldid 1368673256) points to this passage
  (in another translation) and to Condorcet 1793 (via McLean & Hewitt 1994,
  pp. 245–246, not read).
Context: the next sentence says the selection "passes into the hands of a
  few"; Hegel argues for representation by estates/branches of society
  rather than by "mere separate individuals".
