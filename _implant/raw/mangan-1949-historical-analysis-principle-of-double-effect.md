# Mangan 1949 — the four conditions, Aquinas as historical beginning, Alonso's dissent, Gury as "first of the moderns"

> "Some authors express four conditions, others taking one or another condition for granted express only three or two conditions."  (p. 43)

> "A person may licitly perform an action that he foresees will produce a good and a bad effect provided that four conditions are verified at one and the same time: 1) that the action in itself from its very object be good or at least indifferent; 2) that the good effect and not the evil effect be intended; 3) that the good effect be not produced by means of the evil effect; 4) that there be a proportionately grave reason for permitting the evil effect."  (p. 43)

> "Vicente M. Alonso, S.J., concludes that St. Thomas held merely that the killing of an unjust aggressor may be willed as a means but not as an end in itself."  (p. 45; Alonso, El principio del doble efecto en los comentadores de Santo Tomas de Aquino, Rome 1937)

> "Although Alonso's presentation of arguments and conclusions is quite formidable, it does not eliminate the reasonableness of the"  (p. 46, continued "more or less traditional interpretation of St. Thomas")

> "He finds, further, that the four conditions of the principle of the double effect can be verified, so that an act of self-defense with the bad effect included can be lawful."  (p. 50, of Aquinas, II-II q. 64 a. 7)

> "he seems to have been the first to do so."  (p. 51, of Aquinas enunciating the principle)

> "Cajetan seems to be the first to apply the principle of the double effect explicitly to the killing of innocent people."  (pp. 52–54)

> "In Gury's early editions, the first of which appeared in 1850, the treatment is a complete modern one in brief form."  (p. 59)

> "Principle. It is lawful to actuate a morally good or indifferent cause from which will follow two effects, one good and the other evil, if there is a proportionately serious reason, and the ultimate end of the agent is good, and the evil effect is not the means to the good effect."  (pp. 60–61, Mangan's translation of Gury, Compendium Theologiae Moralis, 5th German ed., Ratisbon 1874, t. 1, tr. "De actibus humanis", c. 2, n. 9)

> "Article seven of question 64 of the Secunda Secundae of St. Thomas' Summa Theologica is the historical beginning of the principle of the double effect as a principle."  (p. 61, Conclusion)

> "Although the principle as such was not accepted generally before the sixteenth century, it was accepted generally in its application to particular cases by the moralists of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries and by all who have succeeded them."  (p. 61, Conclusion)

> "And the moralist who in a true sense is the first of the moderns in his explanation and application of the principle of the double effect is Gury whose Compendium Theologiae Moralis was first published in 1850."  (p. 61, Conclusion)

Relied on for: the four-condition formulation (quoted by McIntyre, SEP
  §1); Mangan's attribution of the principle's beginning to Aquinas and the
  dissent he reports from Alonso (1937); Cajetan's application to the
  innocent; Gury's 1850/1874 three-clause statement as Mangan translates it.
Context: Mangan's article is a history from Aquinas through Cajetan, the
  sixteenth-century commentators, the Salmanticenses (mid-seventeenth
  century) to Gury; it says it discusses "in detail only the historical
  beginnings". Gury's Latin original is not held; only Mangan's English.
