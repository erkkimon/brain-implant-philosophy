## 21b — the oracle's riddle

> "for I know that I have no wisdom, small or great. What then can he mean when he says that I am the wisest of men?"  (21b, Jowett)

> "For I am conscious that I am not wise either much or little. What then does he mean by declaring that I am the wisest?"  (21b, Fowler)

Greek (21b, Burnet): "ἐγὼ γὰρ δὴ οὔτε μέγα οὔτε σμικρὸν σύνοιδα ἐμαυτῷ σοφὸς ὤν"

## 21d — after examining the politician

> "Well, although I do not suppose that either of us knows anything really beautiful and good, I am better off than he is,—for he knows nothing, and thinks that he knows; I neither know nor think that I know. In this latter particular, then, I seem to have slightly the advantage of him."  (21d, Jowett)

> "I am wiser than this man; for neither of us really knows anything fine and good, but this man thinks he knows something when he does not, whereas I, as I do not know anything, do not think I do either. I seem, then, in just this little thing to be wiser than this man at any rate, that what I do not know I do not think I know either."  (21d, Fowler)

Greek (21d, Burnet): "κινδυνεύει μὲν γὰρ ἡμῶν οὐδέτερος οὐδὲν καλὸν κἀγαθὸν εἰδέναι, ἀλλ᾽ οὗτος μὲν οἴεταί τι εἰδέναι οὐκ εἰδώς, ἐγὼ δέ, ὥσπερ οὖν οὐκ οἶδα, οὐδὲ οἴομαι: ἔοικα γοῦν τούτου γε σμικρῷ τινι αὐτῷ τούτῳ σοφώτερος εἶναι, ὅτι ἃ μὴ οἶδα οὐδὲ οἴομαι εἰδέναι."

## 22c–d — the craftsmen

> "I was conscious that I knew nothing at all, as I may say, and I was sure that they knew many fine things"  (22c–d, Jowett)

> "For I was conscious that I knew practically nothing, but I knew I should find that they knew many fine things."  (22d, Fowler)

Greek (22c–d, Burnet): "ἐμαυτῷ γὰρ συνῄδη οὐδὲν ἐπισταμένῳ ὡς ἔπος εἰπεῖν, τούτους δέ γ᾽ ᾔδη ὅτι εὑρήσοιμι πολλὰ καὶ καλὰ ἐπισταμένους."

## 23a–b — the oracle's meaning (Jowett)

> "He, O men, is the wisest, who, like Socrates, knows that his wisdom is in truth worth nothing."  (23b, Jowett)

## 29a–b — fear of death; what he does know

> "Is not this ignorance of a disgraceful sort, the ignorance which is the conceit that a man knows what he does not know?"  (29b, Jowett)

> "whereas I know but little of the world below, I do not suppose that I know: but I do know that injustice and disobedience to a better, whether God or man, is evil and dishonourable"  (29b, Jowett)

> "But I do know that it is evil and disgraceful to do wrong and to disobey him who is better than I, whether he be god or man."  (29b, Fowler)

Relied on for: what Socrates says in the Apology about his knowledge and
ignorance, in two public-domain translations and the Greek; that the
formula "I know that I know nothing" does not occur at these places in
that form; that at 29b Socrates claims to know something.
Context: Chaerephon has asked the Delphic oracle whether anyone is wiser
than Socrates and been told no (21a); Socrates tests the oracle on
politicians (21b–e), poets (22a–c) and craftsmen (22c–e), and concludes
that the god uses his name as an example (23a–b). The 29a–b passage is
from his later explanation of why he will not stop philosophising for fear
of death.
