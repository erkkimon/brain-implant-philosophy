# Lucian, The Sale of Creeds — the "Electra" and the "Man in the Hood" (Fowler tr.)

Source: Lucian of Samosata, The Sale of Creeds (Vitarum auctio), the lot of
  Chrysippus, in The Works of Lucian of Samosata, tr. H. W. Fowler and
  F. G. Fowler, vol. 1 (Oxford: Clarendon Press).
Original: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6327 (Project Gutenberg eBook
  #6327; public domain translation). Excerpt only.
Retrieved: 2026-09-27 (plain-text file pg6327.txt read; quotation copied
  and checked by string match).

> "There is the 'Reaper.' There is the 'Rightful Owner.' Better still, there is the 'Electra' and the 'Man in the Hood.'"  (Chrysippus speaking)

> "She was the Electra, the daughter of Agamemnon, to whom the same thing was known and unknown at the same time. She knew that Orestes was her brother: yet when he stood before her she did not know (until he revealed himself) that her brother was Orestes."

> "Well, but the Man in the Hood is your father. You don't know the Man in the Hood. Therefore you don't know your own father."

Relied on for: the wording of the Electra and the hooded-man arguments in an
ancient source; Lucian puts them in the mouth of Chrysippus and does not
name Eubulides.
Context: a satire in which Zeus and Hermes auction philosophers; the
Stoic lot (Chrysippus) first offers the crocodile dilemma, then these. The
buyer answers "if I take his hood off, I shall get at the facts." The
Gutenberg text marks emphasis with underscores (_the_ Electra), removed
here.
