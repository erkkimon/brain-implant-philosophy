# Hegel 1807, Phenomenology of Mind (Baillie 1910) — recognition, the life-and-death struggle, lordship and bondage

Source: G. W. F. Hegel, *The Phenomenology of Mind*, translated with an
  introduction and notes by J. B. Baillie, vol. I (London: Swan Sonnenschein;
  New York: Macmillan, 1910), "Independence and Dependence of
  Self-Consciousness: Lordship and Bondage", pp. 175–188. Paragraph numbers
  ¶178–196 are those of the Marxists Internet Archive HTML of the same
  Baillie translation (Φ 178 ff.).
Original: https://archive.org/details/cu31924097557171 (Cornell scan of
  vol. I, 1910; "There are no known copyright restrictions in the United
  States on the use of the text."); paragraph-numbered HTML:
  https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/ph/phba.htm
  (public domain: 1910 translation; translator d. 1940)
Retrieved: 2026-10-08 (djvu OCR text and the HTML fetched with curl; each
  quotation compared in both; spelling below follows the 1910 scan, OCR
  slips such as "hfe" for "life" corrected from the HTML)

> "Self-consciousness exists in itself and for itself, in that, and by the fact that it exists for another self-consciousness; that is to say, it is only by being acknowledged or "recognised.""  (¶178)

> "The relation of both self-consciousnesses is in this way so constituted that they prove themselves and each other through a life-and-death struggle."  (¶187, p. 179)

> "And it is solely by risking life, that freedom is obtained;"  (¶187, p. 180)

> "The one is independent whose essential nature is to be for itself, the other is dependent whose essence is life or existence for another. The former is the Master, or Lord, the latter the Bondsman."  (¶189, p. 182)

> "Through work and labour, however, this consciousness of the bondsman comes to itself."  (¶195, p. 186)

> "Labour, on the other hand, is desire restrained and checked, evanescence delayed and postponed; in other words, labour shapes and fashions the thing."  (¶195, p. 186)

Relied on for: the opening thesis of the section (self-consciousness exists
only as recognised), the life-and-death struggle, the naming of master and
bondsman, and the bondsman's coming to himself through labour.
Context: Baillie prefaces the section with his own bracketed summary
("The equality of the selves is the truth, or completer realisation, of
self in another self"), which is the translator's gloss, not Hegel's text.
The section is followed by "Freedom of Self-consciousness: Stoicism,
Scepticism, and the Unhappy Consciousness". Redding (SEP "Hegel" §3.1.1)
reports the Kojèvean and later non-Kojèvean readings of it.
