# Jung 1923 — Psychological Types (Baynes trans.): Definition 29 "Individuation" and the principium individuationis

Source: C. G. Jung, *Psychological Types, or The Psychology of
  Individuation*, translated by H. Godwin Baynes (London: Kegan Paul,
  Trench, Trubner & Co.; New York: Harcourt, Brace & Company, 1923;
  International Library of Psychology, Philosophy and Scientific Method),
  ch. XI "Definitions", no. 29 "Individuation", pp. 561–563; ch. II
  p. 78; ch. III "The Apollonian and the Dionysian", pp. 172–173.
  (German original *Psychologische Typen*, 1921; in the Collected Works
  this is CW 6, where Sharp 1991 locates Definition "Individuation" at
  pars. 757–761 — see `sharp-1991-jung-lexicon-individuation.md`.)
Original: https://archive.org/details/psychological_types
  (scan; OCR file psychological_types_djvu.txt). The 1923 English edition
  is public domain in the United States; Jung died 1961, so the German
  original and the translation may be protected elsewhere — excerpts only.
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (OCR text downloaded and grepped; quotations below
  copied from the OCR with line-break hyphenation joined and spacing
  normalised; title page read: "Psychological Types or The Psychology of
  Individuation ... Translated by H. GODWIN BAYNES".)

> "In general, it is the process of forming and specializing the individual nature ; in particular, it is the development of the psychological individual as a differentiated being from the general, collective psychology."  (Def. 29, p. 561)

> "Individuation, therefore, is a process of differentiation , having for its goal the development of the individual personality."  (Def. 29, p. 561)

> "the process of individuation must clearly lead to a more intensive and universal collective solidarity, and not to mere isolation ."  (Def. 29, p. 562)

> "Individuation always finds itself more or less in opposition to the collective norm, since it means a separation and differentiation from the general, and a building up of the particular"  (Def. 29, p. 562)

> "An actual conflict with the collective norm takes place only when an individual way is raised to a norm, which, moreover, is the fundamental aim of extreme individualism."  (Def. 29, p. 563)

> "Individuation is practically the same as the development of consciousness out of the original state of identity"  (Def. 29, p. 563)

> "the ego owes its existence to the principium individuationis, i.e. to individual differentiation"  (ch. II, p. 78)

> "describe Apollo as the splendid * divine image of the principii individuation is ."  (ch. III, p. 173, Jung quoting Nietzsche, *Birth of Tragedy*, "p. 26"; OCR spacing and the stray "*" as in the scan; the quotation begins "One might almost" on p. 172)

> "It represents horror at the annihilation of the principle of individuation"  (ch. III, p. 173, of the Dionysian)

Relied on for: Jung's own definition of individuation as psychological
  differentiation of the individual from collective psychology; its
  distinction from "extreme individualism"; its link to the development of
  consciousness; that Jung also uses the older Latin phrase
  *principium individuationis* and the Nietzschean/Schopenhauerian
  Apollo–Dionysus usage of "principle of individuation".
Context: Definition 29 opens "The concept of individuation plays no small
  role in our psychology." It also says individuation cannot be "the unique
  goal of psychological education" before adaptation to collective
  standards. The p. 78 sentence comes from a contrast of Freud's
  "instinctive psychology" and Adler's "ego-psychology". The pp. 172–173
  passage summarises Nietzsche's Apollonian/Dionysian pair.
