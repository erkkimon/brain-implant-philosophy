> "The golden rule is endorsed by all the great world religions; Jesus , Hillel, and Confucius used it to summarize their ethical teachings."  (1; spacing as in the page)

> "The golden rule is best seen as a consistency principle."  (1)

> "It only prescribes consistency - that we not have our actions (toward another) be out of harmony with our desires (toward a reversed situation action)."  (1)

> "R.M. Hare's Freedom and Reason ( Oxford 1963 ) greatly influenced my thinking on the golden rule."  (1)

> "Jeff Wattles's The Golden Rule ( Oxford 1996 ) emphasizes historical and religious aspects and thus complements my logical-rational approach."  (1)

> "Treat others only as you consent to being treated in the same situation."  (2, and 3)

> "To apply it, you'd imagine yourself on the receiving end of the action in the exact place of the other person (which includes having the other person's likes and dislikes)."  (2)

> "If you want X to do something to you, then do this same thing to X."  (3, "The literal golden rule")

> "Ima Masochist: I want to be hurt. Does GR tell me to hurt others?"  (3)

> "Have her imagine herself in the place of her non-masochist victim."  (3)

> "Criticize Ima’s irrational desires."  (3)

> "A nurse giving a shot to a baby, a judge sentencing a dangerous criminal to jail."  (4)

> "“Treat others as you want to be treated” ≠ “Treat others as they treat you.”"  (3)

> "The soft GR fallacy assumes that we should always do what the other person wants."  (4)

Relied on for: Gensler's consistency reading and formula; his naming of
Hare and Wattles as influences/complements; the literal formula he says
"leads to absurdities"; the masochist case and his replies; the judge case
and his "soft GR fallacy" label.
Context: (3) states that the literal rule "leads to absurdities in two
cases: different situations" and "flawed desires", with the masochist as
the flawed-desire case; it adds that "Satisfying GR-consistency doesn’t
guarantee that her action is right." (4) places the judge case under the
question "Am I willing that if I were in the same situation then this be
done to me?". The same deck has a box "GR and Kant" saying the GR
consistency view is partly inspired by Kant's view but differs from it
about GR, which, the box says, Kant rejects (wording paraphrased here
because the slide hyphenates across lines).
