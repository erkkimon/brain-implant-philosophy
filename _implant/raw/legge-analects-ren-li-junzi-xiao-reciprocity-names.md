# Legge (trans.) — Confucian Analects: ren, li, the superior man, filial piety, reciprocity, rectifying names

> "The superior man bends his attention to what is radical. That being established, all practical courses naturally grow up. Filial piety and fraternal submission!-- are they not the root of all benevolent actions?"  (1.2, the philosopher Yu speaking)

> "That parents, when alive, be served according to propriety; that, when dead, they should be buried according to propriety; and that they should be sacrificed to according to propriety."  (2.5)

> "The filial piety of now-a-days means the support of one's parents. But dogs and horses likewise are able to do something in the way of support;-- without reverence, what is there to distinguish the one support given from the other?"  (2.7)

> "If a man be without the virtues proper to humanity, what has he to do with the rites of propriety? If a man be without the virtues proper to humanity, what has he to do with music?"  (3.3)

> "A transmitter and not a maker, believing in and loving the ancients, I venture to compare myself with our old P'ang."  (7.1)

> "To subdue one's self and return to propriety, is perfect virtue."  (12.1)

> "Look not at what is contrary to propriety; listen not to what is contrary to propriety; speak not what is contrary to propriety; make no movement which is contrary to propriety."  (12.1)

> "not to do to others as you would not wish done to yourself"  (12.2, answering Chung-kung on perfect virtue)

> "There is government, when the prince is prince, and the minister is minister; when the father is father, and the son is son."  (12.11)

> "Fan Ch'ih asked about benevolence. The Master said, 'It is to love all men.' He asked about knowledge. The Master said, 'It is to know all men.'"  (12.22)

> "What is necessary is to rectify names."  (13.3)

> "If names be not correct, language is not in accordance with the truth of things. If language be not in accordance with the truth of things, affairs cannot be carried on to success."  (13.3)

> "Among us here there are those who may be styled upright in their conduct. If their father have stolen a sheep, they will bear witness to the fact."  (13.18, the Duke of Sheh)

> "Among us, in our part of the country, those who are upright are different from this. The father conceals the misconduct of the son, and the son conceals the misconduct of the father. Uprightness is to be found in this."  (13.18, Confucius)

> "The cultivation of himself in reverential carefulness."  (14.45, what constitutes the superior man)

> "The determined scholar and the man of virtue will not seek to live at the expense of injuring their virtue. They will even sacrifice their lives to preserve their virtue complete."  (15.8 in Legge's numbering; Csikszentmihalyi's SEP §4 cites this passage as 15.9)

> "Is not RECIPROCITY such a word? What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others."  (15.23)

> "By nature, men are nearly alike; by practice, they get to be wide apart."  (17.2)

> "While you are not able to serve men, how can you serve their spirits?"  (11.11)

Context: Legge renders ren variously ("perfect virtue" 12.1–2, "benevolence"
  12.22, "the virtues proper to humanity" 3.3), li as "propriety" / "rites
  of propriety", junzi as "the superior man". 1.2 is spoken by the disciple
  Yu (You Ruo), not by Confucius. Legge's chapter divisions occasionally
  differ by one from other editions (here 15.8/15.9); Legge's text also
  numbers two consecutive chapters of Book II "XVII" (2.17 and 2.18).
