# Senay, Albarracín & Noguchi 2010 — interrogative self-talk ("Will I?" vs "I will") and goal-directed behaviour

Source: Ibrahim Senay, Dolores Albarracín, Kenji Noguchi, "Motivating Goal-Directed Behavior Through Introspective Self-Talk: The Role of the Interrogative Form of Simple Future Tense", Psychological Science 21(4), 2010, pp. 499–504 (published online 2010-03-09). Quoted from the NIH author manuscript (PMC3626423, available in PMC 2013-04-15).

## Abstract

> "In Experiment 1, participants were more likely to solve anagrams if they prepared for the task by asking themselves whether they would work on anagrams as opposed to declaring that they would."  (abstract)

> "In the next three experiments, merely writing Will I as opposed to I will as part of an ostensibly unrelated handwriting task produced better anagram-solving performance and stronger intentions to exercise, which suggests that priming the interrogative structure of self-talk is enough to motivate goal-directed behavior."  (abstract)

> "This effect was found to be mediated by the intrinsic motivation for action and moderated by the salience of the word order of the primes."  (abstract)

## Question and hypothesis

> "How does the way in which you talk to yourself shape your future actions?"  (introduction, first sentence)

> "Several independent lines of research and practice suggest that engaging in interrogative as opposed to declarative talk (e.g., Will I vs. I will ) may lead to increased intrinsic motivation."  (introduction)

## Experiments

> "Fifty-three introductory psychology students enrolled in the study in exchange for course credit."  (Experiment 1, Method)

> "The participants solved significantly more anagrams when they were told to think about whether they would do anagrams ( M = 2.60, SD = 1.72) than when they were told to think that they would do anagrams ( M = 1.84, SD = 1.42), t (49) = 2.11, p = .04, g = 0.6."  (Experiment 1, Results and discussion)

> "Nonetheless, these results were obtained with explicit instructions."  (Experiment 1, Results and discussion)

> "Participants were 50 introductory psychology students, who received course credit for their participation."  (Experiment 2, Method; primes Will I, I will, I, Will written in an ostensible handwriting task, then 10 anagrams)

> "the Will I prime produced better performance than any other prime, t (46) = 3.1, p = .003, g = 1.03."  (Experiment 2, Results and discussion)

> "In contrast, there were no significant differences across the I will, I , and Will primes, t (49)s < 1, n.s."  (Experiment 2, Results and discussion)

> "Participants were 46 introductory psychology students, who enrolled in the study in exchange for course credit."  (Experiment 3, Method; patterned vs random number sequences before the Will I / I will primes; outcome: intended hours of exercise next week)

> "participants previously primed with patterned sequences had stronger intentions to exercise in the Will I as compared with the I will condition, t (42) = 7.45, p < .001, g = 3.05."  (Experiment 3, Results and discussion)

> "However, this effect disappeared when participants had previously written random sequences, t (42) = 1.06, n.s."  (Experiment 3, Results and discussion)

> "Participants were 56 introductory psychology students, who received course credit for their participation."  (Experiment 4, Method; intention to exercise rated 1–7, and 12 reasons for exercising, 6 intrinsic and 6 extrinsic)

> "These results, along with a significant Sobel test ( z = 2.03, p = .043, R 2 = .06), confirmed the role of intrinsic motivation as a mediator of the effect of the prime on exercise intentions."  (Experiment 4, Results and discussion)

## General Discussion

> "Our findings identified interrogative self-talk as an important motivator of goal-directed behavior."  (General Discussion, first sentence)

> "our studies did not specifically show that the performance of a goal-directed behavior was determined by intentions to perform this behavior, which could have been partially under the control of processes beside intention."  (General Discussion)

> "Despite this limitation, the present results are clear in showing that the general forms of thought implicitly elicited through the grammatical structure of self-talk are capable of motivating and altering behavior."  (General Discussion)

> "Future research may investigate whether the interrogative forms of other verbs (e.g., can, should , or would ) and other grammatical structures (e.g., passive vs. active voice) can produce similar effects on behavior."  (General Discussion)
