# Plato, Republic IV 427e (Jowett tr.) — the city "wise and valiant and temperate and just"

Source: Plato, The Republic, Book IV, tr. Benjamin Jowett, Project
  Gutenberg eBook #1497. The Gutenberg text prints no Stephanus numbers;
  the passage opens the search for the four virtues, which Frede & Lee (SEP
  "Plato's Ethics: An Overview", §3.2) place in 427d–434c,
  so the locator 427e is approximate.
Original: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1497 (text:

  translation (1871; this text from his 3rd ed.) is public domain.
Retrieved: 2026-10-15 (plain text fetched with curl; quotation a verbatim
  substring, line breaks normalised)

> "And being perfect, is therefore wise and valiant and temperate and just."  (IV, c. 427e; Socrates)

Relied on for: vocabulary/virtue-and-vice.md — the four virtues of the
  well-ordered city as Socrates lists them in Book IV.
Context: Socrates first assumes "that our State, if rightly ordered, is
  perfect", then proposes to find justice as the residue once the other
  three qualities are found. Shorey's translation of Republic IV 433a and
  441e is excerpted in plato-republic-gorgias-philebus-shorey-lamb-fowler-perseus.md.
