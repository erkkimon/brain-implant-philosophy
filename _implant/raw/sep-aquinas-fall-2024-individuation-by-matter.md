# SEP Fall 2024 — Pasnau, "Thomas Aquinas" §4: matter as the principle of individuation

Source: Robert Pasnau, "Thomas Aquinas", *Stanford Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy* (Fall 2024 Edition), ed. Edward N. Zalta & Uri Nodelman;
  first published 7 Dec 2022. §4 "Form and Matter".
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/aquinas/

Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (archive HTML fetched; quotations copied from the
  tag-stripped text)

> "Substantial forms both unify a substance at a given time and individuate a substance over time."  (§4)

> "In external reality (in re), “no commonness is found in Socrates; rather, whatever is in him has been individuated” (On Being and Essence 3.80–2; Leftow 2003; Brower 2016). The principle of individuation, for forms, is matter (King 2000; Klima 2000)."  (§4)

> "The account of individuation, then, comes in two stages: matter individuates the form at the start, when the form first inheres in that matter, and from that point forward the form possesses a fixed identity of its own."  (§4)

Relied on for: Aquinas's answer to the scholastic question (matter
  individuates the form; the form then individuates the substance over
  time), as Pasnau reports it; the tie between individuation and identity
  over time.
Context: The passage follows Pasnau's account of substantial forms
  actualising each part of a substance. "King 2000" in the bibliography is
  Peter King, "The Problem of Individuation in the Middle Ages", Theoria
  66: 159–84, doi:10.1111/j.1755-2567.2000.tb01161.x (DOI resolved by
  content negotiation, 2026-10-01; publisher Wiley).
