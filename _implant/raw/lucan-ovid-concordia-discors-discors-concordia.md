# Lucan 1.98 "concordia discors" and Ovid, Metamorphoses 1.433 "discors concordia"

Source: M. Annaeus Lucanus, De bello civili (Pharsalia) I, line 98 (between
  the printed line numbers 95 and 100); P. Ovidius Naso, Metamorphoses I,
  line 433 (printed number 430 three lines above). Latin texts at The
  Latin Library.
Original: https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/lucan/lucan1.shtml ;
  https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/ovid/ovid.met1.shtml (public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-09-28 (pages fetched; lines located by grep and counted from the printed numbers)

> "fraterno primi maduerunt sanguine muri.                  95
> nec pretium tanti tellus pontusque furoris
> tunc erat: exiguum dominos commisit asylum.
>       temporis angusti mansit concordia discors
> paxque fuit non sponte ducum;"  (Lucan 1.95–99)

> "quippe ubi temperiem sumpsere umorque calorque,               430
> concipiunt, et ab his oriuntur cuncta duobus,
> cumque sit ignis aquae pugnax, vapor umidus omnes
> res creat, et discors concordia fetibus apta est."  (Met. 1.430–433)

Relied on for: two further occurrences of the Horatian pairing, listed on
the page as occurrences only.
Context: Lucan — the uneasy alliance of Caesar, Pompey and Crassus;
Ovid — life generated after the flood from moisture and heat. No read
source on this page calls these lines oxymora; they appear on the page
as the same word-pair as Horace Epistles 1.12.19.
