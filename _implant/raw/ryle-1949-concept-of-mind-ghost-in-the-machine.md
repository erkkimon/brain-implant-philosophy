# Ryle 1949 — The Concept of Mind, ch. I "Descartes' Myth": the official doctrine, the Ghost in the Machine, the category-mistake

Source: Gilbert Ryle, The Concept of Mind (London: Hutchinson's University
  Library, 1949; reprinted 1950, 1951), chapter I "Descartes' Myth",
  §1 "The Official Doctrine" and §2 "The Absurdity of the Official
  Doctrine" (pp. 11–16 in the Hutchinson edition; the OCR copy read does
  not carry page numbers, so section titles are used as locators).
Original: https://archive.org/details/gilbert-ryle-concept-of-mind
  (Internet Archive scan of the Hutchinson printing, 1951 reprint;
  copyrighted; excerpts only). ISBN of the current Routledge edition:
  9780415485470 (not verified against the copy read).
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (read in the archive.org OCR text
  "Gilbert Ryle, Concept of Mind_djvu.txt"; the title page, imprint and
  quoted passages checked in the OCR)

> "There is a doctrine about the nature and place of minds which is so
>  prevalent among theorists and even among laymen that it deserves to be
>  described as the official theory. [...] The official doctrine, which
>  hails chiefly from Descartes, is something like this. With the doubtful
>  exceptions of idiots and infants in arms every human being has both a
>  body and a mind. [...] His body and his mind are ordinarily harnessed
>  together, but after the death of the body his mind may continue to
>  exist and function."  (ch. I, §1 "The Official Doctrine")

> "Such in outline is the official theory. I shall often speak of it, with
>  deliberate abusiveness, as 'the dogma of the Ghost in the Machine'. I
>  hope to prove that it is entirely false, and false not in detail but in
>  principle. It is not merely an assemblage of particular mistakes. It is
>  one big mistake and a mistake of a special kind. It is, namely, a
>  category-mistake. It represents the facts of mental life as if they
>  belonged to one logical type or category (or range of types or
>  categories), when they actually belong to another. The dogma is
>  therefore a philosopher's myth."  (ch. I, §2 "The Absurdity of the
>  Official Doctrine")

Relied on for: the claims that Ryle labelled Cartesian dualism "the dogma
of the Ghost in the Machine" (and said the label was chosen "with
deliberate abusiveness"), and that his objection to it is that it commits a
category-mistake — treating mental-conduct concepts as if they named
episodes of the same logical type as physical ones.
Context: between the two quoted passages Ryle sets out the doctrine's
commitment to two histories (public bodily, private mental) and to
privileged access; after the second he illustrates category-mistakes with
the visitor who, having seen the colleges and libraries, asks where the
University is. He adds that he aims "at doing nothing more than rectify
the logic of mental-conduct concepts". The ellipses omit sentences on
minor reservations of the doctrine's adherents and on "every human being
is both a body and a mind".
