# SEP "Joseph Butler's Moral Philosophy" (Aaron Garrett) — self-love that obsesses over its interest

Source: Aaron Garrett, "Joseph Butler's Moral Philosophy", The Stanford
  Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri
  Nodelman (eds.); first published 2012-10-17, substantive revision
  2023-11-27. §5 ("Self-Love and Benevolence").
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/butler-moral/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (HTML of the Fall 2024 archive page read; copyright
  line "Copyright © 2023 by Aaron Garrett")

> "Or we might obsess over our interest to such a degree that we would continually be exercising the principle of self-love but not actually satisfying our particular passions and consequently not be happy."  (§5)

> "And although satisfying a desire may make us happy, and all of our happiness may be the result of particular passions, no particular passion has happiness in general as its object."  (§5)

Relied on for: Garrett's report of Butler's view that self-love which
  engrosses the agent leaves the particular passions unsatisfied.
Context: the paragraph continues with Butler's "Happiness consists in the
  Gratification of certain Affections, Appetites, Passions, with Objects
  which are by Nature adapted to them," (Sermon IX §16 in Garrett's
  citation). §5 opens with Broad's remark that Butler "killed the theory"
  of psychological egoism "so thoroughly" (Broad 1930, 55).
