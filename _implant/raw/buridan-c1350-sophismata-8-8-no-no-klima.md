> "The eighth sophism is, ‘Plato says something false’, and it seems to me to be more difficult."  (p. 971)

> "Let it be the case that Socrates utters this proposition: ‘Plato says something false’, and none other, and, conversely, Plato utters this: ‘Socrates says something false’, and none other."  (p. 971)

> "for there is no reason why Socrates’ proposition should be true or false rather than Plato’s, or conversely, for they are related to each other in exactly the same way."  (p. 971, argument P.1 for "true")

> "The opposite is argued: for if Socrates says something true, then by the same token Plato says something true; and if Plato says something true, since he says that Socrates says something false, it follows that Socrates says something false; therefore, Socrates’ proposition is not true but false."  (p. 972, O.1)

> "We should briefly reply that Socrates’ proposition is false and not true, for every proposition is false that, along with something true, entails something false;"  (p. 972, Buridan's determination)

> "and similarly, for the same reason, we would have to say that Plato’s proposition would be false."  (p. 972)

> "Therefore, we only have to respond to the arguments, which are very difficult."  (p. 972)

> "Socrates’ proposition and Robert’s proposition are similar in utterance and intention of the speaker and hearer alike, and yet they are not equivalent, because Plato’s proposition, of which both of them were speaking, is referring to [habet reflexionem super] Socrates’ proposition and not to Robert’s proposition."  (pp. 972–973, reply to P.3)

Relied on for: the no-no paradox page — Buridan's wording of the case
(false, not "not true"), the symmetry argument, the opposite argument, his
determination that both propositions are false, and his reply to the
Robert variant.
Context: the seventh sophism ("What I am saying is false") precedes it, and
Buridan says to "revert to the points made in connection with the previous
sophism", i.e. that every proposition with the case "entails that it is
true". P.2–P.4 argue that Socrates' proposition is true (from its being
affirmative with co-suppositing terms; from Robert, who says the same words
about Plato without reference back; from John, who contradicts Socrates).
His reply to P.4: Socrates' and John's propositions "are both false and do
not contradict [each other]"; the contradictory must be "‘Plato does not say
something false or A is not true’". The ninth sophism ("Socrates says
something true") is the 'yes'-'no' case.
