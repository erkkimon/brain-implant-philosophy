# Kant 1785 — Groundwork, Second Section: "Autonomy of the will" as the supreme principle of morality, and heteronomy (Abbott translation)

Source: Immanuel Kant, Grundlegung zur Metaphysik der Sitten (1785), Second
  Section, the headed paragraphs "The Autonomy of the Will as the Supreme
  Principle of Morality" and "Heteronomy of the Will as the Source of all
  spurious Principles of Morality"; English: Fundamental Principles of the
  Metaphysic of Morals, trans. Thomas Kingsmill Abbott (Project Gutenberg
  eBook #5682).
Original: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5682 (plain text
  https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5682/pg5682.txt); canonical locator:
  the Gutenberg text carries no Academy pagination; Johnson & Cureton, SEP
  "Kant's Moral Philosophy", Fall 2024, cite the heteronomy sentence as
  "G 4:441"; the autonomy paragraph immediately precedes it (public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-10-09 (plain text downloaded with curl, HTTP 200; passage read

> "Autonomy of the will is that property of it by which it is a law to itself (independently of any property of the objects of volition)."  (Groundwork, Second Section, "The Autonomy of the Will as the Supreme Principle of Morality")

> "If the will seeks the law which is to determine it anywhere else than in the fitness of its maxims to be universal laws of its own dictation, consequently if it goes out of itself and seeks this law in the character of any of its objects, there always results heteronomy."  (Groundwork, Second Section, "Heteronomy of the Will as the Source of all spurious Principles of Morality"; G 4:441 per Johnson & Cureton)

Cross-check of the locator, Johnson & Cureton, SEP "Kant's Moral Philosophy"
  (Fall 2024 archive, https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/kant-moral/ ,
  §10, fetched 2026-10-09, other translation):

> "For instance, Kant states that “if the will seeks the law that is to determine it anywhere else than in the fitness of its maxims for its own giving of universal law…heteronomy always results” (G 4:441)."  (SEP kant-moral §10)

Relied on for: Kant's definition of autonomy of the will and its contrast,
  heteronomy.
Context: between the two sentences Kant states "The principle of autonomy
  then is" a choice such that "the same volition shall comprehend the maxims
  of our choice as a universal law", says this "cannot" be proved "by a mere
  analysis" because it is "a synthetical proposition", and that "the
  principle of autonomy in question is the sole principle of morals".
