# SEP "The Lambda Calculus" (Fall 2024) — early illative systems inconsistent; consistency of the pure calculus

Source: Jesse Alama and Johannes Korbmacher, "The Lambda Calculus",
  Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), first published
  2012-12-12, substantive revision 2023-07-25, §§2 (history) and 4.1
  (consistency).
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/lambda-calculus/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (read at the URL above with curl)

> "In the hands of Curry, Church, Kleene, and Rosser (some of the pioneers in the subject) the focus was on defining mathematical objects and carrying out logical reasoning inside the these new systems."  (§2)

> "It turned out that these early attempts at so-called illative \(\lambda\)-calculus and combinatory logic were inconsistent. Curry isolated and polished the inconsistency; the result is now known as Curry’s paradox."  (§2; the entry points to the Curry's paradox entry and appendix B of Barendregt 1985)

> "In this context, inconsistency means that all terms are equal: one can reduce any \(\lambda\)-term \(M\) to any other \(\lambda\)-term \(N\). That this is not the case is an early result of \(\lambda\)-calculus."  (§2)

> "Early formulations of the idea of \(\lambda\)-calculus by A. Church were indeed inconsistent; see (Barendregt, 1985, appendix 2) or (Rosser, 1985) for a discussion."  (§4.1)

> "We can thus take inconsistency of \(\lambda\) to mean: all equations are derivable."  (§4.1)

Relied on for: the illative systems' inconsistency and Curry's role, as
these authors report it; the distinction from the pure calculus, which is
not inconsistent in the equational sense.
Context: §4.1 opens "Is the λ-calculus consistent? The question might not
be well-posed", since the pure calculus has no apparent notion of
contradiction; the Church–Rosser theorem is then used to show K = I is not
derivable. The entry does not name Kleene–Rosser or Richard's paradox.
