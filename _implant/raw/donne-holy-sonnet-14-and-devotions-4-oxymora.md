# Donne — Holy Sonnet XIV "Batter my heart" (lines 13–14) and Devotions IV "beggarly riches"

Source: John Donne, Holy Sonnets XIV ("Batter my heart, three person'd
  God"), lines 13–14, in H. J. C. Grierson (ed.), The Poems of John Donne,
  vol. 1 (Oxford, 1912); Devotions upon Emergent Occasions (1624),
  IV. Meditation.

Retrieved: 2026-09-28 (plain texts fetched; passages located by grep)

> "Except you'enthrall mee, never shall be free,
>   Nor ever chast, except you ravish mee."  (Holy Sonnet XIV.13–14, Grierson spelling)

> "O miserable abundance, O beggarly riches! how much do we lack of having
> remedies for every disease, when as yet we have not names for them?"  (Devotions IV, Meditation)

Relied on for: the Donne example rows.
Context: the sonnet's speaker asks God to overpower him; the closing couplet
pairs enthralment with freedom and ravishment with chastity. In
Devotions IV Donne, ill, reflects on the number of diseases. English
Wikipedia's "Oxymoron" article lists "beggarly riches" (Donne, citing
the Devotions, 1624) among its literary examples
(wikipedia-oxymoron-article-lederer-carlin-oed.md). No read source calls
the sonnet's couplet an oxymoron; the page lists it with "—" in its
"Labelled so by" column (coupled contraries only).
