# SEP Fall 2024, "Well-Being" (Crisp) and "Hedonism" (A. Moore) — Mill's higher and lower pleasures

## Crisp, "Well-Being", §4.1

Source: Roger Crisp, "Well-Being", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
  (Fall 2024 edition), first published 6 Nov 2001, substantive revision
  15 Sep 2021; §4.1 "Hedonism".
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/well-being/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (archive HTML fetched, tags stripped, §4.1 read)

> "But there is an alternative to simple hedonism, outlined famously by J.S. Mill, using his distinction (itself influenced by Plato’s discussion of pleasure at the end of his Republic (Plato 1992 [C4 BCE], 582d-583a)) between ‘higher’ and ‘lower’ pleasures (1863 [1998], ch. 2)."  (§4.1)

> "Mill added a third property to the two determinants of value identified by Bentham, duration and intensity."  (§4.1)

> "A long-standing objection to Mill’s move here has been to claim that his position can no longer be described as hedonism proper (or what I have called ‘explanatory hedonism’)."  (§4.1)

> "Now it has to be admitted that Mill is sailing close to the wind here. But there is logical space for a hedonist position which allows properties such as nobility to determine pleasantness, and insists that only pleasantness determines value."  (§4.1)

Context: the passage follows Carlyle's "philosophy of swine" charge and an
  oyster-life thought experiment against Bentham's "simple hedonism"; the
  paragraph after it introduces the experience machine as "a yet more
  weighty objection both to hedonism and to the view that well-being
  consists only in conscious states" (excerpted in
  sep-well-being-fall-2024-experience-machine.md).

## A. Moore, "Hedonism", §2.2

Source: Andrew Moore, "Hedonism", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
  (Fall 2024 edition), first published 20 Apr 2004, substantive revision
  17 Oct 2013.
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/hedonism/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (archive HTML fetched, tags stripped, passage read)

> "Responding especially to the charge that a Benthamite account is a ‘doctrine worthy only of swine’, J.S. Mill (ch. 2) developed an alternative approach according to which there is ‘higher’ and ‘lower’ pleasure, and its value is irreducibly a matter of its quality as well as its quantity."

> "The standard criticism of this qualitative hedonism is that pleasure's quality reduces either to its quantity, or to some anti-hedonist claim about value."

Relied on for: Crisp's and A. Moore's reports of Mill's qualitative
  hedonism, its sources (Plato, Bentham), the standard objection and
  Crisp's assessment.
Context: in A. Moore the passage closes a discussion of quantitative
  (Benthamite) hedonism and is followed by "The best sort of reply for
  qualitative hedonists is to present an account that does not suffer
  from either such reduction or such collapse."
