# IEP "Liar Paradox" (Dowden) — Prior's way out: the liar sentence is simply false (§3d)

Source: Bradley Dowden, "Liar Paradox", Internet Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy, section 3d "Barwise and Etchemendy"; bibliography entries
  for Prior 1958 and 1961.
Original: https://iep.utm.edu/par-liar/ (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (HTML fetched and tag-stripped; text read in the page)

> "Another way out says the Liar Sentence is meaningful and is true or else false, but one special step of the argument in the Liar Paradox is incorrect, namely, the inference from the Liar Sentence’s being false to its being true. Arthur Prior, following the informal suggestions of Jean Buridan and C. S. Peirce, takes this way out and concludes that the Liar Sentence is simply false."  (§3d)

> "Prior, Arthur N. “Epimenides the Cretan,” Journal of Symbolic Logic, 23 (1958), 261-266."  (References)

Relied on for: Prior's position as Dowden reports it.
Context: the passage opens §3d; it continues "So do Jon Barwise and John
Etchemendy" and then describes their proposition-based treatment. The
section is about the liar sentence in general, not Epimenides' sentence in
particular; Prior 1958 is the first of two Prior papers in the
bibliography (the other: "On a Family of Paradoxes", 1961).
