> "By freedom Arendt does not mean the ability to choose among a set of possible alternatives, but spontaneity, i.e. the capacity to begin, to start something new, to do the unexpected, with which all human beings are endowed by virtue of being born."  (§4.3)

> "Her claim is that “revolutions are the only political events which confront us directly and inevitably with the problem of beginning,” (OR, 21) since they represent the attempt to found a new political space, a space where freedom can appear as a worldly reality."  (§4.3; OR = On Revolution, New York: Viking Press, 1963, per the entry's bibliography)

> "The first is the space of appearance, a space of political freedom and equality which comes into being whenever citizens act in concert through the medium of speech and persuasion."  (§5.1)

> "Arendt proposed a federated system of councils through which citizens could effectively determine their own political affairs."  (§5.2)

Relied on for: the encyclopedia authors' reading of Arendt's concept of
  freedom, of On Revolution, and of her council proposal.
Context: §4.3 lists the revolutionary examples the authors say Arendt
  uses (American Revolution, French revolutionary clubs, Paris Commune,
  Soviets, French Resistance, Hungary 1956). The council sentence in §5.2
  follows the authors' report of her criticism of representation through
  "bureaucratic parties and state structures".
