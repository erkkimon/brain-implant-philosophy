# Condorcet 1785 — Essai, Discours préliminaire: a majority for a "contradictory" system of propositions

Source: Marie-Jean-Antoine-Nicolas de Caritat, marquis de Condorcet, Essai
  sur l'application de l'analyse à la probabilité des décisions rendues à la
  pluralité des voix, Paris: Imprimerie Royale, 1785; Discours préliminaire,
  pp. lx–lxi (page heads "lx Discours" and "PRÉLIMINAIRE. lxj").
Original: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k417181 (the locator
  the Wikipedia article "Condorcet paradox" cites; Gallica returned a
  Cloudflare block to curl, so the text was read in two archive.org scans of
  the same 1785 edition: https://archive.org/details/essaisurlapplica00cond
  (University of Toronto copy) and https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_TxQGwC1kTnkC
  (Google Books scan)). Public domain.
Retrieved: 2026-10-09 (archive.org djvu OCR text fetched with curl, passage
  grepped and compared line by line across both scans; long s "ſ", OCR as
  "f", is rendered "s", and OCR misreadings are corrected where the two scans
  agree on the reading; spelling otherwise as printed)

> "On peut demander maintenant si la pluralité peut avoir lieu en faveur d'un de ces systèmes contradictoires, & on trouvera que cela est possible."  (p. lxi)

> "Le système qui obtient la pluralité sera donc composé des trois propositions, A vaut mieux que B, C vaut mieux que A, B vaut mieux que C."  (p. lxi)

> "Ce système est le troisième, & un de ceux qui impliquent contradiction."  (p. lxi)

> "Nous examinerons donc le résultat de cette forme d'élection, 1.° en n'ayant aucun égard à ces combinaisons contradictoires, 2.° en y ayant égard."  (p. lxi, the sentence following)

Numbers in the example (p. lxi): 60 voters, first preferences 23 for A,
  19 for B, 18 for C; the pairwise majorities come out 42 to 18 for "B vaut
  mieux que C", 35 to 25 for "C vaut mieux que A", and 33 to 27 for "A vaut
  mieux que B". On p. lx–lxi Condorcet counts 8 possible systems of
  pairwise propositions for three candidates, of which only 6 do not imply
  contradiction ("il n'y en aura que 6 possibles pour trois Candidats, 24
  pour quatre, 120 pour cinq").

Relied on for: Condorcet's own statement that a plurality can form for a
  system of propositions that "implies contradiction"; the numbers of his
  example; the counts 8 and 6; his announced two-way treatment of
  contradictory combinations.
Context: The passage is in the Discours préliminaire's discussion of an
  election among three candidates in which each voter's opinion is resolved
  into pairwise propositions ("A vaut mieux que B" — A is better than B).
  Pacuit (SEP, "Voting Methods" §3.1) notes "Condorcet’s original example
  was more complicated" than the three-voter illustration, and gives the
  English translation as McLean and Urken 1995, pp. 91–113 (not read).
