> "If we define a consequence relation in terms of preservation of these designated values, then we have the paraconsistent logic LP (Priest 1979). In LP, ECQ is invalid."  (§3.6)

> "As we can see, LP invalidates ECQ by assigning a designated value, both true and false, to a contradiction."  (§3.6)

> "If this interpretative strategy is successful, we can separate LP from necessarily falling under dialetheism."  (§3.6)

> "Hence modus ponens for \(\supset\) is invalid in LP."  (§3.6)

> "The view that a consequence relation should be paraconsistent does not entail the view that there are true contradictions."  (§1.1)

> "Hence paraconsistency must be distinguished from dialetheism."  (§1.1)

> "A school developed around them in Canberra which included Brady and Mortensen, and later Priest who, together with R. Routley, incorporated dialetheism to the development."  (§1.3)

> "Despite the fact that dialetheism and paraconsistency must be distinguished, dialetheism can be a motivation for paraconsistent logic. One candidate for a dialetheia (a true contradiction) is the liar paradox."  (§2.1.2)
