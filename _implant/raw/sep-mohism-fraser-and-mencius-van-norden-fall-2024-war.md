## Mohism (Fraser)

> "They condemned unprovoked military aggression and attempted to dissuade warlike rulers from attacking other states. Far from being pacifists, however, they saw a strong defense force as crucial to state survival."  (§1)

> "Some Mohists became specialists in defensive warfare, rushing to the aid of cities and states under threat of attack."  (§1)

> "Military aggression is wrong for the same reasons that theft, robbery, and murder are: it harms others in pursuit of selfish benefit, while ultimately failing to benefit Heaven, the spirits, or society as a whole."  (§2, Inclusive Care and Condemning Aggression)

## Mencius (Van Norden)

> "In a memorable turn of phrase, Mencius warned one ruler that to attempt to conquer the world through warfare is as hopeless as “climbing a tree in search of a fish” [1A7]."

> "Mencius was asked, unofficially, whether it might be legitimate to invade Yan to restore order. He replied that it was. However, after Qi successfully invaded and annexed Yan, Mencius complained that he had not encouraged the specific actions that Qi took, which apparently included widespread killing of noncombatants and taking spoils of war (see 2B8, 1B10, 1B11, and 2B9, in that order)."

Relied on for: the Chinese entries in the just-war page's thinkers list,
  reported as Fraser and Van Norden report them.
Context: Fraser places the Mohists in the Warring States period, "each of
  the larger, more powerful states aspiring to conquer its neighbors";
  "Condemning Aggression" is one of the ten Mohist doctrines (Books 17–19
  of the Mozi). Van Norden's remarks come in his biographical section
  (Mencius held office in Qi, 2B6–7) and in comparing Confucius's view of
  force as "a last resort".
