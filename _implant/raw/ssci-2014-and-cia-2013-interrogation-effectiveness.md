(a) SSCI:

> "#1: The CIA's use of its enhanced interrogation techniques was not an effective means of acquiring intelligence or gaining cooperation from detainees."  (Findings, #1)

> "While being subjected to the CIA's enhanced interrogation techniques and afterwards, multiple CIA detainees fabricated information, resulting in faulty intelligence."  (Findings, #1)

> "#2: The CIA's justification for the use of its enhanced interrogation techniques rested on inaccurate claims of their effectiveness."  (Findings, #2)

(b) CIA:

> "We concluded that all of the examples fit within and support CIA's overall representations that information obtained from its interrogations produced unique intelligence that helped the US disrupt plots, capture terrorists, better understand the enemy, prevent another mass casualty attack, and save lives."  (¶38)

> "In commenting on the value of the information derived from detainees, we are not arguing in favor of the decision to use the enhanced techniques to which these detainees were subjected. We are not endorsing those techniques, we are not making an "ends-justify-the-means" case for them, nor are we implying that those techniques were the only way to obtain the information from detainees."  (¶37)

> "We must add, however, that in some of the Agency's representations it failed to meet its own standards for precision and accuracy of detail."  (¶38)

Relied on for: the two opposed official assessments of whether the
techniques produced otherwise unavailable intelligence — each attributed to
its author body; the CIA's own disclaimer that it is not arguing for the
techniques.
Context: the SSCI study is the Committee majority's; its minority views and
the full 6,000-page report were not read. The CIA memorandum answers the
Study's draft conclusions; ¶37 says the Agency reviewed "the Study's 20
examples" (Tab C, not read). Neither document uses the word "torture" in the
passages quoted; the SEP "Torture" entry (§4) cites "Senate Select Committee
on Intelligence 2014" in connection with Abu Ghraib and Guantanamo.
