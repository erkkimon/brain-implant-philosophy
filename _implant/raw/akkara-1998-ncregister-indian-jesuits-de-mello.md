# Akkara 1998 (National Catholic Register) — Indian Jesuits offer insight into Vatican's censure of de Mello

Source: Anto Akkara, "Indian Jesuits Offer Insight Into Vatican's Censure of de Mello", National Catholic Register, 27 September 1998.
Original: https://www.ncregister.com/news/indian-jesuits-offer-insight-into-vaticans-censure-of-de-mello (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (fetched with curl, HTML converted to text, excerpts copied verbatim; whitespace normalised)

> "publisher of the nine official books of Father de Mello, who died of a heart attack in 1987 at age 56."

> "“The Vatican is right because there is the possibility of misunderstandings,” said Father Lisbert d'Souza, president of the Jesuit Conferences of South Asia and superior of the region's 3,500 Jesuits."

> "[The nine official books of Father de Mello are Sadhana, The Song of the Bird, Wellsprings, One Minute Wisdom, The Prayer of the Frog Vol. I, The Prayer of the Frog Vol. II, Contact with God, Call to Love and One Minute Nonsense, the last three published posthumously.]"

> "“Father Tony's books were not doctrinal expositions on theology,” Father d'Souza said. “His primary concern was to help people find God — believers (in Christ), non-believers, and even atheists. Hence, there is very little religious language used.”"

> "“The books of Father Anthony de Mello were written in a multireligious context to help the followers of other religions, agnostics, and atheists in their spiritual search, and they were not intended by the author as manuals of instruction of the Catholic faithful in Christian doctrine or dogma.”"

> "Jesuit theologian Gispert Sauch told the Register, “If one does not have a certain spiritual and theological maturity, these [books] could be misunderstood. The [Vatican] warning is perfectly justifiable.”"

> "said Father de Mello's books were “basically wisdom literature … his writing [does not offer] doctrine, but advice through stories.”"

> "Father de Mello was trained in psychology and counseling, the Jesuit theologian said, and was “not dealing with doctrinal truth but helping people to overcome their problems.”"

> "“He followed the dialectic style of raking up controversy to awaken people. There is a certain irony and sarcasm in his stories. If you take them literally, you are likely to be scandalized,”"

> "A better option, Father Mathew suggested, would have been “to delete the controversial portions or to add explanations,”"

Relied on for: death in 1987 at 56; the nine official books (list includes Sadhana and The Song of the Bird); Lisbert d'Souza accepting the Vatican action while describing the books as non-doctrinal; the 1997 publisher clarification; Gispert Sauch calling the warning justifiable and the books wisdom literature; K. T. Mathew preferring deletion or explanations.
Context: News report from New Delhi. The publisher Gujarat Sahitya Prakash (spelled "Gujrath Sahitya Prakash" in the article) had stopped selling the books after the Vatican directive. d'Souza also said only nine books are authentic and that unauthorised books circulate in Spain and the Americas. The article quotes the notification itself in part.
