> "In every nonempty tavern, there is a person such that if that person is drinking, then everyone in the tavern is drinking."  (abstract)

> "Due to its counterintuitive nature it is called a paradox, even though it actually is a classical tautology. However, it is not minimally (or even intuitionistically) provable."  (abstract)

> "The same can be said of its dual, which is (equivalent to) the well-known principle of independence of premise,"  (abstract)

> "We show first that these principles are independent of the law of excluded middle and of each other"  (abstract)

> "Starting from minimal logic, we can get to intuitionistic logic by adding ex falso quodlibet (EFQ), and to classical logic, by adding double negation elimination (DNE)."  (§1)

> "These principles are all classically derivable."  (§3, after the list that includes DP(Px) := ∃y(Py → ∀xPx) and Hε(Px) := ∃y(∃xPx → Py))

> "The drinker paradox, which was popularised by Smullyan in his book of puzzles [12], is the scheme"  (§4)

> "Liberally interpreted, it states that (in every nonempty tavern) there exists a person such that if that person is drinking, then everyone (in the tavern) is drinking."  (§4)

> "Classically this is true because there is always a last person to be drinking, and it is true for that person."  (§4)

> "Due to various non-classical interpretations of “there is”, however, countermodels may be formed (see Figure 1)."  (§4)

> "Notably, the constructivist may object that it is not always clear who is the last to drink—except in the case of a tavern in which the number of patrons is an enumerable positive integer amount."  (§4)

> "Hε resembles an axiom scheme form of Hilbert’s Epsilon operator [2]."  (§4, on the dual ∃y(∃xPx → Py))

> "Milly Maietti has communicated to us the—currently unpublished—result that Hilbert’s Epsilon operator implies the drinker paradox."  (§4, n. 9)

> "The models are evocative of the intuitions. For, recall the “last drinker in the tavern” reason for accepting DP as true; similarly Hε can be justified by pointing to “the first person to drink”."  (§4)

> "DP and Hε are independent of each other in minimal logic with LEM (and so certainly over decidable predicates)."  (Corollary 7)
