## Metaphysics XIII.4 — induction and universal definition

> "for two things may be fairly ascribed to Socrates-inductive arguments and universal definition, both of which are concerned with the starting-point of science):-but Socrates did not make the universals or the definitions exist apart: they, however, gave them separate existence, and this was the kind of thing they called Ideas."  (Ross)

## Nicomachean Ethics VII.2 — Socrates on incontinence (akrasia)

> "for it would be strange-so Socrates thought-if when knowledge was in a man something else could master it and drag it about like a slave. For Socrates was entirely opposed to the view in question, holding that there is no such thing as incontinence; no one, he said, when he judges acts against what he judges best-people act so only by reason of ignorance. Now this view plainly contradicts the observed facts"  (Ross)

> "In fact Socrates used to combat the view altogether, implying that there is no such thing as Unrestraint, since no one, he held, acts contrary to what is best, believing what he does to be bad, but only through ignorance."  (1145b, Rackham; footnote markers removed)

> "the position that Socrates sought to establish actually seems to result"  (Ross, end of VII.3)

Relied on for: Aristotle as a fourth ancient witness on Socrates
(definitions and induction; the denial of akrasia) and Aristotle's own
verdict on that denial.
Context: Metaphysics XIII.4 distinguishes Socrates from those who
"gave them separate existence" (the Ideas); NE VII.2 sets out the
reputable opinions on incontinence before Aristotle's own analysis in
VII.3, which ends with the sentence quoted last.
