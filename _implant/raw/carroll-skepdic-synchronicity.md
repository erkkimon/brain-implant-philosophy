# Carroll, The Skeptic's Dictionary — "synchronicity" entry

Source: Robert Todd Carroll, "synchronicity", The Skeptic's Dictionary (skepdic.com, est. 1994; page copyright 1994–2016).
Original: https://www.skepdic.com/jung.html (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (HTML fetched with curl, converted to text, grepped)

> "Synchronicity is an explanatory principle, according to its creator, Carl Jung."

> "What it explains is more simply and elegantly explained by the ability of the human mind to find meaning and significance where there is none (apophenia)."

> "However, if you think of all the pairs of things that can happen in a person's lifetime, and add to that our very versatile ability of finding meaningful connections between things, it then seems likely that most of us will experience many meaningful coincidences. The coincidences are predictable but we are the ones who give them meaning."

> "What guide could one possibly use to determine the correctness of an interpretation?"

> "The concept of synchronicity is but an expression of apophenia."

Epigraph the entry attributes to Steven Strogatz (not traced to its source by this file): "No evidence has "ever been found for Carl Jung's idea of 'synchronicity'."

Relied on for: the skeptic's objection (apophenia; meaning supplied by the observer; no criterion of interpretation), attributed to Carroll (vocabulary/synchronicity.md).
Context: the entry also quotes Jung's Letters (1973, vol. 2, pp. 374, 426, 540) on statistics and the improbable, and lists further reading including Ernest Gallo, "Synchronicity and the Archetypes", Skeptical Inquirer.
