> "Otherwise, the danger arising from such pure, inward abstraction is that morality is an “abstract universality … without content” (PR §135)."  (Morality)

> "Perhaps no area of the PR has attracted more controversy than Hegel’s so-called “emptiness charge” against Kant: Kant is accused of offering no more than an empty formula of “duty for duty’s sake” where our task is to avoid contradicting a set formula (PR §135, see Hoy 1989, Freyenhagen 2012, Stern 2012)."  (Morality)

> "Hegel’s criticisms centre on Kant’s Formula of Universal Law stating we should “act only in accordance with that maxim through which you can at the same time will that it become a universal law” (Kant 1997, 4:421)."  (Morality)

> "Undoubtedly, Kant does offer Hegel some ammunition for this criticism."  (Morality)

> "But, of course, as Kant’s defenders are correct to point out, Hegel’s criticism is unfair and misses its mark (Wood 1989)."  (Morality)

> "In fact, Kant purposefully offers multiple versions of his formula to help draw our considerations of morality away from a purely rationalist exercise and bring it “nearer to feeling” (note 15)."  (Morality)

> "We should recall that Hegel’s emptiness charge might be made against moral philosophy."  (Morality)

> "Wood, Allen, 1989, “The Emptiness of the Moral Will,” Monist, 72: 454–83."  (Bibliography)

Relied on for: Hegel's emptiness charge (PR §135) as Brooks reports it; that it targets the
Formula of Universal Law; Brooks's own assessment that the charge "is unfair and misses its
mark", citing Wood 1989; Brooks's remark that the charge might be made against moral
philosophy as Hegel redefines it.
Context: Brooks states that for Hegel "the moral point of view" is "a purely abstract and
hypothetical exercise" (PR §105) and that Hegel "is using his own redefinition of morality to
critique how others understand the same term defined differently"; he then compares Kant's
approach with Hegel's remarks on religion and the state (PR §270, Brooks 2013).
