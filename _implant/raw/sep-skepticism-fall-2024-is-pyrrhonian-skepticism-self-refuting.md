# SEP "Skepticism" (Comesaña & Klein) — is absolute (Pyrrhonian) skepticism self-refuting? The Commitment Iteration Principle

Source: Juan Comesaña and Peter Klein, "Skepticism", The Stanford
  Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri
  Nodelman (eds.); first published 8 Dec 2001, substantive revision
  5 Dec 2019 (Copyright © 2019 by the authors). §2 "Two Basic Forms of
  Philosophical Skepticism".
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/skepticism/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (HTML of the Fall 2024 archive page read as text)

> "Is Pyrrhonian Skepticism so understood self-refuting? It is certainly formally consistent"  (§2)

> "But consider the principle that whenever someone is committed to a proposition p they are also (perhaps implicitly) committed to the proposition that belief is the (or at least a) justified attitude towards p. Call this the “Commitment Iteration Principle”. If the Commitment Iteration Principle holds, then Pyrrhonian Skepticism is indeed self-refuting."  (§2)

> "But Pyrrhonian skeptics need not hold the Commitment Iteration Principle."  (§2)

> "It is not clear, then, that the charge of self-refutation represents an independent indictment of Pyrrhonian Skepticism."  (§2)

Relied on for: the global sceptical thesis applied to itself, and the
authors' assessment of the self-refutation charge against it.
Context: the authors stipulate "Without any claim to historical accuracy"
that Pyrrhonian Skepticism is "the thesis that suspension of judgment is
