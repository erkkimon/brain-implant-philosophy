# SEP "Spinoza's Psychological Theory" (LeBuffe) — the striving doctrine and orthodox vs predominant egoism

Source: Michael LeBuffe, "Spinoza's Psychological Theory", The Stanford
  Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri
  Nodelman (eds.); first published 2001-10-23, substantive revision
  2020-05-26. Preamble, §1.3, §2.2.
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/spinoza-psychological/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (HTML of the Fall 2024 archive page read; quotations matched by script)

> "Second, Spinoza attempts to show that moral concepts, such as the concepts of good and evil, virtue, and perfection, have a basis in human psychology."  (preamble)
> "IIIp9 suggests that Spinoza is a psychological egoist of some sort. That is, it suggests that he believes that what human beings desire to do is to secure their own interests (construed here as perseverance in being)."  (§1.3)
> "IIIp9 might be supposed to support a very strong version of psychological egoism, orthodox egoism (perhaps Delahunty, 221, holds this view). Orthodox egoism, is the view that human beings are always consciously selfish."  (§1.3)
> "Other evidence suggests that Spinoza is not an orthodox egoist, however."  (§1.3)
> "IVp44s suggests that Spinoza holds a different kind of view, predominant egoism, the view that most people, most of the time consciously desire perseverance in their own being."  (§1.3)
> "However, the weight of the textual evidence supports the view that he is a predominant, not an orthodox, egoist."  (§1.3)
> "Spinoza’s predominant egoism, together with some of his still stronger statements of psychological egoism such as that at I Appendix, suggest that individuals are not, or are not often, altruistic."  (§2.2)
> "However, Spinoza does offer an argument (IVp37) for the view that any good that I want for myself I will have reason to want for others as well,"  (§2.2)

Relied on for: the readings of Spinoza as an orthodox egoist (LeBuffe names
Delahunty 1985, p. 221, as perhaps holding it) and as a predominant egoist
(LeBuffe's own conclusion from IVp44s and IVp20); the IVp37 argument.
Context: LeBuffe quotes Curley's translation. Between the "orthodox" and
"predominant" sentences he quotes IVp44s on greed, ambition and lust as
"species of madness", ends other than perseverance in being.
