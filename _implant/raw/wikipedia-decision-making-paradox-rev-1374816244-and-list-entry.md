> "Decision-making paradox: Selecting the best decision-making method is a decision problem in itself."  (List of paradoxes, "Decision theory")

> "It was first described by Triantaphyllou, and has been recognized in the related literature as a fundamental paradox in multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA), multi-criteria decision making (MCDM) and decision analysis since then."  (lead)

> "It arises from the observation that different decision-making methods, both normative and descriptive, yield different results, when fed with exactly the same decision problem and data."  (Description; tagged citation needed)

> "It was found that when a method was used, say method X (which is one of the previous four methods), the conclusion was that another method was best (say, method Y). When method Y was used, then another method, say method Z, was suggested as being the best one, and so on."  (Description; cites Triantaphyllou & Mann 1989 and Triantaphyllou 2000)

> "For such problems, the weighted sum model (WSM) is the widely accepted approach, thus, their results were compared with the ones derived from the WSM."  (Description, first evaluative criterion)

> "The following multi-criteria decision-making methods have been confirmed to exhibit this paradox:The analytic hierarchy process (AHP) and some of its variants, the weighted product model (WPM), the ELECTRE (outranking) method and its variants and the TOPSIS method."  (Methods affected; "paradox:The" without a space as in the source)

> "Other methods that have not been tested yet but may exhibit the same phenomenon include the following:"  (Other methods; followed by ANP, PROMETHEE, OPA, MAUT, DRSA, AIRM, NSFDSS, GRA, SIR, PAPRIKA, VA, with no reference attached)

Rank reversals article:

> "In decision-making, a rank reversal is a change in the rank ordering of the preferability of alternative possible decisions when, for example, the method of choosing changes or the set of other available alternatives changes."  (lead)

> "Thus the following question emerges: How can one evaluate decision-making methods? This is a very difficult issue and may not be answered in a globally accepted manner."  (lead)

> "A few years later it was observed that the AHP, as well as a new variant to it that was introduced by Professor Thomas Saaty (the inventor of the AHP) in response to the previous observation by Belton and Gear, may exhibit rank reversals when a non-optimal alternative is replaced by a worse one (and not a copy of an alternative as in Belton and Gear's experiment)."  ("Rank reversal")

> "It is something that continues to be considered controversial by many and is frequently debated."  ("Rank reversal")

> "The weighted product model (WPM) does not exhibit the previous types of rank reversals, due to the multiplication formula it uses. However, the WPM does cause rank reversals when it is compared with the weighted sum model (WSM) and under the condition that all the criteria of a given decision problem can be measured in exactly the same unit."  ("Rank reversals of Type 5")
