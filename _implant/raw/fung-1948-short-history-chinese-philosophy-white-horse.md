# Fung Yu-lan 1948 — Kung-sun Lung's theory of universals: the white horse

Source: Fung Yu-lan, A Short History of Chinese Philosophy, ed. Derk Bodde,
  New York: Macmillan, 1948 (copyright 1948; scanned printing 1958), ch. 8
  "The School of Names", section "Kung-sun Lung's Theory of Universals",
  pp. 87–88.
Original: https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.260423 (Digital
  Library of India scan; OCR text file 2015.260423.A-Short_djvu.txt)
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (OCR text fetched; line-break hyphens rejoined; each
  quotation checked by script against that text; page numbers from the
  running heads in the OCR)

> "Lung replied: “My horse is white, and a white horse is not a horse.” And so saying, he passed with his horse."  (p. 87)

> "Instead of emphasizing, as did Hui Shih, that actual things are relative and changeable, Kung-sun Lung emphasized that names are absolute and permanent. In this way he arrived at the same concept of Platonic ideas or universals that has been so conspicuous in Western philosophy."  (p. 87)

> "This proposition Kung-sun Lung tries to prove through three arguments."  (p. 87)

> "In terms of Western logic, we may say that this argument emphasizes the difference in the intension of the terms “horse,” “white,” and “white horse.”"  (p. 87)

> "In terms of Western logic, we may say that this argument emphasizes the difference in the extension of the terms “horse” and “white horse.”"  (p. 88)

> "Kung-sun Lung seems to emphasize the distinction between the universal, “horseness,” and the universal, “white-horseness.”"  (p. 88)


Relied on for: Fung's universals reading and his intension/extension
analysis of the arguments he quotes; the frontier anecdote as he tells it.
Context: Fung contrasts Kung-sun Lung with Hui Shih and goes on to the
"hard and white" chapter. The SEP cites the same book as "Fung 1958" (the
printing), Yi 2018 as "Fung (1948)".
