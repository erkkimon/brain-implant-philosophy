## Snarey 1985 (cultural universality)

John R. Snarey, "Cross-cultural universality of social-moral development:
A critical review of Kohlbergian research", Psychological Bulletin 97(2),
1985, pp. 202–232. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.97.2.202 (Crossref
record verified, author, title, volume, issue, pages as given; doi.org
returns 403 to scripts). PubMed 3983300 carries no abstract. Not read.
Its content is recorded only as Gibbs et al. 2007 report it (below).

## Gibbs, Basinger, Grime & Snarey 2007

John C. Gibbs, Karen S. Basinger, Rebecca L. Grime, John R. Snarey, "Moral
judgment development across cultures: Revisiting Kohlberg's universality
claims", Developmental Review 27(4), 2007, pp. 443–500.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dr.2007.04.001 (resolves, 200). Abstract as
printed in ERIC EJ778927 (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ778927):

> "Snarey [...] examined Kohlberg's claims in a survey of 45 cross-cultural studies in 27 countries that used Kohlberg's dilemma method of stage assessment (the "Moral Judgment Interview, MJI" [...])."

> "Snarey's review identified a basic stage trend but also the cultural specificity of Kohlberg's highest stages. As a remedy, Snarey proposed a culturally inclusive elaboration of the highest stages."

> "Snarey's and Gibbs's revisionist models are compared and used as frameworks for interpreting not only the MJI findings but also newer SRM-SF findings from 75 cross-cultural studies conducted in 23 countries. Despite continuing questions for research, multimethod convergence is found for common moral values, basic moral judgment stage development, and related social perspective-taking across cultural groups."

## Simpson 1974 (cultural bias)

Elizabeth Léonie Simpson, "Moral Development Research: A Case Study of
Scientific Cultural Bias", Human Development 17(2), 1974, pp. 81–106.
https://doi.org/10.1159/000271335 (Crossref record verified: author,
title "Moral Development Research", Human Development, pp. 81–106; the
year 1974 from ERIC EJ103005; volume and issue not in the Crossref record
and not stated on the page). Abstract as printed in the Crossref record:

> "Findings suggest that the definitions of stages and the assumptions underlying them, including the view that the scheme is universally applicable, are ethnocentric and culturally-biased."

## Jaffee & Hyde 2000 (meta-analysis)

Sara Jaffee and Janet Shibley Hyde, "Gender differences in moral
orientation: A meta-analysis", Psychological Bulletin 126(5), 2000,
pp. 703–726. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.126.5.703 (Crossref record
verified). Abstract as printed in PubMed 10989620
(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10989620/):

> "The meta-analysis revealed small differences in the care orientation favoring females (d = -.28) and small differences in the justice orientation favoring males (d = .19)."

> "These findings do not offer strong support for the claim that the care orientation is used predominantly by women and that the justice orientation is used predominantly by men."

## Keefer 1993 (read in full; reports Walker et al. 1987)

Matthew Wilks Keefer, "Gilligan's Moral Orientation Hypothesis:
Strategies of Justification and Practical Deliberation", paper presented
at the 60th meeting of the Society for Research in Child Development,
New Orleans, 25 March 1993. ERIC ED357236,
https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED357236.pdf (PDF read with
pdftotext; locators are pages of the ERIC PDF, since the typescript's
page numbering is not legible throughout).

> "Using this same criterion Walker et. al (1987) found that only 53% of subjects exhibited consistent orientation reasoning. Further, when consistency was assessed by grouping considerations across three hypothetical and one real-life dilemma, Walker et al. (1987) found that only 40 of 240 subjects (16.7%) were consistent in their use of moral reasoning strategy."  (PDF p. 3; the criterion: "75% or more of the coded considerations in the dilemma belonged to either a justice or a care category", set by Gilligan and Attanucci 1988)

> "They conclude, contra Gilligan and Attanucci (1988) that most individuals use both orientations to a significant degree."  (PDF p. 3, of Walker et al. 1987)

> "In Kohlberg's Moral Judgement Interview (Colby and Kohlberg 1987) subjects are read the Heinz dilemma and then asked 'Should Heinz steal the drug?' The structured interview requires that the yes or no answer to this question be followed up by the question 'Why?' or 'Why not?'"  (PDF p. 11)

> "This encourages subjects towards a strategy of justifying the solution which they might favour."  (PDF p. 11)

> "The results supported the hypothesis that subjects oriented to deliberation (mostly women) perceive this dilemma as a problem of prospective judgement (deliberation) as demonstrated by their use of narrative structures (interactive plans and social planning episodes); while subjects oriented to justification (mostly men) perceived the dilemma as a problem of retrospective justification"  (author's abstract, PDF p. 2; the study "examined 32 adults' responses (16 men and 16 women) to three questions pertaining to Kohlberg's Heinz dilemma")

> "Analyses conducted to assess the relationship between gender and structures applied to the third question graded most clearly toward retrospective evaluation revealed that women and men were equally likely to adduce principles and rationales in defense of Heinz's action"  (PDF p. 14)
