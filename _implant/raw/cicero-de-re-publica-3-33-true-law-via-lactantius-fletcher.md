# Cicero, De re publica III.33 — "true law, right reason", as preserved by Lactantius (Divine Institutes VI.8)

Source: Lactantius, Divine Institutes, Book VI, ch. 8 ("Of the Errors of
  Philosophers, and the Variableness of Law"), quoting Cicero, De re
  publica, book III; tr. William Fletcher, Ante-Nicene Fathers vol. 7, ed.
  Roberts, Donaldson and Coxe (Buffalo, NY: Christian Literature
  Publishing Co., 1886). The passage is known as De re publica III.33 (the
  section number is the conventional locator named in the brief; the
  fetched text gives it only as "his third book respecting the Republic").
Original: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/07016.htm
  (public domain translation, 1886; revised for New Advent by Kevin Knight)
Retrieved: 2026-10-15 (page fetched with curl, tags stripped, quotations
  string-matched; the Latin Library text of De re publica III was also
  fetched and does not contain the passage, which survives through
  Lactantius)

> "that sacred, that heavenly law, which Marcus Tullius, in his third book respecting the Republic, has described almost with a divine voice"  (Lactantius, VI.8)

> "There is indeed a true law, right reason, agreeing with nature, diffused among all, unchanging, everlasting, which calls to duty by commanding, deters from wrong by forbidding"  (Cicero as quoted)

> "It is not allowable to alter the provisions of this law, nor is it permitted us to modify it, nor can it be entirely abrogated. Nor, truly, can we be released from this law, either by the senate or by the people"  (Cicero as quoted)

> "Nor will there be one law at Rome and another at Athens; one law at the present time, and another hereafter: but the same law, everlasting and unchangeable, will bind all nations at all times; and there will be one common Master and Ruler of all, even God, the framer, arbitrator, and proposer of this law"  (Cicero as quoted)

Relied on for: positions/rule-of-higher-law.md — the classical statement
  that a law of right reason cannot be repealed by senate or people.
Context: Lactantius quotes the passage to argue that Cicero, though "far
  removed from the knowledge of the truth", described the law of God.
