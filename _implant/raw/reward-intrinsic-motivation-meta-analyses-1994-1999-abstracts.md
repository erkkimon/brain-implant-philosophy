## Cameron & Pierce 1994

Source: Judy Cameron and W. David Pierce, "Reinforcement, Reward, and Intrinsic Motivation: A Meta-Analysis", Review of Educational Research 64(3), 1994, pp. 363–423.
Original: doi:10.3102/00346543064003363 (also registered as doi:10.2307/1170677; both checked by content negotiation)

> "The main meta-analysis included 96 experimental studies that used between-groups designs to compare rewarded subjects to nonrewarded controls on four measures of intrinsic motivation. Results indicate that, overall, reward does not decrease intrinsic motivation."  (abstract, via Crossref)

> "When interaction effects are examined, findings show that verbal praise produces an increase in intrinsic motivation. The only negative effect appears when expected tangible rewards are given to individuals simply for doing a task."  (abstract)

> "Under this condition, there is a minimal negative effect on intrinsic motivation as measured by time spent on task following the removal of reward."  (abstract)

## Eisenberger & Cameron 1996

Source: Robert Eisenberger and Judy Cameron, "Detrimental effects of reward: Reality or myth?", American Psychologist 51(11), 1996, pp. 1153–1166.
Original: doi:10.1037/0003-066x.51.11.1153 (checked); PMID 8937264

> "An analysis of a quarter century of research on intrinsic task interest and creativity revealed, however, that (a) detrimental effects of reward occur under highly restricted, easily avoidable conditions"  (abstract, via PubMed)

## Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999

Source: Edward L. Deci, Richard Koestner and Richard M. Ryan, "A meta-analytic review of experiments examining the effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation", Psychological Bulletin 125(6), 1999, pp. 627–668.
Original: doi:10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627 (checked); PMID 10589297

> "A meta-analysis of 128 studies examined the effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation. As predicted, engagement-contingent, completion-contingent, and performance-contingent rewards significantly undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation (d = -0.40, -0.36, and -0.28, respectively), as did all rewards, all tangible rewards, and all expected rewards."  (abstract, via PubMed)

> "Positive feedback enhanced both free-choice behavior (d = 0.33) and self-reported interest (d = 0.31)."  (abstract)

> "The authors review 4 previous meta-analyses of this literature and detail how this study's methods, analyses, and results differed from the previous ones."  (abstract)

## The 1999 comments and reply (same issue)

Lepper, Henderlong & Gingras, "Understanding the effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation—Uses and abuses of meta-analysis: Comment on Deci, Koestner, and Ryan (1999)", Psychological Bulletin 125(6), 669–676, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.669 (checked); PMID 10589298:

> "Interestingly, despite their common focus, these reviews have offered dramatically opposed bottom-line conclusions about the meaning and implications of this literature."  (abstract, via PubMed)

> "In this comment, the authors examine differences among these 3 reviews and conclude that the findings of this literature have been more accurately captured by the reviews of Deci et al. and Tang and Hall (1995) than by that of Cameron and Pierce (1994)."  (abstract)

Eisenberger, Pierce & Cameron, "Effects of reward on intrinsic motivation—Negative, neutral, and positive: Comment on Deci, Koestner, and Ryan (1999)", Psychological Bulletin 125(6), 677–691, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.677 (checked; PubMed lists no abstract).
