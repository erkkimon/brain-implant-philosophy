# Campkin 2013 — reading Douglas's *Purity and Danger* (1966): "matter out of place", taboo as coding practice, Steiner

Source: Ben Campkin, "Placing “Matter Out of Place”: Purity and Danger as
  Evidence for Architecture and Urbanism", *Architectural Theory Review*
  18(1) (2013): 46–61; accepted manuscript, UCL Discovery eprint 1379451.
Original: https://doi.org/10.1080/13264826.2013.785579 ; manuscript
  https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1379451/ (copyrighted; excerpts
  only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-15 (PDF via curl, pdftotext; DOI verified by Crossref
  content negotiation; quotations checked by script). Douglas's book itself
  (London: Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1966; Routledge 2000/2002 reprints) was
  not available to read: Internet Archive copies are lending-restricted.
  Douglas's words below are as Campkin quotes them, with his page
  references to the Routledge 2000 edition.

> "If we can abstract pathogenicity and hygiene from our notion of dirt, we are left with the old definition of dirt as matter out of place."  (Campkin's epigraph, citing Douglas, Purity and Danger, p. 36)

> "In her preface to the final edition of Purity and Danger produced in her lifetime Douglas again places emphasis on the spatiality of her subject, writing that ‘Taboo is a spontaneous coding practice which sets up a vocabulary of spatial limits and physical and verbal signals to hedge around vulnerable relations’."  (Campkin, citing Douglas p. xiii)

> "Douglas’ view is that pollution taboos – rules in society that guard against the unclassifiable, the impure and the hybrid – have a physical expression."  (Katherine Shonfield, quoted by Campkin)

> "In her understanding of taboo, Douglas drew on the work of the anthropologist Franz Steiner, who had been her teacher at the University of Oxford; Franz Steiner, Taboo, New York: Philosophical Library, 1956."  (Campkin, note 27)

> "the aphorism Douglas invokes, ‘dirt is matter out of place’, originates in discussions of nineteenth-century urbanisation."  (Campkin, abstract)

Relied on for: Douglas's definition of dirt and her later definition of
  taboo, as Campkin quotes them; Shonfield's summary; the Steiner link and
  Steiner's 1956 *Taboo* bibliographic data, as Campkin gives them.
Context: Campkin surveys the book's reception in architecture and argues
  that her structuralist approach raises problems that critical-theory,
  feminist and psychoanalytic readings brought out (his assessment).
