## Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015 (review)

Source: Ben Alderson-Day, Charles Fernyhough, "Inner speech: Development, cognitive functions, phenomenology, and neurobiology", Psychological Bulletin 141(5), 2015, pp. 931–965.
Original: doi:10.1037/bul0000021 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)

> "Imagining questions in inner speech prior to starting the task (e.g., statements such as “Will I . . .?”) were associated with better anagram performance and intention to exercise compared with imagining declarative statements (e.g., “I will . . .”), with the latter being mediated by changes in the intrinsic motivation to exercise."  (section on inner speech and self-regulation)

> "Such protocols have their limitations: they do not include a control for checking that participants were actually engaging in the kinds of self-talk they were instructed to use, nor whether participants also deployed self-talk during the subsequent performance tasks (i.e., anagrams)."  (same paragraph; "Such protocols" covers Senay et al. 2010 and Dolcos & Albarracín 2014)

## Lohmann, Jones & Albarracín 2019

Source: Sophie Lohmann, Christopher R. Jones, Dolores Albarracín, "The modulating role of self-posed questions in repeated choice: Integral and incidental questions can increase or decrease behavioral rigidity", Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 85, 2019, 103840.
Original: doi:10.1016/j.jesp.2019.103840 ; author manuscript https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7442229/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)

> "Questions generally increased speed in the upcoming task, supporting past findings that even exposure to question-like syntax can enhance performance."  (abstract)

> "Introducing questions thus sped up responses regardless of what question was asked ( Table 1 ), which is consistent with the findings of Senay and colleagues (2010)."  (results, response times)

## Puchalska-Wasyl 2014 (abstract only)

Source: Małgorzata M. Puchalska-Wasyl, "When Interrogative Self-talk Improves Task Performance: The Role of Answers to Self-posed Questions", Applied Cognitive Psychology 28(3), 2014, pp. 374–381 (online 2014-02-19).
Original: doi:10.1002/acp.3007 (copyrighted; closed access per Unpaywall and Semantic Scholar on 2026-10-09; abstract read from the Crossref metadata returned by DOI content negotiation; full text not read)

> "However, the findings of Senay, Albarracin, and Noguchi suggest that interrogative self‐talk produces better task performance than declarative one. Two of the experiments reported here were meant to replicate that study, but the expected differences were not confirmed."  (abstract)

> "Experiment 3 showed that if a self‐posed question about future behavior was answered positively, task performance was better than in groups exposed either to the self‐statement ‘I will do it’ or to a negative answer following the question. However, these differences occurred only in those who self‐reported the awareness of the impact of self‐talk on their thought processes."  (abstract)

> "This effect and the possible reasons why between‐group differences were not found in Experiments 1 and 2 are discussed."  (abstract)
