> "Sin is an offense against reason, truth, and right conscience; it is failure in genuine love for God and neighbor caused by a perverse attachment to certain goods."  (§1849)

> "Mortal sin destroys charity in the heart of man by a grave violation of God's law"  (§1855)

> "Mortal sin is sin whose object is grave matter and which is also committed with full knowledge and deliberate consent."  (§1857, a quotation within the Catechism, its footnote 131)

> "One commits venial sin when, in a less serious matter, he does not observe the standard prescribed by the moral law, or when he disobeys the moral law in a grave matter, but without full knowledge or without complete consent."  (§1862)

> "However venial sin does not set us in direct opposition to the will and friendship of God; it does not break the covenant with God."  (§1863)

> "Sin is a personal act."  (§1868)

> "are the expression and effect of personal sins"  (§1869, of "Structures of sin")

Relied on for: the Catechism's definition (§1849, which goes on to quote
  Augustine's "an utterance, a deed, or a desire contrary to the eternal
  law"); the three conditions of mortal sin; venial sin; personal and
  "social sin".
Context: §1854 says the mortal/venial distinction is "already evident in
  Scripture" and "became part of the tradition of the Church". §1858
  specifies grave matter by the Ten Commandments. §1864 treats
  blasphemy against the Holy Spirit. §1866 says vices can be classified
  by the virtues they oppose "or also be linked to the capital sins", and
  lists the capital sins
  (pride, avarice, envy, wrath, lust, gluttony, sloth or acedia).
