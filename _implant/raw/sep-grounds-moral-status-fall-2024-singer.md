> "Both McMahan (2002) and Singer (1993) hold, on quite different grounds, that death is not very bad for most animals, while it is very bad for ordinary adult human beings."  (§2.4)

> "Singer (1993), famously associated with the equal consideration view, might fit this interpretation. However, because he thinks that self-conscious beings have interests that merely conscious beings lack, he allows that the former beings might have rights that the latter lack."  (§5.3)

Relied on for: the authors' reading of Singer's Practical Ethics
(2nd ed. 1993) on killing and on rights of self-conscious beings.
Context: §2.4 distinguishes reasons constitutive of moral status from other
reasons; §5.3 "Rudimentary Cognitive Capacities" discusses equal
consideration.
