# SEP "Nelson Goodman" (Cohnitz & Rossberg) — §5 the old problem as pseudo-problem, the new riddle, positionality, Goodman's solution (entrenchment, projectibility)

> "Perhaps his most famous contribution is the “grue-paradox”, which points to the problem that in order to learn by induction, we need to make a distinction between projectible and non-projectible predicates."  (preamble)

> "Accordingly, the old problem of induction, which requires such a justification of induction, is a pseudo-problem."  (§5.1, reporting Goodman's view)

> "We have two mutually incompatible predictions, both confirmed to the same degree by the past evidence. We could obviously define infinitely many grue-like predicates that would all lead to new, similarly incompatible predictions."  (§5.3)

> "For valid inductive inferences the choice of predicates matters."  (§5.3)

> "An immediate reply is that the illegitimate generalization L4 involves a temporal restriction, just as L2 was restricted spatially (see e.g., Carnap 1947)."  (§5.3; L4 is "all emeralds are grue")

> "The trouble is that this reply makes it relative to a language whether or not a predicate is projectible."  (§5.3)

> "Instead of providing a theory that would ultimately justify our choice of predicates for induction, he develops a theory that provides an account of how we in fact choose predicates for induction and projection."  (§5.4)

> "A hypothesis is projectible iff it is supported, unviolated, and unexhausted, and all hypotheses conflicting with it are overridden."  (§5.4, given as Goodman's definition, "FFF, 108")

> "A hypothesis is nonprojectible iff it and a conflicting hypothesis are supported, unviolated, unexhausted, and not overridden."  (§5.4)

> "(The critical literature on Goodman’s New Riddle is too extensive to do it justice here; see Stalker 1994 and Elgin 1997c for selections of important essays on the topic."  (§5.4)

> "For the relation of the grue-paradox to Kripke’s Wittgenstein’s rule following puzzle (1982), see, for instance, Wright 1984; Hacking 1993; Cohnitz and Rossberg 2006, 205–208; Kowalenko 2022.)"  (§5.4)

> "Goodman’s solution makes projectibility essentially a matter of what language we use and have used to describe and predict the behaviour of our world."  (§5.4)
