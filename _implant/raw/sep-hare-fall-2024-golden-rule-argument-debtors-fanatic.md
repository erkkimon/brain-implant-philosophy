Relied on for: the Golden-Rule form of Hare's argument as Price states it;
the debtors' case; the later attempt to close off the fanatic's evasion.
Context: The first quotation directly follows "generate a “Golden Rule”
form of argument" (in the existing Hare excerpt). The debtors' case is
Hare 1963: 90–1 per Price: A owes B, B owes C, and the law lets creditors
imprison debtors; if B says "I ought to put A into prison" he commits
himself to a principle implying "Let C put me into prison". "This
possibility" in the third quotation is the evasion by the "fanatic" who
disregards his own interests for an impersonal ideal.
