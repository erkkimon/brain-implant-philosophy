> "The ship on which Theseus sailed with the youths and returned in safety, the thirty-oared galley, was preserved by the Athenians down to the time of Demetrius Phalereus. They took away the old timbers from time to time, and put new and sound ones in their places, so that the vessel became a standing illustration for the philosophers in the mooted question of growth, some declaring that it remained the same, others that it was not the same vessel."  (Thes. 23.1)

Loeb editor's note 29, on Demetrius Phalereus:

> "Regent of Athens for Cassander of Macedon, 317‑307 B.C. Cf. Plato, Phaedo, p58."  (note 29)

Relied on for: the ancient report of the ship (Plutarch as the source); the
  dating of the preservation "down to the time of Demetrius Phalereus"
  (317–307 BCE per the Loeb note); the report that philosophers disputed it
  on both sides; the phrase "the mooted question of growth". Also for the
  textual observation that the passage says the old timbers were taken away
  "from time to time" and does not itself say that all were replaced or that
  the old ones were reassembled.
Context: the chapter follows Plutarch's account of Theseus's return from
  Crete and the Athenian festivals instituted in memory of it (ch. 22);
  23.2 continues with the Oschophoria. Nothing else in the chapter concerns
  identity.
