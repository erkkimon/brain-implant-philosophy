# Sharp 1991 — Jung Lexicon, entry "Individuation" (with Collected Works paragraph locators)

Source: Daryl Sharp, *Jung Lexicon: A Primer of Terms & Concepts*
  (Toronto: Inner City Books, 1991), entry "Individuation". The entry
  quotes Jung's Collected Works (Hull translation) and gives CW paragraph
  numbers in brackets.
Original: http://www.psychceu.com/jung/sharplexicon.html (author-authorised
  online text, "Copyright ©1991 Daryl Sharp All rights reserved");
  copyrighted, excerpts only.
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (HTML fetched, tags stripped, entry located by grep;
  the "Ibid." before par. 757 follows a citation of "Definitions," CW 6,
  par. 756 in the preceding entry "Individuality").

> "A process of psychological differentiation , having for its goal the development of the individual personality. In general, it is the process by which individual beings are formed and differentiated; in particular, it is the development of the psychological individual as a being distinct from the general, collective psychology. [ Ibid., par. 757.]"

> "Individuation is a process informed by the archetypal ideal of wholeness, which in turn depends on a vital relationship between ego and unconscious."  (Sharp's gloss)

> "the process of individuation must lead to more intense and broader collective relationships and not to isolation. [Definitions," CW 6, par. 758.]"

> "Individuation differs from individualism in that the former deviates from collective norms but retains respect for them, while the latter eschews them entirely."  (Sharp's gloss, introducing CW 6, par. 761)

> "The goal of the individuation process is the synthesis of the self. [The Psychology of the Child Archetype," CW 9i, par. 278.]"

> "In Jung's view, no one is ever completely individuated."  (Sharp's gloss)

Relied on for: the CW 6 paragraph numbers (757, 758, 761) of the
  "Individuation" definition; the CW 9i locator (par. 278) for the self as
  goal; Sharp's own glosses, attributed to him.
Context: Sharp's preface says Jung "died in 1961, without ever having
  presented a systematic summary of his psychology". The entry also quotes
  CW 7 par. 269, CW 8 par. 432, CW 16 pars. 400 and 448 and CW 18
  pars. 1095f.
