> "Alasdair MacIntyre and Charles Taylor argued that moral and political judgment will depend on the language of reasons and the interpretive framework within which agents view their world, hence that it makes no sense to begin the political enterprise by abstracting from the interpretive dimensions of human beliefs, practices, and institutions"  (Bell, §1)

> "In After Virtue, Alasdair MacIntyre defended the Aristotelian ideal of the intimate, reciprocating local community bound by shared ends, where people simply assume and fulfill socially given roles (MacIntyre 1984). But this pre-modern Gemeinschaft conception of an all-encompassing community that members unreflectively endorse seemed distinctly ill-suited for complex and conflict-ridden large-scale industrialized societies."  (Bell, §1)

> "New Relativism, as we shall see, offers a novel take on the old question of alethic relativism and gives weight to Alasdair MacIntyre’s observation that relativism may have been refuted a number of times too often, whereas genuinely refutable doctrines only need to be refuted once (MacIntyre 1985: 5)."  (Baghramian & Carter, §4.3.1)

> "For example, Alasdair MacIntyre (1988: ch. 18 and 1994) has argued that, in some circumstances, it is possible to realize, through an exercise in imagination, that a conflicting and incommensurable moral tradition is rationally superior to one’s own tradition."  (Gowans, §5)

> "They may say that the Davidsonian account cannot assure sufficient common ground to resolve conflicts between moral frameworks (or to ensure that there is really only one framework), and that MacIntyre’s approach is likely to work at best only in some cases."  (Gowans, §5, stating the reply of proponents of metaethical moral relativism)

> "Following in the footsteps of Anscombe (1958) and MacIntyre (1985), Talbot Brewer (2009) argues that to work with the categories of rightness and wrongness is already to get off on the wrong foot."  (Hursthouse & Pettigrove, §3)

> "Is it not the case that different cultures embody different virtues, (MacIntyre 1985) and hence that the v-rules will pick out actions as right or wrong only relative to a particular culture?"  (Hursthouse & Pettigrove, §3, the cultural-relativity objection)

Relied on for: MacIntyre among the communitarian critics of liberal theory
and Bell's criticism of his model of community; his remark on relativism
(1985, "Relativism, Power and Philosophy", DOI 10.2307/3131644); his claim
that one tradition can be judged rationally superior and the relativist
reply; his place in the virtue-ethics literature.
Context: Bell's §1 continues with Walzer's caste example, under the claim
that 1980s communitarians were "less-than-successful at putting forward
attractive visions of non-liberal societies"; Gowans gives MacIntyre's view
as one of several objectivist responses to the incommensurability argument
and then the relativists' rejoinder.
