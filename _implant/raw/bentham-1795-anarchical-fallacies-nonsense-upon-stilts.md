# Bentham 1795 — "nonsense upon stilts": natural rights in the examination of the French Declaration of Rights (Anarchical Fallacies, Art. II)

Source: Jeremy Bentham, "Anarchical Fallacies; being an Examination of the
  Declarations of Rights issued during the French Revolution", in The Works
  of Jeremy Bentham, ed. John Bowring, vol. II (Edinburgh: William Tait,
  1838–43), commentary on Article II of the Declaration of the Rights of Man
  and the Citizen. Crimmins (SEP "Jeremy Bentham", §1 and bibliography) dates
  the text 1795 and cites it as "Nonsense upon Stilts, Or Pandora's Box
  Opened ..." in the Collected Works volume Rights, Representation, and
  Reform (2002), 317–401, at p. 330.

> "The end in view of every political association is the preservation of the natural and imprescriptible rights of man. These rights are liberty, property, security, and resistance to oppression."  (Article II of the Declaration, as Bentham prints it)

> "That there are no such things as natural rights--no such things as rights anterior to the establishment of government—no such things as natural rights opposed to, in contradistinction to, legal"  (Art. II commentary)

> "But reasons for wishing there were such things as rights, are not rights; — a reason for wishing that a certain right were established, is not that right-want is not supply - hunger is not bread."  (Art. II commentary)

> "Natural rights is simple nonsense: natural and imprescriptible rights, rhetorical nonsense, -nonsense upon stilts."  (Art. II commentary)

Relied on for: thinkers/bentham.md — Bentham's own wording of the
  "nonsense upon stilts" remark and the claim that rights are creatures of
  law and government.
Context: the commentary goes on: "But this rhetorical nonsense ends in the
  old strain of mischievous nonsense: for immediately a list of these
  pretended natural rights is given, and those are so expressed as to
  present to view legal rights." Crimmins (SEP §1) quotes the phrase as
  "nonsense on stilts" from the 2002 Collected Works text; the Bowring text
  on Wikisource reads "nonsense upon stilts".
