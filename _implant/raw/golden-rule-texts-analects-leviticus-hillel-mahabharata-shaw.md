> "Tsze-kung asked, saying, 'Is there one word which may serve as a rule of practice for all one's life?'"  (Analects 15.23, Legge; the question to which the RECIPROCITY answer replies)

> "Tsze-kung said, 'What I do not wish men to do to me, I also wish not to do to men.' The Master said, 'Ts'ze, you have not attained to that.'"  (Analects 5.11, Legge)

> "not to do to others as you would not wish done to yourself"  (Analects 12.2, Legge; part of the Master's answer to Chung-kung's question about "perfect virtue")

> "19:18 Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD."  (Leviticus 19:18, KJV)

> "Convert me on the condition that thou teach me the whole Torah while I stand on one foot."  (Shabbat 31a, Rodkinson; a Gentile to Shamai, who "pushed him away")

> "What is hateful to thee, do not unto thy fellow; this is the whole law. All the rest is a commentary to this law; go and learn it."  (Shabbat 31a, Rodkinson; Hillel's answer)

> "One should never do that to another which one regards as injurious to one's own self. This, in brief, is the rule of Righteousness."  (Mahabharata XIII, Anusasana Parva, Section CXIII, Ganguli)

> "Do not do unto others as you would that they should do unto you. Their tastes may not be the same."  (Shaw, Maxims for Revolutionists, under the heading "THE GOLDEN RULE")

> "The golden rule is that there are no golden rules."  (Shaw, same section)

> "Man denke ja nicht, daß hier das triviale: quod tibi non vis fieri etc. zur Richtschnur oder Princip dienen könne."  (Kant, AA IV 430, footnote)

Relied on for: the negative-form statements in the Analects beside 15.23;
the Leviticus verse; Hillel's negative form and its setting; the
Mahabharata statement; Shaw's inversion; the Akademie locator of Kant's
footnote (4:430).
Context: In 5.11 the disciple, not the Master, states the rule, and the
Master answers that he has not attained it. Leviticus 19:18 closes a run
of commands in ch. 19. In Shabbat 31a Hillel's answer is one of three
stories of Gentiles who came to Shamai and then to Hillel. In the
Mahabharata passage the speaker continues: "In refusals and gifts, in
happiness and misery, in the agreeable, and the disagreeable, one should
judge of their effects by a reference to one's own self." Kant's footnote
is attached to the sentence introducing "Dieses Princip der Menschheit"
(the formula of humanity, 4:429).
