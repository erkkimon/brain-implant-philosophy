> "He observed that assertions of the form ‘p, but I don’t believe that p’ are strikingly incoherent, while assertions of the form ‘p, but she doesn’t believe that p’ are perfectly fine."  (p. 2)

> "The proper explanation of this is that when I assert p, I somehow commit myself to believing that p, but not by asserting that I believe that p."  (p. 2)

> "Note also that constructions like ‘I believe grass is green, but it ain’t’ are likewise incoherent. Theorists call the second construction “commissive” as opposed to “omissive” versions of Moore’s paradox."  (p. 4, n. 13)

> "In what follows, I show that we do not find moral versions of Moore’s paradox where we would expect, given the expressivist story about expression."  (p. 4)

Relied on for: the omissive/commissive terminology as "theorists" use it;
  Woods' commitment explanation (his own view); the use of Moore's paradox
  as a test for metaethical expressivism.
Context: Woods argues against hermeneutic expressivism in metaethics
  (Blackburn is his example): if moral assertions expressed disapproval as
  ordinary assertions express belief, "moral versions of Moore's paradox"
  should be incoherent, and he argues they are not. "Expressivism" here is
  the metaethical view, not the Wittgensteinian expressivism about avowals.
