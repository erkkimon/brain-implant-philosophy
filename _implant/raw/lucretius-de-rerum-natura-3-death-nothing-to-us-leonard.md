# Lucretius, De Rerum Natura III — praise of Epicurus; "death to us is nothing"; the mirror of time before birth (Leonard tr.)

> "O thee I follow, glory of the Greeks"  (III, proem)

> "Therefore death to us / Is nothing, nor concerns us in the least, / Since nature of mind is mortal evermore."  (III, "Folly of the Fear of Death")

> "Nothing to us was all fore-passed eld / Of time the eternal, ere we had a birth. / And Nature holds this like a mirror up / Of time-to-be when we are dead and gone."  (III, end of "Folly of the Fear of Death")

> "Even Epicurus went, his light of life / Run out, the man in genius who o'er-topped / The human race, extinguishing all others, / As sun, in ether arisen, all the stars."  (III, same section)

Relied on for: thinkers/epicurus.md — Lucretius' own statement of the
death argument and of the mirror (symmetry) image; his praise of
Epicurus, as Lucretius' assessment.
Context: The proem addresses the man who "first uplifted in such dark /
So clear a torch aloft"; Epicurus is named in the second passage, in a
list of the great who died (Ancus, kings, Xerxes, Scipio, Homer,
Democritus). SEP "Death" §5.2 quotes the mirror passage in another
translation ("Lucretius 1951").
