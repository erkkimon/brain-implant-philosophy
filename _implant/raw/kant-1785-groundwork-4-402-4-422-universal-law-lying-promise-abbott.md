# Kant 1785 — Groundwork 4:402, 4:421–422, 4:429: universal law, the lying promise, humanity (Abbott tr.)

Source: Immanuel Kant, *Fundamental Principles of the Metaphysic of Morals* (Grundlegung zur
  Metaphysik der Sitten, 1785), tr. Thomas Kingsmill Abbott, Project Gutenberg eBook #5682.
  Akademie locators are those given for the same passages by Johnson & Cureton, SEP "Kant's
  Moral Philosophy" (G 4:402, 4:421) and Korsgaard 1986 (G 422, G 429); Abbott's text carries
  no Akademie pagination.
Original: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5682 (public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-09-27 (plain text downloaded from gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5682/pg5682.txt)

> "I am never to act otherwise than so that I could also will that my maxim should become a universal law."  (4:402)

> "Then I presently become aware that while I can will the lie, I can by no means will that lying should be a universal law. For with such a law there would be no promises at all"  (Section I, same paragraph as 4:402)

> "Act only on that maxim whereby thou canst at the same time will that it should become a universal law."  (4:421)

> "Act as if the maxim of thy action were to become by thy will a universal law of nature."  (4:421)

> "When I think myself in want of money, I will borrow money and promise to repay it, although I know that I never can do so."  (4:422)

> "the promise itself would become impossible, as well as the end that one might have in view in it, since no one would consider that anything was promised to him, but would ridicule all such statements as vain pretences."  (4:422)

> "So act as to treat humanity, whether in thine own person or in that of any other, in every case as an end withal, never as means only."  (4:429)

Relied on for: the universalizability test and the lying-promise example, in Kant's words, as
the test the "traditional reading" (Varden 2010) and Korsgaard 1986 apply to the murderer case;
the Formula of Humanity Korsgaard applies.
Context: 4:402–403 is in Section I (the false promise "in distress"); 4:421–422 in Section II,
the second of four examples (perfect duty to others); 4:429 introduces the formula of humanity.
No Akademie page is assigned here to the continuation of the 4:402 paragraph.
