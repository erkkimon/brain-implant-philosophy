> "The term ‘constructivism’ entered debates in moral theory with John Rawls’ seminal Dewey Lectures “Kantian Constructivism in Moral Theory” (Rawls 1980), wherein Rawls offered a reinterpretation of the philosopher Immanuel Kant’s ethics and of its relevance for political debates."  (§1)

> "To this extent, Kant’s theory is regarded as providing a metaethical alternative both to realism and skepticism about the existence and nature of moral truths."  (§1)

Rawls 1980: *Journal of Philosophy* 77(9): 515–572, DOI 10.2307/2025790 (verified 2026-10-08 by
content negotiation; not read).

Relied on for: the origin of the term in Rawls 1980 as a reinterpretation of Kant; Bagnoli's
report that on this reading Kant's theory is an alternative to realism and scepticism.
Context: between the two sentences Bagnoli summarises Rawls's concern with disagreement in
pluralistic societies and his practical conception of objectivity, which "He attributes to Kant"
(citing Rawls 1971: 34, 39–40, 49–52).
