Secondary sources that attach the name "Münchhausen trilemma" to Albert
(metadata read via DOI content negotiation / Crossref; bodies not read
unless stated):

- Vladimír Havlík, "Hans Albert, Münchhausenovo trilema a bootstrapová
  racionalita", Filosofický časopis 73 (2025): 483–502,
  doi:10.46854/fc.2025.3r.483. Its English abstract (in the Crossref
  record, read) says:

> "In this article, I focus on a fundamental problem of traditional epistemology, which Hans Albert reformulates in his Treatise on Critical Reason, referring to it as “Münchhausen’s Trilemma”."

> "I try to compare different epistemological interpretations of the trilemma within and outside critical rationalism, and at the same time the differences in the attitudes of Popper and his students towards the obligations of critical rationalism, which most of them accept as the only possible solution to the trilemma."

> "The treatise and the trilemma are the result of Albert’s journey towards critical rationalism and discussions with contemporaneous currents of thought in German philosophy, especially the so-called transcendental pragmatism of Karl-Otto Apel."

Relied on for: the bibliographic existence of Albert's Traktat (1968) and
its English translation (1985); that a published secondary source
(Havlík 2025, abstract) reports Albert calls the problem "Münchhausen's
Trilemma" in the Treatise; the existence of the debate literature listed
above (Albert vs. transcendental pragmatism, per Havlík's abstract;
Habermas; Klein/Wright on infinitism).

Context: the claim that Albert "coined" the term in 1968 is NOT
established by anything read here — Havlík's abstract says only that
Albert refers to the problem by that name in the Treatise. The content
of Albert's trilemma is reported from Wettersten (IEP), who does not use
the name.
