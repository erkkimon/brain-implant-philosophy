> "Think about it: the answer to the question “Is it opposite day?” will always be no. So how do you figure out if it is, in fact, opposite day?"  (p. 1, header)

> "Students will understand the concept of paradoxes, specifically the opposite day paradox and the liar paradox, and learn how logic and inference can be used to analyze and solve these types of puzzles."  (p. 1, "Lesson Objective"; laid out in a column in the PDF, rejoined here)

> "How does the answer to 'Is it opposite day?' always end up being 'no'?"  (p. 1, "Explore", among the probing questions)

Relied on for: a children's-science source that pairs the "opposite day
paradox" with the liar paradox and gives the question form whose answer
"will always be no".
Context: a lesson sheet for grades 3–5; the other probing questions ask
what a paradox is and "What do you think the 'liar paradox' means?". The
sheet does not itself argue the claim; it refers to the episode.
