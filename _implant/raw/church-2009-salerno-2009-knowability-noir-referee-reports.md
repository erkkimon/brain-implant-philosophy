## (a) Church 2009 — publisher abstract

> "This chapter presents the earliest version of the knowability proof by Alonzo Church which appears in a 1945 referee report for the Journal of Symbolic Logic. Church anonymously conveyed the proof to Fitch."  (abstract)

> "The proof had the effect of undermining a certain definition of ‘value’ that Fitch was articulating — a definition that is trivialized if there are unknowable truths."  (abstract)

> "Church offers a number of potentially promising ways to block the proof. He is most sympathetic to a rejection of closure principles for knowledge and belief, and a fortiori the principle that knowledge is closed under conjunction-elimination."  (abstract)

## (b) Salerno 2009b — publisher abstract

> "It argues that Fitch's intent was to pinpoint a disruptive set of logical properties that lend themselves to the trivialization of conditional analyses."  (abstract)

> "If this is right, then Fitch does not take the knowability proofs to be paradoxical, but instead takes them to be a lesson about how intensional operators interact, surprisingly, to thwart the efforts of conditional analyses."  (abstract)

## (c) Salerno's archival documentation page

> "Church, Alonzo. \"First Anonymous Referee Report on Fitch's 'A Definition of Value' \".  January or February 1945. Handwritten by Alonzo Church to Ernest Nagel, coeditor of JSL.  Contains the first proof of the modal epistemic result today known as the knowability paradox."  (archival page, first item; inner quotation marks escaped here)

> "Nagel, E. \"Letter to Church: April 13, 1945\".   Notes that Fitch has withdrawn his paper owing to a defect in his definition of value."  (archival page)

> "(FFP)  Frederic B. Fitch Papers :  Manuscripts and Archives.  Yale University Library. (ACP) Alonzo Church Papers : Department of Rare Books and Special Collections.  Princeton University Library. (ENP) Ernest Nagel Papers :  Rare Book and Manuscript Library. Columbia University."  (archival page, key; whitespace as extracted)

Relied on for: Church as the anonymous 1945 referee and the proof's
first version; the report's route (to Nagel, co-editor of JSL); Fitch's
withdrawal of the 1945 paper; Church's own preferred way to block the
proof, as the publisher abstract reports it; Salerno's reading that
Fitch did not take the proofs to be paradoxical.
Context: the abstracts are OUP's chapter summaries, not the chapters;
the archival page is Salerno's own catalogue, which says his "discussion
of their content appears in \"Knowability Noir: 1945-1963\"".
