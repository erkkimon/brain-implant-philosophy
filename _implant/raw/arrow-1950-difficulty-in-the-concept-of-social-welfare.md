> "If consumers' values can be represented by a wide range of individual orderings, the doctrine of voters' sovereignty is incompatible with that of collective rationality."  (§V.1, pp. 342–343)

Relied on for: Arrow's own framing (voting and market as the two methods);
  his use of the "paradox of voting"; that the 1950 conditions are five
  and include citizens' sovereignty (non-imposition) and positive
  association (Condition 2) — the later canonical list (Pareto in place of
  these) is Morreau's report of the 1963 restatement; Arrow's name
  "Possibility Theorem"; his reading of it as a conflict of voters'
  sovereignty with collective rationality; the dead-candidate motivation
  of independence.
Context: The paper is the article version of the Cowles Commission
  monograph Social Choice and Individual Values (Wiley 1951; 2nd ed. 1963),
  announced in its first footnote as "to be published by John Wiley &
  Sons". The proof is given for two individuals and three alternatives
  (§IV.2).
