> "Parrondo's paradox: It is possible to play two losing games alternately to eventually win."  (List of paradoxes, "Decision theory")

> "It is named after its creator, Juan Parrondo, who discovered the paradox in 1996."  (article, lead)

> "In Game A, we toss a biased coin, Coin 1, with probability of winning P_1=(1/2)-\epsilon, where \epsilon is some small positive constant."  (article, "Saw-tooth example"; math markup flattened)

> "In fact, Game B is a Markov chain, and an analysis of its state transition matrix (again with M=3) shows that the steady state probability of using coin 2 is 0.3836, and that of using coin 3 is 0.6164."  (article, "Saw-tooth example")

> "Harmer and Abbott show via simulation that if M=3 and ε = 0.005, Game B is an almost surely losing game as well."  (article, "Saw-tooth example"; math markup flattened, \epsilon rendered as ε)

> "Not all alternating sequences of A and B result in winning games. For example, one game of A followed by one game of B (ABABAB...) is a losing game, while one game of A followed by two games of B (ABBABB...) is a winning game."  (article, "Saw-tooth example"; unsourced in the article at this point)

> "In summary, Parrondo's paradox is an example of how dependence can wreak havoc with probabilistic computations made under a naive assumption of independence. A more detailed exposition of this point, along with several related examples, can be found in Philips and Feldman."  (article, "Resolving the paradox")

> "Parrondo's games are of little practical use such as for investing in stock markets as the original games require the payoff from at least one of the interacting games to depend on the player's capital."  (article, "Applications"; cited to Iyengar & Kohli, Complexity 9(1), 23–27, doi:10.1002/cplx.10112 — DOI verified 2026-10-09, paper not read)

> "In the early literature on Parrondo's paradox, it was debated whether the word 'paradox' is an appropriate description given that the Parrondo effect can be understood in mathematical terms."  (article, "Name"; no reference given for this sentence)
