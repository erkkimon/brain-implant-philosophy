> "As was discovered by Russell, any theory that contains the Comprehension Schema is inconsistent."  (§2.3.2)

> "The standard approaches to these problems of inconsistency are, by and large, ones of expedience. A paraconsistent approach makes it possible to have theories of truth and sethood in which the mathematically fundamental intuitions about these notions are respected. For example, as Brady (1989; 2006) has shown, contradictions may be allowed to arise in a paraconsistent set theory, but these need not infect the whole theory."  (§2.3.2)

> "Incurvati (2020, chapter 4) gives a detailed critique of the paraconsistent approach to naive set theory."  (§2.3.2)

Relied on for: the paraconsistent family's own statement of its case (by
Priest and co-authors), the names of its developers (Brady, Libert, Weber,
Omori, Verdée, Batens) and a named critic (Incurvati 2020).
Context: the same subsection derives r ∈ r ↔ r ∉ r from the Comprehension
Schema and then r ∈ r ∧ r ∉ r; it also lists Libert (2005) models, Weber
(2010b, 2012) ordinals and cardinals, Omori (2015) consistency operator,
Verdée (2013) adaptive logic, Batens (2020).
