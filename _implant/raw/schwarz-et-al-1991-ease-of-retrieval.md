# Schwarz et al. 1991 — ease of retrieval as information (the 6-vs-12 examples study)

Source: Norbert Schwarz, Herbert Bless, Fritz Strack, Gisela Klumpp, Helga

> [Translated] "Three experiments are reported showing that individuals
>  use the subjectively experienced ease of recall as information when
>  assessing the significance of what is recalled. … This effect of the
>  valence of the recalled material held only for those subjects who were
>  asked to recall six examples of the relevant valence, which they found
>  easy. When subjects were asked to recall twelve examples, which they
>  found difficult, the effect of valence reversed. … Evidently they
>  concluded that behaviours that were difficult to recall could not be
>  frequent or typical (Experiments 1 and 2). Consistent with this
>  interpretation, the effect of experienced ease of recall was eliminated
>  when subjects could misattribute their subjective experience to a
>  transient external influence (Experiment 3)."  (indexed abstract)

Relied on for: the interpretive claim that the availability effect runs
on experienced ease of retrieval, not on the count of instances recalled
— twelve recalled examples produced a weaker judgement than six.
Context: the study asked people to recall examples of their own assertive
or unassertive behaviour and then rate their own assertiveness. Because
the body was not read, no page-level quotation is given; anyone relying
on more than the design and headline result should fetch the original.
