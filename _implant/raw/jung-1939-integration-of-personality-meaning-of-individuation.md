# Jung 1939 — The Integration of the Personality, ch. 1 "The Meaning of Individuation"

Source: C. G. Jung, *The Integration of the Personality*, translated by
  Stanley Dell (New York: Farrar & Rinehart, 1939), ch. 1 "The Meaning of
  Individuation", pp. 3, 28–29. The chapter says the essays were
  "originally written as lectures given at the Eranos Meeting at Ascona".
Original: https://archive.org/details/integrationofper00jung
  (scan, Harold B. Lee Library; OCR file integrationofper00jung_djvu.txt).
  Copyrighted ("COPYRIGHT 1939, BY FARRAR & RINEHART, INC."); excerpts only.
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (OCR downloaded and grepped; quotations copied with
  line-break hyphenation joined).

> "I will try to explain the term “individuation” as simply as possible. By it I mean the psychological process that makes of a human being an “individual”—a unique, indivisible unit or “whole man.”"  (p. 3)

> "If the process of individuation is an empirical fact, rather than a theory, one must expect the problem to have its history."  (p. 28)

> "The fifth chapter gives a psychological explanation of alchemy, and also presents the idea of individuation as a fundamental alchemistic symbol."  (p. 29)

Relied on for: Jung's 1939 English-language gloss of individuation as the
  process that makes a human being an "indivisible unit" or "whole man";
  his claim of historical parallels in Gnosticism and alchemy.
Context: The first passage is followed by Jung's argument that if
  unconscious processes exist "they must surely belong to the totality of
  the individual". The p. 28 passages say he had known "for twenty-five
  years that Gnosticism contains striking parallels to the symbolism of the
  process of individuation".
