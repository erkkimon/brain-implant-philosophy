# Marcus Aurelius, Meditations (Casaubon tr.) — the passages that name Epictetus

Source: Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, tr. Meric Casaubon (1634), as
  edited in Project Gutenberg eBook #2680 (section numbering of that
  edition, which differs from modern numbering). Book I §IV (on Rusticus),
  Book IV §XXXIV, Book XI §§XXX–XXXI.
Original: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2680 (public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (Gutenberg plain text read)

> "To Rusticus I am beholding, that I first entered into the conceit that my life wanted some redress and cure."  (I.IV)

> "whom also I must thank that ever I lighted upon Epictetus his _Hypomnemata_, or moral commentaries and common-factions: which also he gave me of his own."  (I.IV)

> "What art thou, that better and divine part excepted, but as Epictetus said well, a wretched soul, appointed to carry a carcass up and down?"  (IV.XXXIV)

> "'Of the free will there is no thief or robber:' out of Epictetus"  (XI.XXXI)

Relied on for: Marcus naming Epictetus and crediting Rusticus with
lending him Epictetus' "Hypomnemata"; Marcus quoting Epictetus.
Context: Book I lists Marcus' debts to family and teachers. XI.XXX
quotes another saying "(said Epictetus,)" about a father kissing his
child; the editor's notes locate it at "Arr. 3, 22, 105" (sic, a note
on the Gutenberg page).
