> "The argument runs that if the principle of respect for autonomy and the principle of beneﬁcence justify voluntary euthanasia, then the principle of beneﬁcence justiﬁes non-voluntary euthanasia."  (abstract, p. 84)

> "It concludes that the logical argument stands unrebutted and merits much greater attention."  (abstract, p. 84; Keown's assessment)

> "Voluntary euthanasia (VE) is the intentional shortening of a patient’s life by a doctor at the patient’s request in order to end the patient’s suﬀering."  (§I, p. 84)

> "The Second Reading debate took place in the Lords on 22 October 2021."  (§I, p. 84, on Baroness Meacher's Assisted Dying Bill)

> "That is not, however, the central question in the contemporary legal and political debate. That question is the feasibility of legal control: could the law prevent a slide down the “slippery slope”, especially from VE to NVE?"  (§II, p. 86)

> "It runs that although the ethical case typically advanced for VE focuses on patient autonomy, that case is seriously incomplete without appeal to the principle of beneﬁcence."  (§II, p. 86, the LA)

> "One of the ﬁrst scholars to identify a logical link between VE and NVE was Yale Kamisar, in his classic paper arguing, on utilitarian grounds, against legalisation."  (§II, p. 87; n. 8: Y. Kamisar, "Some Non-Religious Views Against Proposed ‘Mercy-killing’ Legislation" (1958) 42 Minn. L. Rev. 969; n. 9: G. Williams, The Sanctity of Life and the Criminal Law (New York 1957), "that book’s argument for the legalisation of VE")

> "There are generally thought to be two “slippery slope” arguments against legalising VE."  (§II, p. 86)

> "The ﬁrst is the “empirical” argument (EA). It runs that drafting and enforcing safeguards against mistake and abuse is not feasible."  (§II, p. 86)

> "Though the LA tends to be invoked by those opposed to VE, it takes no position on the merits of VE. It simply runs that acceptance of VE logically requires acceptance of NVE."  (§I, p. 85)

> "In short, the absence of autonomy does not cancel the duty of beneﬁcence."  (§II, p. 87)

> "several scholars of bioethics and law have also endorsed the LA"  (§II, p. 87; the list that follows, with footnote numbers omitted here, is "Daniel Callahan", "Leon Kass", "the author", "David Jones" and "Neil Gorsuch". Kamisar 1958 is named just before as "One of the ﬁrst scholars to identify a logical link"; Gorsuch's cited work is "A Reply to Raymond Tallis on the Legalization of Assisted Suicide and Euthanasia" (2007) 28 J. Leg. Med. 327, n. 15.)

> "Gorsuch is now, of course, a Justice of the United States Supreme Court."  (§II, p. 88)

> "Thus,” he concluded, “it turns out that what is couched as a limited right to ‘physician-assisted suicide’ is likely, in eﬀect, a much broader license, which could prove extremely diﬃcult to police and contain.”"  (§II, p. 88, quoting Rehnquist CJ in Washington v Glucksberg 521 U.S. 702 (1997), 732)

> "Several prominent scholars of medical law and medical ethics have either rejected or at least not accepted the argument, including Gerald Dworkin and Raymond Frey; Margaret Battin; Hallvard Lillehammer; Stephen Smith; Robert Young; Emily Jackson; and Steven Daskal."  (abstract, p. 84)

On the Dutch 1990 data, as Keown describes them:

> "the widely reported “1,000 cases” in 1990 in which, Dutch researchers found, patients (who were mainly incapacitous but a signiﬁcant minority of whom had capacity, either wholly or partly) were intentionally given a lethal injection by their doctors without having made an explicit request, in breach of a key requirement for lawful euthanasia."  (§III.B, p. 95)

> "Oddly, however, she made no mention of that commission’s endorsement of NVE among the 1,000 cases, an endorsement grounded on the patients’ suﬀering or, in other words, on the principle of beneﬁcence."  (§III.B, p. 95, on Battin and the Remmelink Commission)

On Young 2007, as Keown reports and answers it:

> "That error lay in assuming that a patient’s best interests could be judged entirely by reference to the patient’s best medical interests, since it was only in relation to these that the physician could possibly lay claim to having a better vantage point than the patient from which to determine a patient’s interests."  (§III.E, p. 100, Keown's summary of Young)

> "The LA does not assume that a patient’s best interests are comprised entirely by the patient’s best medical interests."  (§III.E, p. 100, Keown's reply)

Conclusion:

> "In short, the arguments for PAS logically justify VE, and the arguments for VE logically justify NVE."  (§IV, p. 108)
