# Krötke 2006 — Historical overview of the Barmen Theological Declaration: Barth's authorship, "the one Word of God", limits

> "Karl Barth – essentially the author of the Barmen Theses – understood Jesus Christ himself to be the only Word of God whom we have to trust and obey. Especially in the Lutheran tradition “the one Word of God” was understood as the decisive Word of God through which God speaks to us."  (p. 270)

> "In effect it then declares that the notion that we may recognise God through a source other and besides Jesus Christ a “false doctrine”."  (p. 270, on the reading that excludes "natural theology")

> "Karl Barth, who had to rephrase his draft of this thesis at the synod, could only allude to the concerns"  (p. 271; the sentence continues "for a christological substantiation" in the fifth thesis "with a quote from Hebrews 1:3"; a superscript "th" breaks the PDF text)

> "The declaration is not a document of Christian resistance against a murderous ideology as such."  (p. 270)

> "It contains no direct reference to the persecution of the Jews."  (p. 270)

> "This is a limitation in the confession that we cannot be content with."  (p. 270)

Relied on for: Krötke's attribution of the theses to Barth; the two readings of "the one Word of God"; his criticism of the Declaration's limits (attributed).
Context: Krötke reports that Asmussen told the synod the Declaration was not a protest "against the new state"; its concern "was only preservation the church" (sic). He argues the church must go "with Barmen and beyond Barmen".
