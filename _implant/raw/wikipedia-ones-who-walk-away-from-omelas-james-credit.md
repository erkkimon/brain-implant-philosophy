> "Le Guin gave credit to William James, who mentioned the idea in 1891, and she later wrote: "The fact is, I haven't been able to re-read Dostoyevsky, much as I loved him, since I was twenty-five, and I'd simply forgotten he used the idea.""  (Wikipedia, citing Le Guin 1975, pp. 275–276)

Relied on for: that Le Guin credited James's 1891 essay as a source of the
idea of her 1973 story (secondary report; Le Guin's text not read).
Context: The preceding sentence of the article says the scapegoat idea was
posed by Dostoevsky in The Brothers Karamazov (1880); the article then
quotes James's "lost soul" sentence from "The Moral Philosopher and the
Moral Life" (with "sceptical" where the 1897 Gutenberg text reads
"specifical").
