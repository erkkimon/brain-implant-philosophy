# Stockdale 1993 — Courage under Fire: Epictetus in a Hanoi prison

Source: James Bond Stockdale, Courage under Fire: Testing Epictetus's
  Doctrines in a Laboratory of Human Behavior, Hoover Essays No. 6
  (Stanford: Hoover Institution, 1993), ISBN 0-8179-3692-0. Speech
  delivered at King's College, London, 15 November 1993 (per the text).
  Pp. 1–2, 7–8.
Original: https://hoover-s3-website.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/uploads/documents/StockdaleCourage.pdf
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (PDF from the Hoover Institution's site, read via pdftotext)

> "As the senior naval prisoner of war officer in Hanoi for eight years, he was tortured fifteen times, put in leg irons for two years, and put in solitary confinement for four years."  (front matter, author note)

> "On my last session, he reached high in his wall of books and brought down a copy of The Enchiridion."  (p. 1; "he" is Philip Rhinelander, Stanford)

> "Five years down there, at least. I’m leaving the world of technology and entering the world of Epictetus."  (p. 7, on ejecting over North Vietnam)

> "a Stoic always kept separate files in his mind for (A) those things that are “up to him” and (B) those things that are “not up to him.”"  (p. 7)

Relied on for: Stockdale's own report that he met the Enchiridion at
Stanford through Rhinelander and used its dichotomy as a prisoner.
Context: Stockdale was shot down in September 1965 (Hoover Institution
summary); the essay also quotes Discourses on "life is a soldier's
service" and Enchiridion 32 on good and evil lying within the will.
