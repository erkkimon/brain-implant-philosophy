# SEP Fall 2026 "Martin Buber" — the Buber–Jung exchange

Source: Michael Zank and Zachary Braiterman, "Martin Buber", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Fall 2026 Edition (first published 20 Apr 2004; substantive revision 12 May 2026), §6 "Distance and Relation: Late Philosophical Anthropology" and §7 "Humanistic Psychology and Psychotherapy".
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2026/entries/buber/ (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (archive HTML fetched with curl, converted to text, grepped for "Jung"; the Fall 2024 archive, revision of 28 Jul 2020, has only the §6 sentence and lacks §7)

> "Against Sartre, Heidegger, and also Carl Jung, Buber rejected the picture of self-enclosed human subjects and self-enclosed human life-worlds beyond which there are no external, independent realities."  (§6)

> "Jung didn’t read Buber, whereas Buber read Jung extensively. The two never corresponded directly."  (§7)

> "Jung nevertheless opined that Buber “was ignorant of psychiatric observation,” that he “lacked practical experience in depth psychology,” and that he therefore “doesn’t know anything about the autonomy of complexes” (MBW 10, 201; transl. MZ, emphasis in the original)."  (§7)

> "Buber takes Jung to task for reducing religion to something immanent, at the expense of divine transcendence."  (§7, on "Religion and Modern Thought", Merkur 1952, in The Eclipse of God, 1952)

> "The debate falters because Buber argues that Jung’s statements about the psychic origin of religious beliefs exceed the strict confines of empirical psychology, while Jung dismisses Buber and other critics beholden to traditional systems of thought as “naturally incapable of understanding” him correctly."  (§7; sources given as MBW 10, 86–89 and 266–274)

> "Buber was critical of Freud but appreciated C.G. Jung, and he occasionally lectured at the Eranos conferences convened by Jung"  (§7)

Relied on for: the Buber–Jung exchange of 1952 and the 1960 letters as Zank and Braiterman report them (thinkers/jung.md).
Context: §7 places Buber's view of psychology as that of "a social philosopher or philosophical anthropologist"; Jung's reply appeared in Merkur as "Religion and Psychology" (the entry's English title).
