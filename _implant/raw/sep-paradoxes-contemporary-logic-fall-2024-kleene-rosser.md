# SEP "Paradoxes and Contemporary Logic" (Fall 2024) — Kleene–Rosser, Curry 1941 and 1942

Source: Andrea Cantini and Riccardo Bruni, "Paradoxes and Contemporary
  Logic", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), first
  published 2007-10-16, substantive revision 2021-04-20, §4.1
  (illative combinatory logic and Church's system).
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/paradoxes-contemporary-logic/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (read at the URL above with curl)

> "Church’s hope was that contradictions could be avoided by ensuring the possibility that a propositional function be undefined for some argument."  (§4.1)

> "However, the theories of Curry and Church were almost immediately shown inconsistent in 1934, by Kleene and Rosser, who (essentially) proved a version of the Richard paradox (both systems can provably enumerate their own provably total definable number theoretic functions)."  (§4.1)

> "The result was triggered by Church himself in 1934, when he used the Richard paradox to prove a kind of incompleteness theorem (with respect to statements asserting the totality of number theoretic functions)."  (§4.1)

> "The reason for the inconsistencies was eventually clarified by Curry’s 1941 essay."  (§4.1)

> "Curry then remarks that the paradox of Kleene-Rosser arises because Church’s and Curry’s systems satisfy both kinds of completeness, thus showing that the two properties are incompatible."  (§4.1)

> "Curry’s proof of the inconsistencies of combinatory systems is unsatisfactory because it heavily uses a detour through number theory and Gödelization, which, as a matter of fact, is unnecessary as Curry himself soon discovered and presented in a paper with the same title as the one by Kleene and Rosser, “in deference to the original discoverers of the contradiction” (Curry 1942)."  (§4.1)

> "Curry suggests that these two routes are akin, respectively, to Russell’s paradox and to the Liar."  (§4.1)

> "It is interesting to note that the two ways correspond to by-now standard tools, the so-called first fixed point theorem and second fixed point theorem of combinatory logic and lambda calculus (Barendregt 1984, p. 131 and p. 143"  (§4.1)

Relied on for: the dating (the entry says 1934; publication 1935), the
Richard core, Church's 1934 use of Richard, Curry 1941's diagnosis,
Cantini & Bruni's assessment of Curry 1941's proof, Curry 1942's two
constructions and their link to the fixed point theorems.
Context: the "two routes" are, per the entry, direct self-reference (A = HH
with H = λY(N(YY)), N = λX(X ⊃ B)) and indirect self-reference through an
enumerator T and Gödel numbering "exploit[ing] the machinery of Curry 1941
and Kleene-Rosser"; the theorem stated is that a combinatorially complete
system with an implication satisfying three listed rules proves every term.
The entry's bibliography gives Kleene & Rosser 1935 as Annals of
Mathematics 36: 630–637.
