# Siniscalchi 2009 — "Two out of three ain't bad": a comment on Al-Najjar & Weinstein

Source: Marciano Siniscalchi, "Two Out of Three Ain't Bad: A Comment on 'The
  Ambiguity Aversion Literature: A Critical Assessment'", Economics and
  Philosophy 25(3), 2009, pp. 335–356.
Original: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266267109990277 (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-08 (DOI verified by content negotiation and Crossref.
  Text read in a pdftotext extraction of the author's final version,
  https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/msi661/AlNajjarWeinsteinComment-final.pdf ;
  page numbers below are that manuscript's, not the journal's)

> "This literature seeks to generalize Leonard Savage’s axiomatization of subjective expected utility (Savage, 1954) so as to accommodate phenomena such as the Ellsberg paradox (Ellsberg, 1961)."  (§1, ms. p. 1)

> "Furthermore, the individual’s attitudes toward ambiguity are viewed as a matter of taste, just like her attitudes toward risk."  (§1, ms. p. 2)

> "My response, in a nutshell, is this: perhaps NW themselves would be embarassed by these choices, but there is no fundamental canon of rationality according to which every DM should feel similarly uncomfortable."  (§1, ms. p. 2; "embarassed" as in the source)

> "In particular, Al-Najjar and Weinstein are uncomfortable with the possibility that an ambiguity-averse individual may reject freely available information; however, this phenomenon simply reflects a trade-off between the intrinsic value of information, which is positive even in the presence of ambiguity, and the value of commitment."  (abstract)

> "As an empirical matter, the amount of experimental evidence confirming Ellsberg’s observations is, by now, rather substantial."  (§5, ms. p. 23)

> "Moreover, some recent experiments actually attempt to control for possible misapplied heuristics: see for instance Hey, Lotito, and Maffioletti (2008)."  (§5, ms. p. 23)

Relied on for: the reply to Al-Najjar & Weinstein on information aversion
and on misapplied heuristics, side by side with their critique.
Context: Siniscalchi says he "fully agree[s]" with the critique of the naive
(non-sophisticated) approach (ms. p. 7, n. 4) and that the critique "can be interpreted constructively
by proponents of ambiguity" (ms. p. 23); "NW" abbreviates Al-Najjar and
Weinstein.
