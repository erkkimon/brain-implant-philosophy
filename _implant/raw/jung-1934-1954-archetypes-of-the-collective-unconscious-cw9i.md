# Jung 1934/1954 — the collective unconscious and the archetypes defined; Jung's own genealogy of the word (CW 9i)

Source: C. G. Jung, *The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious*, Collected Works vol. 9, part I, ed. Herbert Read, Michael Fordham, Gerhard Adler, William McGuire, trans. R. F. C. Hull, 2nd edn, Princeton University Press (Bollingen Series XX), 1968 (copyright 1959 Bollingen Foundation; 1969 Princeton University Press), ISBN 0-691-09761-5 / 0-691-01833-2 (pbk). Essays used: "Archetypes of the Collective Unconscious" (first published Eranos-Jahrbuch 1934, revised in *Von den Wurzeln des Bewusstseins*, Zurich 1954, from which the translation is made — editors' note), paras. 1–6; "The Concept of the Collective Unconscious" (lecture, St Bartholomew's Hospital, London, 19 Oct 1936), paras. 88–99; "Concerning the Archetypes, with Special Reference to the Anima Concept" (1936, revised 1954), para. 136; "Psychological Aspects of the Mother Archetype" (Eranos-Jahrbuch 1938, revised 1954), paras. 148–160.

> "A more or less superficial layer of the unconscious is undoubtedly personal. I call it the personal unconscious. But this personal unconscious rests upon a deeper layer, which does not derive from personal experience and is not a personal acquisition but is inborn. This deeper layer I call the collective unconscious."  (para. 3)

> "It is, in other words, identical in all men and thus constitutes a common psychic substrate of a suprapersonal nature which is present in every one of us."  (para. 3)

> "The contents of the collective unconscious, on the other hand, are known as archetypes."  (para. 4)

> "The term “archetype” occurs as early as Philo Judaeus, with reference to the Imago Dei (God-image) in man. It can also be found in Irenaeus, who says: “The creator of the world did not fashion these things directly from himself but copied them from archetypes outside himself.”"  (para. 5; footnote marker omitted)

> "The term “archetype” is not found in St. Augustine, but the idea of it is. Thus in De diversis quaestionibus LXXXIII he speaks of “ideae principales, ‘which are themselves not formed ... but are contained in the divine understanding.”"  (para. 5)

> "“Archetype” is an explanatory paraphrase of the Platonic [εἶδος]."  (para. 5; the Greek word is OCR-garbled in the text layer, supplied in brackets)

> "the contents of the collective unconscious have never been in consciousness, and therefore have never been individually acquired, but owe their existence exclusively to heredity."  (para. 88)

> "Mythological research calls them “motifs”; in the psychology of primitives they correspond to Lévy-Bruhl’s concept of “représentations collectives,” and in the field of comparative religion they have been defined by Hubert and Mauss as “categories of the imagination.” Adolf Bastian long ago called them “elementary” or “primordial thoughts.”"  (para. 89)

> "my idea of the archetype—literally a pre-existent form—does not stand alone but is something that is recognized and named in other fields of knowledge."  (para. 89)

> "This collective unconscious does not develop individually but is inherited. It consists of pre-existent forms, the archetypes, which can only become conscious secondarily and which give definite form to certain psychic contents."  (para. 90)

> "there is good reason for supposing that the archetypes are the unconscious images of the instincts themselves, in other words, that they are patterns of instinctual behaviour."  (para. 91)

> "the concept of the collective unconscious is neither a speculative nor a philosophical but an empirical matter."  (para. 92)

> "There are as many archetypes as there are typical situations in life. Endless repetition has engraved these experiences into our psychic constitution, not in the form of images filled with content, but at first only as forms without content, representing merely the possibility of a certain type of perception and action."  (para. 99)

> "It is not, therefore, a question of inherited ideas but of inherited possibilities of ideas."  (para. 136)

> "“Archetype,” far from being a modern term, was already in use before the time of St. Augustine, and was synonymous with “Idea” in the Platonic usage."  (para. 149)

> "Were I a philosopher, I should continue in this Platonic strain and say: Somewhere, in “a place beyond the skies,” there is a prototype or primordial image of the mother that is pre-existent and supraordinate to all phenomena in which the “maternal,” in the broadest sense of the term, is manifest. But I am an empiricist, not a philosopher"  (para. 149)

> "Significantly enough, it is Kant’s doctrine of categories, more than anything else, that destroys in embryo every attempt to revive metaphysics in the old sense of the word, but at the same time paves the way for a rebirth of the Platonic spirit."  (para. 150)

> "It is necessary to point out once more that archetypes are not determined as regards their content, but only as regards their form and then only to a very limited degree."  (para. 155)

> "The archetype in itself is empty and purely formal, nothing but a facultas praeformandi, a possibility of representation which is given a priori. The representations themselves are not inherited, only the forms"  (para. 155)

> "The existence of the instincts can no more be proved than the existence of the archetypes, so long as they do not manifest themselves concretely."  (para. 155)

> "They form the “treasure in the realm of shadowy thoughts” of which Kant spoke"  (para. 160)

Relied on for: Jung's definitions of the personal and collective unconscious and of the archetype; his own genealogy of the word (Philo, Irenaeus, Corpus Hermeticum, Dionysius, Augustine, Plato) and his references to Kant; his "empirical, not philosophical" self-description; form-not-content and "possibilities of ideas" (vocabulary/archetype-and-collective-unconscious.md).
Context: para. 5 continues with the Corpus Hermeticum ("archetypal light") and Dionysius the Areopagite, then Lévy-Bruhl; para. 149 sets the "temperament" that treats ideas as real against nominalism and empiricism; para. 155 compares the archetype's form to the axial system of a crystal; para. 153 names Plato, Bastian, Hubert and Mauss, and Usener as predecessors and claims as Jung's own share that archetypes "can rearise spontaneously".
