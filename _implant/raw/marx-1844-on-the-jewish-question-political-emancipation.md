# Marx 1844 — "On the Jewish Question": political emancipation and the "right of man to liberty"

Source: Karl Marx, "Zur Judenfrage" ("On the Jewish Question"), written
  autumn 1843, first published in the Deutsch-Französische Jahrbücher,
  February 1844, Part I. English text as given by the Marxists Internet
  Archive (page states: "Proofed and Corrected: by Andy Blunden, Matthew
  Grant and Matthew Carmody, 2008/9"; the translator is not named there).
Original: https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1844/jewish-question/index.htm
  (German original public domain; the English translation may be
  copyrighted, so short excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-15 (HTML fetched with curl, tags stripped; quotations
  checked by script against that text)

> "Political emancipation is, of course, a big step forward. True, it is not the final form of human emancipation in general, but it is the final form of human emancipation within the hitherto existing world order."  (Part I)

> "It is a question of the liberty of man as an isolated monad, withdrawn into himself."  (Part I)

> "But, the right of man to liberty is based not on the association of man with man, but on the separation of man from man."  (Part I)

> "The practical application of man’s right to liberty is man’s right to private property."  (Part I)

Relied on for: Marx's own wording of the distinction between political
  and human emancipation and of his criticism of the liberal "right of man
  to liberty".
Context: the liberty passages comment on the Declaration of the Rights of
  Man and of the Citizen in the French Constitution of 1793, which Marx
  quotes just before, after distinguishing the "droits de l'homme" from
  the "droits du citoyen"; Marx is replying to Bruno Bauer's "The Jewish
  Question" (1843).
