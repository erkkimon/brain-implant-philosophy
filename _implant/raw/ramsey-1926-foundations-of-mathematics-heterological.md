> "these contradictions fall into two fundamentally distinct groups, which we will call A and B."  (1931, p. 20)

> "(8) Weyl’s contradiction about ‘heterologisch"  (1931, p. 20, last item of group B; footnote: "For the eighth see Weyl, Das Kontinuum, p. 2.")

> "Group A consists of contradictions which, were no provision made against them, would occur in a logical or mathematical system itself. They involve only logical or mathematical terms such as class and number, and show that there must be something wrong with our logic or mathematics. But the contradictions of Group B are not purely logical, and cannot be stated in logical terms alone; for they all contain some reference to thought, language, or symbolism, which are not formal but empirical terms."  (1931, p. 20)

> "So they may be due not to faulty logic or mathematics, but to faulty ideas concerning thought and language."  (1931, pp. 20–21)

> "Some adjectives have meanings which are predicates of the adjective word itself; thus the word ‘short’ is short, but the word ‘long’ is not long. Let us call adjectives whose meanings are predicates of them, like ‘short’, autological; others heterological. Now is ‘heterological’ heterological? If it is, its meaning is not a predicate of it; that is, it is not heterological. But if it is not heterological, its meaning is a predicate of it, and therefore it is heterological. So we have a complete contradiction."  (1931, p. 27)

> "So that when heterological and autological are unambiguously defined, ‘heterological’ is not an adjective in the sense in question, and is neither heterological nor autological, and there is no contradiction."  (1931, p. 27, the solution "According to the principles of Principia Mathematica")

> "According to Principia Mathematica this deduction is illegitimate"  (1931, p. 43; OCR "iUegitimate", "Mathemai^ca" corrected)

> "this solution fails"  (1931, p. 43, of the Principia solution when the range is that of predicative functions)

> "We can easily show that this is really the case, so that the contradiction is simply due to an ambiguity in the word ‘meaning’ and has no relevance to mathematics whatever."  (1931, p. 43)

Relied on for: Ramsey's A/B division with the paradox as item B(8) under
Weyl's name, the type-theoretic solution he attributes to Principia
Mathematica, its failure once the range is predicative functions, and his
own "ambiguity in the word ‘meaning’" solution, on
problems/grelling-nelson-paradox.md.
Context: p. 21 continues with Peano's "Exemplo de Richard non pertine ad
Mathematica, sed ad linguistica" and Ramsey's remark that such an attitude
"is not completely satisfactory". On p. 27 the heterologisch case is chosen
as "a contradiction not given in that work which is particularly free from
irrelevant elements", and the next sentence says the hierarchy of orders
"lands us in an almost equally serious difficulty" (the Axiom of
Reducibility). On pp. 42–43 Ramsey shows that with predicative
functions in his sense F(‘F’) ≡ ~F(‘F’) is again derivable, so the
Principia solution "fails", and locates the fault in the premiss that ‘F’
means F in the sense R used in the definition.
