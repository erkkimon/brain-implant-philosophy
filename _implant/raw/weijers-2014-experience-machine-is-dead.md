> "Recently, however, it has been convincingly argued that Nozick’s scenario should not be used in this way because it elicits judgments marred by status quo bias and other irrelevant factors."  (abstract, p. 513)

> "Unlike the existing experience machine scenarios, when this new scenario is used to assess internalist mental state theories of well-being, it does not provide strong evidence to refute or endorse them."  (abstract, p. 513)

> "less than 13% (3/24) of the participants responding to the De Brigard’s Negative scenario chose reality (De Brigard, 2010, p. 47)."  (p. 518)

> "79 survey sheets on Nozick’s scenario were completed. Through their answers to question 1, about 16% (13/79) of the students indicated that they would connect to an experience machine."  (p. 520; the survey was "on first-year students at Victoria University of Wellington", p. 520)

> "Surprisingly, 34% (20/66) of the main justifications given by the students who didn’t want to connect indicated that they experienced imaginative resistance to the stipulated or implied features of the scenario."  (p. 520)

> "About 34% (27/69) of participants responding to the Self scenario chose to connect to the experience machine."  (p. 521; "79 survey sheets on the Self scenario were completed", p. 521)

> "Over 52% (43/82) of participants responding to the Stranger scenario decided that connecting to an experience machine made the life in question better"  (p. 524)

> "About 55% (42/77) of participants responding to the Stranger NSQ scenario thought Boris should connect to an experience machine"  (p. 526)

> "Finally, the total difference between the Stranger NSQ scenario and Nozick’s scenario is 38%, a large and very highly statistically significant difference."  (p. 527)

> "The experiment consisted in handing out surveys to four different classes of first-year students on August 15, 2012."  (p. 531, note)

> "Two classes were marketing and two were philosophy. Using a one-tailed Fisher’s exact test, I found no statistically significant differences between the results for philosophy students and marketing students"  (p. 531, note)

> "Smith also expresses concern with the representativeness of De Brigard’s all-student sample"  (p. 531, note)

Relied on for: Weijers's figures (with sample sizes, population and date),
  his reading of Nozick's and De Brigard's scenarios, and his
  conclusion — reported as his.
Context: the "Stranger NSQ" scenario asks what a stranger, "Boris", who
  has spent 50% of his life in a machine and 50% in reality, should
  choose; Weijers designs it to neutralise the status quo and is the
  scenario he recommends. The Self and Stranger scenarios are his own
  intermediate versions. The notes also mention an earlier 2011 run with
  a 12% NSQ effect.
