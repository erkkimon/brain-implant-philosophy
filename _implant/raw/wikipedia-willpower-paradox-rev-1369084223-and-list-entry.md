## List of paradoxes, "Decision theory"

> "Willpower paradox: Those who keep their minds open are more goal-directed and more motivated than those who declare their objectives to themselves."

## Article

> "The willpower paradox is the idea that people may do things better by focusing less directly on doing them, implying that the direct exertion of volition may not always be the most powerful way to accomplish a goal."  (lead)

> "Research suggests that intrapersonal communication (talking to oneself) and maintaining a questioning mind are more likely to bring change."  (lead; its only reference is the University of Illinois press release on ScienceDaily, 2010)

> "One 2010 experiment compared the performance of two groups of people doing anagrams. One group thought about their impending anagram task; the other thought about whether or not they would perform anagrams. The second group performed better than those who knew for sure that they would be working on anagrams."  ("Experimental data")

> "The same researcher, Ibrahim Senay (at University of Illinois in Urbana), found similarly that repeatedly writing the question "Will I?" was more powerful than writing the traditional affirmation "I will"."  ("Experimental data"; cites Wray Herbert, "The Willpower Paradox", Scientific American, 2010)

> "Michael J. Taleff writes, "Willpower in our field (psychology) is a paradox". Addiction-affected patients are told that willfulness is less effective than willingness."  ("Willpower and addiction"; cites an archive.today copy of "Willpower", Counselor Magazine, 27 May 2011)
