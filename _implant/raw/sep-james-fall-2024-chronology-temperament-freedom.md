> "1878. Marries Alice Howe Gibbens."  (§1)

> "1899. Publishes Talks to Teachers on Psychology: and to Students on Some of Life’s Ideals (including “On a Certain Blindness in Human Beings” and “What Makes Life Worth Living?”) with Henry Holt. Becomes active member of the Anti-Imperialist League, opposing U. S. policy in Philippines."  (§1)

> "1909. Publishes A Pluralistic Universe with Longmans, Green & Co., based on Hibbert Lectures delivered in England and at Harvard the previous year."  (§1)

> "(His partially completed manuscript published posthumously as Some Problems of Philosophy)."  (§1, under 1910)

> "“Some Remarks on Spencer’s Notion of Mind as Correspondence” (1878) and “The Sentiment of Rationality” (1879, 1882) presage his future pragmatism and pluralism, and contain the first statements of his view that philosophical theories are reflections of a philosopher’s temperament."  (preamble)

> "Faced with the tension between scientific determinism and our belief in our own freedom or autonomy, James—speaking not as a psychologist but as the philosopher he had become—argues that science “must be constantly reminded that her purposes are not the only purposes, and that the order of uniform causation which she has use for, and is therefore right in postulating, may be enveloped in a wider order, on which she has no claims at all” (PP 1179)."  (§3)

> "James first announced his commitment to pragmatism in a lecture at Berkeley in 1898, entitled “Philosophical Conceptions and Practical Results.”"  (§6)

Relied on for: marriage, Talks to Teachers and the Anti-Imperialist League,
A Pluralistic Universe and Some Problems of Philosophy; Goodman's report of
James's view that theories reflect temperament; his report of James on
scientific determinism and freedom in the Principles.
Context: The §3 sentence is followed by Goodman's remark that in discussing

views; PP 1179 is the Harvard edition of The Principles of Psychology.
