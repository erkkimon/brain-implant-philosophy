# Nietzsche 1872 — The Birth of Tragedy §1: Apollo and the principium individuationis (Haussmann trans.)

Source: Friedrich Nietzsche, *The Birth of Tragedy; or, Hellenism and
  Pessimism* (German 1872), translated by William A. Haussmann, §1.
Original: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/51356 (Project Gutenberg eBook
  #51356, released 4 Mar 2016; plain text pg51356.txt). Public domain.
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (Gutenberg plain text downloaded and grepped)

> "Just as in a stormy sea, unbounded in every direction, rising and falling with howling mountainous waves, a sailor sits in a boat and trusts in his frail barque: so in the midst of a world of sorrows the individual sits quietly supported by and trusting in his _principium individuationis_."  (§1, Nietzsche quoting Schopenhauer, *Welt als Wille und Vorstellung*, I. p. 416)

> "we might even designate Apollo as the glorious divine image of the _principium individuationis,_"  (§1)

> "Add to this awe the blissful ecstasy which rises from the innermost depths of man, ay, of nature, at this same collapse of the _principium individuationis,_ and we shall gain an insight into the being of the _Dionysian,_"  (§1)

Relied on for: the Schopenhauer → Nietzsche transmission of the phrase
  *principium individuationis* (Apollo as its image, the Dionysian as its
  collapse), which Jung quotes in *Psychological Types* ch. III.
Context: Nietzsche introduces the quotation saying "we might apply to
  Apollo, in an eccentric sense, what Schopenhauer says of the man wrapt in
  the veil of Mâyâ".
