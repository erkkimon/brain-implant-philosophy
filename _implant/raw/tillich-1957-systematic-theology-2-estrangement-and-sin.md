# Tillich 1957 — Systematic Theology vol. 2, "Estrangement and Sin"

Source: Paul Tillich, Systematic Theology, vol. 2, Existence and the
  Christ (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1957), part III, section
  C.1 "Estrangement and Sin", pp. 44–47, and p. 57; read in the
  three-volumes-in-one printing (University of Chicago Press, 1967), page
  numbers from the running heads ("EXISTENCE 45", etc.).
Original: three volumes in one ISBN 0-226-80336-8, scan
  https://archive.org/details/systematic-theology-three-volumes-in-one-paul-tilich
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-15 (djvu OCR text downloaded and grepped; line-break
  hyphens rejoined)

> "The state of existence is the state of estrangement. Man is estranged from the ground of his being, from other beings, and from himself."  (p. 44)

> "Nevertheless, “estrangement” cannot replace “sin.”"  (p. 46)

> "This has little to do with “sin” as the state of estrangement from that to which one belongs"  (p. 46, of "sins" as "deviations from moral laws")

> "Man’s predicament is estrangement, but his estrangement is sin."  (pp. 46–47)

> "Sin is estrangement; grace is reconciliation."  (p. 57)

Relied on for: Tillich's reinterpretation of sin as estrangement; his
  contrast with sins in the plural as deviations from moral laws; his
  keeping of the word for "personal responsibility".
Context: Tillich says "Estrangement" as a philosophical term "was created
  and applied by Hegel" and used against Hegel by pupils including Marx
  (p. 45); that the word "sin" "has a sharpness which accusingly points to
  the element of personal responsibility" (p. 46); p. 57 contrasts the
  Roman Catholic weighing of mortal and venial sins with the Protestant
  "the Sin", "qualitative and absolute".
