> "We provide a critical assessment of the ambiguity aversion literature, which we characterize in terms of the view that Ellsberg choices are rational responses to ambiguity, to be explained by relaxing Savage’s Sure-Thing principle and adding an ambiguity-aversion postulate."  (abstract, p. 249)

> "First, admitting Ellsberg choices as rational leads to behaviour, such as sensitivity to irrelevant sunk cost, or aversion to information, which most economists would consider absurd or irrational."  (abstract, p. 249)

> "Third, the anomaly of the Ellsberg choices can be explained simply and without tampering with the foundations of choice theory. These choices can arise when decision makers form heuristics that serve them well in real-life situations where odds are manipulable, and misapply them to experimental settings."  (abstract, p. 249)

> "The seminal works of Schmeidler (1989) and Gilboa and Schmeidler (1989) laid a formal foundation for this enterprise by modifying Savage’s (1954) subjective expected utility model."  (§1, pp. 249–250)

> "(1) Ellsberg choices are expressions of rational decision makers facing ambiguity; (2) ambiguity is to be modelled by relaxing the sure thing principle while keeping other aspects of Savage’s subjective framework intact; and (3) the decision maker’s attitude towards ambiguity is a matter of taste."  (§1, p. 250, the three positions by which the authors define the literature)

> "The all-consuming concern of the ambiguity aversion literature is the Ellsberg “paradox”."  (§1, p. 251)

> "Referring to choices in Ellsberg’s thought experiment as “paradoxical” implicitly confers on them an aura of rationality. Since we question the rationality of these choices, we prefer the more neutral term anomaly, which refers to “a deviation, irregularity, or an unexpected result”."  (p. 251, n. 2)

> "For if there were a probability measure P underlying these choices, then f1 ≻ f2 implies P(b) > P(r), while f4 ≻ f3 implies P(r) + P(y) > P(b) + P(y); a contradiction."  (§2.1, p. 256; the strict-preference sign is lost in the extraction and restored as "≻")

> "The most popular of ambiguity models is Gilboa and Schmeidler’s (1989) minimax expected utility (MEU)"  (§2.1, p. 256)

> "When C is a singleton, this reduces to standard expected utility. Otherwise, C captures the idea that the decision maker is unsure about the probability to assign to each event and takes a pessimistic, ambiguity-averse, attitude to the evaluation of acts"  (§2.1, p. 257, of V(f) = min over P in C of E_P v(f(s)))

> "In fact, one of the key lessons of the ambiguity aversion literature is that ambiguity-sensitive behaviour is observationally indistinguishable from the behaviour of a player in a game."  (§5, p. 276)

> "Myerson (1991: 26) noted that commonly encountered real-world situations may contaminate subjects’ behaviour in experiments."  (§5, p. 276)

> "Moreover, by recognizing Ellsberg choices as mistakes, a behavioural approach also provides a more subtle understanding because it distinguishes between situations with manipulable probabilities, when ambiguity aversion is a rational strategic response, from non-manipulable situations when it is a mistake."  (§1, p. 255)

> "The ambiguity aversion literature has generated exotic mathematical objects to be interpreted as “beliefs”: sets of priors, capacities, second order probabilities, to name a few."  (§1, p. 250)

> "This point, already made by Myerson (1991) and others, account for the Ellsberg anomaly in experiments by viewing decision makers as carrying over these heuristics to artificial experimental settings where probabilities are not manipulable."  (§1, p. 255; "account" as in the source)

> "We argue here that these same experimental findings are equally consistent with other explanations, and thus lend no special support to the ambiguity models."  (§5, p. 276)

Relied on for: the critique's three arguments, its definition of the
literature, its refusal of the label "paradox", its MEU statement of the
case, and the misapplied-heuristic explanation (credited by the authors to
