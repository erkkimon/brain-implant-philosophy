## The canonical derivation (pp. 247–248)

> "Nicod Condition (NC): For any object a and any predicate F and G, the proposition that a has both F and G confirms the proposition that every F has G."  (p. 247)

> "Equivalence Condition (EC): For any propositions H1, H2, and E, if E confirms H1 and H1 is (classically) logically equivalent to H2, then E confirms H2."  (p. 248)

> "Paradoxical Conclusion (PC): The proposition that a is both non-black and a non-raven, [~Ba · ~Ra], confirms the proposition that every raven is black, [(∀x)(Rx ⊃ Bx)]."  (p. 248)

> "2. By Classical Logic, [(∀x)(~Bx ⊃ ~Rx)] is equivalent to [(∀x)(Rx ⊃ Bx)]."  (p. 248, step 2 of "The canonical derivation of (PC) from (EC) and (NC)")

## Hempel and Goodman (p. 248)

> "Hempel (1945) and Goodman (1954) didn’t view (PC) as paradoxical. Indeed, Hempel and Goodman viewed the argument above from (1) and (2) to (PC) as sound."  (p. 248; their "Goodman (1954)" is the London (Athlone) printing of Fact, Fiction, and Forecast)

## Quine (p. 249)

> "In his influential paper “Natural Kinds”, Quine (1969) offers an analysis of the paradox of confirmation that deviates radically from the Hempel–Goodman line. Unlike Hempel and Goodman, Quine rejects the paradoxical conclusion (PC)."  (p. 249)

> "According to Quine, the failure of instantial confirmation in these cases stems from the fact that the predicates ‘non-black’ [~B] and ‘non-raven’ [~R] are not natural kinds – i.e., the objects falling under ~B and ~R are not sufficiently similar to warrant instantial confirmation of universal laws involving ~B or ~R."  (p. 249)

> "To summarize, Quine thinks (PC) is false, and that the (valid) canonical argument for (PC) is unsound because (NC) is false."  (p. 249)

## Fitelson & Hawthorne on Hempel's own theory (pp. 250–251)

> "As it turns out, Hempel’s official theory of confirmation is logically incompatible with his intuitive characterization of what is going on."  (p. 251)

> "(PC*) If one observes that an object a – already known to be a non-raven – is non-black (hence, is a non-black non-raven), then this observation confirms that all ravens are black."  (p. 250; their reading of the claim Hempel warned against conflating with (PC), after Maher 1999)

## Good and Hempel 1967 (p. 253)

> "Hence, Good has described a background corpus K relative to which [Ra · Ba] disconfirms [(∀x)(Rx ⊃ Bx)]."  (p. 253; Good 1967: under H there are 100 black ravens, no non-black ravens and 1 million other birds; under not-H 1,000 black ravens, 1 white raven and 1 million other birds; the object is sampled at random from all the birds — "we’re paraphrasing here", F&H)

> "Hempel (1967) responded to Good by claiming that [(NC_s)] is not what he had in mind, since it smuggles too much “unkosher” (a posteriori) empirical knowledge into K."  (p. 253)

## Maher (p. 254)

> "More recently, Maher (2004) has convincingly argued (contrary to what he had previously argued in his (1999)) that, within a proper neo-Carnapian Bayesian framework, Hempel’s [(NC_⊤)] is false, and so is its Quinean “restriction” [(QNC_⊤)]."  (p. 254)

> "While Maher’s neo-Carnapian analysis is very illuminating, it is by no means in the mainstream of contemporary Bayesian thought."  (p. 254)

## The canonical Bayesian approaches (pp. 255–257)

> "Perhaps somewhat surprisingly, almost all contemporary Bayesians implicitly assume that the paradoxical conclusion is true. And, they aim only to “soften the impact” of (PC) by trying to establish certain comparative and/or quantitative confirmational claims."  (p. 255)

> "Basically, assumption (1) relies on our knowledge that (according to [K_α]) there are more non-black objects in the universe than there are ravens."  (p. 256; K_α is their "actual background corpus"; (1) P[~Ba | K_α] > P[Ra | K_α])

> "Assumptions (2) and (3) are more controversial."  (p. 256; (2) P[Ra | H · K_α] = P[Ra | K_α]; (3) P[~Ba | H · K_α] = P[~Ba | K_α])

> "Vranas then argues that Bayesians have given no good reason for assuming this (necessary and sufficient) condition."  (p. 257, on Vranas 2004)

## Their result (pp. 258–260)

> "Thus, this result strips the Bayesian solution to bare bones, decoupling it from any of the usual assumptions, and then permits the introduction of whatever additional suppositions may seem plausible and fitting (e.g. those leading to positive confirmation) to be added separately."  (p. 258)

> "Theorem 1 and its Corollaries show that for a very wide range of probabilistic confirmation functions P, a black raven is more confirming of ‘All ravens are black’ than is a non-black non-raven."  (p. 261)
