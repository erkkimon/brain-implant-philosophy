# McCune & Wilson 2024/2025 — frequency of the negative participation paradox in three-candidate instant runoff

Source: David McCune & Jennifer Wilson, "The Negative Participation Paradox
  in Three-Candidate Instant Runoff Elections", Theory and Decision 98
  (2025) 537–559 (per Crossref; journal version not read); arXiv
  2403.18857v3, 23 Oct 2024, read here: §1, §4 "Empirical Results".
Original: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18857 (CC BY 4.0);
  doi:10.1007/s11238-025-10026-2 (Crossref record)
Retrieved: 2026-10-09 (arXiv PDF via pdftotext, passages grepped)

> "An election is said to demonstrate a negative participation paradox under a given voting method if the addition of voters who rank candidate A last causes A to go from losing to winning."  (§1)

> "IRV is also susceptible to paradoxes of negative participation, a lesser-studied type of paradox."  (§1)

> "In total, we have access to the preference profiles for 361 single-winner IRV elections with at least three candidates."  (§4)

Table 5 (§4), "Probability of a negative participation paradox in real-world elections": overall 3.3% (actual partial ballots) and 6.4% (completed ballots); given no majority candidate 4.9% / 9.4%; given IRV winner ≠ plurality winner 46.2% / 56.1%. Data: US political elections (FairVote repository), American Psychological Association elections, Scottish local elections.

Relied on for: the definition of the negative participation paradox and
  the authors' frequency findings, reported as theirs.
Context: The paper also gives limiting probabilities under impartial
  culture and impartial anonymous culture models; it notes that completing
  ballots can "introduce" paradoxes absent from actual voter profiles.
