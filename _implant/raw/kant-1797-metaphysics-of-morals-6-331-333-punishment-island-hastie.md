# Kant 1797 — the right to punish: never merely as a means, the principle of retaliation, and the island (Metaphysics of Morals, Doctrine of Right, AA 6:331–333; Hastie tr. 1887)

Source: Immanuel Kant, Die Metaphysik der Sitten, Part I "Metaphysische
  Anfangsgründe der Rechtslehre" (1797), General Remark E "Vom Straf= und
  Begnadigungsrecht", Akademie-Ausgabe vol. 6, pp. 331–333. English:
  Kant, The Philosophy of Law: An Exposition of the Fundamental Principles
  of Jurisprudence as the Science of Right, translated by W. Hastie,
  Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1887, pp. 195–198 ("The Principles of Public
  Right").
Original: Hastie 1887 scan, Internet Archive item thephilosophyofl00kantuoft
  (https://archive.org/details/thephilosophyofl00kantuoft); German text
  AA 6:331–333 at https://korpora.org/kant/aa06/331.html (…/332.html,
  …/333.html). (public domain: Kant d. 1804; Hastie's translation 1887)
Retrieved: 2026-09-27 (Internet Archive OCR text fetched and read; the
  German Akademie pages fetched to fix the locators: "Richterliche Strafe
  … kann niemals bloß" begins at 6:331 line 20; the island clause
  "Insel bewohnende Volk … der letzte im Gefängniß befindliche Mörder" is
  at 6:333 lines 19–23)

Transcription note: the OCR text breaks words at line ends ("admini
stered", "Mur derer"); these are joined below and nothing else is changed.

> "Juridical Punishment can never be administered merely as a means for promoting another Good either with regard to the Criminal himself or to Civil Society, but must in all cases be imposed only because the individual on whom it is inflicted has committed a Crime."  (Hastie p. 195; AA 6:331)

> "He must first be found guilty and punishable, before there can be any thought of drawing from his Punishment any benefit for himself or his fellow-citizens."  (Hastie p. 195; AA 6:331)

> "The Penal Law is a Categorical Imperative"  (Hastie p. 195; AA 6:331)

> "for Justice would cease to be Justice, if it were bartered away for any consideration whatever."  (Hastie p. 196; AA 6:332 — closing Kant's rejection of sparing a condemned man who agrees to dangerous medical experiments)

> "the undeserved evil which any one commits on another, is to be regarded as perpetrated on himself."  (Hastie p. 196; AA 6:332)

> "But whoever has committed Murder, must die."  (Hastie p. 198; AA 6:333)

> "Even if a Civil Society resolved to dissolve itself with the consent of all its members— as might be supposed in the case of a People inhabiting an island resolving to separate and scatter themselves throughout the whole world— the last Murderer lying in the prison ought to be executed before the resolution was carried out. This ought to be done in order that every one may realize the desert of his deeds, and that bloodguiltiness may not remain upon the people ; for otherwise they might all be regarded as participators in the murder as a public violation of Justice."  (Hastie p. 198; AA 6:333)

Relied on for: Kant's retributivism on problems/justification-of-punishment.md —
  punishment only because of the crime, never merely as a means; the
  principle of equality / retaliation; the island example.
Context: the passage follows Kant's distinction between public and private
  crimes and precedes his discussion of the Scottish rebels (death vs.
  penal servitude) and of the right of pardon. Between the first and the
  second quotation Kant writes "woe to him who creeps through the
  serpent-windings of Utilitarianism" (Hastie's rendering of
  "Schlangenwindungen der Glückseligkeitslehre", literally "doctrine of
  happiness"); the SEP entry "Legal Punishment" (Duff & Hoskins, §3) reads
  Kant as also having "suggested that as long as we reserve punishment only
  for those found guilty of crimes, then it is permissible to punish with an
  eye toward potential benefits (Kant 1797: 473)".
