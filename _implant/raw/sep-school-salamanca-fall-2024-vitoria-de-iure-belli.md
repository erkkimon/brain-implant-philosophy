# SEP "The School of Salamanca" (Izbicki & Kaufmann) — Vitoria's De Indis and De iure belli (1539)

Source: Thomas Izbicki and Matthias Kaufmann, "School of Salamanca", The
  Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta
  & Uri Nodelman (eds.); first published 2019-03-22, substantive revision
  2023-09-07 (copyright line "Copyright © 2023 by Thomas Izbicki, Matthias
  Kaufmann"). Section on Vitoria and the Indies / just war.
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/school-salamanca/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-27 (HTML of the Fall 2024 archive page read). Vitoria's
  own text (Political Writings, ed. Pagden & Lawrance, Cambridge 1991 =
  "Vitoria 1991b") was not read.

> "He addressed the Indies in three relectiones: De temperantia (1537), De Indis (1539) and De iure belli (1539)."

> "The second lecture lists seven “unjust titles” for the war against the Amerindians."

> "The third lecture, finally, lists eight “just titles” for the war against Amerindians, among them the violation of the right of Spaniards to travel in the Indies and to conduct missions, the protection of innocents, etc. (ibid., 278–291)."

> "He discusses who can wage war—private persons only for self-defense, the commonwealth and the prince also for other reasons (Vitoria 1991b, 299–302); what cannot be considered just reasons for waging war, such as difference in religion and enlargement of empire (ibid., 302–304); what can be done in a just war (ibid., 304–306, 314–317); and the view that it may not be enough if a prince believes that his war is just (ibid., 306–307)."

> "Vitoria, however, applied a criterion of proportional harm to wars, so that a war was unjust if it caused more harm than good. Moreover, a ruler who had waged an unjust war was obliged to make restitution."

> "Vitoria weakened his defense of the Indians by saying that Spain might govern them for their own benefit."

> "Molina, however, seems to have believed that, at least from a subjective point of view, both sides may believe that their cause is just (Fernandes"

Relied on for: Vitoria's just-war doctrine as Izbicki and Kaufmann report
  it (who may wage war, unjust reasons, proportional harm, restitution,
  a prince's belief not sufficient), the just/unjust titles, and
  Molina's view that both sides may subjectively believe their cause just.
Context: the passage also says the Salamancans built on Aquinas and on
  the "classical doctrine of just war" (Orend 2006); Suárez's De bello
  (disputation 13 of De caritate) held similar ideas "legitimate authority;
  just cause, which only one side can have; trying all other ways of
  conflict resolution". The assessment "weakened his defense" is the
  entry authors'.
