> "Legal positivism is the thesis that the existence and content of law depends on social facts and not on its merits."  (preamble)

> "The existence of law is one thing; its merit and demerit another. Whether it be or be not is one enquiry; whether it be or be not conformable to an assumed standard, is a different enquiry."  (preamble, quoting Austin 1832 [1995: 157])

> "its first full elaboration is due to Jeremy Bentham (1748–1832) whose account Austin adopted, modified, and popularized."  (§1)

> "No legal positivist argues that the systemic validity of law establishes its moral validity, i.e., that it should be obeyed by subjects or applied by judges."  (§2)

> "Bentham and Austin, as utilitarians, hold that such questions always turn on the consequences, and both acknowledge that disobedience is therefore sometimes fully justified."  (§2)

> "an enormous overvaluation of the importance of the bare fact that a rule may be said to be a valid rule of law, as if this, once declared, was conclusive of the final moral question: “Ought this law to be obeyed?”"  (§2, quoting Hart 1958: 75)

> "The most influential criticisms of legal positivism all flow, in one way or another, from the suspicion that it fails to give morality its due."  (§3)

> "positivism’s critics maintain that the most important features of law are not to be found in its source-based character, but in law’s capacity to advance the common good, to secure human rights, or to govern with integrity."  (§3)

> "it becomes hard to see how the natural law theory he develops rivals rather than accommodates the truth of legal positivism (see Gardner 2001, 225–227)."  (§3, of Finnis)

> "there is little, if anything, to be said in favor of a clear, consistent, prospective, public and impartially administered system of racial segregation, for example."  (§3, against Fuller)

> "how “an amoral datum called law could have the peculiar quality of creating an obligation to obey it” (Fuller 1958: 656)."  (§3, Green & Adams quoting Fuller)

> "Many other philosophers, encouraged also by the title of Hart’s famous essay, “Positivism and the Separation of Law and Morals”, (1958) treat the theory as the denial that there is a necessary connection between law and morality"  (§4.2)

> "it rejects any dependence of the existence of law on its merits (Gardner 2001)."  (§4.2)
