# Russell 1908 — Richard's paradox as contradiction (6), and its solution: "all definitions" is an illegitimate notion

Source: Bertrand Russell, "Mathematical Logic as Based on the Theory of
  Types", American Journal of Mathematics 30(3), 1908, pp. 222–262;
  quotations from §I, pp. 223–225: statement of contradiction (6),
  p. 223, with footnote; its solution, p. 225; the general rule, p. 225.
Original: https://doi.org/10.2307/2369948 ; open copy (JSTOR Early
  Journal Content) https://archive.org/details/jstor-2369948
  (public domain; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (archive.org OCR text 2369948_djvu.txt fetched with
  curl and grepped; page headers of the OCR used for page numbers; DOI
  data as verified for `russell-1908-theory-of-types-berry-paradox.md`).

> "Richard's paradox is akin to that of the least indefinable ordinal."  (p. 223, contradiction (6); OCR has a stray "{" footnote mark after "paradox")

> "Consider all decimals that can be defined by means of a finite number of words; let E be the class of such decimals."  (p. 223)

> "Nevertheless we have defined N in a finite number of words, and therefore N ought to be a member of E. Thus N both is and is not a member of E."  (p. 223)

Footnote to (6), as OCR'd: "Cf. Poincaré, "Les mathématiques et la
logique," Revue de Métaphysique et de Morale, Mai, 1906, especially
sections VII and IX; also Peano, Revista de Mathematica, Vol. VIII, No. 5
(1906), p. 149 ff." (OCR errors "Poincar6", "Bevue", "Bevista" corrected
here; not used as a quotation on any page.)

> "This is solved, like (5), by remarking that "all definitions" is an illegitimate notion. Thus the number E is not defined in a finite number of words, being in fact not defined at all."  (p. 225, solution of (6))

> "Thus all our contradictions have in common the assumption of a totality such that, if it were legitimate, it would at once be enlarged by new members defined in terms of itself."  (p. 225)

> "Whatever involves all of a collection must not be one of the collection"  (p. 225, the rule)

Relied on for: Russell's 1908 statement of Richard's paradox (digit rule:
nth figure of N is p + 1, or 0 if p = 9), his solution, and the
vicious-circle rule it falls under.
Context: (6) follows (5), "the least indefinable ordinal", and precedes
(7) Burali-Forti; p. 224 names "self-reference or reflexiveness" as
what all seven share. Russell writes "the number E" in the solution
(the class is called E and the number N in the statement). In §IV
(p. 241) the least indefinable ordinal is treated by relativising
"definable" to "some given apparatus of fundamental ideas"; Richard's
paradox is not separately re-treated there.
