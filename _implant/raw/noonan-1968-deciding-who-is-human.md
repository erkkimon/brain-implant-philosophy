# Noonan 1968 — "Deciding Who Is Human": conception as the line, the probabilities argument

Source: John T. Noonan, Jr., "Deciding Who Is Human", Natural Law Forum 13
  (1968), pp. 134–140 (a reply to John O'Connor, "On Humanity and Abortion",
  in the same volume).

> "What the theologians maintained, however, and what I maintain in following them, is that everyone is human who is conceived by human beings; that human beings* may not be discriminated by their varying potentialities."  (p. 134)

> "The argument I made is that it is wrong to kill humans, however poor, weak, defenseless, and lacking in opportunity to develop their potential they may be."  (p. 134)

> "It is easy but wrong for the more powerful to justify destroying the weaker by pointing to one of the differences in potential, whether the difference be indicated by race, religion, nationality, sex, social class, intelligence, or age."  (p. 135)

> "The appeal is a "buttressing" consideration, showing the plausibility of the standard adopted."  (p. 136)

> "One evidence of the nonarbitrary character of the line drawn is the difference of probabilities on either side of it."  (p. 136)

> "If a spermatozoon is destroyed, one destroys a being which had a chance of far less than 1 in 300 million of developing into a reasoning being, possessed of the genetic code, a heart and other organs, and capable of pain."  (p. 136)

> "If a fetus is destroyed, one destroys a being already possessed of the genetic code, organs, and sensitivity to pain, and one which had an 80% chance of developing further into a baby outside the womb who, in time, would reason."  (p. 136)

> "If their efforts are unsuccessful, the theologians' criterion is left in possession: Whoever is conceived of human beings is human."  (p. 137)
