# Neher 1996 — "Jung's Theory of Archetypes: A Critique" (abstract, from Crossref)

Source: Andrew Neher, "Jung's Theory of Archetypes: A Critique", *Journal of Humanistic Psychology* 36(2), April 1996, pp. 61–91 (SAGE).
Original: https://doi.org/10.1177/00221678960362008 (copyrighted; abstract excerpt only; full text not read)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (DOI metadata and abstract by content negotiation: curl -sL -H "Accept: application/vnd.citationstyles.csl+json" https://doi.org/10.1177/00221678960362008)

> "The critique focuses on Jung's belief that the origins of archetypes (and their basis in the collective unconscious) transcend the individual, in that they reflect an ancestral or universal essence."  (abstract)

> "These notions of Jung's are found to be seriously flawed. In spite of this, the critique suggests that Jung's belief in the genetic basis of certain unconscious content holds some promise."  (abstract)

Relied on for: Neher's own summary of his critique (vocabulary/archetype-and-collective-unconscious.md). His p. 86 sentence on case-by-case demonstration is quoted from Roesler 2022 (see roesler-2022-iaap-report-archetype-theory.md), not from Neher's full text.
Context: the abstract also says the critique "examines a related notion of Jung's, that the collective unconscious unites us with the world around us in an immediate paranormal or synchronistic sense" and closes with "suggestions ... concerning the kind of evidence required for its support".
