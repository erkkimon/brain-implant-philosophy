> "Autonomous agents are self-governing agents."  (preamble)

> "Philosophers have offered a wide range of competing answers to this question."  (preamble)

> "When people living in some region of the world declare that their group has the right to live autonomously, they are saying that they ought to be allowed to govern themselves."  (§1)

> "According to one popular story, an agent’s point of view is constituted by her highest-order desires regarding which of her first-order desires moves her to act."  (§2)

Relied on for: the definition of autonomous agents as self-governing; the
  authors' report that answers to "the necessary and sufficient features of
  this self-relation" compete; the parallel between group and individual
  claims to autonomy; the "coherentist" account with Frankfurt's
  highest-order desires as one version.
Context: §2 is headed "Four More or Less Overlapping Accounts of Personal
  Autonomy"; it names the first "coherentist" and lists, besides Frankfurt's
  higher-order desires, evaluative judgements, plans, caring, character
  traits and integrated states as rival candidates for the agent's point of
  view. §1 says most reasons for individual autonomy "have correlates in the
  case of demands for group autonomy", with one exception taken from the
  metaphysics of agency.
