# Simondon 1964 — "The Position of the Problem of Ontogenesis" (Flanders trans., Parrhesia 7, 2009)

Source: Gilbert Simondon, "The Position of the Problem of Ontogenesis",
  translated by Gregory Flanders, *Parrhesia* 7 (2009): 4–16; the
  translator's note 1 says it is "the first part of Simondon’s
  introduction" to *L’individuation psychique et collective* (English
  Wikipedia's bibliography gives "1964; Paris: Aubier, 1989"; not
  checked against the book).
Original: https://www.parrhesiajournal.org/parrhesia07/parrhesia07_simondon1.pdf
  (copyrighted translation published with permission of U of Minnesota P
  and Flammarion, per note 1; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (PDF downloaded, text extracted with pdftotext,
  quotations copied with line breaks joined)

> "both presuppose the existence of a principle of individuation that is anterior to the individuation itself, one that may be used to explain, produce, and conduct this individuation."  (p. 4, of substantialism and the hylomorphic schema)

> "and to know the individual through the individuation, rather than the individuation through the individual."  (p. 5)

> "We would like to show that the search for the principle of individuation must be reversed, by considering as primordial the operation of individuation from which the individual comes to exist"  (p. 5)

> "Pre-individual being is being in which there is no phase; the being in which individuation occurs is that in which a resolution appears through the division of being into phases."  (p. 6)

> "Metastable equilibrium was not known; being was implicitly supposed to be in a state of stable equilibrium."  (p. 6)

Relied on for: Simondon's reversal of the scholastic question — individuation
  as an operation prior to the individual, from a pre-individual,
  metastable state — and his explicit critique of the search for a
  "principle of individuation".
Context: Simondon names atomism and hylomorphism as the two approaches
  that start "from the constituted and given individual"; he proposes
  crystallisation from a supersaturated solution as a paradigm of physical
  individuation.
