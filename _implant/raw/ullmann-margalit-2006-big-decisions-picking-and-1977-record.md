From (a):

> "By ‘small’ decisions, I have in mind cases where we are strictly indifferent with regard to the alternatives before us, where our preferences over the alternatives are completely symmetrical."  (p. 157)

> "Every time I pick a bottle of Coke or a can of Campbell soup from the shelves of the supermarket, I have made a small decision in this sense."  (p. 157)

> "To the extent that we take choosing to be choosing for a reason, and choosing for a reason to presuppose preferences, it looks like we have to conclude that in such cases rational choice is precluded."  (p. 157)

> "I have elsewhere dealt with such cases of choice without preference, referring to them as instances of picking rather than choosing."  (pp. 157–158, footnote 1 to "Picking and Choosing", Social Research 44(4), 1977)

> "One chooses for reasons; one picks when reasons cannot prevail. This happens when the alternatives are entirely symmetrical (or incommensurate)."  (p. 171)

From the Crossref abstract of the 2017 reprint (b):

> "Sometimes people choose: they decide on the basis of reasons. But sometimes people are strictly indifferent as between the alternatives: they pick."  (abstract, OUP 2017)

Relied on for: the picking/choosing distinction and its definition as
Ullmann-Margalit states it; that it was introduced in the 1977 joint paper
(her own footnote); the 1977 bibliographic data (volume, issue, first
page) — the content of the 1977 paper is not reported beyond this.
Context: (a) quotes Leibniz's Theodicy, "In things which are absolutely
indifferent there can be no choice ... since choice must have some reason
or principle", as the view that such cases preclude choice; the paper's
subject is the opposite extreme ("big" decisions, "opting"), and §VIII
suggests picking and opting "meet" at the end of the chain of reasons.
