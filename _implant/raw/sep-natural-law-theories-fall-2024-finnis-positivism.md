# SEP Fall 2024 — Finnis, "Natural Law Theories": natural law and legal positivism as each side frames the relation

Source: John Finnis, "Natural Law Theories", Stanford Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy, Fall 2024 archive edition (first published 2007-02-05;
  substantive revision 2020-06-03), preamble and §1.4.
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/natural-law-theories/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-15 (HTML fetched with curl, quotations matched by script)

> "Legal theorists who present or understand their theories as “positivist”, or as instances of “legal positivism”, take their theories to be opposed to, or at least clearly distinct from, natural law theory."  (preamble)

> "Natural law theorists, on the other hand, did not conceive their theories in opposition to, or even as distinct from, legal positivism (contra Soper 1992 at 2395)."  (preamble)

> "2. Human persons are not law’s creatures but its proper point"  (§1.4, numbered thesis)

> "The term “positive law” was put into wide philosophical circulation first by Aquinas, and natural law theories of his kind share, or at least make no effort to deny, many or virtually all “positivist” theses—except of course the bare thesis that natural law theories are mistaken, or the thesis that a norm is the content of an act of will."  (preamble)
