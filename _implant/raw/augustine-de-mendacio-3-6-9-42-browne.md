# Augustine, De mendacio (On Lying) 1, 3, 6, 9, 42 (Browne tr.) — the definition of a lie, the refugee case, the eight kinds

Source: Augustine, *On Lying* (*De mendacio*), sections 1, 3, 6, 9 and 42,
  translated by H. Browne, in Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, First Series,
  Vol. 3, ed. Philip Schaff (Buffalo, NY: Christian Literature Publishing

> "There is a great question about Lying, which often arises in the midst of our every day business, and gives us much trouble, that we may not either rashly call that a lie which is not such, or decide that it is sometimes right to tell a lie, that is, a kind of honest, well-meant, charitable lie."  (1)

> "Wherefore, that man lies, who has one thing in his mind and utters another in words, or by signs of whatever kind."  (3)

> "On the other hand, those who say that we must never lie, plead much more strongly, using first the Divine authority, because in the very Decalogue it is written You shall not bear false witness; under which general term it comprises all lying:"  (6)

> "suppose a man should seek shelter with you who by your lie may be saved from death?"  (9)

> "How then can it be said without the greatest perverseness, that to the end one man may have life of the body, it is another man's duty to incur death of the soul?"  (9)

> "In these eight kinds, however, a man sins less when he tells a lie, in proportion as he emerges to the eighth: more, in proportion as he diverges to the first."  (42)

> "But whoever shall think there is any sort of lie that is not sin, will deceive himself foully, while he deems himself honest as a deceiver of other men."  (42)

Relied on for: Augustine's definition of lying, his report of the two
  parties (sections 5–6), the case of the refugee whose life a lie would
  save and the answer given by those who forbid every lie, and his closing
  ranking of eight kinds of lie as all sinful (thinkers/augustine.md).
Context: the treatise is set out as a disputation; sections 5 and 6
  present the arguments of those who allow lies and of those who forbid
  them, and section 9 gives the latter party's reply about the refugee
  (Augustine introduces it with "Which thing being fixed, who of them which
  assert this will be moved by those examples"). Section 9 continues
  "The love of our neighbor has its bounds in each man's love of himself."
  Section 42 closes the work's classification of eight kinds of lie.
