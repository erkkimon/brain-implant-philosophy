## Aquinas, ST II-II q. 40 a. 1

> "Objection 1. It would seem that it is always sinful to wage war. Because punishment is not inflicted except for sin. Now those who wage war are threatened by Our Lord with punishment, according to Matthew 26:52: "All that take the sword shall perish with the sword." Therefore all wars are unlawful."  (obj. 1)

> "I answer that, In order for a war to be just, three things are necessary. First, the authority of the sovereign by whose command the war is to be waged. For it is not the business of a private individual to declare war, because he can seek for redress of his rights from the tribunal of his superior."  (respondeo)

> "Secondly, a just cause is required, namely that those who are attacked, should be attacked because they deserve it on account of some fault."  (respondeo)

> "Thirdly, it is necessary that the belligerents should have a rightful intention, so that they intend the advancement of good, or the avoidance of evil."  (respondeo)

> "For it may happen that the war is declared by the legitimate authority, and for a just cause, and yet be rendered unlawful through a wicked intention."  (respondeo)

Aquinas quotes Augustine for the just-cause condition (as "QQ. in Hept., qu. x, super Jos."):

> "A just war is wont to be described as one that avenges wrongs, when a nation or state has to be punished, for refusing to make amends for the wrongs inflicted by its subjects, or to restore what it has seized unjustly."  (respondeo)

The translation's own bracket notes that one quotation Aquinas gives as Augustine's is not his:

> "The words quoted are to be found not in St. Augustine's works, but Can. Apud. Caus. xxiii, qu. 1"  (respondeo, translator's bracket)

## Augustine, Contra Faustum XXII.74 (Stothert)

> "The real evils in war are love of violence, revengeful cruelty, fierce and implacable enmity, wild resistance, and the lust of power, and such like; and it is generally to punish these things, when force is required to inflict the punishment, that, in obedience to God or some lawful authority, good men undertake wars"  (XXII.74)

## Augustine, City of God XIX.7 (Dods)

> "But, say they, the wise man will wage just wars. As if he would not all the rather lament the necessity of just wars, if he remembers that he is a man; for if they were not just he would not wage them, and would therefore be delivered from all wars. For it is the wrongdoing of the opposing party which compels the wise man to wage just wars"  (XIX.7)

Relied on for: the just-war page's historical line — Aquinas's three
  conditions (authority, cause, intention), the pacifist objection he
  answers (Matthew 26:52), Augustine's list of "real evils in war" and
  his lament over just wars.
Context: q. 40 a. 1 is the first of four articles on war; a. 2–4 treat
  clerics fighting, ambushes and fighting on holy days. The sed contra
  quotes Augustine's letter to Marcellinus (Ep. 138) that John the Baptist
  told soldiers "be content with your pay" rather than to give up
  soldiering. In XXII.74 Augustine defends the wars of Moses against the
  Manichaean Faustus. XIX.7 comes in a chapter on the miseries of human
  society; Augustine ends it saying one who thinks on such evils "without
  mental pain" has "lost human feeling".
