## Structure (table of contents)

Introduction; AWARENESS, Exercises 1–14 (1 The Riches of Silence … 14 Awareness of the Other), followed by unnumbered sections "Personal Benefits from Awareness", "Group Benefits of Awareness", "Contemplation Easier in a Group", "The Special Value of Body Awareness"; FANTASY, Exercises 15–32 (15 There and Here … 22 Ignatian Contemplation, 24 Healing of Hurtful Memories, 28 Your Funeral, 32 Awareness of Persons); DEVOTION, Exercises 33–47 (33 The "Benedictine" Method, 35 The Jesus Prayer, 36 The Thousand Names of God, 40 The Prayer of Intercession, 41 Petition, 47 The Prayer of Praise). An unnumbered section "Awareness and Contemplation" stands between Exercises 5 and 6.

## Awareness

> "But contemplation is a very simple thing, really."  (Exercise 3)

> "I once made a Buddhist retreat at which we spent as many as twelve to fourteen hours each day concentrating on our breathing, on the air coming in and going out of our nostrils."  (Exercise 3)

> "Many mystics tell us that, in addition to the mind and heart with which we ordinarily communicate with God, we are, all of us, endowed with a mystical mind and mystical heart, a faculty which makes it possible for us to know God directly, to grasp and intuit him in his very being, though in a dark manner, apart from all thoughts and concepts and images."  ("Awareness and Contemplation", p. 29)

> "But what do I gaze into when I gaze silently at God? An imageless, formless reality. A blank!"  ("Awareness and Contemplation", pp. 30–31)

> "And so we are led to the seemingly disconcerting conclusion that concentration on your breathing or your body sensations is very good contemplation in the strict sense of the word."  ("Awareness and Contemplation", p. 34)

> "Become aware of your breathing for a while . . ."  (Exercise 7, p. 38)

## Fantasy

> "There is an unsuspected and untapped source of power and life in our fantasy."  (Exercise 15)

## Devotion

> "This is a form of prayer that was widely used for centuries in the Church and has been attributed to Saint Benedict, who popularized it and refined its use."  (Exercise 33)

> "If I had to choose the one form of prayer that has made the presence of Christ most real in my life and given me the deepest sense of being supported and surrounded by the loving providence of God, I would unhesitatingly choose this, the last form of prayer I propose in this book, the Prayer of Praise."  (Exercise 47)
