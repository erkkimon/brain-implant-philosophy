# Keynes 1921 — A Treatise on Probability, ch. VI §6: weight of argument and the two urns

> "In each case we require the probability of drawing a white ball; in the first case we know that the urn contains black and white in equal proportions; in the second case the proportion of each colour is unknown, and each ball is as likely to be black as white. It is evident that in either case the probability of drawing a white ball is 1/2 but that the weight of the argument in favour of this conclusion is greater in the first case."  (ch. VI §6, p. 83)

> "Weight cannot, then, be explained in terms of probability."  (ch. VI §7, p. 83)
