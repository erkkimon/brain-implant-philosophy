> "TABOO (also written Tabu and Tapu) is the name given to a system of religious prohibitions which attained its fullest development in Polynesia (from Hawaii to New Zealand; see vol. xix. p. 426), but of which under different names traces may be discovered in most parts of the world."  (EB9, opening)

> "Although it was employed for civil as well as religious purposes, the taboo was essentially a religious observance."  (EB9, "Meaning")

> "For we shall scarcely err in believing that even in advanced societies the moral sentiments, in so far as they are merely sentiments and are not based on an induction from experience, derive much of their force from an original system of taboo."  (EB9, closing paragraphs)

> "Thus taboo is so far a negative application of practical magic. Positive magic or sorcery says, "Do this in order that so and so may happen." Negative magic or taboo says, "Do not do this, lest so and so should happen.""  (Golden Bough 1922, Chap. III §1)

Relied on for: Frazer's definition of taboo as a system of religious
  prohibitions found "in most parts of the world" (EB9); his conjecture
  that moral sentiments derive force from taboo; his later classification
  of taboo as negative magic.
Context: the EB9 article's literature list cites "Cook, Voyages, vol. v.
  p. 427 sq., vol. vii. p. 146 sq. (ed. 1809)" for the Polynesian taboo. In
  the Golden Bough passage Frazer describes the "savage" as abstaining from
  acts which "in accordance with his mistaken notions of cause and effect,
  he falsely believes would injure him" — Frazer's own terms.
