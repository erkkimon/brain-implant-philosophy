# Harmer & Abbott 1999 — "Losing strategies can win by Parrondo's paradox" (opening of the Nature brief communication)

Source: Gregory P. Harmer and Derek Abbott, "Losing strategies can win by
  Parrondo's paradox", Nature 402 (6764), 23 December 1999, p. 864
  (brief communication, "Game theory").
Original: https://doi.org/10.1038/47220 (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-09 (the publisher's public preview of the opening
  paragraph, https://www.nature.com/articles/47220; DOI verified by content
  negotiation: title, authors, Nature 402(6764) 864)

> "But can two losing gambling games be set up such that, when they are played one after the other, they becoming winning? The answer is yes."  (opening paragraph; "they becoming" as printed in the preview)

> "This is a striking new result in game theory called Parrondo's paradox, after its discoverer, Juan Parrondo"  (opening paragraph, refs 1–2)

> "Here we model this behaviour as a flashing ratchet"  (opening paragraph, ref. 3)

> "The rule is that we play coin 2 if our capital is a multiple of an integer M and play coin 3 if it is not."  (opening paragraph, game B)

> "If we assign a poor probability of winning to coin 2, such as p 2 =1/10−ε, then this would outweigh the better coin 3 with p 3 =3/4−ε, making game B a losing game overall."  (opening paragraph; subscripts flattened in the HTML text)

Relied on for: the game-B rule and probabilities, the authors' description
of the result as new and of the flashing-ratchet model.
Context: the preview also says "engineers know that two unstable systems, if
combined in the right way, can paradoxically become stable" and that "for
simplicity let us say that M =3"; the rest of the communication (game A,
simulation, Fig. 1) is behind the paywall and was not read. Refs 1–2 are
conference proceedings with Taylor and Parrondo (per the article's
citation metadata).
