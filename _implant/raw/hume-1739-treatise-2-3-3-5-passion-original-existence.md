# Hume 1739 — Treatise 2.3.3.5: a passion is "an original existence" with no "representative quality"

Source: David Hume, A Treatise of Human Nature (1739), Book 2, Part 3,
  Section 3 "Of the influencing motives of the will", paragraph 5
  (T 2.3.3.5, SBN 415), as numbered on Hume Texts Online.
Original: https://davidhume.org/texts/t/2/3/3 (Hume's text: public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-10-08 (HTML fetched with curl, tags stripped; quotation
  copied from that text, paragraph label read beside it)

> "A passion is an original existence, or, if you will, modification of existence, and contains not any representative quality, which renders it a copy of any other existence or modification."  (T 2.3.3.5, SBN 415)

Relied on for: the Vocabulary entry on "passion" in knowledge/thinkers/hume.md.
Context: the paragraph follows T 2.3.3.4 ("Reason is, and ought only to be
the slave of the passions", excerpted in
hume-1739-1751-treatise-enquiries-induction-passions-is-ought.md) and
continues "When I am angry, I am actually possest with the passion, and in
that emotion"; T 2.3.3.6 draws the "not contrary to reason" examples from it.
Cohon (SEP "Hume's Moral Philosophy" §3) calls this line of argument the
"Representation Argument".
