> "Barber paradox: A male barber shaves all and only those men who do not shave themselves. Does he shave himself? (Russell's popularization of his set theoretic paradox.) Not to be confused with the Barbershop paradox."  (List of paradoxes, "Logic" / "Self-reference")

> "The barber paradox is a puzzle derived from Russell's paradox. It was suggested to Bertrand Russell as an illustration of the paradox, but he deemed it an invalid modification of his paradox."  (article lead; cites Russell 1919, Collected Papers vol. 8, p. 228)

> "The puzzle shows that an apparently plausible scenario is logically impossible. Specifically, it describes a barber who is defined such that he both shaves himself and does not shave himself, which implies that no such barber exists."  (lead; cites a UMSL course page, now 404, and Oxford Reference, 403 — neither read here)

> "In its original form, this paradox has no solution, as no such barber can exist. The question is a loaded question in that it assumes the existence of a barber who could not exist, which is a vacuous proposition, and hence false."  ("Paradox")

> "This paradox is often incorrectly attributed to Bertrand Russell (e.g., by Martin Gardner in Aha!)."  ("History"; no source given for the claim beyond the Gardner example)

> "Nobody is such a barber, so there is no solution to the paradox."  ("In first-order logic", after the formula (∃x)(person(x) ∧ (∀y)(person(y) ⟹ (shaves(x, y) ⟺ ¬shaves(y, y)))) and the remark that assigning x to y gives "an instance of the contradiction a ⟺ ¬a")
