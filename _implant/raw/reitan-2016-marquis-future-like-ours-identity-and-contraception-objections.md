> "One reason for the persistent appeal of Don Marquis' ‘future like ours’ argument (FLO) is that it seems to offer a way to approach the debate about the morality of abortion while sidestepping the difficult task of establishing whether the fetus is a person."  (abstract)

> "This essay argues that in order to satisfactorily address both of the chief objections to FLO – the ‘identity objection’ and the ‘contraception objection’ – Marquis must take a controversial stand on what is most essential to being the kind of entity that an adult human being is."  (abstract)

Relied on for: Reitan's naming of the two "chief objections" to Marquis
  (identity, contraception) and his claim that answering them requires a
  stand on what we essentially are.
Context: the abstract continues that such a stand "amounts to a
  controversial account of personhood". Marquis's own reply to the
  contraception objection is in 1989 §V (see
  `marquis-1989-why-abortion-is-immoral-future-like-ours.md`).
