## Sin (Muslim), Edward Sell

> "There are various words used in the Qur’an to express the ideas of fault, crime, sin."  (p. 567)

> "The word dhanb expresses all forms of unbelief and wrong actions proceeding from such unbelief."  (p. 567)

> "The word ithm seems to imply a wrong attitude to others and so injustice."  (p. 567)

> "Man does not inherit a sinful nature, but simply a weak one."  (p. 567)

Also on p. 567 (OCR reads "dhanh"): the commission of a dhanb "constitutes
  one a khati, or sinner".
Assessment by Sell, recorded so a reader can judge the author's stance
  (not relied on for any claim about Islam): "The Qur’an marvels at the
  sin of man, his obstinate disobedience, and calls upon him to exercise
  faith and repentance, but it is deficient in its call for a broken and
  contrite heart." Sell was a Church Missionary Society secretary in
  Madras (ERE vol. 11, list of authors).

## Sin (Hindu), A. Berriedale Keith

> "the more primitive conception of sin as a pollution which can be removed by physical means"  (p. 560, Rigveda and Atharvaveda)

> "Varuna is the god to whom the sinner addresses himself"  (p. 560, Rigveda VII.86)

> "the principle is accepted that each deed brings its recompense, sometimes on earth, but more normally in a tuture birth"  (p. 561, Mahābhārata and early Dharmaśāstras; "tuture" is the OCR's)

## Sin (Buddhist), T. W. and C. A. F. Rhys Davids

> "No one holding the doctrine of karma, in any one of its various forms, could accept tbe doctrine of sin."  (p. 533; "tbe" is the OCR's)

> "What the European calls ‘ sin ’ he would call ‘ folly,’ a result of ignorance."  (p. 533)

> "To him no personal deity ever made the moral law."  (p. 533)

Also p. 533: such collisions with the cosmic moral law the Buddhist "would
  call evil, wrong, bad, demeritorious, corrupt" — the OCR reads "(papa,
  michchhd, akusala, apunrux, sahkilittha)", i.e. pāpa, micchā, akusala,
  apuñña, saṅkiliṭṭha.

Relied on for: the Qur'anic terms dhanb, ithm, khaṭī as Sell reports
  them, and his report that man inherits a weak, not sinful, nature;
  Keith's Vedic sin as offence against Varuṇa and as pollution, and
  karma in early Hinduism; the Rhys Davids' contrast of karma with the
  European doctrine of sin, and pāpa among the Buddhist terms.
Context: Sell's article draws on Gardner, The Qur'ānic Doctrine of Sin
  (1914), Hughes's Dictionary of Islam (1895) and Macdonald. Keith's
  article also describes sin-transfer and parental sin in the epics.
  The Rhys Davids' article opens: the doctrine of sin "as held in
  Europe, is a complex idea of many strands"; its literature note says
  its remarks "are gleaned from countless passages in Buddhist canonical
  and early commentarial literature".
