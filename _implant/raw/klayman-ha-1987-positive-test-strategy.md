# Klayman & Ha 1987 — the positive test strategy is not confirmation bias

Source: Joshua Klayman & Young-Won Ha (1987), "Confirmation,
  Disconfirmation, and Information in Hypothesis Testing", Psychological
  Review 94(2), 211–228; abstract, p. 211.
Original: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.2.211
  (copyrighted; excerpts only — the abstract's central sentences)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (DOI verified by content negotiation; text read at
  psy.ucsd.edu/~mckenzie/KlaymanHaPsychReview1987.pdf)

> "We show, however, that many phenomena labeled 'confirmation bias' are
>  better understood in terms of a general positive test strategy. With
>  this strategy, there is a tendency to test cases that are expected (or
>  known) to have the property of interest rather than those expected (or
>  known) to lack that property. This strategy is not equivalent to
>  confirmation bias in the first sense; we show that the positive test
>  strategy can be a very good heuristic for determining the truth or
>  falsity of a hypothesis under realistic conditions. It can, however,
>  lead to systematic errors or inefficiencies."  (p. 211)

Relied on for: the interpretive claim that Wason-task behaviour is a
positive test strategy rather than a preference for confirmation; the
claim that the strategy can be a good heuristic under realistic
conditions.
Context: the abstract's preceding sentences state the received view ("people
tend to test those cases that have the best chance of verifying current
beliefs rather than those that have the best chance of falsifying them")
that the paper argues against. The paper's formal analysis of when the
strategy is efficient is not excerpted.
