> "A decision maker must be assured that the arithmetic operations of any such decision process are the right ones—that they surface the correct ranking and values of the alternatives and preserve or alter ranks appropriately when new alternatives are added or deleted."  (Saaty 1987, abstract)

> "In this paper it will be shown that with absolute measurement, rank always is preserved, with relative measurement, rank changes with nspect to scveral criteria only because of the structural dependence (involving both numbers and measurements) of criteria on alternatives. A discussion of the effect on rank of replicas and near replicas of the alternatives also is given."  (Saaty 1987, abstract; "nspect" and "scveral" are as returned by the DOI metadata)

> "The analytic hierarchy process (AHP) is flawed as a procedure for ranking alternatives in that the rankings produced by this procedure are arbitrary."  (Dyer 1990, abstract)

> "The key to correcting this flaw is the synthesis of the AHP with the concepts of multiattribute utility theory."  (Dyer 1990, abstract)

> "These priorities may also be expressed in the ideal form by dividing each priority by the largest one, 0.333 for International Company, as given in Table 7. The effect is to make this alternative the ideal one with the others getting their proportionate value."  (Saaty 2008, §5, p. 90)

> "The idealised priorities are always used for ratings."  (Saaty 2008, §6 "The ratings mode")

Relied on for: Saaty's defence of the AHP (rank preserved under absolute
  measurement; changes under relative measurement attributed to
  structural dependence; replicas) and Dyer's opposite assessment, side by
  side; Saaty's own description of the ideal form of priorities.
Context: The four Management Science 1990 papers form one exchange in the
  same issue (Dyer; Saaty's reply; Harker & Vargas's reply; Dyer's
  clarification). Saaty 2008 is a tutorial; the ideal-form passage follows
  a worked job-choice example with normalised priorities summing to 1.
