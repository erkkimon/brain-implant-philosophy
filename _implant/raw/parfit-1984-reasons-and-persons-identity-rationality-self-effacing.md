## Part One, §9 "How S Might Be Self-effacing"

> "But to be self-effacing is not to be self-defeating. It is not the aim of a theory to be believed."  (§9; S is the Self-interest Theory)

> "That a theory is self-effacing does not show that it is not the best theory."  (§9)

## Part Two "Rationality and Time", §46 "Can Desires Be Intrinsically Irrational, or Rationally Required?"

> "But he never cares about possible pains or pleasures on a future Tuesday. Thus he would choose a painful operation on the following Tuesday rather than a much less painful operation on the following Wednesday."  (§46, the man with "Future-Tuesday-Indifference")

## Part Three "Personal Identity", §79 "The Other Views"

> "I call this the view that personal identity is what matters. This is the natural view."  (§79)

> "The rival view is that personal identity is not what matters."  (§79; the OCR continues "I claim" before a page break)

> "What matters is Relation R: psychological connectedness and/or continuity, with the right kind of cause."  (§79)

> "(4) Personal identity is not what matters. What fundamentally matters is Relation R, with any cause."  (§79, in the list of conclusions Parfit says he will argue for)

> "(1) We are not separately existing entities, apart from our brains and bodies, and various interrelated physical and mental events."  (§79, same list)

## §95 "Liberation from the Self"

> "Is the truth depressing? Some may find it so. But I find it liberating, and consoling."  (§95)

> "My life seemed like a glass tunnel, through which I was moving faster every year, and at the end of which there was darkness. When I changed my view, the walls of my glass tunnel disappeared."  (§95)

## Part Four, §131 "The Repugnant Conclusion"

> "The Repugnant Conclusion: For any possible population of at least ten billion people, all with a very high quality of life, there must be some much larger imaginable population whose existence, if other things are equal, would be better, even though its members have lives that are barely worth living."  (§131)

## §150 "Impersonality"; §154 "How Both Human History, and the History of Ethics, May Be Just Beginning"

> "Part Two argued that we should reject the Self-interest Theory about rationality."  (§150)

> "We should accept the Critical Present-aim Theory, or CP."  (§150)

> "Part Three argued for another kind of impersonality."  (§150)

> "Like Rawls and Nagel’, I believe the opposite."  (§154; the stray apostrophe is the OCR of a note marker; "the opposite" of the belief "that there cannot be progress in Ethics")

> "We cannot yet predict whether, as in Mathematics, we will all reach agreement. Since we cannot know how Ethics will develop, it is not irrational to have high hopes."  (§154, the last sentences of the main text)
