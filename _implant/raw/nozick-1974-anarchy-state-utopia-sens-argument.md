# Nozick 1974 — "Sen's Argument": rights as constraints on social choice, not positions in a social ordering

Source: Robert Nozick, Anarchy, State, and Utopia (New York: Basic Books,
  1974; Oxford: Blackwell reprint, ISBN 0-631-19780-X), ch. 7 "Distributive
  Justice", section "Sen's Argument", pp. 164–166; n. 3 (to ch. 7) cites
  Sen, Collective Choice and Social Welfare (Holden-Day, 1970), chs. 6 and 6*.
Original: ISBN 0-631-19780-X; scan at
  https://archive.org/details/robert-nozick-anarchy-state-and-utopia
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (read in the archive.org OCR text of that scan; page
  numbers from the running heads)

> "Our conclusions are reinforced by considering a recent general argument of Amartya K. Sen."  (p. 164)

> "Person A prefers W to X to Y to Z, and person B prefers Y to Z to W to X."  (p. 165)

> "There is no transitive social ordering satisfying all these conditions, and the social ordering, therefore, is nonlinear. Thus far, Sen."  (p. 165; printed "con-ditions" across a line break)

> "The trouble stems from treating an individual’s right to choose among alternatives as the right to determine the relative ordering of these alternatives within a social ordering."  (p. 165)

> "Individual rights are co-possible; each person may exercise his rights as he chooses."  (p. 166)

> "Rights do not determine a social ordering but instead set the constraints within which a social choice is to be made, by excluding certain alternatives, fixing others, and so on."  (p. 166)

> "Rights do not determine the position of an alternative or the relative position of two alternatives in a social ordering; they operate upon a social ordering to constrain the choice it can yield."  (p. 166)

Relied on for: Nozick's restatement of Sen's result (A decisive on (X,Y),
  B on (Z,W)) and his diagnosis and alternative view of rights.
Context: The section follows Nozick's argument that patterned principles
  of distributive justice are unstable; between the diagnosis and the
  alternative view he rejects a second scheme (individuals rank pairs, the
  holder of the right chooses within the top pair) because "an alternative
  may be selected although everyone prefers some other alternative". After
  the quoted passage he applies the view to entitlements to holdings.
