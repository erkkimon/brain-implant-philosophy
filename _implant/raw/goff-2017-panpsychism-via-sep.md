# Goff 2017 / SEP "Panpsychism" — the definition of panpsychism and Goff's simplicity argument

> "Panpsychism is the view that mentality is fundamental and ubiquitous in
>  the natural world. [...] For its proponents panpsychism offers an
>  attractive middle way between physicalism on the one hand and dualism
>  on the other."  (SEP, preamble)

> "The word 'panpsychism' literally means that everything has a mind.
>  However, in contemporary debates it is generally understood as the view
>  that mentality is fundamental and ubiquitous in the natural world."
>  (SEP, §2.1)

Goff's own argument, as the SEP entry (which Goff co-authored) states it:

> "Goff (2016, 2017: ch. 7, 2019a: ch. 4) has argued that from this
>  epistemic starting point there is a clear 'simplicity argument' in
>  favour of panpsychism: in the absence of any reason to suppose
>  otherwise, the most simple, elegant, parsimonious hypothesis is that the
>  matter outside of brains is continuous with the matter of brains in
>  also having a consciousness-involving nature."  (SEP, §3.2)

On the standard objection:

> "This is the form of panpsychism that suffers most acutely from the
>  combination problem"  (SEP, §2, on constitutive micropsychism)

> "These objections are the inspiration for the so-called 'combination
>  problem', around which much of the twenty first century literature on
>  panpsychism focuses."  (SEP, §1, on William James's "mind dust"
>  objections)

Relied on for: the contemporary definition of panpsychism; that Goff 2017
is the main book-length defence and that its argument is a
parsimony/continuity argument from the intrinsic-nature gap in physics;
the combination problem as the central objection.
Context: the SEP entry's lead author is Goff himself, so its presentation
of the "simplicity argument" is a proponent's; the entry notes replies by
Taylor (2019) and Dainton. The entry also treats Russellian monism,
cosmopsychism and the anti-emergence argument (Strawson's), not
reproduced. No sentence from Goff's book is quoted because the book was
not read.
