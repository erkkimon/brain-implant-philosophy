> "No-no paradox: Two sentences that each say the other is not true."  (List of paradoxes, "Logic")

> "It derives its name from the fact that it consists of two sentences each simply denying what the other says."  (No–no paradox, lead)

> "The paradox itself appears as the eighth sophism of chapter 8 of John Buridan’s Sophismata."  (No–no paradox, "History"; cites Klima tr. 2001, p. 971)

> "A variation on the paradox occurs already in Thomas Bradwardine’s Insolubilia."  (No–no paradox, "History"; cites Bradwardine, ed. Roure 1970, pp. 304–305)

> "Yet, both of those possibilities would also seem repugnant, as, on each of them, two perfectly symmetrical sentences would mysteriously diverge in truth value."  (No–no paradox, "Formulation")

> "Generally speaking, the paradox instantiates the problem of determining the status of ungrounded sentences that are not inconsistent."  (No–no paradox, "Discussion"; cites Herzberger 1970)

> "These and similar conclusions have however been contested by other philosophers on the grounds that, as evidenced by Curry's paradox, joint reliance on (T) and classical logic might be problematic even when it does not lead to inconsistency."  (No–no paradox, "Discussion"; cites López de Sa & Zardini 2007)

not true" / (N2) "(N1) is not true", and four classical possibilities, of
which the two matching ones are excluded by (T); it cites Sorensen 2001
pp. 165–184 and p. 176 (truthmaker maximalism), Priest 2005, Armour-Garb &
Woodbridge 2006, Greenough 2011, Armstrong 2004. The list entry sits under
"Liar paradox", between "Card paradox" and "Pinocchio paradox".
