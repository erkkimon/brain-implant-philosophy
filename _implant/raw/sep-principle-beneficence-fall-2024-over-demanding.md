> "Peter Singer’s theory has been the most widely discussed account."  (§4)

> "However, Singer leaves it open what counts as being of comparable importance and as being an appropriate level of sacrifice."  (§4)

> "Singer’s proposals have struck many as overly demanding, impracticable, and a significant departure from the demands of ordinary morality."  (§4)

> "They argue that the line between the obligatory and the supererogatory has been unjustifiably erased by such a principle."  (§4)

> "He therefore has proposed a more guarded formulation of the principle, arguing that we should strive for donations of a percentage of income, such as 10 per cent, which amounts to more than a token donation and yet also is not so high as to make us miserable or turn us into moral saints."  (§4)

> "An individual is only required to aid others beneficently at the level that would produce the best consequences if all in society were to give their fair share."  (§4, on Liam Murphy)

> "Murphy’s cooperative principle is intuitively attractive, but it may not have the necessary moral punch to address issues such as global poverty."  (§4 (Beauchamp's assessment))

> "This is not to say that Singer’s approach is superior. The more demanding a principle is, the less likely are people to comply with its demands."  (§4 (Beauchamp's assessment))

> "A variety of proposals regarding the limits of beneficence have been made by philosophers, but no agreement exists on even a single general principle."  (§4)
