# Diogenes Laertius, Lives IX.72 (and IX.25) — "A moving body moves neither where it is nor where it is not"

Source: Diogenes Laërtius, Lives of Eminent Philosophers, Book IX, tr.
  R. D. Hicks (Loeb Classical Library, 1925), §72 (Pyrrho: the sceptics'
  forerunners) and §25 (Zeno of Elea).
Original: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/Book_IX
  (Wikisource, from the archive.org scan
  https://archive.org/details/diogeneslaertius0002unse) (public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-09-27 (read in the Wikisource raw wikitext)

> "Furthermore, they find Xenophanes, Zeno of Elea, and Democritus to be sceptics"  (IX.72)

> "and Zeno because he would destroy motion, saying, “A moving body moves neither where it is nor where it is not”"  (IX.72)

> "Zeno, then, was all through a pupil of Parmenides and his bosom friend. He was tall in stature, as Plato says in his Parmenides."  (IX.25)

> "Aristotle says that Zeno was the inventor of dialectic, as Empedocles was of rhetoric."  (IX.25)

Relied on for: the one-line form of the Arrow attributed to Zeno by
Diogenes (Hicks translation); Diogenes' own pointer to Plato's Parmenides;
the report that Aristotle called Zeno the inventor of dialectic.
Context: IX.72 lists figures whom the Pyrrhonists ("they") claimed as
forerunners of scepticism; the Zeno line is cited as evidence for that
claim, not as part of a life of Zeno. The SEP (Huggett, §3.3) quotes the
same sentence in a different translation (Kirk, Raven & Schofield 1983,
p. 273): "What is in motion moves neither in the place it is nor in one in
which it is not".
