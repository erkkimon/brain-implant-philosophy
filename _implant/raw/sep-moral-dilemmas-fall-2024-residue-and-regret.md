# SEP "Moral Dilemmas" (McConnell) — §6 the argument from moral residue and the regret objection

Source: Terrance McConnell, "Moral Dilemmas", The Stanford Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri Nodelman (eds.);
  first published 2002-04-15, substantive revision 2022-07-25. §6.
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/moral-dilemmas/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-27 (text of the Fall 2024 archive page read)

> "But either remorse or guilt is appropriate only if the agent properly believes that he has done something wrong (or failed to do something that he was all-things-considered required to do)."  (§6)

> "An agent can appropriately experience regret even when she does not believe that she has done something wrong."  (§6)

> "Since regret is warranted even in the absence of such a belief, to assume that remorse is appropriate is to assume, not argue, that the agent’s situation is genuinely dilemmatic."  (§6)

> "This appeal to moral residue, then, does not by itself establish the reality of moral dilemmas."  (§6)
