# Waldinger 2022 — generalising a theorem for a stronger induction hypothesis (unification synthesis)

Source: Richard Waldinger (SRI International), "Deductive Synthesis of the Unification Algorithm:
The Automation of Introspection", in D. S. Warren, P. Van Roy, Y. A. Liu (eds.), *Proceedings of
the 2nd Workshop on Logic and Practice of Programming (LPOP)*, arXiv:2211.09923, 2022, pp. 12ff.
(passage on PDF page 14). References there: "[Pólya 57] Pólya, G. (1957). How to solve it: A new aspect of mathematical method." and "[Boyer Moore 79] Boyer, R. S. and Moore, J S. (1979). A Computational Logic, Academic Press".
Original: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09923 (doi:10.48550/arXiv.2211.09923)
  (arXiv non-exclusive distribution licence; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-08 (PDF through pdftotext)

> "To conduct a proof by mathematical induction, it is often necessary to generalize the theorem to obtain the benefit of a stronger induction hypotheses. (This observation is an instance of the [Polya 57] Inventor’s Paradox and was famously exploited in the [Boyer Moore 79] theorem prover.)"  (PDF p. 14)

> "For the unification synthesis proof, we have found it necessary to strengthen the specification by requiring that the most-general unifier be idempotent"  (PDF p. 14)

Relied on for: a program-synthesis author tying the generalisation of induction theorems to Pólya's name and to the Boyer–Moore prover.
Context: a short workshop paper reporting a machine-assisted (SNARK) synthesis of the unification algorithm following Manna and Waldinger 1981; "hypotheses" is as printed.
