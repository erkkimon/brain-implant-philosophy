> "He insists he doesn't want to kill me. He simply thinks it would have been better, all things considered, to have given my parents the option of killing the baby I once was, and to let other parents kill similar babies as they come along and thereby avoid the suffering that comes with lives like mine and satisfy the reasonable preferences of parents for a different kind of child."  (p. 1)

> "He is the man who wants me dead. No, that's not at all fair. He wants to legalize the killing of certain babies who might come to be like me if allowed to live."  (p. 1)

> "What does it take to be a person? Awareness of your own existence in time. The capacity to harbor preferences as to the future, including the preference for continuing to live."  (p. 1)

> "I am the token cripple with an opposing view."  (p. 1)

> "A: He behaved in every way appropriately, treated me as a respected professional acquaintance and was a gracious and accommodating host."  (p. 1)

> "But now I've read some of Singer's writing, and I admit it does make sense -- within the conceptual world of Peter Singer."  (p. 1)

> "Harriet McBryde Johnson is a lawyer in solo practice in Charleston, S.C. She has been a disability rights activist and advocate for more than 25 years."  (author note)

Relied on for: Johnson's statement of Singer's view and her report of
his conduct as host; her self-description and the author note.
Context: Johnson describes two engagements at Princeton in spring 2002
(a talk on selective infanticide to undergraduates, a dinner discussion on
