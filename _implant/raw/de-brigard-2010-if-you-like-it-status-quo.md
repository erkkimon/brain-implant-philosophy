> "In an informal survey I conducted a while ago, I introduced my students to Nozick’s original version of the experience machine thought-experiment and then I asked them whether they would plug in or not. Although the majority preferred not to plug into the experience machine, when I asked them why, most of them—60%, to be more precise—gave reasons that had nothing to do with their preference for a real life over a virtual one."  (p. 45)

> "In this study I presented participants with a backward-looking experience machine thought-experiment."  (p. 46; hyphen at a line break, "thought-" / "experiment", kept)

> "to pick one of two options (‘‘Remain connected’’ or ‘‘Go back to reality’’) and to explain their answer."  (pp. 46–47)

> "Participants were randomly assigned to one of three conditions, with 24 participants in each condition and each participant receiving only one vignette."  (p. 46)

> "Participants were undergraduate students from UNC with no previous exposure to philosophy"  (p. 47)

> "In reality you are a prisoner in a maximum security prison in West Virginia."  (p. 47, Negative vignette)

> "In reality you are a multimillionaire artist living in Monaco."  (p. 47, Positive vignette)

> "For the Negative scenario, only 13% of the participants said to prefer reality; 87% of them would prefer to remain connected."  (p. 47)

> "Finally, when assessing the Neutral scenario, 54% of the participants said they would like to go back to reality, whereas 46% would prefer to remain connected."  (p. 48)

> "I conducted a follow-up study with 80 participants, all undergraduates from UNC with no previous exposure to philosophy."  (p. 49)

> "With the Second Neutral vignette the results shifted strikingly (figure 2): 59% of participants wanted to remain connected, while only 41% wanted to disconnect."  (p. 49)

> "only a third of the participants that wanted to disconnect explained their choice by making explicit reference to their preference for reality."  (p. 49)

> "And if this is the case, then we have reason to believe that people may be choosing not to plug in to the experience machine because of their aversion to relinquish their status quo, and not—as Nozick and many others think—because they prefer to be ‘‘in contact with reality.’’"  (p. 53)

The Positive vignette result (p. 47–48, split across a page break in the

Relied on for: the design (three backward-looking vignettes, 24 per
  condition, UNC undergraduates without prior philosophy; follow-up N = 80
  with a "Second Neutral" vignette), the results, and De Brigard's
  status-quo interpretation; his note crediting Kolber 1994.
Context: the vignette tells the participant a "Mr. Smith" has revealed
  they were plugged in by error; the Second Neutral vignette adds that
  "your life outside is not at all like the life you have experienced so
  far". De Brigard's §3 explains the results with the status quo bias
  (Samuelson & Zeckhauser 1988) and prospect theory; §4 argues that a
  status-quo-driven intuition is not thereby irrational.
