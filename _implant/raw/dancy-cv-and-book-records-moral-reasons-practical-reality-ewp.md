- Moral Reasons — Dancy, Oxford: Blackwell, 1993; ISBN 0631177752 / 9780631177753
  (Open Library record). Reviews: Hubin, Ethics 106 (1995): 187–189,
  DOI 10.1086/293784; Philosophical Quarterly 45 (1995), DOI 10.2307/2219861.
- Practical Reality — DOI 10.1093/0199253056.001.0001 (online record dated 2002).

> "Practical Reality is about the relation between the reasons why we do things and the reasons why we should."  (abstract)

> "Motivating reasons are not mental states of the agent, but states of affairs."  (abstract)

- Ethics Without Principles — DOI 10.1093/0199270023.001.0001 (2004).

> "It introduces and defends a distinction between reasons and enabling conditions, and considers some other roles that considerations that are morally relevant but not reasons might play."  (abstract)

> "The second part uses these results to argue for holism in the theory of reasons, and to construct an argument from that holism to particularism in ethics, which is characterised as the view that moral thought and judgement in no way depend on a suitable provision of moral principles."  (abstract)

> "The third part is concerned with the theory of value, in particular with a form of holism there which is analogous to holism in the theory of reasons."  (abstract)

- Practical Shape: A Theory of Practical Reasoning — DOI 10.1093/oso/9780198805441.001.0001 (2018).

> "This book offers a theory of practical reasoning which is Aristotelian in spirit, since it maintains that one can reason to action in very much the same ways as those in which one can reason to belief."  (abstract)

- Hooker & Little (eds.), Moral Particularism — DOI 10.1093/oso/9780198238843.001.0001
  (Oxford, 2000). Chapters verified by DOI: Hooker, "Moral Particularism: Wrong
  and Bad", pp. 1–22 (…003.0001); Crisp, "Particularizing Particularism",
  pp. 23–47 (…003.0002); Raz, "The Truth in Particularism", pp. 48–78
  (…003.0003); Jackson, Pettit & Smith, pp. 79–99 (…003.0004); Dancy, "The
  Particularist's Progress", pp. 130–156 (…003.0006).

> "Spurred in large part by reactions to the writings of John McDowell and Jonathan Dancy, philosophers continue to divide between those who find particularism’s claims insightful and those who find them exaggerated or wrong-headed."  (abstract)

- McKeever & Ridge, Principled Ethics: Generalism as a Regulative Ideal —
  DOI 10.1093/0199290652.001.0001 (Oxford, 2006).

> "However, the underlying assumption that this is the best approach has received almost no defence, and has been attacked by particularists who argue that the traditional link between morality and principles is little more than an unwarranted prejudice."  (abstract)

> "Generalism as a regulative ideal incorporates what is most insightful in particularism (e.g., the possibility that reasons are context sensitive - ‘holism about reasons’) while rejecting every major particularist doctrine."  (abstract)

> "It argues that in so far as moral knowledge and wisdom are possible, all of morality can and should be codified in a manageable set of principles, even if we are not yet in possession of those principles."  (abstract)
