> "Peoples are to honor human rights."  (§5.1, one of the eight principles)

> "One major reason that liberal peoples tolerate decent peoples, Rawls says, is that decent peoples secure for all persons within their territory a core list of human rights."  (§5.3)

> "These core human rights include rights to subsistence, security, personal property, and formal equality before the law, as well as freedoms from slavery, protections of ethnic groups against genocide, and some measure of liberty of conscience (but not, as we have seen, a right to democratic participation)."  (§5.3)

> "These core human rights are the minimal conditions required for persons to be able to engage in social cooperation in any real sense, so any well-ordered society must protect them."  (§5.3)

> "The role of human rights in the law of peoples is thus to set limits on international toleration."  (§5.3)

> "Societies that violate human rights overstep the limits of toleration, and may rightly be subject to economic sanctions or even military intervention."  (§5.3)
