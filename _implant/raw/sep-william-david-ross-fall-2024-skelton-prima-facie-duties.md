> "William David Ross (1877–1971) made contributions to ancient philosophy and to moral philosophy."  (preamble)

> "In it and other works, Ross develops a novel (pluralistic) deontological ethical theory rivalling Kantianism and utilitarianism. The most innovative element of this theory is the notion of a prima facie duty."  (preamble)

> "Ross subscribes to five underivative or foundational duties"  (§4.1)

> "A duty of fidelity, that is, a duty to keep our promises (RG 21; FE 76–77; KT 21); A duty of reparation, that is, a duty to correct a previous wrong we have done (RG 21; FE 76; KT 21); A duty of gratitude, that is, a duty to return services to those from whom we have in the past accepted benefits (RG 21; FE 76; KT 21); A duty of beneficence, that is, a duty to maximise aggregate or general good"  (§4.1)

> "A duty of non-maleficence, that is, a duty not to harm or injure others"  (§4.1)

> "Ross’s major innovation involves characterising these as prima facie duties rather than absolute or exceptionless duties."  (§4.1)

> "Prima facie duties do not state our actual obligation or duty proper – the thing we ultimately ought do – in a particular situation (RG 20)."  (§4.1)

> "Ross says while there are no general rules ‘[f]or the estimation of the comparative stringency of . . . prima facie obligations’ (RG 41), his judgement is on reflection saving the accident victims is weightier than keeping your promise (RG 18)"  (§4.1)

> "He writes: ‘We think the principle ‘do evil to no one’ more pressing than the principle ‘do good to every one’, except when the evil is very substantially outweighed by the good’ (FE 75)."  (§4.1)
