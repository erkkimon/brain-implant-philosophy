> "(1886 – 1965), German-American theologian and philosopher, was born in Starzeddel (now Starosiedle, Poland), in Brandenburg, Germany, on August 20, 1886, the son of a Lutheran pastor."  (opening)

> "After passing his second theological examination at Halle, he was ordained into the ministry in 1912."  (opening)

> "In content, this was a cautious analysis of socialism and a critique of unrestrained capitalism."  ("Career and Theory Formulation", on *Die sozialistische Entscheidung*, 1933)

> "Tillich left Germany in October 1933. In February 1934 he began his long teaching career at Union Theological Seminary in New York , remaining there until his retirement in 1955."  ("Career")

> "He then became University Professor at Harvard — a great distinction — and in 1962 he became, with similar distinction, the Nuveen Professor of Theology at the University of Chicago ."  ("Career")

> "shortly before his death on October 22, 1965,"  ("Career")

> "Tillich's major work is the three-volume Systematic Theology , in which Tillich undertakes to interpret Christian symbols so as to show how they provide answers to ontological questions."  ("Theory of Religion and Symbols")

> "The correlation between the two is formulated in the statement "God is being-itself.""  ("Theory of Religion and Symbols")

> "Tillich's definition of faith as "ultimate concern" — in the sense of one's being ultimately concerned about that which concerns one unconditionally — implies both the ontological question of the meaning of being and also the symbol God as the presence of being-itself, which, as such, is beyond both being and nonbeing."  ("Theory of Religion and Symbols")

> "Tillich's wide influence, especially in the United States , is attributable to the ecumenical character of this theology, to the effectiveness of his teaching, the appeal of his work to professionals as well as to the laity, and, no doubt, to his extraordinary ability to relate theology to the issues of the time."  (closing paragraph)
