> "We shall now do the very opposite: we will consider some principles which at first seem downright crazy, but turn out to be valid after all."  (C, intro, p. 208)

> "There is a certain principle which plays an important role in modern logic and which some of my graduate students have affectionately dubbed "The Drinking Principle.""  (250)

> "Gimme a drink, and give everyone elsch a drink, caush when I drink, everybody drinksh!"  (250, the joke)

> "Does there really exist someone such that if he drinks, everybody drinks? The answer will surprise many of you."  (250)

> "A more dramatic version of this problem emerged in a conversation I had with the philosopher John Bacon: Prove that there is a woman on earth such that if she becomes sterile, the whole human race will die out."  (250, p. 209)

> "A dual version of The Drinking Principle is this: Prove that there is at least one person such that if anybody drinks, then he does."  (250, p. 209)

> "Solution. Yes, it really is true that there exists someone such that whenever he (or she) drinks, everybody drinks. It comes ultimately from the strange principle that a false proposition implies any proposition."  (250, p. 209)

> "Let us look at it this way: Either it is true that everybody drinks or it isn't."  (p. 209)

> "Since everybody drinks and Jim drinks, then it is true that if Jim drinks then everybody drinks."  (p. 209)

> "Since it is false that Jim drinks, then it is true that if Jim drinks, everybody drinks."  (p. 209)

> "The upshot of the matter is that if everyone drinks, then anyone can serve as the mysterious person, and if it is not the case that everybody drinks, then any nondrinker can serve as the mysterious person."  (p. 209)

> "either there is at least one person who drinks or there isn't."  (p. 210, the dual)

> "When I told The Drinking Principle to my students Linda Wetzel and Joseph Bevando, they were delighted."  (Epilogue, p. 210)

> "Student / But that implies that at some time, everyone was drinking at once. Surely that never happened!"  (Epilogue)

> "Logician / You didn't listen to what I said."  (Epilogue)

> "Logician I Didn't you tell me that you never drink?"  (Epilogue; "I" is OCR for "/")
