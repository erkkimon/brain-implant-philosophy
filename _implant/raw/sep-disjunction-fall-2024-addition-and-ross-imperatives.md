# SEP "Disjunction" (Aloni) — addition disputed for imperatives (Ross's letter)

Source: Maria Aloni, "Disjunction", The Stanford Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri Nodelman (eds.);
  first published 2016-03-23. §2.2 "Disjunctive syllogism and addition"
  and §6 "Modal accounts of disjunction and free choice".
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/disjunction/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (HTML of the Fall 2024 archive page read; line
  breaks and example-number links joined)

> "The validity of addition has also been disputed in relation to imperative logic."  (§2.2)

> "(17) Post this letter! (18) Post this letter or burn it!"  (§2.2)

> "One way to tackle this would be to treat or in (18) as a case of intensional disjunction."  (§2.2)

> "This solution however would fail to account for a characteristic aspect of the interpretation of disjunctive imperatives which arguably explains the failure of addition in these cases, namely their choice offering potential."  (§2.2)

See also sep-disjunction-fall-2024-free-choice.md for the choice-offering
  explanation, the Mastop/Aloni sentence, von Wright's label and Aloni 2007
  (not duplicated here).

Relied on for: the imperative version (Post this letter!), the intensional-
  disjunction option and Aloni's assessment of it, the choice-offering
  (free choice) explanation, the link to the paradox of free choice
  permission (ross-paradox.md).
Context: §2.2 closes with this remark after discussing Anderson and
  Belnap's intensional disjunction, for which addition fails; §6 states
  the free choice principle P(α ∨ β) → Pα ∧ Pβ and the deontic validity
  of Pα → P(α ∨ β), and lists pragmatic (implicature) and semantic
  accounts.
