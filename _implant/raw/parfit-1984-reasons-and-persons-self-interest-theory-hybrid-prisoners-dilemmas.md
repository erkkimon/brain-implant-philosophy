> "I shall first discuss the Self-interest Theory, or S. This is a theory about rationality. S gives to each person this aim: the outcomes that would be best for himself, and that would make his life go, for him, as well as possible."  (§1)

> "To apply S, we must ask what would best achieve this aim. Answers to this question I call theories about self-interest. As Appendix I explains, there are three plausible theories."  (§1; the Hedonistic, Desire-Fulfilment and Objective List Theories)

> "In Prisoner’s Dilemmas, S is individually successful. Since it is only collectively self-defeating, S does not fail in its own terms."  (§32)

> "In Prisoner’s Dilemmas, the Self-interest Theory is directly collectively self-defeating. In these cases, if we all pursue self-interest, this will be worse for all of us."  (§41)

> "Sidgwick’s Egoistic Prudence is the Self-interest Theory about rationality."  (§54)

> "The Self-interest Theory is not pure. It is a hybrid theory. S rejects the requirement of personal neutrality, but requires temporal neutrality."  (§55)

> "(CP5) Each of us is rationally required to care about his own self- interest. And this concern should be temporally neutral."  (§52; "self- interest" hyphen-space as in the OCR, a line-break hyphen)
