# SEP "Russell's Paradox" (Irvine & Deutsch) — §4 on the Barber as a "pseudo paradox": supplementary sentences

  Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta
  & Uri Nodelman (eds.); first published 1995-12-08, substantive revision
  2020-10-12. §4.
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/russell-paradox/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (HTML fetched with curl, tags stripped, every

  sep-russell-paradox-fall-2024-history-and-responses.md, which holds the

> "Reading the dyadic predicate letter “\(F\)” as “is a member of”, this says that it is not the case that there is a \(y\) such that for any \(x, x\) is a member of \(y\) if and only if \(x\) is not a member of \(x\)."  (§4, on T269)

> "Does this mean that Russell’s paradox reduces to T269? Certainly the proof of T269 distills the essence of Russell’s argument, its pattern of reasoning."  (§4)

> "But that pattern also underwrites an endless list of seemingly frivolous “paradoxes” such as the famous paradox of the barber who shaves all and only those who do not shave themselves or, similarly, the paradox of the benevolent but efficient God who helps all and only those who do not help themselves."  (§4)

> "How do these “pseudo paradoxes,” as they are sometimes called, differ, if at all, from Russell’s paradox?"  (§4; "these" are the barber and the "benevolent but efficient God")

> "(However, as von Neumann showed, it is not necessary to go quite this far. Von Neumann’s method instructs us not that such things as \(R\) do not exist, but just that we cannot say much about them, inasmuch as \(R\) and the like cannot fall into the extension of any predicate that qualifies as a class.)"  (§4)

> "Even so, psychological talk of “habits of thought” is not particularly illuminating."  (§4, after quoting Quine 1966, 14)

> "This verdict, however, is not quite fair to fans of the Barber or of T269 generally."  (§4)

> "This question is virtually the same as that raised by Russell’s paradox itself."  (§4, of "what non-paradoxical objects there are")
