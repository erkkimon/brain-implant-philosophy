# Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I-II q.71, 81, 82, 88 — sin defined, original sin, mortal and venial sin (English Dominican tr.)

> "Sin is a word, deed, or desire, contrary to the eternal law."  (q.71 a.6 obj. 1, the definition from Augustine, Contra Faust. xxii, 27)

> "sin is nothing else than a bad human act"  (q.71 a.6 corpus)

> "Further, sin is compared to vice, as act to habit, as stated above (Article 1)."  (q.71 a.2 obj. 4)

> "The theologian considers sin chiefly as an offense against God; and the moral philosopher, as something contrary to reason."  (q.71 a.6 ad 5)

> "According to the Catholic Faith we are bound to hold that the first sin of the first man is transmitted to his descendants, by way of origin."  (q.81 a.1 corpus)

> "Hence original sin is concupiscence, materially, but privation of original justice, formally."  (q.82 a.3 corpus)

> "But if mortal be taken metaphorically, as applied to sin, it is opposed to that which is venial."  (q.88 a.1 corpus)

> "through not destroying the order to the last end, wherefore it deserves temporal, but not everlasting punishment"  (q.88 a.2 corpus, of venial sin)

Relied on for: Aquinas's acceptance of Augustine's definition and its two
  rules (human reason, eternal law); theologian vs moral philosopher;
  transmission of the first sin; original sin as privation of original
  justice; mortal vs venial.
Context: In q.71 a.6 Aquinas says the definition names the matter of sin
  ("word," "deed," "desire") and its form ("contrary to the eternal
  law"), and that the two rules of the human will are human reason and
  the eternal law. q.81 a.1 says the contrary "is part of the Pelagian
  heresy". q.88 a.2 derives "venial" from venia (pardon).
