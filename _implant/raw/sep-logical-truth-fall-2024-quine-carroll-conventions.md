# SEP Fall 2024 "Logical Truth" §1.1 — Quine's argument against conventionalism, "derived from Carroll 1895"

> "Quine (1936, §III) famously criticized the Hobbesian view noting that since the logical truths are infinite in number, our ground for them must not lie just in a finite number of explicit conventions, for logical rules are presumably needed to derive an infinite number of logical truths from a finite number of conventions (an argument derived from Carroll 1895; see Soames 2018, ch. 10, for exposition and Gómez-Torrente 2019 for criticism of the argument)."  (§1.1)

Bibliography entry: "Carroll, L., 1895, “What the Tortoise Said to
Achilles”, Mind, 4: 278–80."

Relied on for: the conventionalism connection (Quine 1936) and its
attribution to Carroll; who expounds and who criticises the argument.
Context: the paragraph surveys views on a priori knowledge of logical
truths: the "Hobbesian" (conventionalist) view, Mill, Bolzano, then
Quine's later criticisms of Carnap. Soames 2018 and Gómez-Torrente 2019
were not read here.
