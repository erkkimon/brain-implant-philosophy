# SEP "Hume on Religion" (Russell) — the readings of Hume's religious views: soft sceptic, atheist, irreligion

Source: Paul Russell, "Hume on Religion", The Stanford Encyclopedia of

  first published 2005-10-04, substantive revision 2017-03-27. Preamble,
  §§10–11.

> "There are, however, some significant points of disagreement about the exact nature and extent of Hume’s irreligious intentions."  (preamble)

> "One of the most hotly debated issues arising out of Hume’s philosophy is whether or not he was an atheist."  (§10)

> "The view that has, perhaps, been most dominant during the past century has been that Hume was a skeptic and, as such, stands in a position that endorses neither theism nor atheism."  (§10)

> "Against this (soft) skeptical reading critics have argued both that it exaggerates and that it underestimates Hume’s skeptical commitments."  (§10)

> "The most accurate and informative label for describing Hume’s views on this subject is perhaps irreligion."  (§11)

Relied on for: the question whether Hume was an atheist, the "soft
skeptic" reading Russell calls dominant in the past century, the critics of
it, and Russell's own proposed label "irreligion" (his assessment).
Context: §10 notes that many contemporaries regarded Hume as an atheist and
that he wrote "at a time of severe religious persecution"; it distinguishes
"thin" from "robust" theism. Russell's §5 on the Dialogues' "Epicurus's old
questions" is excerpted in epicurean-trilemma-attribution-sources-okeefe-russell-wikipedia.md.
