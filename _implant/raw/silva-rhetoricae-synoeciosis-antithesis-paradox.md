>  own" Also sp. syneciosis oxymoron contrapositum crosse copling A coupling

Relied on for: the relation of oxymoron to synoeciosis (listed as its
alternative name, with Puttenham's "crosse copling") and to antithesis
(synoeciosis brings contraries together "not in order to oppose them");
the rhetorical sense of "paradox" to which oxymoron is "A compressed
paradox".
