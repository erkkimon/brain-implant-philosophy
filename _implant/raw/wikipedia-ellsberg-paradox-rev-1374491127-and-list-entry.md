> "Ellsberg paradox: People exhibit ambiguity aversion (as distinct from risk aversion), in contradiction with expected utility theory."  (List of paradoxes, "Decision theory")

> "John Maynard Keynes published a version of the paradox in 1921."  (article, lead; cited to Keynes 1921, "pp. 75–76, paragraph 315, footnote 2")

> "It is generally taken to be evidence of ambiguity aversion, in which a person tends to prefer choices with quantifiable risks over those with unknown, incalculable risks."  (article, lead)

> "Other alternative explanations include the competence hypothesis and the comparative ignorance hypothesis. Both theories attribute the source of the ambiguity aversion to the participant's pre-existing knowledge."  (article, "Alternative explanations"; cited to Heath & Tversky 1991, doi:10.1007/bf00057884, and Fox & Tversky 1995, doi:10.2307/2946693 — both DOIs verified by content negotiation on 2026-10-08; the papers were not read)
