> "Crocodile dilemma: If a crocodile steals a child and promises its return if the father can correctly guess exactly what the crocodile will do, how should the crocodile respond in the case that the father guesses that the child will not be returned?"  (List of paradoxes, "Logic")

> "Similar to the crocodile dilemma."  (List of paradoxes, "Philosophy", end of the Buridan's bridge entry)

> "The crocodile paradox, also known as crocodile sophism, is a paradox in logic in the same family of paradoxes as the liar paradox."  (article, lead; cites Barile, MathWorld)

> "The premise states that a crocodile, who has stolen a child, promises the parent that their child will be returned if and only if they correctly predict what the crocodile will do next."  (lead)

> "The transaction is logically smooth but unpredictable if the parent guesses that the child will be returned, but a dilemma arises for the crocodile if the parent guesses that the child will not be returned."

> "If the crocodile decides to keep the child, he violates his terms: the parent's prediction has been validated, and the child should be returned. However, if the crocodile decides to return the child, he still violates his terms, even if this decision is based on the previous result: the parent's prediction has been falsified, and the child should not be returned."

> "The question of what the crocodile should do is therefore paradoxical, and there is no justifiable solution."  (cites Siekmann ed., Lecture Notes in Artificial Intelligence, 1989, p. 14; Young 2005; Murray's Compendium of Logic, 1847, p. 159)

> "The crocodile dilemma exposes some of the logical problems posed by metaknowledge. In this regard, it is similar in construction to the unexpected hanging paradox, which Richard Montague (1960) used to demonstrate that the following assumptions about knowledge are inconsistent when tested in combination:"  (followed by three numbered assumptions about knowledge)

> "Ancient Greek sources were the first to discuss the crocodile dilemma."  (cites MathWorld)
