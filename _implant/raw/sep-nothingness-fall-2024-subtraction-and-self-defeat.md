# SEP "Nothingness" (Sorensen) — Hugo on nihilism, Baldwin's subtraction argument, Parmenides on negative existentials

Source: Roy Sorensen, "Nothingness", The Stanford Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri Nodelman (eds.);
  first published 28 Aug 2003, substantive revision 28 Feb 2022
  (Copyright © 2022 by Roy Sorensen). §1, §7 "The subtraction argument",
  §10 "Is there any nothingness?".
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/nothingness/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (HTML of the Fall 2024 archive page read as text)

> "All roads are blocked to a philosophy which reduces everything to the word ‘no.’ To ‘no’ there is only one answer and that is ‘yes.’ Nihilism has no substance."  (§1, quoting Victor Hugo, Les Misérables, 1862, 439)

> "As far as simplicity is concerned, there is a tie between the nihilistic rule ‘Always answer no!’ and the inflationary rule ‘Always answer yes!’. Neither rule makes for serious metaphysics."  (§1)

> "Thomas Baldwin (1996) reinforces the possibility of an empty world by refining the following thought experiment: Imagine a world in which there are only finitely many objects. Suppose each object vanishes in sequence. Eventually you run down to three objects, two objects, one object and then Poof! There’s your empty world."  (§7)

> "Since every fact requires at least one object, a world without objects would be a world without facts. But a factless world is a contradiction in terms. Therefore, the empty world is impossible."  (§7, reporting Wittgenstein's Tractatus view)

> "Whether or not Armstrong has contradicted himself, he has illustrated the persuasiveness of the subtraction argument."  (§7)

> "Parmenides maintained that it is self-defeating to say that something does not exist."  (§10)

Relied on for: the subtraction argument for metaphysical nihilism (the
argument Wikipedia's "metaphysical" variant concerns) and the objections
Sorensen reports; Parmenides on the self-defeat of negative existentials.
Context: §7 also reports Maddy, Lowe and Armstrong; the Wittgenstein line
is Sorensen's report of the Tractatus position, not Sorensen's own claim.
The entry does not use the phrase "paradox of nihilism".
