# Wittgenstein 1953 — Philosophical Investigations Part II §x on Moore's paradox (Anscombe tr.)

Source: Ludwig Wittgenstein, Philosophical Investigations, tr. G. E. M.
  Anscombe, the English text of the third edition (New York: Macmillan,
  1958 printing; © Basil Blackwell & Mott 1958; first published 1953),
  Part II, section x, pp. 190e–192e.
Original: archive.org scan, identifier philosophicalinvestigations_20191119
  (https://archive.org/details/philosophicalinvestigations_20191119);
  canonical locator PI II.x, p. 190 of the 1953/1958 pagination
  (copyrighted translation; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-27 (djvu text downloaded; every quotation checked against
  the rendered page images of pp. 190e, 191e and 192e, PDF pages 196–198;
  Wittgenstein's italics are not reproduced)

> "Moore’s paradox can be put like this: the expression “I believe that this is the case” is used like the assertion “This is the case”; and yet the hypothesis that I believe this is the case is not used like the hypothesis that this is the case."  (p. 190e)

> "If there were a verb meaning ‘to believe falsely’, it would not have any significant first person present indicative."  (p. 190e)

> "—“I believe ...., and it isn’t so” would be a contradiction."  (p. 191e; said of an imagined use of "I believe" prefixed to reports "when they serve to give information about the reporter")

> "Moore’s paradox would not exist in this language; instead of it, however, there would be a verb lacking one inflexion."  (p. 191e; the language in which "I believe it is so" is expressed only by the tone of "It is so")

> "And then it would also be possible for someone to say “It is raining and I don’t believe it”, or “It seems to me that my ego believes this, but it isn’t true.” One would have to fill out the picture with behaviour indicating that two people were speaking through my mouth."  (p. 192e)

> "Don’t regard a hesitant assertion as an assertion of hesitancy."  (p. 192e)

Relied on for: Wittgenstein's formulation of "Moore's paradox" (the name
  appears on p. 190, which Smithies 2016 cites as where the paradox "was
  named"); the asymmetry between asserting "I believe p" and supposing it;
  the "It is raining and I don't believe it" sentence.
Context: Section x opens "How did we ever come to use such an expression as
  “I believe ... ”?" and treats "I believe" as an expression that "throws
  light on my state" much as expressions of emotion do; the section is
  about the first-person present of "believe", with Moore's paradox as its
  organising case.
