> "Ideal theory makes two types of idealizing assumptions about its subject matter."  (§2.3)

> "First, ideal theory assumes that all actors (citizens or societies) are generally willing to comply with whatever principles are chosen."  (§2.3)

> "Second, ideal theory assumes reasonably favorable social conditions, wherein citizens and societies are able to abide by principles of political cooperation."  (§2.3)

> "Completing ideal theory first, Rawls says, yields a systematic understanding of how to reform our non-ideal world, and fixes a vision (mentioned above) of what is the best that can be hoped for."  (§2.3)

> "Once ideal theory is completed for a political sub-domain, non-ideal theory can be set out by reference to the ideal."  (§2.3)

> "The difference principle thus expresses a positive ideal, an ideal of social unity."  (§4.3)

> "The principles selected in the international original position contain provisions for non-ideal situations: situations in which nations are unwilling to comply with the ideal principles, or are unable to cooperate on their terms."  (§5.5)

Relied on for: the ideal / non-ideal theory distinction as Wenar reports
Rawls's method; "ideal" as a predicate of principles and of a social order.
Context: §2.3 opens with "Within each sub-domain of the political Rawls also
follows a sequence: ideal theory before non-ideal theory." (in the
supplemented excerpt). Between the second and third quotations the entry
says ideal theory "idealizes away the possibility of law-breaking".
