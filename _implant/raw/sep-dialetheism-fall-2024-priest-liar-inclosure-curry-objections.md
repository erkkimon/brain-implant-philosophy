> "The word ‘dialetheism’ was coined by Graham Priest and Richard Routley (later Sylvan) in 1981 (Priest et al. 1989, p. xx)."  (§1)

> "Dialetheism is the view that some contradictions are true."  (§1)

> "By adopting a paraconsistent logic, a dialetheist can countenance some contradictions without being thereby committed to countenancing everything and, in particular, all contradictions."  (§1)

> "In 1979, Priest’s paper “The Logic of Paradox”, developed eventually into the book In Contradiction (1987/2006), presented what are now the most famous arguments for dialetheism."  (§2.4)

> "In the collaboration between Priest and Routley, the contemporary dialetheic program was launched."  (§2.4)

> "Probably the master argument used by modern dialetheists invokes the logical paradoxes of self-reference."  (§3.1)

> "Dialetheists agree, but draw the conclusion in the other direction: the appropriate formalization of a language such as ours, because it is semantically closed and is not hierarchically stratified, will be inconsistent (Priest 1987, Ch. 1, Beall 2009, Ch. 1)."  (§3.2)

> "Tarski, in short, identified the cause of the semantic paradoxes to be semantic closure—the fact that natural languages such as English satisfy the T-schema."  (§3.2)

> "The two most prominent such theories to date are presented in Priest 1987 and Beall 2009."  (§3.2)

> "It is admitted that some sentences—notably, the Liars—are truth-value gluts, that is, both true and false"  (§3.2)

> "For a sustained critical engagement with paraconsistent dialetheism as a solution to the semantic paradoxes, see Field 2008, part 5 (chapters 23–26)."  (§3.2)

> "Priest argues that the paradoxes share an underlying structure (which he calls the Inclosure Schema in Priest 2002)."  (§3.3)

> "This is used in tandem with what Priest dubs the principle of uniform solution (“same kind of paradox, same kind of solution”) to urge that, since all the set-theoretic and semantic paradoxes are of a kind, dialetheism presents a uniquely unified solution."  (§3.3)

> "A dialetheist, though, cannot simply accept that the Curry sentence is both true and false, because if it is true then \(\bot\) follows."  (§3.3)

> "A standard dialetheic strategy to deal with the Curry paradox has consisted in exploiting paraconsistent logics with a ‘noncontractive’ conditional (see again Priest 1987, Ch, 6, Beall 2009, Ch. 2)"  (§3.3)

> "Stronger forms of the paradox, though, validity curry, seem to show that dropping these principles is not enough (Beall and Murzi 2013)."  (§3.3)

> "Curry’s paradox puts pressure on the dialetheic story about uniform solution to the paradoxes. Beall (2014a, 2014b) urges this point; Weber et al. 2014 is a reply."  (§3.3)

> "A critical analysis of Aristotle’s arguments is given point for point by Priest (1998b/2006 Ch. 1), who finds them to often conflate dialetheism with trivialism"  (§4)

> "A standard argument against dialetheism is to invoke the logical principle of Explosion, in virtue of which dialetheism would entail trivialism."  (§4.1)

> "Since this argument assumes that Explosion is logically valid, it will carry no weight against a dialetheic paraconsistentist."  (§4.1)

> "One argument from exclusion, with a more ad hominem twist, claims that the dialetheist has trouble with expressing disagreement with rival positions in debates (see Parsons 1990, Shapiro 2004, Littman and Simmons 2004)."  (§4.2)

> "How can he indicate that he genuinely disagrees with you? The natural choice is for him to say ‘\(A\) is not true.’ However, the truth of this assertion is also consistent with \(A\)’s being true—for a dialetheist, anyway. (Parsons 1990, p. 345)"  (§4.2, quoting Parsons 1990, p. 345)

> "Priest has argued that the logical operation of negation should be distinguished from the speech acts of denial and the cognitive state of rejection."  (§4.2)

> "Then the dialetheist can rule out that \(A\) is the case by denying \(A\); and this does not amount to the assertion of \(\neg A\) (Priest 2006, Ch. 6)."  (§4.2)

> "A version of this objection to dialetheism is due to Slater (1995); see also Restall 1993."  (§4.3)

> "On a dialetheic interpretation, advocated by Priest, Garfield, and Deguchi (see Priest 2002 Ch. 16, Deguchi et al. 2008), readers should take Nagarjuna and others in the Madhyamaka school at their word"  (§2.2)

> "do not need to keep running through richer and richer meta-languages in order to chase our semantic tails…. We embrace some contradictions in the semantics, and get it all from the start. (Shapiro 2002, p. 818)"  (§3.2, quoting Shapiro 2002, p. 818)

> "the standard Liar is attributed to the Greek philosopher Eubulides, probably the greatest paradox-producer of antiquity"  (§3.2)
