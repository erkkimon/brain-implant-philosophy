# Boethius (6th c.) — Consolation of Philosophy V.3 and V.6: foreknowledge, eternity, two necessities

## V.3 — the problem, in Boethius' own voice

> "It seems,' said I, 'too much of a paradox and a contradiction that God should know all things, and yet there should be free will."  (V pr. 3)

> "For if God foresees everything, and can in no wise be deceived, that which providence foresees to be about to happen must necessarily come to pass."  (V pr. 3)

> "Wherefore, if from eternity He foreknows not only what men will do, but also their designs and purposes, there can be no freedom of the will"  (V pr. 3)

## Translator's summary of Book V (James 1897 edition, not Boethius' text)

> "But if man has no freedom of choice, it follows that rewards and punishments are unjust as well as useless; that merit and demerit are mere names; that God is the cause of men's wickednesses; that prayer is meaningless."  (summary of ch. III)

## V.6 — Philosophy's answer

> "Now, eternity is the possession of endless life whole and perfect at a single moment."  (V pr. 6)

> "thou wilt more rightly deem it not foreknowledge as of something future, but knowledge of a moment that never passes."  (V pr. 6)

> "Does the act of vision add any necessity to the things which thou seest before thy eyes?"  (V pr. 6)

> "So, then, there are two necessities--one simple, as that men are necessarily mortal; the other conditioned, as that, if you know that someone is walking, he must necessarily be walking."  (V pr. 6)

> "For my answer would be that the same future event is necessary from the standpoint of Divine knowledge, but when considered in its own nature it seems absolutely free and unfettered."  (V pr. 6)

Relied on for: Boethius' statement of the problem (V.3) and the eternity
  solution and the simple/conditional necessity distinction (V.6).
Context: Book V is headed in this edition "FREE WILL AND GOD'S
  FOREKNOWLEDGE". In V.3 the prisoner (Boethius) states the argument and its
  consequences (rewards and punishments, prayer); in V.4–V.6 Philosophy
  replies: knowledge follows the faculty of the knower, God is eternal, and
  his knowledge is of an eternal present.
