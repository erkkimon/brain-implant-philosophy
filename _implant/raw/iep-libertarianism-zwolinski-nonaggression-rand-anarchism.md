> "There is no single theory that can be safely identified as the libertarian theory, and probably no single principle or set of principles on which all libertarians can agree."  (§1)

> "that the only proper use of coercion is defensive or to rectify an error; that governments are bound by essentially the same moral principles as individuals;"  (§1, from the list of what libertarians "are committed to")

> "The most well-known version of this conclusion finds expression in the so-called “minimal state” theories of Robert Nozick , Ayn Rand , and others (Nozick 1974; Rand 1963a, 1963b) which hold that states may legitimately provide police, courts, and a military, but nothing more."  (§1; spaces before commas as printed)

> "Murray Rothbard’s claim that the only alternatives to self-ownership are slavery or communism (Rothbard 1973, 29)"  (§2.c)

> "have been met with the response that a denial of the permissibility of slavery, communism, and eye-transplants can be made—and usually better made—on grounds other than self-ownership."  (§2.c, continuing the Rothbard sentence; the sentence's subject is Nozick's eye-transplant concern (Nozick 1977, Cohen 1995) "or" Rothbard's claim)

> "For Hayek, the procedures that lend justifying force to institutions are, essentially, ones that leave individuals free to act as they wish so long as they do not act aggressively toward others."  (§3.b)

> "For Hayek, however, this principle is not a moral axiom but rather follows from his beliefs regarding the limits and uses of knowledge in society."  (§3.b)

> "Once the bright line of non-aggression has been crossed, it is difficult to find a compelling substitute."  (§4, on minimal-state libertarians)

> "If respect for robust rights of self-ownership and property in external goods, as libertarians understand them, entail anarcho-capitalism, why not then reject these rights rather than embrace anarcho-capitalism?"  (§4)

> "Rothbard, Nozick and other natural rights libertarians are notoriously lacking in foundational arguments to support their strong belief in these rights."  (§4)

> "While rejecting the label “libertarian,” Rand here advocates a minimal state that uses force only in retaliation as the only political system compatible with man’s rational nature."  (§6, annotation to Rand 1963a, 1963b)

> "Argues that Ayn Rand’s defense of a minimal state is incompatible with her more basic views regarding men’s natural rights against the initiation of force, and that a proper respect for those rights requires anarcho-capitalism."  (§6, annotation to Childs 1994 [1969], "Objectivism and the State: An Open Letter to Ayn Rand")

> "that liberty, understood as non-interference, is the only thing that can be legitimately demanded of others as a matter of legal or political right;"  (§1, same list)

> "See, however, Rothbard’s essay “Isaiah Berlin on Negative Freedom” in The Ethics of Liberty (1982) for a libertarian criticism of this distinction and Berlin’s argument for it."  (§6, annotation to Berlin, "Two Concepts of Liberty")
