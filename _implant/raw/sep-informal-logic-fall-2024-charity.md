# SEP "Informal Logic" (Fall 2024 archive) — the principle of charity

Source: Leo Groarke, "Informal Logic", The Stanford Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri Nodelman (eds.),
  §3.1 "Implicit premises and conclusions".
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/logic-informal/
  (copyright © 2021 by Leo Groarke; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25

> "In cases in which it is clear that an unstated premise or conclusion
>  needs to be recognized, it can often be interpreted in different ways.
>  One principle that many systems of informal logic use to choose between
>  alternative interpretations of implicit argument components is the
>  'Principle of Charity.' It favors an interpretation of an argument which
>  makes it as credible as possible. In many situations, this can best be
>  accomplished by attributing it a 'logical minimum,' understood as the
>  weakest implicit component needed to successfully connect the
>  argument's premises to its conclusion."  (§3.1)

> "In some cases, the result has been fallacy oriented textbooks which
>  introduce tens or hundreds of fallacies used to dismiss straw man
>  reasoning, hasty generalizations, slippery slope reasoning, and so on
>  (see, e.g., Bennett 2018)."  (§2.4, on fallacy-theory systems of
>  informal logic)

Relied on for: the statement of the principle of charity as the standard
counter-rule to the straw man, and the "logical minimum" reading of it.
Context: the entry describes charity as an interpretive principle for
reconstructing implicit components of arguments; it does not itself
present charity as a response to the straw man — that pairing is made on
the knowledge page that cites this excerpt.
