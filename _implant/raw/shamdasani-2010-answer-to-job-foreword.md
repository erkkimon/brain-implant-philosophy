> "On 29 May 1951, Jung wrote to Aniela Jaffé from his tower at Bollingen, “I have landed the great whale; I mean ‘Answer to Job.’ ”"  (foreword; note 2 cites C. G. Jung Letters vol. 2, pp. 17–18)

> "As Marie-Louise von Franz recalled, Jung “wrote in one burst of energy and with strong emotion, during an illness and after a high fever, and when he finished it he felt well again.”"  (foreword; note 4: von Franz 1975, p. 161)

> "No wonder: for it was in Answer to Job that the theology first articulated in Liber Novus—the themes of the progressive incarnation of the God, the necessity for “Christification,” and the replacement of the one-sided Christian God image with one that encompassed evil within it—found its definitive expression and elaboration."  (foreword)

> "Answer to Job is faithful to the force of Jung’s theophany, now presented in the form of psychotheological and historical argument."  (foreword)

> "On November 25, 1953, Jung wrote to Richard Hull that “the clouds of dust it has raised at times nearly suffocated me!” To this day, the controversies around this work have not been stilled."  (foreword; note 9 points to Bishop 2002 and Lammers and Cunningham 2007, The Jung-White Letters)

Relied on for: composition in 1951, the link to Liber Novus, the reception as Shamdasani reports it (works/answer-to-job.md).
Context: the foreword first describes Jung's self-experimentation from 1913 and the Red Book; the "Answer to Job" section also quotes a 1951 letter by Ximena Roelli reporting that Jung's secretary found the first draft "very violent and blasphemous in tone" and that he had "toned it down".
