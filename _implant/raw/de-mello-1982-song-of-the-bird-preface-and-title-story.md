> "This book has been written for people of every persuasion religious and non religious, I cannot, however, hide from my readers the fact that I am a priest of the Catholic Church. I have wandered free-ly in mystical traditions that are not Christian and not religious and I have been profoundly influenced by them. It is to my Church, however, that I keep returning, for she is my spiritual home"  (front matter)

> "Everyone loves stories and you will find plenty of them in this book: Stories that are Buddhist, Christian, Zen, Hasidic, Russian, Chinese, Hindu, Sufi; stories ancient and contemporary."  (front matter)

> "And they all have a special quality: if read in a cer-tain kind of way, they will produce spiritual growth."  (front matter)

> "HOW TO READ THEM
> There are three ways:
> 1 . Read a story once. Then move on to another. This manner of reading will give you entertainment.
> 2. Read a story twice. Reflect on it. Apply it to your life. That will give you a taste of theology. [...]
> 3. Read the story again, after you have reflected on it. Create a silence within you and let the story reveal to you its inner depth and meaning: something beyond words and reflections. This will give you a feel for the mystical."  (front matter, "How to read them")

> "Let it speak to your heart, not to your brain."  (front matter, "How to read them")

> "It is with this mystical end in view that most of these stories were originally told."  (front matter, "How to read them")

> "CAUTION
> Most of the stories have a comment appended to them. The comment is meant to be a sample of the kind of comment you yourself may want to make. Make your own."  (front matter, "Caution")

> "Beware of applying the story to anyone (priest, mullah, and church, neighbour) other than yourself. If you do so the story will do you damage. Every one of these stories is about you, no one else."  (front matter, "Caution")

> "When you read the book for the first time read the stories in the order in which they are set down here. The order imparts a teaching and a spirit which will be lost if the stories are read haphazardly."  (front matter)

> "Theology: The art of telling stories about the Divine. Also the art of listening to them."  (front matter, "Glossary")

> "Mysticisms: The art of tasting and feeling in your heart the inner meaning of such stories to the point that they transform you."  (front matter, "Glossary")

> "Said the Master, “God is Unknown, the Unknowable. Every statement about Him, every answer to your questions, is a distortion of the Truth. ”"  (story "The Song of the Bird")

> "“Why does the bird sing? ” said the Master.
> Not because he has a statement, but because he has a song."  (story "The Song of the Bird")

> "The stories in this book come from a variety of countries, cultures and religions. They belong to the spiritual heritage — and popular humour — of the human race. All that the author has done is string them together with a specific aim in mind. His task has been that of the weaver and the dyer. He takes no credit at all for the cotton and the thread."  (closing note)
