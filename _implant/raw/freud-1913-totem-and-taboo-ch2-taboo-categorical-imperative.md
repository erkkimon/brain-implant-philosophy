# Freud 1913 — *Totem und Tabu* / *Totem and Taboo* (Brill trans. 1919): taboo, Wundt, the categorical imperative

Source: Sigmund Freud, *Totem und Tabu. Einige Übereinstimmungen im

German (public domain):

> "obwohl negativ gefaßt und auf andere Inhalte gerichtet, ist es seiner psychologischen Natur nach doch nichts anderes als der »kategorische Imperativ« Kant's, der zwangsartig wirken will und jede bewußte Motivierung ablehnt."  (Vorwort)

> "Wundt(26) nennt das Tabu den ältesten ungeschriebenen Gesetzeskodex der Menschheit."  (ch. II §1; note 26: Völkerpsychologie, II. Bd., 1906, II, p. 308)

English (Brill 1919):

> "but according to its psychological nature, it is still nothing else than Kant’s ‘Categorical Imperative’, which tends to act compulsively and rejects all conscious motivations."  (Preface)

> "The taboo restrictions are different from religious or moral prohibitions. They are not traced to a commandment of a god but really they themselves impose their own prohibitions"  (ch. II §1)

> "Wundt[32] calls taboo the oldest unwritten code of law of humanity."  (ch. II §1)

> "The oldest and most important taboo prohibitions are the two basic laws of totemism: namely not to kill the totem animal, and to avoid sexual intercourse with totem companions of the other sex."  (ch. II)

Relied on for: Freud's comparison of taboo with Kant's categorical
  imperative; his report of Wundt's phrase; his distinction of taboo from
  religious and moral prohibitions; his identification of the two oldest
  taboo prohibitions.
Context: Freud says in the preface that the taboo analysis is offered as a
  secured solution, while the totemism essay is more tentative; ch. II
  quotes N. W. Thomas's EB1911 article as its "impartial presentation" and
  then explains taboo through the ambivalence observed in obsessional
  neurosis.
