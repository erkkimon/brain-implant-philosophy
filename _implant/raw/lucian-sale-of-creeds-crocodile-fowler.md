# Lucian, The Sale of Creeds and Dialogues of the Dead — the crocodile (Fowler tr.)

Source: Lucian of Samosata, The Sale of Creeds (Vitarum auctio), the lot of
  Chrysippus (§22 in Prantl's citation, below), and Dialogues of the Dead,
  Diogenes and Pollux, in The Works of Lucian of Samosata, tr. H. W. Fowler
  and F. G. Fowler, vol. 1 (Oxford: Clarendon Press).
Original: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6327 (Project Gutenberg eBook
  #6327; public domain translation). Excerpts only.
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (plain-text file pg6327.txt read; quotations copied

> "I have verbal involutions, for the better hampering, crippling, and muzzling of my antagonists. This is performed by the use of the far-famed syllogism."  (Chrysippus, Sale of Creeds)

> "A crocodile catches him as he wanders along the bank of a river, and promises to restore him to you, if you will first guess correctly whether he means to restore him or not. Which are you going to say?"  (Chrysippus, to the buyer, of the buyer's child)

> "A difficult question. I don't know which way I should get him back soonest. In Heaven's name, answer for me, and save the child before he is eaten up."  (the buyer)

> "Ha, ha. I will teach you far other things than that."  (Chrysippus, who then lists the Reaper, the Rightful Owner, the Electra and the Man in the Hood)

> "Charge them generally to give up playing the fool, quarrelling over metaphysics, tricking each other with horn and crocodile puzzles"  (Diogenes' message to the philosophers, Dialogues of the Dead, Diogenes and Pollux)

Relied on for: the case as Lucian's Chrysippus poses it (guess "whether he
means to restore him or not"); that it is offered as a specimen of the
Stoic "syllogism"; that neither speaker answers it; Lucian's pairing of
horn and crocodile puzzles.
Context: a satire in which Zeus and Hermes auction philosophers; the buyer
does not answer and Chrysippus moves on. See also
lucian-sale-of-creeds-electra-hooded-fowler.md for the arguments that
follow.
