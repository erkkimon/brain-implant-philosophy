> "The so-called Agrippan trilemma is an important argument for contemporary skepticism."  (§6)

> "José de Teresa (Mexico) (2000, 2013, 2014) developed an anti-skeptical strategy inspired in Plato’s dialectic."  (§6)

> "According to de Teresa, Plato’s strategy is effective against the trilemma presented by the skeptic, because it escapes the three alternatives under consideration."  (§6)

> "Among the Agrippan modes, however, the most important for Latin American skepticism seems to be diaphonía or disagreement. We saw how important it is to Porchat’s neo-Pyrrhonism (1969, 1991, 1993)."  (§6)

Relied on for: the trilemma's standing in Latin American epistemology as
the entry's authors state it; de Teresa's Plato-inspired reply (reported,
not read); the weight given to the mode of disagreement (diaphonía) and
Porchat's neo-Pyrrhonism.

Context: the entry's introduction says Oswaldo Porchat (1991) and Robert
J. Fogelin (1994) "coined the word “neo-Pyrrhonism”". Right after the
last excerpt the entry turns to Diego Machuca's (2013a) "idea that
diaphonía is different from disagreement".
