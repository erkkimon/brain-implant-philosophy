# Allen 1998 (NCR) — De Mello censure reflects Vatican misgivings about Eastern thinking

Source: John L. Allen Jr., "De Mello censure reflects Vatican misgivings about Eastern thinking", National Catholic Reporter, 4 September 1998.
Original: https://natcath.org/NCR_Online/archives2/1998c/090498/090498k.htm (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (fetched with curl, HTML converted to text, excerpts copied verbatim; whitespace normalised)

> "“Nobody can be said to have attained the pinnacle of Truth,” Jesuit Fr. Anthony de Mello once wrote, “until a thousand sincere people have denounced him for blasphemy.”"

> "as the Vatican’s Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith condemned the works of the Indian Jesuit -- known for his attempts to bridge Eastern and Western spirituality -- for “relativizing” faith and thus leading to “religious indifferentism.”"

> "In the United States, reaction has consisted largely of puzzlement among de Mello supporters, both as to the content and the timing of the statement, and alarm among publishers."

> "In India, meanwhile, Jesuit officials have suggested that the Vatican action may have been prompted by writings published after de Mello’s death, which do not fairly represent his thinking."

> "His many books, tapes and retreats combined traditional Christian concepts with insights from Eastern religions."

> "the censure of de Mello seems to reflect deep misgivings in Rome about the impact of Eastern religious thinking on Christianity."

> "“The two philosophies are fundamentally different,” he said in a 1996 address to the doctrinal commissions of Latin American bishops conferences. “Nonetheless, they seem to mutually confirm one another in their metaphysical and religious relativism."

> "That concern was echoed Aug. 25 by the current South Asia Jesuit provincial, Fr. Lisbert D’Souza, who said some of these post-humously published works have led to de Mello being “grossly misunderstood.” The Indian Jesuits, he said, regard only nine books as authentic."

> "“It’s extremely hard for me to believe that anyone would find anything de Mello says to be anything other than orthodox,” said Jesuit Fr. Francis Stroud. “He was a very devout churchman.”"

> "“De Mello did emphasize that God is a mystery,” Stroud said. “But he would quote Thomas Aquinas saying the very same thing. ... He never denied anything like a personal concept of God."

> "“It seems rather strange to condemn someone who has no right of reply,” said Eric Major, director of the religious books program at Doubleday -- through its Image imprint, the largest publisher of de Mello’s works in the United States. “Why do it now?”"

> "Jesuit Fr. Norris Clarke, a philosopher at Fordham who has written about de Mello, said the Indian Jesuit’s continuing popularity may explain why Rome felt it necessary to act. “His books and tapes are circulating all around the world,” Clarke said. “Many think he’s a great spiritual new leader, and his influence is quite a living thing. That may be what concerned them.”"

> "“He would talk about theology as pointing a finger at the moon, and we come to mistake the finger for the moon,” Clarke said. “That doesn’t have to mean anything unorthodox, although you could read it that way.”"
