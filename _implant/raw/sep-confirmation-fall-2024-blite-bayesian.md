# SEP "Confirmation" (Crupi) — the blite paradox under Hempelian, HD and Bayesian confirmation

Source: Vincenzo Crupi, "Confirmation", The Stanford Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta & Uri Nodelman (eds.);
  first published 2013-05-30, substantive revision 2020-01-28. §1.2
  (blite), §2.2 (HD and blite), §3.6 (Bayesian treatment of blite).
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/confirmation/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (archive HTML fetched with curl, converted to text,
  each quotation checked against it; the natural-kinds sentence of §1.2
  is in `sep-confirmation-fall-2024-ravens-paradox.md` and not repeated)

> "Consider the peculiar predicate “blite”, defined as follows: an object is blite just in case (i) it is black if examined at some moment t up to some future time T (say, the next expected appearance of Halley’s comet, in 2061) and (ii) it is white if examined afterwards."  (§1.2; the entry sets "blite" in math italics, removed here; the case is introduced as "Blite (Goodman 1955)")

> "So why should the time threshold T in blite or blurple be a reason to dismiss those predicates?"  (§1.2; math markup removed)

> "(The water example comes from Howson 2000, 31–32."  (§1.2)

> "So, all in all, HD-confirmation can not tell black from blite any more than Hempel-confirmation can."  (§2.2)

> "So, as long as the black hypothesis is perceived as initially more credible than its blite counterpart, the former will be more strongly confirmed than the latter."  (§3.6; the preceding sentence credits the result to "Gaifman 1979, 127–128; Sober 1994, 229–230; and Fitelson 2008, 131")

> "Lacking some interesting, non-question-begging story as to why that inequality should obtain, no solution of the paradox seems to emerge."  (§3.6)

> "Without a doubt, (i) and (ii) fall far short of a satisfactory solution of the blite paradox. Yet it seems at least a legitimate minimal requirement for a compelling solution (if any exists) that it implies both."  (§3.6)

Relied on for: the blite variant, Howson's water example against a
natural-kinds restriction, HD's failure to separate black from blite,
the Bayesian prior-based result (Gaifman, Sober, Fitelson) and Crupi's
assessment of it, Crupi's minimal requirements (i)–(ii).
Context: (i) is that e confirms h ("all ravens are black") relative to k
for every probability function; (ii) that for some function e confirms a
post-T raven will be black and not that it will be white. Crupi adds
that "confirmation as firmness is inconsistent with (i), while Hempelian
and HD-confirmation are inconsistent with (ii)".
