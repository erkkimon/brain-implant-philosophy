# Marx 1843 — "On the Jewish Question": the rights of man as rights of "egoistic man"

Source: Karl Marx, "On the Jewish Question" (written autumn 1843; first
  published February 1844 in the Deutsch-Französische Jahrbücher), Part I,
  the commentary on the French Declarations of the Rights of Man; English
  text on the Marxists Internet Archive ("Proofed and Corrected: by Andy
  Blunden, Matthew Grant and Matthew Carmody, 2008/9"; the page does not
  name the translator).
Original: https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1844/jewish-question/
  (German original public domain — Marx d. 1883; the English translation's
  status is not stated on the page, so short excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-15 (HTML fetched with curl, tags stripped, quotations
  matched by script)

> "Above all, we note the fact that the so-called rights of man, the droits de l’homme as distinct from the droits du citoyen, are nothing but the rights of a member of civil society – i.e., the rights of egoistic man, of man separated from other men and from the community."  (Part I)

> "None of the so-called rights of man, therefore, go beyond egoistic man, beyond man as a member of civil society – that is, an individual withdrawn into himself, into the confines of his private interests and private caprice, and separated from the community."  (Part I)

Secondary report (Christopher Stephen Lutz, "Alasdair MacIntyre",
  Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://iep.utm.edu/mac-over/ ,
  retrieved 2026-10-15, copyrighted; excerpts only):

> "Marx dismissed the notion of “natural rights” as a residue of feudal society in the book review, “On The Jewish Question.”"

> "For Marx, “rights” could arise only from laws made by governments."
