# Tillich 1957 — Dynamics of Faith: faith as ultimate concern; eros and agape; faith and morals

Source: Paul Tillich, *Dynamics of Faith* (New York: Harper & Row, 1957), ch. I §1 "Faith as Ultimate Concern"; ch. VI §3 "Faith, Love and Action" and §4.
Original: online text at Religion Online, https://www.religion-online.org/book-chapter/what-faith-is/ and https://www.religion-online.org/book-chapter/vi-the-life-of-faith/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)

> "Faith is the state of being ultimately concerned: the dynamics of faith are the dynamics of man’s ultimate concern."  (ch. I §1, first sentence)

> "No love is real without a unity of eros and agape. Agape without eros is obedience to a moral law, without warmth, without longing, without reunion."  (ch. VI §3)

> "Without an ultimate concern as its basis every system of morals degenerates into a method of adjustment to social demands, whether they are ultimately justified or not."  (ch. VI §4)

Relied on for: the definition of faith as ultimate concern; love as the unity of eros and agape; Tillich's claim that morality needs an ultimate concern.
Context: ch. VI §3 asks how faith relates to love and action and answers that if faith is "the state of being ultimately concerned", "love and action are implied in faith and cannot be separated from it." The ch. VI §4 sentence follows Tillich's remark that "one can point to a nonreligious morality of a high order", which he says depends on a faith "still effective in it".
