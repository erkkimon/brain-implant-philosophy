## Republic II — Glaucon's Gyges story (359d, 360c, Shorey)

> "which men say once came to the ancestor of Gyges the Lydian."  (359d)

> "And yet this is a great proof, one might argue, that no one is just of his own will but only from constraint, in the belief that justice is not his personal good, inasmuch as every man, when he supposes himself to have the power to do wrong, does wrong."  (360c)

## Republic IV — justice in city and soul (433a, 441e, Shorey)

> "And again that to do one's own business and not to be a busybody is justice,"  (433a)

> "Does it not belong to the rational part to rule, being wise and exercising forethought in behalf of the entire soul, and to the principle of high spirit to be subject to this and its ally?"  (441e)

## Gorgias — Socrates to Polus (469c, 470e, Lamb)

> "if it were necessary either to do wrong or to suffer it, I should choose to suffer rather than do it."  (469c)

> "for a good and honorable man or woman, I say, is happy, and an unjust and wicked one is wretched."  (470e)

## Philebus — the two theses (11b, Fowler)

> "Philebus says that to all living beings enjoyment and pleasure and gaiety and whatever accords with that sort of thing are a good;"  (11b; Socrates' counter-thesis, 11b–c, is that "wisdom and thought and memory and their kindred" are better than pleasure for all who can partake of them)

Context: At 359b–c Glaucon proposes giving the just and the unjust man
licence to do as they please; 359d–360b tells of the shepherd and the ring
that makes him invisible; 360c draws the conclusion as the case Glaucon
puts for others, not his own view. 433a recalls the founding principle
that each does the one task his nature suits. 441e follows the
argument for three parts of the soul. At Gorgias 469b–c Socrates says
doing wrong is the greatest evil; at 470e he says he cannot tell whether
the Great King is happy without knowing his education and justice.
Philebus 11b states the dialogue's opening question: Philebus has left the
conversation and Protarchus takes up his thesis.
