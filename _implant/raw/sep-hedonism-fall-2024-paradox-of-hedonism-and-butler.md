# SEP "Hedonism" (Andrew Moore) — the paradox of hedonism and Butler's critique of motivational hedonism

Source: Andrew Moore, "Hedonism", The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy

  published 2004-04-20, substantive revision 2013-10-17. §1 ("Psychological

> "Weakness of agency can see our motivation fail to generate our action (see weakness of will); and the related ‘paradox of hedonism’ is the plausible claim that some of our hedonically motivated or determined action actually secures less pleasure than we would otherwise have got (e.g., Sidgwick: 48f)."  (§1)

> "This thesis was a target of Bishop Joseph Butler in his 1729 work Fifteen Sermons Preached at the Rolls Chapel."  (§1.2)

> "By appeal to such cases Butler and Hume arguably refuted the strong motivational hedonist thesis that one's every desire has one's own pleasure and that alone as its object."  (§1.2)

Relied on for: Moore's definition of the paradox as a claim about
  hedonically motivated action, his placing it beside weakness of will
  within psychological hedonism, and his page reference to Sidgwick.
Context: "Sidgwick: 48f" refers to the entry's bibliography item
  "Sidgwick, H. (1907/1982), The Methods of Ethics, 7th edition, John Rawls
  (ed.), Indianapolis: Hackett." The preceding sentences introduce "the more
  modest claim that only pleasure or displeasure motivates us". The §1.2
  "thesis" is "that each of one's desires or passions or appetites has one's
  own pleasure and only this as its object". The entry dates the Sermons
  1729 (the second edition); Butler's first edition is 1726.
