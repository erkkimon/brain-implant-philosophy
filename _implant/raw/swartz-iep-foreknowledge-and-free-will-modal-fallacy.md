# Swartz (IEP) — "Foreknowledge and Free Will": the modal-fallacy reply

Source: Norman Swartz (Simon Fraser University), "Foreknowledge and Free
  Will", Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy (ISSN 2161-0002), summary, §2,
  §6b.
Original: https://iep.utm.edu/foreknow/ (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (page fetched with curl, HTML stripped; each quotation
  checked against that text)

> "Ultimately the alleged incompatibility of foreknowledge and free will is shown to rest on a subtle logical error. When the error, a modal fallacy, is recognized and remedied, the problem evaporates."  (summary)

> "But the problem would arise if anyone at all (that is, anyone whatsoever) were to have knowledge of our future actions."  (§2)

Swartz's reconstruction of Maimonides (§6b):

> "One tacit assumption of this argument is the necessary truth, “it is not possible both for God to know that D and for D to be false”, or, in symbols, “~◊(gKD & ~D)”."

> "But even with this repair, the argument remains invalid."

Relied on for: the modal-fallacy response, attributed to Swartz; his claim
  that the problem generalises to any foreknower.
Context: the article treats epistemic and logical determinism together and
  argues that both rest on reading "necessarily (if p then q)" as "if p then
  necessarily q". Wikipedia's "Argument from free will" summarises this
  section ("Norman Swartz, however, contends ...").
