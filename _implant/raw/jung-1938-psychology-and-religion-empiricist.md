# Jung 1938 — "I am an empiricist": the Terry Lectures on psychology and religion

Source: C. G. Jung, *Psychology and Religion* (The Terry Lectures, delivered at Yale University 1937), New Haven: Yale University Press; London: Oxford University Press, copyright 1938, eighth printing August 1950; chapter 1 "The Autonomy of the Unconscious Mind", pp. 1–4; chapter 3 "The History and Psychology of a Natural Symbol", pp. 113–114. Written in English (the Encyclopedia.com/IESS bibliography lists it as "First published in English"; reprinted in Collected Works vol. 11, pp. 3–105).
Original: https://archive.org/details/x-psychology-religion (scan of the 1950 printing, djvu text layer: https://archive.org/download/x-psychology-religion/x%20psychology%20religion_djvu.txt) (copyrighted; excerpts only)

> "Notwithstanding the fact that I have often been called a philosopher, I am an empiricist and adhere to the phenomenological standpoint."  (p. 1)

> "As this statement indicates, I approach psychological matters from a scientific and not from a philosophical standpoint."  (p. 2)

> "I restrict myself to the observation of phenomena and I refrain from any application of metaphysical or philosophical considerations. I do not deny the validity of other considerations, but I cannot claim to be competent to apply them correctly."  (p. 2)

> "While having little to do with philosophy, it has much to do with empirical facts"  (p. 2, of psychology)

> "This standpoint is exclusively phenomenological, that is, it is concerned with occurrences, events, experiences, in a word, with facts. Its truth is a fact and not a judgment."  (p. 3)

> "It is psychologically true in as much as it exists."  (p. 3, of the idea of the virgin birth)

> "I should emphasize, perhaps, that we do not establish a metaphysical truth through such formulations. It is merely a statement that the mind functions in such a way."  (p. 113, on the mandala)

> "Concerning the former I have nothing to reply, being no metaphysical expert, but concerning the latter, I must point out that there is no question of belief, but of experience. Religious experience is absolute. It is indisputable."  (p. 113)

Relied on for: Jung's own statement that he is called a philosopher and is an empiricist; his disclaimer of metaphysics; "psychological truth" as existence of an idea (thinkers/jung.md).
Context: the lectures open by saying the Terry Foundation invites science as well as philosophy to discuss religion; the "former" in the p. 113 passage is the rationalist's charge of "driving out the devil by Baalzebub", the "latter" the charge of replacing a neurosis "by the cheat of a religious belief".
