Kārikā numbering per Desnitskaya: "Numeration of the kārikās is given according to Rau’s critical edition." (p. 211, n.)

> "This problem arises in connection with the so-called Unnameability Thesis (the term introduced by H. and R. Herzberger (Herzberger H., and R. Herzberger 1981)). This thesis claims that there is something which is unnameable. An example of such unnameable thing is relation which, being extremely dependent upon the entities it joins, can be defined only through them, but not per se."  (p. 217)

> "As a result, the question naturally arises whether to call something ‘insignifiable’ means to signify it. If yes, it would thus be apprehended as signifiable, otherwise there would be no understanding of the issue spoken about."  (p. 217)

> "These kārikās (VP 3.3.20–21) are well-known as Bhartçhari’s paradox, similar to the Liar paradox in Greek philosophy, and were extensively studied by different scholars (for an exhaustive analysis of these inquiries, see Houben (Houben 1995b) and also a more recent article by Parsons (Parsons 2001) where a formalized study of the paradox is undertaken)."  (p. 217)

> "As Houben (Houben 1995c: 232) points out, “in contrast with most Western treatments of the paradox, for Bhartçhari ‘true or false’ is not an interesting question, but whether or not the speaker succeeds in expressing his point.” Thus, VP 3.3.20–1 can be called a paradox not in the formal logical, but in the etymological sense: something ‘against’ (‘para’) ‘[common] opinion’ (‘dox’) (REPh 1998)."  (p. 217)

> "Therefore, Bhartçhari claims, when something is spoken of as unsignifiable, the very situation of unsignifiability is not prohibited.26 A signifier cannot be the signified thing in the same context (pravçtta). That by means of which something is expressed cannot be expressed at the same time.27"  (p. 217)

> "That by means of which something is expressed cannot be expressed at the same time.27 According to Helārāja, it happens so because of the unidirectional function (vyāpārasyaikatva) of the sense organs on account of which it is impossible to fix attention on the meaning and the proper form of the word simultaneously (Vākyapadīya 1991: 266)."  (p. 217)

> "A very strong objection to the idea of self-reference can be found in the Saübandha-samuddeśa (VP 3.3.26), which claims that a signifier cannot be the signified thing in the same context (pravçtta)."  (p. 217)

> "This is why, in the Saübandha-samuddeśa 28 (VP 3.3.28), Bhartçhari proposes a very advanced and distinguished solution. He claims that during one process no other process is operative, therefore there would be no contradiction or infinite regress.29 That is to say, while expressing the meaning of the word ‘unsignifiable,’ the very process of expressing cannot simultaneously be the object of another activity.30"  (p. 218)

> "Thus, in this kārikā the paradox is eliminated by the recourse to the successfulness of verbal communication."  (p. 218)

> "Therefore, paradoxes appear only in a formal, logical treatment of the language, in which the specific character of different kinds of activity is ignored."  (p. 219)

Sanskrit of the kārikās as Desnitskaya prints them (nn. 25–27, 29; p. 217–218; text of an ancient work, public domain):

> "avācyam iti yad vācyaü tad avācyatayā yadā / vācyam ity avasīyeta vācyam eva tadā bhavet // 3.3.20 //"

> "athāpy avācyam ity evaü na tad vācyaü pratīyate / vivakùitāsya yāvasthā saiva nādhyavasīyate // 3.3.21 //"

> "tathānyathā sarvathā ca yasyāvācyatvam ucyate / tatrāpi naiva sāvasthā taiþ śabdaiþ pratiùidhyate // 3.3.22 //"

> "na ca vācakarūpeõa pravçttasyāsti vācyatā / pratipādyaü na tat tatra yenānyat pratipadyate // 3.3.26 //"

> "vyāpārasyāparo yasmān na vyāpāro ‘sti kaś ca na / virodham anavasthāü vā tasmāt sarvatra nāśrayet // 3.3.28 //"

Relied on for: the statement of the paradox; the term "Unnameability

Context: the paper argues that each controversial question in the
Vākyapadīya reduces to an antinomy of one and many; this passage is its
"et contra" / "Synthesis" example of self-reference of the word, set
against VP 1.51–70 (the word reveals its own form as well as its object).
Footnote 30 quotes Helārāja on 3.3.28 from the 1991 edition, p. 271.
