## C. Callimachus, Hymn I, To Zeus, lines 4–9 and Mair's note

Source: Callimachus, Hymns and Epigrams, translated by A. W. Mair, Loeb
  Classical Library 129, London: William Heinemann, 1921; Hymn I, with the
  translator's footnote, as transcribed by Theoi.
Original: https://www.theoi.com/Text/CallimachusHymns1.html
  (1921 translation, public domain in the US; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (HTML fetched and tag-stripped; text read in the page;
  footnote numbers removed from the hymn line)

> "O Zeus, some say that thou wert born on the hills of Ida; others, O Zeus, say in Arcadia; did these or those, O Father lie? “Cretans are ever liars.” Yea, a tomb, O Lord, for thee the Cretans builded; but thou didst not die, for thou art for ever."  (Hymn I, 4–9)

> "This proverbial saying, attributed to Epimenides, is quoted by St. Paul."  (Mair's note to "Cretans are ever liars")

> "The Cretan legend was that Zeus was a prince who was slain by a wild boar and buried in Crete."  (Mair's next note, on the tomb)

Relied on for: the wording of the saying in Titus, its attribution to
Epimenides by Clement and by Mair, and its use by Callimachus about the
tomb of Zeus.
Context: in Clement the attribution comes in a list of candidates for the
seventh of the seven sages. Mair's note goes on to report Athenodorus of
Eretria's explanation, that Medea, losing a beauty dispute judged by
Idomeneus of Crete, "said, “Cretans are always liars” and cursed them that
they should never speak the truth."
