## Nails & Monoson, "Socrates"

> "He had a reputation for irony, though what that means exactly is controversial; at a minimum, Socrates’s irony consisted in his saying that he knew nothing of importance and wanted to listen to others, yet keeping the upper hand in every discussion."  (§1)

> "He was known for confusing, stinging, and stunning his conversation partners into the unpleasant experience of realizing their own ignorance, a state sometimes superseded by genuine intellectual curiosity."  (§1)

> "Gregory Vlastos (1907–1991) of the analytic."  (§2.2, naming the doyen of the analytic strand of Socrates scholarship)

## Vogt, "Ancient Skepticism"

> "Socrates knows that he does not know about these most important matters (megista; 22d); his interlocutors, it appears, do not know that they lack this knowledge."  (§2.2)

> "In the course of recounting his conversations with others, Socrates says something enigmatic: “About myself I knew that I know nothing” (22d; cf. Fine 2008). Some readers (ancient and modern) take Socrates to profess that he knows nothing. But the context of the dialogue allows us to read his pronouncement as unproblematical. Socrates knows that he does not know about important things."  (§2.2)

> "Socrates’ commitment to reason—examination as the way to find out—inspires the skepticism of the Hellenistic Academy (Cooper 2004b, Vogt 2013)."  (§2.2)

Bibliography entry: "Fine, G. ... 2008, “Does Socrates Claim To Know That He Knows Nothing?”, Oxford Studies in Ancient Philosophy, 35: 49–88."

## Frede & Lee, "Plato's Ethics: An Overview"

> "Nor would such optimism seem warranted, given Socrates’ disavowal of knowledge."  (§2.1)

> "As far as I am concerned, the result is that I know nothing, for when I don’t know what justice is, I’ll hardly know whether it is a kind of virtue or not, or whether a person who has it is happy or unhappy."  (§3.1, quoting Republic 354c in their translation)

## Ichikawa & Steup, "The Analysis of Knowledge"

> "Most epistemologists have found it overwhelmingly plausible that what is false cannot be known."  (§1.1)

> "One can only know things that are true."  (§1.1)
