# Thomas 2017 — what really happened in Y2K (Gresham lecture)

Source: Martyn Thomas, "What Really Happened in Y2K?", Gresham College
  lecture transcript, 4 April 2017.
Original: https://www.gresham.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2017-04-04-MartynThomas_Y2K-T.pdf
  (© Martyn Thomas CBE FREng, 2017; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-09 (PDF through pdftotext, grepped)

> "Then nothing happened, or so it seemed, and the feeling grew that the whole thing had been a myth or a scam invented by rapacious consultants and supported by manufacturers who wanted to compel their customers to throw away perfectly good equipment and buy the latest version."  (Introduction)

> "I led the Y2K services internationally for Deloitte & Touche Consulting Group for several years in the 1990s and later acted as the ultimate auditor of the Y2K programme for the UK National Air Traffic Services, NATS, so I was well placed to see what happened before, during and after the first of January 2000."  (Introduction)

> "Thousands of errors were found and corrected during the 1990s, avoiding failures that would otherwise have occurred."  ("What Failures were prevented?")

> "The reactor’s computers couldn’t recognize the date (1/1/00) and shut down the reactor"  ("What Failures were prevented?", Swedish nuclear plant test, mid-1998)

> "Y2K remediation should be seen as a major success, showing that it is possible to deliver major IT systems on time if they have clear objectives, clear timescales, senior management support, and commitment to provide the necessary resources, clear communications and acceptance throughout the affected organisations that the project has the right objectives and deserves the highest priority."  (conclusions)

> "Unfortunately, little seems to have changed, perhaps because Y2K remediation was so successful and because it is only after a catastrophe has occurred that major reorganisations are brought about in the way an industry operates."  (conclusions)

Relied on for: the case that Y2K remediation prevented failures; Thomas's
role; his examples.
Context: other examples listed are the UK Rapier missile system, BP
Exploration oil platforms, US Coast Guard ships and VISA card terminals;
Thomas does not use the term "preparedness paradox".
