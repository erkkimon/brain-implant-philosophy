> "Green paradox: Policies intending to reduce future CO2 emissions may lead to increased emissions in the present."  (List of paradoxes, "Decision theory")

> "The Green Paradox is a controversial book by German economist, Hans-Werner Sinn, describing the observation that an environmental policy that becomes greener with the passage of time acts like an announced expropriation for the owners of fossil fuel resources, inducing them to accelerate resource extraction and hence to accelerate global warming."  (The Green Paradox, lead)

> "That is the green paradox: environmental policy slated to become greener over time acts as an announced expropriation that provokes owners to react by accelerating the rate of extraction of their fossil fuel stocks, thus accelerating climate change."  ("Main line of reasoning"; cites Long 1975, JET 10, and Konrad, Olson & Schöb 1994, JEEM 26)

> "Sinn emphasises that a condition for the green paradox is that the resource be scarce in the sense that its price will always be higher than the unit extraction and exploration costs combined."  ("Main line of reasoning"; no citation on the sentence)

> "They build on his earlier studies on supply reactions of the owners of natural resources to announced price changes."  ("Works on the subject"; cites Sinn 1982 and Long & Sinn 1985)
