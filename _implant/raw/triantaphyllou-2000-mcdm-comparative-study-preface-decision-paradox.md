> "In most cases the authors and supporters of these methods have identified some weaknesses of the previous methods and then they propose a new method claiming to be the best method."  (Preface, p. xxvi)

> "Although the final goal of determining the best method seems to be unattainable and utopian, some useful lessons have been learned in the process and are presented here in a comprehensive and systematic manner."  (Preface, p. xxvi)

> "What became clear very soon is that there is no single method which outperforms all the other methods in all aspects."  (Preface, p. xxvi)

> "However, for one to answer the problem of which is the best MCDM method, he/she will first need to use the best MCDM method! Thus, a decision paradox is reached."  (Preface, p. xxvi)

> "This is the main reason why a comparative approach is needed in dealing with MCDM methods."  (Preface, p. xxvi)

> "Some of the findings of these comparative analyses are so startling and counter intuitive, that are presented as decision making paradoxes."  (Preface, p. xxvii)

> "Therefore, the question “Which is the best method for a given problem?” has become one of the most important but also most difficult to answer."  (Foreword, Zimmermann, p. xxiii)

> "Rather than suggesting another MCDM method without any convincing justification, he concentrates on the best known and most frequently used methods."  (Foreword, Zimmermann, p. xxiii)

Relied on for: the book's own statement of the decision paradox (the
  regress of choosing a method with a method), the author's assessment that
  the goal "seems to be unattainable", the comparative approach he draws
  from it, and Zimmermann's framing of the question in the foreword.
Context: The preface surrounding the quotations describes the author's
  research since graduate school at Penn State, the "plethora of
  alternative methods for solving the same MCDM problem", and a methodology
  of evaluative criteria and simulated test problems ("hundreds of
  thousands or millions"). The book chapters themselves were not read.
