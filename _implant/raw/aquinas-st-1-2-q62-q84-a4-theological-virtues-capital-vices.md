> "Such like principles are called "theological virtues": first, because their object is God, inasmuch as they direct us aright to God: secondly, because they are infused in us by God alone: thirdly, because these virtues are not made known to us, save by Divine revelation, contained in Holy Writ."  (I-II q.62 a.1 co.)

> "On the contrary, The precepts of the Law are about acts of virtue. Now the Divine Law contains precepts about the acts of faith, hope, and charity:"  (I-II q.62 a.1 s.c.)

> "Wherefore the theological virtues are specifically distinct from the moral and intellectual virtues."  (I-II q.62 a.2 co.)

> "On the contrary, The Apostle says (1 Cor. 13:13): "Now there remain faith, hope, charity, these three.""  (I-II q.62 a.3 s.c.)

> "Objection 1: It would seem that we ought not to reckon seven capital vices, viz. vainglory, envy, anger, sloth, covetousness, gluttony, lust."  (I-II q.84 a.4 obj.1)

> "On the contrary, stands the authority of Gregory who enumerates them in this way (Moral. xxxi, 17)."  (I-II q.84 a.4 s.c.)

> "As stated above (Article [3]), the capital vices are those which give rise to others, especially by way of final cause."  (I-II q.84 a.4 co.)

> "Wherefore pride, like a universal vice, is not counted along with the others, but is reckoned as the "queen of them all," as Gregory states (Moral. xxxi, 27)."  (I-II q.84 a.4 ad 4)
