# Agüera y Arcas 2022 — "Do Large Language Models Understand Us?" (with the Lemoine/LaMDA report)

## A. Agüera y Arcas, Daedalus

Source: Blaise Agüera y Arcas (2022), "Do Large Language Models
  Understand Us?", Daedalus 151(2): 183–197 (Spring 2022 issue "AI &
  Society"); the abstract as registered with Crossref by MIT Press.
Original: https://doi.org/10.1162/daed_a_01909
  (CC BY-NC 4.0 per the Crossref licence record; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (DOI and abstract verified via
  https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1162/daed_a_01909; the article body
  at direct.mit.edu and amacad.org returned HTTP 403 to every fetch
  method available and was NOT read — no body quotations)

> "It is sometimes claimed, though, that machine learning is 'just
>  statistics,' hence that, in this grander ambition, progress in AI is
>  illusory. Here I take the contrary view that LLMs have a great deal to
>  teach us about the nature of language, understanding, intelligence,
>  sociality, and personhood. Specifically: statistics do amount to
>  understanding, in any falsifiable sense."  (Abstract)

> "Complex sequence learning and social interaction may be a sufficient
>  basis for general intelligence, including theory of mind and
>  consciousness. Since the interior state of another being can only be
>  understood through interaction, no objective answer is possible to the
>  question of when an 'it' becomes a 'who,' but for many people, neural
>  nets running on computers are likely to cross this threshold in the
>  very near future."  (Abstract)

## B. The LaMDA episode (news report, bibliographic)

Source: Nitasha Tiku (2022), "The Google engineer who thinks the
  company's AI has come to life", The Washington Post, 11 June 2022.
Original: https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2022/06/11/google-ai-lamda-blake-lemoine/
  (copyrighted; excerpt only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (page reached via a proxied fetch; only the byline
  and opening paragraph were returned, the rest of the body was not
  rendered)

> "Google engineer Blake Lemoine opened his laptop to the interface for
>  LaMDA, Google's artificially intelligent chatbot generator, and began
>  to type."  (opening sentence)

Google's response as quoted from this report by Chalmers (2023,
arXiv:2303.07103, §1, attributed there to "Washington Post, June 11,
2022"): "Our team has reviewed Blake's concerns and has informed him the
evidence doesn't support his claims. He was told there was no evidence
that LaMDA was sentient (and lots of evidence against it)." — this is a
second-hand quotation; the Post text itself was not read in full.

Relied on for: the position of a Google researcher that statistical
sequence learning "may be a sufficient basis" for consciousness, stated
in the same year that Google dismissed Lemoine's sentience claim; the
date and place of the Lemoine report.
Context: Agüera y Arcas was at the time a Google VP working on the LaMDA
family; the Daedalus issue was edited by James Manyika. The article
argues from dialogue with LaMDA and is a first-person essay, not an
empirical study. The abstract's claim "statistics do amount to
understanding" is the direct opposite of Bender & Koller 2020's "a system
trained only on form has a priori no way to learn meaning".
