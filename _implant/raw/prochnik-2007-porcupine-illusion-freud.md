# Prochnik 2007 — "The Porcupine Illusion": Freud, Schopenhauer and the porcupine

Source: George Prochnik, "The Porcupine Illusion: Freud's prickly secret",
*Cabinet* 26 ("Magic"), Summer 2007.
Original: https://www.cabinetmagazine.org/issues/26/prochnik.php
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-08 (HTML via curl; grep)

> "Sometime in the summer of 1909, not long before Sigmund Freud was due to embark on his only visit to the United States, he was enjoying a cigar in the company of his inner circle in the busy Biedermeier interior of Berggasse 19, when he suddenly announced, “I am going to America to catch sight of a wild porcupine and to give some lectures.”"

> "Thereafter, his disciple Ernst Jones reported, “The phrase, ‘to find one’s porcupine,’ became a recognized saying in our circle.”"

> "Schopenhauer’s tale was later quoted by Freud in a footnote to his 1921 essay Group Psychology and the Analysis of the Ego, where it was invoked to illustrate what Freud called the “sediment of feelings of aversion and hostility” adhering to any long-lasting human relationship."

> "One could say that the dilemma of the porcupine, as rendered by Schopenhauer, is the Freudian relationship problematic as such."

> "Freud steadfastly denied having read Schopenhauer before he was in his sixties, writing in 1925 that he came to the philosopher’s work “very late in life.”"

Relied on for: the 1909 porcupine remark (as Prochnik reports it, via
Jones), Prochnik's reading of the parable as Freud's "relationship
problematic", and Freud's 1925 statement as Prochnik reports it.
Context: an essay, not a scholarly edition; Prochnik goes on to speculate
on why Freud did not cite Schopenhauer as the source of his porcupine
remark. Excerpts are quoted with the article's own spelling ("Ernst Jones").
