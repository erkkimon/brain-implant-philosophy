> "In Vagueness and Contradiction (2001), Roy Sorensen defends and extends his epistemic account of vagueness."  (p. 695)

> "These appeals come mainly in Chapter 11, where Sorensen offers a solution to what he calls the no-no paradox—a “neglected cousin” of the more famous liar—and attempts to use this solution as a precedent for an epistemic account of the sorites paradox."  (p. 695)

> "The original source for this case is Jean Buridan’s Eighth Sophism from Chapter 8 of Sophismata."  (p. 695, n. 1, citing Hughes 1982)

> "while the open pair is pathological, it is not paradoxical because it does not force inconsistency."  (p. 695, n. 1)

> "It is widely appreciated that this phenomenon bifurcates into two symptoms: inconsistency, as manifested in liar sentences (e.g., ‘This sentence is false’), and indeterminacy, as manifested in truth-teller sentences (e.g., ‘This sentence is true’)."  (p. 695)

> "In fact, this case will manifest either symptom of resistance, depending on what we say about these sentences."  (p. 696)

> "(1) and (2) together yield inconsistency, if we ascribe them matching truth-values. But inconsistency is not inevitable here; we can avoid it by claiming one of these sentences is true and the other false. This, however, yields indeterminacy, as there are two ways of ascribing divergent truth-values, and nothing appears to favor one over the other."  (p. 696; the open pair is "(1) (2) is false / (2) (1) is false.")

> "Goldstein attempts to resolve the semantic pathology of (1) and (2) by arguing that they are gappy, i.e., without truth-values."  (p. 696, on Goldstein 1992)

> "maintains that (1) and (2) have determinate, consistent truth-values, though which truth-value each sentence has is epistemically indeterminate."  (p. 696, on Sorensen, who aims to minimize “truth-bearer illusions” (p. 183))

> "(DA) If each statement in [the open pair] has a unique truth-value, then each has the opposite value of the other."  (p. 697)

> "(SA) If each statement in [the open pair] has a unique truth-value, then each has the same value as the other."  (p. 697)

> "As Goldstein rejects inconsistency, he concludes that the antecedent of both (DA) and (SA) is false and thus that each sentence suffers from a truth-value gap."  (p. 697)

> "(5) (6) is false (6) (6) is false → (5) is false."  (p. 698, the "asymmetric open pair"; displayed on two lines in the original)

> "where a truthmaker gap obtains when a true sentence is not made true by anything else in the world."  (p. 698, on Sorensen)

> "(7) (8) has no truthmaker (8) (7) has no truthmaker."  (p. 699, the revenge pair; displayed on two lines)

> "As this approach already denies the apparent force of symmetry, the version of the open pair that thwarts Goldstein’s reliance on (SA) poses no challenge for Sorensen."  (p. 699)

> "We take this case to form a version of what we call the curried open pair, on the model of Curry’s paradox."  (p. 700, n. 11)

> "Here Sorensen notes, “Symmetry precludes one from being true while the other is false” (p. 228)."  (p. 700, n. 10, on Sorensen 2003, "A Definite No-No")

> "Sorensen does not meet this condition of adequacy, thereby leaving the semantic pathology of the open pair both undiagnosed and untreated."  (p. 701)

> "Priest, in his contribution to this symposium, argues that the open pair requires a dialetheic resolution."  (p. 702)

> "maintaining that (1) and (2) have different semantic properties is “a manifest a priori repugnance” (Priest (2004), p. 6)."  (p. 702, reporting Priest)

> "For, although the dialetheist can ascribe the same truth-value to (5) and (6), (9) and (10), or (13) and (14), he can also consistently ascribe them divergent truth-values, with no apparent reason for favoring any matching or divergent assignment over any other."  (p. 702)

> "“the truthmaker gap solution to the [open pair] is a precedent for an epistemic solution to the sorites paradox” (p. 176)."  (p. 702, quoting Sorensen 2001)

> "Priest (2004), fn. 3 argues that an ascription of gaps leads to an assignment of both gaps and gluts, so it is simpler, methodologically speaking, just to go with gluts."  (p. 702, n. 12)

> "While Priest rejects Sorensen’s approach to the open pair, he agrees with Sorensen that the sorites and the open pair are of a piece."  (p. 702, n. 13)
