> "an infraction of a command committed in ignorance of the existence or meaning of that command"  (introduction, of ḥeṭ, "the lightest")

> "a breach of a minor commandment committed with a full knowledge of the existence and nature of that commandment"  (introduction, of 'awon, "the second kind")

> "a presumptuous and rebellious act against God"  (introduction, of pesha' or mered, "the gravest kind")

> "The Talmudic word "'aberah" carries the idea of trespass, transgression, and includes both sin and crime."  (Various Sins)

> "Man is responsible for sin because he is endowed with free will"  (Original Sin)

> "the majority, however, do not hold Adam responsible for the sins of mankind"  (Original Sin)

Relied on for: the three grades ḥeṭ / 'awon / pesha' as the article
  states them, with the Yom Kippur confession order (Yoma 36b) and
  Ps. 106:6; the term 'aberah covering sin and crime; free will
  (beḥirah); the division among Jewish theologians on Adam's sin.
Context: The article defines sin "Under the Jewish theocracy" as wilful
  disregard of the positive or infraction of the negative commands "as
  proclaimed by Moses and interpreted by the Rabbis", and confines itself
  to sins against God. It adds that some Jewish theologians teach that
  the tendency to evil was inherited from Adam's yielding to temptation;
  "the majority, however," do not. It cites Gen. 8:21 ("the imagination
  of man's heart is evil from his youth"). Bibliography: Hehn 1903;
  Köberle 1905.
