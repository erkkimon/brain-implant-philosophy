# Jebb 1900 — commentary on Sophocles, Antigone 74: "ὀξύμωρον (a paradox with a point)"

Source: Sir Richard C. Jebb, Sophocles: The Plays and Fragments, Part III:
  The Antigone (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1900), commentary on
  line 74 (ὅσια πανουργήσασ᾽).
Original: Perseus Digital Library, Perseus:text:1999.04.0023,
  https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.04.0023:text=comm:commline=74
  (public domain)
Retrieved: 2026-09-28 (HTML fetched; note located by grep)

> "The phrase is an “ ὀξύμωρον ” (a paradox with a point), like ‘splendide
> mendax’; i.e. the qualification (“ ὅσια ”) seems contrary to the essence
> of the thing qualified. Cp. Milton ( Tetrachordon ), ‘Men of the most
> renowned virtue have sometimes by transgressing most truly kept the
> law’; which is not an oxymoron, because the words, ‘most truly,’ suggest
> an explanation by showing that ‘kept’ is not used in its ordinary sense."  (on Ant. 74)

Relied on for: Jebb's definition ("a paradox with a point"; the
qualification "seems contrary to the essence of the thing qualified");
his calling Horace's "splendide mendax" an oxymoron; his criterion for
what is not one (a phrase that itself explains the contrast).
Context: Antigone says she will lie with her brother "having committed a
holy crime" (ὅσια πανουργήσασ᾽); the note begins "having broken a human
law in a manner which the gods permit". Wiktionary's usage note cites
this note for "a paradox with a point".
