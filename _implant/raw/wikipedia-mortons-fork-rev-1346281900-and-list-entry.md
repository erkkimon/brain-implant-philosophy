> "Morton's fork: a type of false dilemma in which contradictory observations lead to the same conclusion."  (List of paradoxes, "Decision theory")

> "A Morton's fork is a type of false dilemma in which contradictory observations lead to the same conclusion. Its name refers to the rationalising of a benevolence by the 15th century English prelate John Morton."  ("Morton's fork", lead)

> "The term in its broad application dates at least to the mid-19th century, although Francis Bacon noted in 1622 that it was established by that point in specific reference to Morton's original argument."  (lead; cites the Oxford Dictionary of Phrase and Fable via Encyclopedia.com)

> "The phrase "Morton's fork" may have been coined by another of Henry's supporters, Richard Foxe."  ("Morton's argument"; cites S. B. Chrimes, Henry VII, Yale University Press, ISBN 978-0-300-21294-5, p. 203)

> ""Morton's fork coup" is a manoeuvre in the game of bridge that uses the principle of Morton's fork."  ("Other uses"; cites Frey et al. 1976, The Official Encyclopedia of Bridge, p. 295, ISBN 0-517-52724-3, and Robert Gray, The Bridge World, March 1973)
