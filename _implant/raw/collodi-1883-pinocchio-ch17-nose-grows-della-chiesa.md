# Collodi 1883 — The Adventures of Pinocchio, ch. 17: the nose grows at each lie (Della Chiesa translation)

Source: Carlo Collodi, The Adventures of Pinocchio, chapter 17, translated
  from the Italian by Carol Della Chiesa.
Original: Project Gutenberg eBook #500 (release 2006-01-12),
  https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/500 (public domain in the USA per
  Project Gutenberg)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (plain text https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/500/pg500.txt)

> "“I lost them,” answered Pinocchio, but he told a lie, for he had them in his pocket."  (ch. 17)

> "As he spoke, his nose, long though it was, became at least two inches longer."  (ch. 17)

> "At this second lie, his nose grew a few more inches."  (ch. 17)

> "“Lies, my boy, are known in a moment. There are two kinds of lies, lies with short legs and lies with long noses. Yours, just now, happen to have long noses.”"  (ch. 17, the Fairy)

Relied on for: the fictional source of the premise that Pinocchio's nose
grows when he lies.
Context: the Fairy asks where the gold pieces are; Pinocchio lies three
times and his nose lengthens each time. The novel states that the nose
grows at lies; it does not state the converse (that it grows only at lies).
