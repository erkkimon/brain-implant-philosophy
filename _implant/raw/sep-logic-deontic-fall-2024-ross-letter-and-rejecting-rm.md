# SEP "Deontic Logic" (McNamara & Van De Putte) — the letter example and the RM-rejecting approaches

Source: Paul McNamara and Frederik Van De Putte, "Deontic Logic", The
  Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2024 Edition), Edward N. Zalta
  & Uri Nodelman (eds.); first published 2006-02-07, substantive revision
  2021-03-11. §6.3 "Rejecting Core Principles of SDL: OB-NEC and OB-RM",
  and the bibliography.
Original: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/logic-deontic/
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-01 (HTML of the Fall 2024 archive page read; SEP's
  LaTeX macro \OB rendered as "OB", \vee as "∨")

> "“It is obligatory that the letter is mailed” formalized as OB m, we can derive: (2) “It is obligatory that the letter is mailed (m) or the letter is burned (b).”"  (§6.3; numbering (1) dropped)

> "While weakening from m to m ∨ b gives problems, similar issues arise when we weaken a conjunction to one of its conjuncts."  (§6.3, introducing the Good Samaritan, Prior 1958)

> "It seems rather odd to say that an obligation to mail the letter entails an obligation to mail the letter or burn it (where burning the letter is presumably forbidden), one that can be fulfilled by burning the letter, which invites from the offender: “Well at least I did one thing right by burning it”."  (§6.3)

> "Two accessible and closely related examples of approaches to deontic logic that reject OB-RM from a principled philosophical perspective are Jackson 1985 and Goble 1990a."  (§6.3)

> "A third, somewhat different principled strategy is proposed in Cariani 2013 and studied in formal detail in Van De Putte 2019."  (§6.3)

> "He also sees OB-RM as the main culprit in the paradoxes of standard deontic logic, and thus he methodically explores non-standard frameworks where OB-RM is not"  (§6.3, of S. O. Hansson 1990, 2001; sentence continues)
