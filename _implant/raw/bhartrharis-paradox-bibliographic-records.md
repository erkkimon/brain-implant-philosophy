> "In Chapter 5 it transpired that certain states of affairs are ineffable; but, of course, much is said about them. This chapter shows how such a paradox can be handled with a plurivalent version of the five-valued cateṣkoṭi, in which states of affairs can have more than one value. That some Buddhist texts appear to endorse the thought that something can be effable and ineffable is then shown by a consideration of the Vimalakīrti Sūtra."
