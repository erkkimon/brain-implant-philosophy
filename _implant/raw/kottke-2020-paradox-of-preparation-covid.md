# Kottke 2020 — "The Paradox of Preparation" (COVID-19 commentary roundup)

Source: Jason Kottke, "The Paradox of Preparation", kottke.org, March 16,
  2020, quoting Chris Hayes, James Hamblin, Mari Armstrong-Hough, Vaughn Tan
  and Ian Bogost (The Atlantic).
Original: https://kottke.org/20/03/the-paradox-of-preparation
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-09 (HTML fetched and grepped)

> "The paradox of preparation refers to how preventative measures can intuitively seem like a waste of time both before and after the fact."  (Kottke)

> "A doctor I spoke to today called this the “paradox of preparation” and it’s the key dynamic in all this."  (Chris Hayes, quoted)

> "The thing is if shutdowns and social distancing work perfectly and are extremely effective it will seem in retrospect like they were totally unnecessary overreactions."  (James Hamblin, quoted)

> "When the best way to save lives is to prevent a disease rather than treat it, success often looks like an overreaction."  (Mari Armstrong-Hough, epidemiologist, quoted)

> "This means that any effective actions taken against coronavirus in the few days before the epidemic curve shoots upward in any country will always look unreasonable and disproportionate."  (Vaughn Tan, quoted)

> "Ultimately, overreaction is a matter of knowledge-an epistemological problem."  (Ian Bogost, "Now Is the Time to Overreact", The Atlantic, quoted)
