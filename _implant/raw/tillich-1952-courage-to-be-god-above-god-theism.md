# Tillich 1952 — The Courage to Be: courage as ethical and ontological, the "God above God", theism transcended, Kierkegaard

Source: Paul Tillich, *The Courage to Be* (New Haven: Yale University Press, 1952; Terry Lectures), pp. 1, 125, 181, 182, 185, 186, 187 (page numbers as printed in the scanned Yale reprint).
Original: Yale University Press; PDF of a Yale reprint at https://psychxspirit.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Paul-Tillich-The-Courage-to-Be-Yale-University-Press-2000.pdf ; library scan https://archive.org/details/couragetobe00till
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-08 (PDF downloaded, pdftotext, grepped; page numbers from the printed page numbers in the text layer)

> "Courage is an ethical reality, but it is rooted in the whole breadth of human existence and ultimately in the structure of being itself. It must be considered ontologically in order to be understood ethically."  (p. 1)

> "When Kierkegaard broke away from Hegel's system of essences he did two things: he proclaimed an existential attitude and he instigated a philosophy of existence."  (p. 125)

> "There are no valid arguments for the "existence" of God, but there are acts of courage in which we affirm the power of being, whether we know it or not."  (p. 181)

> "The content of absolute faith is the "God above God." Absolute faith and its consequence, the courage that takes the radical doubt, the doubt about God, into itself, transcends the theistic idea of God."  (p. 182)

> "This is the God Nietzsche said had to be killed because nobody can tolerate being made into a mere object of absolute knowledge and absolute control. This is the deepest root of atheism."  (p. 185)

> "The ultimate source of the courage to be is the "God above God"; this is the result of our demand to transcend theism."  (p. 186)

> "The God above the God of theism is present, although hidden, in every divine-human encounter."  (p. 187)

Relied on for: courage as an ethical reality understood ontologically; the "God above God"; Tillich's account of theological theism and of Nietzsche's dead God; his reading of Kierkegaard against Hegel.
Context: p. 1 opens the Terry Lectures with Plato's *Laches*. pp. 182–190 form the last chapter, "Courage and Transcendence", sections "Theism transcended" (p. 182) and "The God above God and the courage to be" (p. 186). p. 185 describes "theological theism", whose God "deprives me of my subjectivity"; the passage continues that this atheism "is justified as the reaction against theological theism and its disturbing implications." p. 125 is in "Courage and Individualization", on existentialism.
