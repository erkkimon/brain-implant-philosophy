> "This response led Jonathan Haidt to design a study in which he explored people’s reactions to what he characterized as “harmless taboo violations”, (Haidt 2012: 19)."  (Machery & Stich §7)

> "Haidt and colleagues had collected information about the socio-economic status (SES) of their participants, and they found that lower SES groups “moralized” more than higher SES groups in all three cities, and that the effect of SES was much larger than the effect of location."  (Machery & Stich §7)

> "When Royzman et al. repeated Nichols’ spitting in the water glass experiment and explicitly asked “was anyone negatively affected by [the protagonist’s] action?” 71% of their participants judged that other guests at the diner table were negatively affected"  (Machery & Stich §9)

> "Royzman and colleagues conclude that we should not assume that the sort of socioeconomic differences that Haidt found in his moral/conventional task studies are due to fundamentally divergent values."  (Machery & Stich §9)

> "Much more work is needed before accepting this conclusion, and careful skepticism is warranted"  (Machery & Stich §9, on Royzman, Landy & Goodwin 2014)

> "When asked to give reasons for our moral intuitions, we are often “dumbfounded,” finding nothing to say in their defense (Haidt 2001)."  (Richardson §1.2)
