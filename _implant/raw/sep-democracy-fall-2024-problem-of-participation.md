§4.1:
> "Some have argued that it is not rational to vote since the chances that an individual’s vote will a decide the outcome of an election (i.e., will determine whether a candidate gets elected or not) are nearly indistinguishable from zero."
> "For example, one widely accepted estimate puts the odds of an individual casting the deciding vote in a United States presidential election at 1 in 100 million."
> "Worse still, Anthony Downs has argued that almost all of those who do vote have little reason to become informed about how best to vote (Downs 1957: ch.13)."
> "These observations pose challenges for any robustly egalitarian or deliberative conception of democracy."

Relied on for: the stakes of the problem for democratic theory, as these
  authors state them; Downs's rational-ignorance argument (ch. 13).
Context: the paragraph sits among "criticisms Plato and Hobbes made" of
  mass participation; §4.2 then sets out elite, interest-group and
  deliberative responses. The "1 in 100 million" estimate is not sourced
  in the sentence.
