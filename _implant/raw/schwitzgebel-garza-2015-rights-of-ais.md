# Schwitzgebel & Garza 2015 — the No-Relevant-Difference argument for AI rights

Source: Eric Schwitzgebel & Mara Garza (2015), "A Defense of the Rights of
  Artificial Intelligences", Midwest Studies in Philosophy 39(1): 98–119
  (September 2015); abstract and section 1 "The No-Relevant-Difference
  Argument".
Original: https://doi.org/10.1111/misp.12032
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (DOI verified by content negotiation; wording read in
  the authors' penultimate draft of 15 September 2015 at
  https://faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzPapers/AIRights-150915.htm —
  page numbers of the published version not available from that copy)

> "There are possible artificially intelligent beings who do not differ in
>  any morally relevant respect from human beings. Such possible beings
>  would deserve moral consideration similar to that of human beings."
>  (Abstract)

> "Premise 1. If Entity A deserves some particular degree of moral
>  consideration and Entity B does not deserve that same degree of moral
>  consideration, there must be some relevant difference between the two
>  entities that grounds this difference in moral status. Premise 2. There
>  are possible AIs who do not differ in any such relevant respects from
>  human beings. Conclusion. Therefore, there are possible AIs who deserve
>  a degree of moral consideration similar to that of human beings."
>  (section 1, penultimate draft)

> "Given our moral obligations to such AIs, two principles for ethical AI
>  design recommend themselves: (1) design AIs that tend to provoke
>  reactions from users that accurately reflect the AIs' real moral status,
>  and (2) avoid designing AIs whose moral status is unclear."  (Abstract)

Relied on for: the earliest widely cited philosophical argument that some
possible AIs would have moral status equal to humans; the "Emotional
Alignment Design Policy" and the recommendation to avoid building systems
of unclear moral status — the recommendation later AI-welfare papers
(Long, Sebo et al. 2024) build on.
Context: the paper's argument is about *possible* AIs, not existing
systems; the authors explicitly do not claim any present AI has moral
status. The draft's apostrophes are lost in the HTML copy and have been
restored in "AIs'". Section 1 goes on to consider objections to each
premise; sections on our special obligations as creators and on the
"destabilization" of ethics are not reproduced.
