> "Pinocchio’s nose grew just when he was stating something untrue (the Pinocchio principle). One day Pinocchio was beguiled by the dark side of logical force into saying ‘My nose is growing’ (the Pinocchio statement)."  (repository abstract)

## B. "Pinocchio against the Semantic Hierarchies" (2018)

Source: Peter Eldridge-Smith, "Pinocchio against the Semantic Hierarchies",
  Philosophia 46(4), December 2018, pp. 817–830.
Original: doi:10.1007/s11406-018-9948-y (verified by DOI content
  negotiation); repository record http://hdl.handle.net/1885/159323
  (abstract only; full text returned 401) (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-10-02 (abstract from the ANU repository REST API metadata)

> "The Liar paradox is an obstacle to a theory of truth, but a Liar sentence need not contain a semantic predicate. The Pinocchio paradox, devised by Veronique Eldridge-Smith, was the first published paradox to show this."  (abstract)

> "Pinocchio’s nose grows if, and only if, what Pinocchio is saying is untrue (the Pinocchio principle). What happens if Pinocchio says that his nose is growing?"  (abstract)

> "Eldridge-Smith and Eldridge-Smith (Analysis, 70(2): 212-5, 2010) posed the Pinocchio paradox against the Tarskian-Kripkean solutions to the Liar paradox that use language hierarchies."  (abstract)

> "Eldridge-Smith (Analysis, 71(2): 306-8, 2011) also set the Pinocchio paradox against semantic dialetheic solutions to the Liar. Beall (2011) argued the Pinocchio story was just an impossible story."  (abstract)

> "Eldridge-Smith (Analysis, 72(3): 749-752, 2012b) responded that unless the T-schema is a necessary truth of some sort (logical, metaphysical or analytic), the Pinocchio principle is possible."  (abstract)

> "Luna (Mind & Matter 14(1): 77–86, 2016) argues that the Pinocchio contradiction proves the principle is false."  (abstract)

> "D’Agostini & Ficara (2016) discuss a more plausible physical truth-tracking trait, the Blushing Liar, and argue that the Pinocchio contradiction is not a metaphysical dialetheia."  (abstract)

> "I respond to Luna, and D’Agostini & Ficara, and prove that the Pinocchio paradox is a counterexample to hierarchical solutions to the Liar."  (abstract)

Relied on for: the claim that a liar sentence need not contain a semantic
predicate; the 2010 target (Tarskian-Kripkean hierarchies); the exchange
with Beall (2011); the 2012 reply on the T-schema; Luna 2016 and
D'Agostini & Ficara 2016 as reported by Eldridge-Smith.
Context: the quotations above cover the whole abstract; it is the
author's own summary of the debate, not an independent survey. Luna 2016
and D'Agostini & Ficara 2016 were not read and not located by DOI.
