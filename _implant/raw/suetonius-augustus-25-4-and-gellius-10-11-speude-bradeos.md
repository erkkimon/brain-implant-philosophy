# Suetonius, Augustus 25.4 and Gellius 10.11.5 — σπεῦδε βραδέως ("make haste slowly")

Source: C. Suetonius Tranquillus, De vita Caesarum, Divus Augustus 25.4,
  Latin/Greek text and J. C. Rolfe's English (Loeb Classical Library,
  1913); A. Gellius, Noctes Atticae 10.11.5, Rolfe's English (Loeb, vol. II,
  1927).

> "Nihil autem minus perfecto duci quam festinationem temeritatemque
> convenire arbitrabatur. Crebro itaque illa iactabat: σπεῦδε βραδέως·
> ἀσφαλὴς γάρ ἐστ’ ἀμείνων ἢ θρασὺς στρατηλάτης. et: "sat celeriter fieri
> quidquid fiat satis bene.""  (Aug. 25.4, Latin)

> "He thought nothing less becoming in a well-trained leader than haste and
> rashness, and, accordingly, favourite sayings of his were: "More haste,
> less speed"; "Better a safe commander than a bold"; and "That is done
> quickly enough which is done well enough.""  (Aug. 25.4, Rolfe 1913)

> "the deified Augustus with two Greek words; for we are told that he used
> to say in conversation, and write in his letters, σπεῦδε βραδέως, that
> is, "make haste slowly," by which he recommended that to accomplish a
> result we should use at once the promptness of energy and the delay of
> carefulness, and it is from these two opposite qualities that maturitas
> springs."  (Gellius 10.11.5, Rolfe 1927)

Relied on for: the example row σπεῦδε βραδέως / festina lente, its
attribution to Augustus, and Gellius's account of what the two "opposite
qualities" combine into.
Context: Suetonius is listing Augustus's military maxims; Gellius is
discussing the word "mature". The Latin form "festina lente" is not in
either text; Wiktionary's entry "festina lente" gives it as a calque of
σπεῦδε βραδέως and calls it "an oxymoron equivalent to" "more haste,
less speed" (wiktionary-festina-lente-and-jumbo-shrimp.md).
