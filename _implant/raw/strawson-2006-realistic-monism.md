# Strawson 2006 — "real physicalism" entails panpsychism

Source: Galen Strawson (2006), "Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails
  Panpsychism", Journal of Consciousness Studies 13(10–11): 3–31; section
  1 "Physicalism" (p. 3), the statement of thesis [NE] (p. 11), and the
  conclusion (pp. 25–26). Reprinted in Strawson, *Real Materialism and
  Other Essays* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 2008), pp. 53–74,
  doi 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199267422.003.0003.
Original: Journal of Consciousness Studies 13(10–11), 2006 (Imprint
  Academic; no DOI registered for the JCS article — the DOI above is the
  2008 book chapter, verified via Crossref)
  (copyrighted; excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (read in the publisher's PDF of the JCS article,
  mirrored at https://pdf.stafforini.com/strawson-2006-realistic-monism-why.pdf;
  page numbers are the journal's)

> "I take physicalism to be the view that every real, concrete phenomenon
>  in the universe is … physical. It is a view about the actual universe,
>  and I am going to assume that it is true."  (p. 3)

> "You're certainly not a realistic physicalist, you're not a real
>  physicalist, if you deny the existence of the phenomenon whose existence
>  is more certain than the existence of anything else: experience,
>  'consciousness', conscious experience, 'phenomenology', experiential
>  'what-it's-likeness', feeling, sensation, explicit conscious thought as
>  we have it and know it at almost every waking moment."  (p. 3)

The thesis Strawson attributes to most self-described physicalists and
argues they must give up:

> "[NE] physical stuff is, in itself, in its fundamental nature, something
>  wholly and utterly non-experiential."  (p. 11)

> "I conclude that real physicalists must give up NE. Real physicalists
>  must accept that at least some ultimates are intrinsically
>  experience-involving. They must at least embrace micropsychism."
>  (p. 25)

> "So now I can say that physicalism, i.e. real physicalism, entails
>  panexperientialism or panpsychism. All physical stuff is energy, in one
>  form or another, and all energy, I trow, is an experience-involving
>  phenomenon. This sounded crazy to me for a long time, but I am quite
>  used to it now that I know that there is no alternative short of
>  'substance dualism', a view for which (as Arnauld saw) there has never
>  been any good argument."  (pp. 25–26)

Relied on for: the thesis sentence of the paper that revived panpsychism in
analytic philosophy; the structure of the argument (realism about
experience + physicalism + rejection of "brute emergence" → micropsychism
→ panpsychism); the "physicSalism" distinction.
Context: the paper distinguishes "real physicalism" from "physicSalism"
(the view that physics can capture the nature of all concrete reality,
p. 4), argues at length (pp. 12–21) that experiential phenomena cannot
"brutely" emerge from wholly non-experiential ones, and concedes that
micropsychism is "not yet panpsychism" before assuming the two coincide
(p. 25). It was a JCS target article with replies from Carruthers, Goff,
Papineau, Seager, Skrbina and others in the same issue, not reproduced.
