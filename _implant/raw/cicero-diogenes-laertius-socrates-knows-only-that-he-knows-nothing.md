## Cicero, Academica 1.16 (Varro speaking, on Socrates)

> "nihil se scire dicat nisi id ipsum, eoque praestare ceteris, quod illi quae nesciant scire se putent, ipse se nihil scire id unum sciat"  (Latin Library, Academica book 1, ch. [4])

> "He says that he knows nothing, except that one fact, that he is ignorant; and that he is superior to others in this particular, that they believe that they do know what they do not, while he knows this one thing alone, that he knows nothing."  (Yonge, Academic Questions I, ch. I)

> "And it is on that account that he imagines he was pronounced by Apollo the wisest of all men, because this alone is the whole of wisdom, for a man not to think that he knows what he does not know."  (Yonge, same place)

## Cicero, Academica 1.45 (Cicero speaking, on Arcesilaus)

> "itaque Arcesilas negabat esse quicquam quod sciri posset, ne illud quidem ipsum quod Socrates sibi reliquisset, ut nihil scire se sciret"  (Latin Library, book 1, ch. [12])

> "therefore Arcesilas asserted that there was nothing which could be known, not even that very piece of knowledge which Socrates had left himself."  (Yonge, Academic Questions I, ch. XII)

## Cicero, Academica 2.74 (Lucullus)

> "one cannot possibly doubt that Socrates thought that nothing could be known. He excepted one thing only, asserting that he did know that he knew nothing; but he made no other exception."  (Yonge, Academic Questions II, ch. XXIII)

## Diogenes Laertius 2.32

> "and that he knew nothing except just the fact of his ignorance."  (2.32, Hicks)

Greek (2.32): "καὶ εἰδέναι μὲν μηδὲν πλὴν αὐτὸ τοῦτο [εἰδέναι]."

Relied on for: the formula's earliest written forms on record (Latin,
first century BCE; Greek, Diogenes); Arcesilaus's denial that even this
one item is known.
Context: Academica 1.15–17 is Varro's sketch of Socrates before the Old
Academy; 1.44–45 is Cicero's account of the New Academy; 2.74 is
Cicero's reply to Lucullus listing earlier philosophers who held that
nothing can be known. Diogenes 2.32 is in a list of Socrates' sayings.
