> "The symbol in this phase is the communicable unit, to which I give the name archetype: that is, a typical or recurring image. I mean by an archetype a symbol which connects one poem with another and thereby helps to unify and integrate our literary experience."  (p. 99)

> "This emphasis on impersonal content has been developed by Jung and his school, where the communicability of archetypes is accounted for by a theory of a collective unconscious—an unnecessary hypothesis in literary criticism, so far as I can judge."  (pp. 111–112; page break after "accounted for by a")

Relied on for: the literary-critical sense of "archetype" and Frye's own statement that the collective-unconscious hypothesis is not needed for it (vocabulary/archetype-and-collective-unconscious.md).
Context: p. 99 goes on: "archetypal criticism is primarily concerned with literature as a social fact and as a mode of communication"; the p. 111 passage follows a contrast between the psychologist, for whom dream symbols are private, and the critic, for whom "there is no such thing as private symbolism", and Freud's reading of *Oedipus Tyrannus*.
