# Words are contracts

Decided by erkkimon on 2026-09-25, founding the philosophy implant
([journal](../journals/2026-09-25.md)); it is given G6 of the
[manifest](../vision/manifest.md).

## The idea

Truth can be found with any vocabulary — even a clumsy one, even one whose
definitions a reader dislikes — *provided* the meanings are clear,
transparent contracts, definite enough to be applied, and fine-grained
enough to do justice to the piece of reality being talked about. What
makes a vocabulary unusable is not that its words are unusual but that they
are ambiguous, hidden, or too coarse. So this implant does not search for
the "right" definition of *knowledge*, *freedom* or *fallacy*; it fixes one
per term, says why, and keeps the alternatives in plain sight.

## The rule

Every term in [vocabulary](../knowledge/vocabulary/index.md) carries two
kinds of definition, each labelled:

1. **Normative** — *how this implant uses the term.* One definition,
   chosen for the sake of the whole vocabulary: so that the terms together
   cover what needs covering, and split it where the splits matter.
   The entry states **why** this definition — what it is separated from,
   what granularity it must carry, which pages depend on it — and links the
   journal entry where it was fixed. This is a decision the implant made
   for coherence. It is *not* a claim that the definition is the correct
   one, and it is never presented as such.
2. **Descriptive** — *how the term is actually used.* One entry per
   distinct sense in the literature or in ordinary language: the sense,
   who uses it (a thinker, a school, a discipline, common usage), and the
   source with locator ([Every claim carries its receipt](every-claim-carries-its-receipt.md)).
   These are reports and follow the reporting rule
   ([Reporting, not endorsing](reporting-not-endorsing.md)).

Both are visible on the same page; neither is buried.

## Consequences

- **A page that departs from the contract says so.** When a thinker uses
  *idea* or *substance* or *dharma* in a sense that differs from this
  implant's normative sense, the page uses the thinker's sense in
  reporting them and marks it: "*idea* — in Locke's sense (descriptive
  entry 3), not the contract sense". Silent drift between senses is how an
  argument is misreported.
- **Persuasion vocabulary is the test case.** *Argumentation*, *rhetoric*,
  *dialectic* and *persuasion* are used in the literature in overlapping,
  competing and sometimes evaluative ways. The implant's normative split
  treats them as regions of one spectrum of techniques for moving a mind,
  distinguished by mechanism rather than by legitimacy
  ([persuasion](../knowledge/persuasion/index.md)); the descriptive entries
  record Aristotle's, the pragma-dialecticians', and the sales literature's
  uses side by side, cited.
- **Granularity is a reason to add a term, not a synonym.** If one word is
  doing two jobs that a page needs to keep apart, the vocabulary gets two
  entries with the boundary stated; it does not get a footnote.
- **Changing a normative definition is a decision.** It is journaled, the
  entry's timestamp is bumped, and every page listed as depending on it is
  re-read. The contract is cheap to read and expensive to break silently.

## What this does not mean

It does not mean the implant invents jargon. The normative sense is the
established sense wherever one established sense exists; the machinery
exists for the many terms where it does not. And it does not mean
descriptive entries are second-class: for a reader who came to understand
how a word *could* be understood, the descriptive entries are the content
and the normative one is the implant's housekeeping.
