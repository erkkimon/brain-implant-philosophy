# Selection is a stated rule

Decided by erkkimon on 2026-09-25, founding the philosophy implant
([journal](../journals/2026-09-25.md)); it is given G5 of the
[manifest](../vision/manifest.md).

## The problem it addresses

A neutral-point-of-view policy governs the sentence: attribute, do not
assert. It says nothing about the page that was never written. Wikipedia's
own policy names the gap. Its "due weight" rule has articles represent
viewpoints "in proportion to the prominence of each viewpoint in those
sources", says that "generally, the views of tiny minorities should not be
included at all", and states outright: "If a viewpoint is held by an
extremely small minority, it does not belong on Wikipedia, regardless of
whether it is true, or you can prove it"
([WP:NPOV, "Due and undue weight"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Neutral_point_of_view#Due_and_undue_weight),
as read on 2026-09-25). Whatever its merits for an encyclopedia, that rule
makes *selection* an editorial judgement of prominence, and an editorial
judgement is where bias lives when every sentence is clean. A position that
can be defended is irrelevant if it is never told — and under that rule, a
provable one can be left out by design.

## The rule

**Admission is mechanical.** A problem, position, argument, thinker, work,
school, persuasion technique, bias or method is in scope if — and only if —
it has a **named proponent or source in a citable published place**. That
is the whole test. It is not asked whether the view is important, current,
respectable, mainstream, Western, academic, or held by anyone with
credentials ([Evidence before authority](evidence-before-authority.md)).

**Proportion is data, not a filter.** How widely a view is held is stated on
its page, with the source that measured it — a survey
([Bourget & Chalmers 2023](https://doi.org/10.3998/phimp.2109)), a citation
count, a bibliography — and the reader weighs it. It is never a reason to
omit the view, shorten its case-for, or write its page less carefully. Nor
is it a reason to inflate it: a page *says* it reports a minority view when
the data say so. That is how false balance is avoided without a verdict.

**The same rule for every tradition.** Indian, Chinese, Islamic, African,
Indigenous, Japanese, Latin American and European traditions enter under the
same test, into the same folders, with the same page templates
([knowledge](../knowledge/index.md)) — not as a "non-Western philosophy"
appendix. A problem page that lists Nyāya and Gettier on the same question
of knowledge is what the rule produces when it is followed.

**Absence is recorded.** If a search for a position's proponent finds none,
that is recorded in the journal as a search that was made, so the next
contributor does not redo it and so the absence itself is a documented
finding rather than a silent omission.

## The coverage ledger

Because "more than Wikipedia" is one of the three benchmarks
([manifest](../vision/manifest.md), *The benchmark*), coverage is counted,
not felt. A coverage ledger, `todo/coverage.md` (tracked in
[open work](../todo/open.md)), keeps, per branch, the count of pages here
against Wikipedia's own lists (its lists of philosophers, of unsolved
problems in philosophy, of fallacies, of cognitive biases), with the date
each was last compared. A branch that falls behind is a todo, not an
impression.

## What this does not mean

It does not mean anything anyone has ever said gets a page. "Citable
published place" excludes private conversation, an unsourced web forum, and
the implant's own contributors; it includes a book, an article, a recorded
lecture, a primary text, a documented oral tradition in a scholarly edition.
The test is that a stranger can go and check. It also does not mean every
admitted item gets equal length: length follows how much *has been said*
about it in citable places — which is again a fact the page can state.
