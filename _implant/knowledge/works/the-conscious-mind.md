# The Conscious Mind

## Author and date

[David J. Chalmers](../thinkers/chalmers.md). Published New York: Oxford
University Press, 1996. ISBN 9780195105537 (hardback), 9780195117899
(paperback), per Open Library. Recorded in
`raw/chalmers-1995-facing-up-hard-problem.md`.

## Locator scheme

Cited by page of the 1996 OUP edition. SEP "Zombies" cites the zombie
argument as "Chalmers 1996, 93–171" (Kirk, Fall 2024, §3; same raw file).

## Editions and translations

1996 hardback and paperback, both Oxford UP. The book is copyrighted and

## Cited for

- [Zombie argument](../arguments/zombie-argument.md). Kirk (SEP "Zombies",
  §1) writes: "The most systematic use of the zombie idea against
  physicalism is by David Chalmers 1996". §3 gives the simplest form of the
  argument: "Zombies are conceivable. Whatever is conceivable is possible.
  Therefore zombies are possible." (`raw/chalmers-1995-facing-up-hard-problem.md`).
- The hard problem. It was stated a year earlier in "Facing Up to the
  Problem of Consciousness" (1995): "The really hard problem of
  consciousness is the problem of experience" (§2, same raw file). The
  [consciousness problem](../problems/consciousness.md) page grounds on
  the 1995 article, not the book.
- Some vocabulary pages cite locators in the book (e.g.
  [qualia](../vocabulary/qualia.md), pp. xii–xiii;
  [computational functionalism](../vocabulary/computational-functionalism.md),
  ch. 3). They were written from memory and are not yet verified (todo).

## Interpretations

The replies to the zombie argument are recorded, attributed, on the
[zombie argument](../arguments/zombie-argument.md) page. They include
denial of the conceivability premise, which that page grounds on SEP's
