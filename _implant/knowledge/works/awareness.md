# Awareness: The Perils and Opportunities of Reality

This page reports who said what about the book. It does not assert that
its author is a philosopher or that what the talks say is true or false
([Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md);
[How claims are graded](../../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md)).

## Author and date

[Anthony de Mello](../thinkers/de-mello.md), S.J. (1931–1987), edited by
J. Francis Stroud, S.J. The Library of Congress-derived record (Open
Library OL1870705M) gives New York: Doubleday, 1990, 1st ed., vi, 184 p.,
ISBN 0385249365, LCCN 90030182, with the statement "by_statement": "Anthony de Mello ; edited by J. Francis Stroud."
The title page of the Image printing reads: "AWARENESS A de Mello Spirituality Conference in His Own Words ANTHONY DE MELLO, S.J. Edited by J. Francis Stroud, S.J."
and its copyright line: "Copyright © 1990 by the Center for Spiritual Exchange"
(`raw/de-mello-1990-awareness-publisher-sample-and-record.md`;
`raw/de-mello-1990-awareness-google-books-snippets.md`).

The book appeared three years after de Mello's death in 1987. The CDF's
Explanatory Note (1998) states: "Not all the works of Father de Mello were authorized for publication by the author himself. Some were published after his death based on his writings, or on notes or recordings of his conferences."
(`raw/cdf-1998-explanatory-note-de-mello-awareness-passages.md`). Stroud's
foreword describes the source as "live dialogue and interaction — touching on all the themes that enliven the hearts of those who listen. Maintaining the spirit of his live words, and sustaining his spontaneity with a responsive audience on the printed page was the task"
(p. 4). The book's back matter describes the editor: "REV. J. FRANCIS STROUD, S.J., a Campus Minister at Fordham University in New York, is also the Executive Director of the De Mello Spirituality Center and worked closely with Anthony de Mello for eight years."
(p. 185; `raw/de-mello-1990-awareness-google-books-snippets.md`).

## Structure

Short titled chapters, as the contents pages list them (p. v–vi;
`raw/de-mello-1990-awareness-google-books-snippets.md`). The opening:
"CONTENTS Foreword 3 On Waking Up 5 Will I Be of Help to You in This Retreat? 6 On the Proper Kind of Selfishness 9 On Wanting Happiness 10"
A middle run: "Self-observation 35 Awareness Without Evaluating Everything 37 The Illusion of Rewards 42 Finding Yourself 43"
Further: "Labels 73 Obstacles to Happiness 74 Four Steps to Wisdom 78 All's Right with the World 84"
The close: "Saying Nothing About Love 161 Losing Control 162 Listening to Life 164 The End of Analysis 166 Dead Ahead 169 The Land of Love 172"

The themes below are given in de Mello's words as the read snippets and
the publisher's sample show them; the grouping is the implant's
(structural note).

- Waking up. The book opens: "Spirituality means waking up. Most people, even though they don't know it, are asleep."
  And: "Even the best psychologists will tell you that, that people don't really want to be cured. What they want is relief; a cure is painful."
  (sample, "On Waking Up";
  `raw/de-mello-1990-awareness-publisher-sample-and-record.md`).
- Self-observation: "As I said to you before, selfobservation means watching - observing whatever is going on in you and around you as if it were happening to someone else."
  (p. 46). On the "I" and the "me", de Mello describes stages: "we begin first with things, with an awareness of things; then we move on to an awareness of thoughts (that's the "me"); and finally we get to awareness of the"
  (p. 47; Google's snippet ends there).
- Labels: "The important thing is not to know who "I" is or what "I" is. You'll never succeed. There are no words for it. The important thing is to drop the labels."
  (p. 73).
- Happiness and attachment: "Uninterrupted happiness is uncaused. True happiness is uncaused. You cannot make me happy. You are not my happiness."
  (p. 77); "An attachment is a belief that without something you are not going to be happy."
  (p. 134); "Love entails clarity of perception, objectivity" (p. 140).
- God and religion: "The fact is that you're surrounded by God and you don't see God, because you "know" about God."
  (p. 102; all `raw/de-mello-1990-awareness-google-books-snippets.md`).

## Cited for

- [Anthony de Mello](../thinkers/de-mello.md): Coleman (America, 2010)
  names it among "earlier writings (Awareness, Sadhana and The Song of the Bird)"
  (`raw/coleman-2010-america-de-mello-seek-god-everywhere.md`).
- The publisher's description characterises the book: "Mixing Christian spirituality, Buddhist parables, Hindu breathing exercises, and psychological insight, de Mello’s words of hope come together in Awareness in a grand synthesis."
  (`raw/de-mello-1990-awareness-publisher-sample-and-record.md`; marketing text).
- The 1998 notification. The CDF Notification (24 June 1998) names no
  book; it states: "But already in certain passages in these early works and to a greater degree in his later publications, one notices a progressive distancing from the essential contents of the Christian faith."
  and concludes that "the above-mentioned positions are incompatible with the Catholic faith and can cause grave harm."
  (`raw/cdf-1998-notification-writings-de-mello.md`). The accompanying
  Explanatory Note cites *Awareness* by page among the later works
  (`raw/cdf-1998-explanatory-note-de-mello-awareness-passages.md`):
  - on God: "According to the author, no one can help us find God just as no one can help a fish in the sea find the ocean (cf. One Minute Wisdom, 67; Awareness, 103)."
  - on Jesus, quoting the book: "The only difference between Jesus and those others was that he was awake and they weren't" (Awareness, 30-31)
  - on the next life: "Why bother about tomorrow? Is there a life after death? Will I survive after death? Why bother about tomorrow? Get into today" (Awareness, 114).
  - on sin: "There is no reason to repent for sins, since the only thing that matters is to be awakened to an awareness of reality: "Don't weep for your sins. Why weep for sins that you committed when you were asleep?" (Awareness, 26; cf. ibid., 43, 150)."
- The authorship question. Allen (National Catholic Reporter, 1998)
  reports Lisbert D'Souza, South Asia Jesuit provincial: "some of these post-humously published works have led to de Mello being “grossly misunderstood.” The Indian Jesuits, he said, regard only nine books as authentic."
  (`raw/allen-1998-ncr-de-mello-censure.md`). Akkara (National Catholic
  Register, 1998) gives the list: "[The nine official books of Father de Mello are Sadhana, The Song of the Bird, Wellsprings, One Minute Wisdom, The Prayer of the Frog Vol. I, The Prayer of the Frog Vol. II, Contact with God, Call to Love and One Minute Nonsense, the last three published posthumously.]"
  (`raw/akkara-1998-ncregister-indian-jesuits-de-mello.md`). Structural
  note: *Awareness* is not in that list; neither source says whether the
  Indian Jesuits counted it as unauthorised.
- The cautionary note. Goodreads, on this book's page, reports that "Some editions of his books have since been supplemented with the insertion of a caution:"
  (`raw/de-mello-books-cautionary-note-and-1998-reception.md`); it does not
  say whether printings of *Awareness* carry it.
- Other de Mello works with pages here: [Sadhana](sadhana.md) and
  [The Song of the Bird](the-song-of-the-bird.md).

## Interpretations

Readings of the book by named writers, as the read sources report them.

- Recommending the book. Fárek (Religions, 2022, §2;
  [doi:10.3390/rel13100904](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel13100904)) reports
  Moss's review (Journal of Religion and Health 31(2), 1992, 175–178;
  [doi:10.1007/BF00986795](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00986795), verified by
  content negotiation, not read): "David Moss, with many years of experience in pastoral care and counselling, strongly recommended book Awareness to readers of the Journal of Religion and Health."
  Moss printed a client's letter, which Fárek quotes: "De Mello has introduced me to a fresh perspective that has enticed me into re-examining some products of my Catholic conditioning."
  (`raw/farek-2022-de-mello-awareness-moss-review.md`).
- Defending the book's orthodoxy. Stroud, its editor, told Allen (1998): "“De Mello did emphasize that God is a mystery,” Stroud said. “But he would quote Thomas Aquinas saying the very same thing. ... He never denied anything like a personal concept of God."
  (`raw/allen-1998-ncr-de-mello-censure.md`). The book itself has de Mello ask what "Thomas Aquinas, Augustine, and all the rest were saying and what the Church teaches constantly when she says that God is mystery, is unintelligible to the human mind?"
  (p. 124; `raw/de-mello-1990-awareness-google-books-snippets.md`).
- Criticising its positions. The Explanatory Note's readings of pp. 26,
  30–31, 42–43, 103 and 114 are quoted above; the Note introduces the
  passage on God by saying de Mello "makes statements about God which ignore his personal nature, if not explicitly denying it"
  (`raw/cdf-1998-explanatory-note-de-mello-awareness-passages.md`).
  Gispert Sauch told Akkara (1998): "If one does not have a certain spiritual and theological maturity, these [books] could be misunderstood. The [Vatican] warning is perfectly justifiable."
  (`raw/akkara-1998-ncregister-indian-jesuits-de-mello.md`; said of de
  Mello's books in general, not of this one).
- On spoken versus written form, Barnes (Thinking Faith, 2014) writes: "Later collections of his writing were not the best of him. He was a speaker not a writer"
  (`raw/barnes-2014-thinking-faith-de-mello.md`; he does not name this book).
