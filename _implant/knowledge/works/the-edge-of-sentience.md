## Cited for

- [Sentience](../vocabulary/sentience.md). The book defines a sentient
  being as "a system with the capacity to have valenced experiences, such
  as experiences of pain and pleasure" (Summary, p. 1).
- **Sentience candidate.** "A system S is a sentience candidate if there is
  an evidence base that (a) implies a realistic possibility of sentience in
  S that it would be irresponsible to ignore when making policy decisions
  that will affect S, and (b) is rich enough to allow the identification of
  welfare risks and the design and assessment of precautions" (Summary,
  p. 1; ch. 7, p. 124).
- [AI consciousness](../problems/ai-consciousness.md). Birch writes of
  "having discussions now, when no artificial sentience candidates yet
  exist" (ch. 17, p. 323). Proposal 25, the run-ahead principle, reads:
  "measures to regulate the development of sentient AI should run ahead of
  what would be proportionate to the risks posed by current technology"
  (Summary, p. 6; ch. 17, p. 324).
- [Moral patient](../vocabulary/moral-patient.md). Framework Principle 2
  reads: "If S is a sentience candidate, then it is reckless/negligent to
  make decisions that create risks of suffering for S without considering
  the question of what precautions are proportionate to those risks"
  (Summary, p. 2).

## Interpretations

- Birch rejects one reading of his framework himself: "the logic of my
  framework is not the following: 'the precautionary principle' is the
  correct general decision rule" (ch. 1, p. 17).
