# The Song of the Bird

This page reports who said what about the book. It does not assert that
its author is a philosopher or that the stories teach anything true or
false ([Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md);
[How claims are graded](../../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md)).

## Author and date

[Anthony de Mello](../thinkers/de-mello.md), S.J. The Internet Archive's
library record gives the first edition as Anand, India: Gujarat Sahitya
Prakash, 1982, 219 pp. The US edition is Garden City, N.Y.: Image Books
(Doubleday), 1984, 172 pp., ISBN 0385196156, per Open Library. Wikipedia's
bibliography lists it as "Image, 1982"; the two records differ on the year
of the Image edition (`raw/de-mello-1982-song-of-the-bird-preface-and-title-story.md`;
`raw/de-mello-books-cautionary-note-and-1998-reception.md`).

## Locator scheme

No standard locator scheme is recorded. The stories carry titles (the
title story is "The Song of the Bird"), so a citation by story title
resolves in any edition. Page numbers differ between the Indian and US
editions (219 against 172 pages, per the records above). This implant
cites by story title or front-matter heading, because the text it read
has no page numbers.

## Structure

As the front matter of the read text sets it out
(`raw/de-mello-1982-song-of-the-bird-preface-and-title-story.md`):

1. A dedicatory preface. De Mello writes: "This book has been written for people of every persuasion religious and non religious, I cannot, however, hide from my readers the fact that I am a priest of the Catholic Church."
   He continues: "I have wandered free-ly in mystical traditions that are not Christian and not religious and I have been profoundly influenced by them. It is to my Church, however, that I keep returning, for she is my spiritual home".
2. The sources of the stories: "Stories that are Buddhist, Christian, Zen, Hasidic, Russian, Chinese, Hindu, Sufi; stories ancient and contemporary."
   And their stated purpose: "if read in a cer-tain kind of way, they will produce spiritual growth."
3. "How to read them": three ways. Read once, which "will give you entertainment"; read twice and reflect,
   which "will give you a taste of theology"; read again in silence, which "will give you a feel for the mystical".
   The reader is told: "Let it speak to your heart, not to your brain." De Mello adds: "It is with this mystical end in view that most of these stories were originally told."
4. "Caution": "Most of the stories have a comment appended to them. The comment is meant to be a sample of the kind of comment you yourself may want to make. Make your own."
   And: "Beware of applying the story to anyone (priest, mullah, and church, neighbour) other than yourself. If you do so the story will do you damage. Every one of these stories is about you, no one else."
   On a first reading the stories are to be read in order: "The order imparts a teaching and a spirit which will be lost if the stories are read haphazardly."
5. A glossary: "Theology: The art of telling stories about the Divine. Also the art of listening to them."
   "Mysticisms: The art of tasting and feeling in your heart the inner meaning of such stories to the point that they transform you."
6. The stories, each with a title, most followed by a comment. The
   publisher's description on Goodreads says the book "shares 124 stories and parables from a variety of traditions both ancient and modern."
   (`raw/de-mello-books-cautionary-note-and-1998-reception.md`, source 4; structural note: the implant did
   not count them).
7. A closing note: "All that the author has done is string them together with a specific aim in mind. His task has been that of the weaver and the dyer. He takes no credit at all for the cotton and the thread."
   The read text ends with two lists of themes, "Vol. 1: PRAYER - AWARENESS - RELIGION - THE SAINTS - THE SELF - LOVE -TRUTH."
   and "Vol. 2: EDUCATION - AUTHORITY - SPIRITUALITY -HUMAN NATURE - RELATIONSHIPS - SERVICE - ENLIGHTENMENT."
   The text read does not say what the two lists label.

The title story. A Master tells disciples: "God is Unknown, the Unknowable. Every statement about Him, every answer to your questions, is a distortion of the Truth. ”"
Asked why he speaks of God at all, he answers: "“Why does the bird sing? ” said the Master."
The story ends: "Not because he has a statement, but because he has a song."

## Cited for

- [Anthony de Mello](../thinkers/de-mello.md): the book is a primary
  source for his stated audience, purpose and method of reading.
- The 1998 notification. The Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith
  (CDF), in a notification signed by Joseph Card. Ratzinger and dated 24
  June 1998, wrote that de Mello's works "almost always take the form of brief stories, contain some valid elements of oriental wisdom."
  It wrote: "Especially in his early writings, Father de Mello, while revealing the influence of Buddhist and Taoist spiritual currents, remained within the lines of Christian spirituality."
  And: "But already in certain passages in these early works and to a greater degree in his later publications, one notices a progressive distancing from the essential contents of the Christian faith."
  Its conclusion: "this Congregation declares that the above-mentioned positions are incompatible with the Catholic faith and can cause grave harm."
  The English text read names no individual book, this one included
  (`raw/cdf-1998-notification-writings-de-mello.md`). The CDF also wrote
  that "not all of these texts were authorized by him for publication" (same file).
- The cautionary note. The Goodreads author description for de Mello
  reports: "Some editions of his books have since been supplemented with the insertion of a caution:"
  and gives its text: "The books of Father Anthony de Mello were written in a multi-religious context to help the followers of other religions, agnostics and atheists in their spiritual search, and they were not intended by the author as manuals of instruction of the Catholic faithful in Christian doctrine or dogma."
  Wikipedia (rev. 1300353226) reports that his books "sometimes include an advisory that they were written in a multi-religious context and are not intended to be manuals on Christian doctrine."
  Neither source says which printings of this book carry the note; the
  implant has not seen it in a copy of the book
  (`raw/de-mello-books-cautionary-note-and-1998-reception.md`).
- Related collections, held bibliographically only (same raw file,
  Wikipedia bibliography; Open Library search records):
  *One Minute Wisdom* (Image, 1985; ISBN 0-385-24290-5) and
  *Taking Flight* (Image, 1988; ISBN 0-385-41371-8). Other de Mello works with
  pages in this implant: [Awareness](awareness.md) and
  [Sadhana](sadhana.md).

## Interpretations

Readings of the book itself, by named scholars, are not yet recorded
(todo). What is recorded is how the 1998 notification on de Mello's
writings was received. Outlook (Menezes, 1998), reporting on it, names
this book among his best-sellers:
"Among De Mello's six other works feature best-sellers The Prayer of the Frog and Song of the Bird, while three posthumous publications have been attributed to him."
(`raw/de-mello-books-cautionary-note-and-1998-reception.md`).

- Agreeing with the notification. The CDF's own reasons are quoted above
  (`raw/cdf-1998-notification-writings-de-mello.md`). Outlook quotes
  Bishop Oswald Gracias, secretary of the Catholic Bishops' Conference of
  India: "We fully accept the findings of the Church,"
  (`raw/de-mello-books-cautionary-note-and-1998-reception.md`).
- Disagreeing with the notification. Outlook quotes Fr Allwyn D'Silva:
  "Anthony de Mello never denied the uniqueness of Christ but simply used stories from the Asian experience."
  (same raw file).

Structural note (G4): each side above is given one quoted statement from
a named person in the same source, Outlook 1998, plus the CDF's own text;
neither is ranked. How de Mello's own preface ("It is to my Church, however, that I keep returning, for she is my spiritual home")
bears on the notification is left to cited readers; none is recorded yet.
