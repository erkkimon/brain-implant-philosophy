# Meditations on First Philosophy

## Author and date

[René Descartes](../thinkers/descartes.md). Published in Latin in 1641
(Paris: Michel Soly), with a second edition in 1642 (Amsterdam: Elzevir)
and a French translation in 1647. Hatfield reports these dates in SEP,
"René Descartes" (Fall 2024), Primary Sources. Excerpt:
`raw/sep-descartes-fall-2024-meditations-editions.md`.

The main title changed between the two editions. Hatfield reports that the
1641 edition "had promised to demonstrate “the immortality of the soul”".
The 1642 edition "promises to demonstrate “the distinction of the human soul
from the body.”" (same source).

## Locator scheme

Scholars cite the Adam and Tannery edition (*Oeuvres de Descartes*, 11
vols., "AT") by volume and page. The Meditations are in volume VII (Latin)
and IX (French). The Cottingham, Stoothoff, Murdoch and Kenny translation
"shows the AT pagination in the margins" (Hatfield, SEP, Bibliography).
SEP's own style, for example, is "Med. 7:229–30".

This implant also cites the Veitch translation by Meditation and paragraph
(e.g. Meditation II, ¶6), because that public-domain text carries no AT
numbers. The AT references given alongside it (II = AT VII 27–28; VI = AT
VII 78) are the standard ones. They have not been checked against an AT
edition; `raw/descartes-1641-meditations-thinking-thing-real-distinction.md`
records this.

## Editions and translations

- 1641 Latin first edition. 1642 Latin second edition, which added the
  seventh set of objections (Bourdin). 1647 French translation (Hatfield,
  SEP, as above).
- John Veitch, English translation, 1853. Public domain. Free text:
  [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Meditations_on_First_Philosophy).
- Cottingham, Stoothoff, Murdoch (and Kenny), *The Philosophical Writings
  of Descartes*, 3 vols. Copyrighted. Named by Hatfield as the translation
  SEP uses.

## Structure

Six meditations. The titles below are from Veitch's translation on
Wikisource (`raw/sep-descartes-fall-2024-meditations-editions.md`):

1. "Of the things of which we may doubt."
2. "Of the nature of the human mind; and that it is more easily known than
   the body."
3. "Of God: that he exists."
4. "Of truth and error."
5. "Of the essence of material things; and, again, of God; that he exists."
6. "Of the existence of material things, and of the real distinction
   between the mind and body of man."

The work was published together with *Objections and Replies*. Hatfield
reports "seven sets of objections". Their authors included Caterus,
Bourdin, Mersenne, Gassendi, Hobbes, Antoine Arnauld and unnamed
theologians (SEP, as above).

## Cited for

  distinct from my body, and may exist without it". Both are in

- [Knowledge](../vocabulary/knowledge.md). Meditation I is the method of

## Interpretations

- **The Cartesian circle.** Hatfield quotes Arnauld's objection from the
  fourth set of objections:
  "I have one further worry, namely how the author avoids reasoning in a circle when he says that we are sure that what we clearly and distinctly perceive is true only because God exists. But we can be sure that God exists only because we clearly and distinctly perceive"
  (SEP, "René Descartes", Fall 2024). Hatfield reports that the second and
  fourth sets of objections "drew attention to" this. The replies given by
  Descartes and later scholars are not yet excerpted here (todo).
- **The real distinction and its critics.** Ryle's "Ghost in the Machine"
  is the reception recorded in [Descartes](../thinkers/descartes.md) and
  `raw/ryle-1949-concept-of-mind-ghost-in-the-machine.md`.
