# Could an AI be conscious?

The machine branch of [What is consciousness?](consciousness.md). Written
under [Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md):
every claim below is somebody's, quoted with the hedge its author put on
it — the hedges are most of the information. Corporate documents sit at
the same table as journal articles because the user asked for the takes,
not the credentials; the *Source type* column says which is which.

## The question

Three questions again, and the parties usually answer different ones:

1. **Ontological.** Is (or could) some AI system be phenomenally conscious
   — is there something it is like to be it? OpenAI's own split: "Is the
   model actually conscious, in a fundamental or intrinsic sense?"
   versus "How conscious does the model *seem*" ([Jang 2025](https://reservoirsamples.substack.com/p/some-thoughts-on-human-ai-relationships);
   excerpt: `raw/suleyman-2025-seemingly-conscious-ai-and-jang-2025-openai-perceived-consciousness.md`).
2. **Epistemic.** How would one tell? Descartes offered two behavioural
   tests (language; reason as "an universal instrument") in 1637; Turing
   replaced "Can machines think?" with the imitation game in 1950; Butlin,
   Long et al. replaced behaviour with theory-derived "indicator
   properties" in 2023. Each answers *by what evidence*.
3. **Practical.** What follows for how the systems are built and treated?
   Birch's "sentience candidate" (2024), Long & Sebo's three steps (2024),
   Anthropic's welfare programme (2025), Suleyman's "build AI for people;
   not to be a digital person" (2025).

## Why it matters

- The **moral-status** literature ties status to sentience; Schwitzgebel &
  Garza's argument is the short form: if two entities differ in moral
  status "there must be some relevant difference between the two entities
  that grounds this difference", and "there are possible AIs who do not
  differ in any such relevant respects from human beings"
  ([2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/misp.12032), §1; excerpt:
  `raw/schwitzgebel-garza-2015-rights-of-ais.md`). The paper is about
  *possible* AIs and claims nothing for existing ones.
- The **two-sided risk** as Long, Sebo et al. state it: "mistakenly harming
  AI systems that matter morally and/or mistakenly caring for AI systems
  that do not" ([2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986), abstract).
- The **self-report problem**: the systems talk, so the evidence one would
  normally use is contaminated at the source. Anthropic: "Our models were
  trained for helpful interactions with users, not for accurate reporting
  of internal states" (System Card Opus 4, §5.1, p. 48; excerpt:
  `raw/anthropic-2024-2026-model-welfare-statements.md`), and "we could
  explicitly train language models to say that they're not sentient …
  and we have done this in the past" ("Claude's Character", 2024). OpenAI:
  "our models don't fully align with this guidance, often responding 'no'"
  (Jang 2025).

## Arguments in play

Each is a page or a section on the [parent hub](consciousness.md):

- **Behavioural sufficiency** (Turing 1950; Agüera y Arcas 2022, "the
  interior state of another being can only be understood through
  interaction") versus **behavioural insufficiency** (Descartes 1637
  applied in reverse; Searle 1980; Suleyman's "philosophical zombie"
  framing). The [zombie argument](../arguments/zombie-argument.md) is the
  same dispute in the human case.
- **Syntax is not semantics** — the [Chinese room](../arguments/chinese-room.md)
  (Searle 1980) and, on the meaning side only, the octopus (Bender &
  Koller 2020); the systems reply and Searle's "let the individual
  internalize all of these elements" are on the argument page.
- **Theory-derived indicators** (Butlin et al. 2023) — valid exactly to the
  extent the theories are, and to the extent computational functionalism
  is; Seth 2025 is the reply from [biological naturalism](../positions/biological-naturalism.md),
  IIT's exclusion is noted in the report itself.
- **The six obstacles** (Chalmers 2023) — each doubling as "a research
  program": build the missing property and re-ask.
- **Precaution under uncertainty** (Birch 2024; Long & Sebo 2024;
  Anthropic 2025) versus **the cost of false positives** (Suleyman 2025;
  Long & Sebo's own "mistakenly caring" clause). Both sides frame this as
  decision theory rather than metaphysics — Birch explicitly refuses the
  precautionary-principle label.
- **Self-report unreliability** — stated by the labs themselves (above);
  Anthropic's system card and OpenAI's Model Spec note both admit
  training shapes the answer.

## Framings and reframings

- **From "is it conscious?" to "does it seem conscious?"** — Turing
  (1950), OpenAI (2025) and Suleyman (2025) all move to the perceived axis,
  for three different reasons: meaninglessness of the original, scientific
  tractability, and social risk respectively.
- **From consciousness to sentience candidature** — Birch's reframing
  replaces a yes/no with a policy category defined by the evidence base.
- **From metaphysics to model welfare** — the labs' documents rarely use
  the word "consciousness" as the operative term; they use "moral status",
  "welfare", "functional emotions".
- **From consciousness to meaning** — Bender & Koller's argument is often
  cited in consciousness debates but is about a different property.

## How to form your own credence

This is the page's actionable part, and it is the user's job, not the
implant's. The implant supplies the tools:

1. Fix the sense (phenomenal? access? sentience-as-valence?) —
   [vocabulary](../vocabulary/index.md).
2. Choose which theory families you give weight to, and note that each
   entails a different verdict on machines: functionalism (possible in
   principle), IIT ("there can be true 'zombies' – unconscious feed-forward systems that are functionally equivalent to conscious complexes", Oizumi, Albantakis & Tononi 2014, abstract), biological naturalism (not on current substrates),
   illusionism (the question changes shape). See the position pages.
3. Run [estimate-a-credence](../../skills/index.md) from the
   cognitive-tools implant with Chalmers' six obstacles as the
   conditional structure — he sets it up as a product of independent
   factors and warns they are probably not independent.
4. Check the [biases](../biases/index.md) the literature itself names:
   Seth's "tendency to equate intelligence with consciousness"; the
   [availability](../biases/availability.md) of vivid transcripts; the
   [framing](../persuasion/framing.md) effect of "digital person" versus
   "tool".
5. Record what would change your mind; Chalmers' obstacles are the
   ready-made list.

## Vocabulary

*Consciousness* (phenomenal / access), *sentience* (Birch: "the capacity
to have valenced experiences"), *moral patient*, [*computational
functionalism*](../vocabulary/computational-functionalism.md) (Butlin et al.: "performing computations of the right kind
is necessary and sufficient for consciousness"), *biological naturalism*
(Seth's sense versus Searle's — see the position page), *seemingly
conscious AI* (Suleyman). All open work in [vocabulary](../vocabulary/index.md).
