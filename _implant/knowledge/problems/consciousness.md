# What is consciousness, and how does it relate to the physical?

The hub for the philosophy of mind on this implant. Written under
[Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md):
every answer below belongs to whoever gave it, the list is not ranked, and
the survey figures are the only proportions on the page. Sources are
excerpted in `raw/` under the file names given; the AI-specific material
has its own hub at [Could an AI be conscious?](ai-consciousness.md).

## The question

Three questions travel under one word, and the literature itself says so:
"Consciousness is a mongrel concept: there are a number of very different
'consciousnesses'" ([Block 1995](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00038188),
abstract; excerpt: `raw/block-1995-phenomenal-and-access-consciousness.md`).

1. **The phenomenal question.** An organism is conscious "if and only if
   there is something that it is like to be that organism—something it is
   like for the organism" ([Nagel 1974](https://doi.org/10.2307/2183914),
   p. 436; excerpt: `raw/nagel-1974-what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat.md`).
   Why is there such a thing, and what is its relation to the physical
   processes that accompany it? Chalmers names this "the really hard
   problem of consciousness … the problem of experience"
   ([Chalmers 1995](https://consc.net/papers/facing.html), §2; excerpt:
   `raw/chalmers-1995-facing-up-hard-problem.md`), against the "easy
   problems" — discrimination, integration, reportability, attention,
   control — "that seem directly susceptible to the standard methods of
   cognitive science" (§2).
2. **The access question.** Which information is "available for use in
   reasoning and rationally guiding speech and action" (Block 1995,
   abstract) — the question the scientific theories mostly answer, and
   the one Dehaene, Lau & Kouider call C1 and C2
   ([Dehaene, Lau & Kouider 2017](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan8871);
   excerpt: `raw/dehaene-lau-kouider-2017-c1-c2-machines.md`).
3. **The attribution question.** Given an answer to 1 and 2, which systems
   have it — other humans, animals, patients, machines — and how would
   one know? This is Descartes' two tests
   (`raw/descartes-1637-discourse-two-tests-for-machines.md`), Turing's
   replacement of "Can machines think?" with the imitation game
   ([Turing 1950](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433), §1, p. 433),
   and the 2023 indicator-property programme
   ([Butlin, Long et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)).

Which of the three a page is about is the first thing to fix; most
disagreements on record are between people answering different ones
(Block's own diagnosis, 1995).

## Why it matters

- **For metaphysics:** it is the test case for physicalism. Smart's reason
  for the identity theory was that sensations would otherwise be
  "nomological danglers" — the one exception to a world that is "nothing
  … but increasingly complex arrangements of physical constituents"
  ([Smart 1959](https://doi.org/10.2307/2182164), p. 142; excerpt:
  `raw/smart-1959-sensations-and-brain-processes.md`). Nagel: it "is what
  makes the mind-body problem really intractable" (1974, p. 435).
- **For science:** which theory is right decides what counts as a measure
  of consciousness in patients, infants and animals — the "hot zone"
  debate over posterior versus prefrontal cortex
  ([Koch, Massimini, Boly & Tononi 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2016.22);
  excerpt: `raw/koch-massimini-boly-tononi-2016-ncc-hot-zone.md`) and the
  2025 adversarial test
  ([Cogitate 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1)).
- **For ethics and AI:** moral status is tied to sentience in most of the
  literature on record — Birch's "sentience candidate" framework
  ([Birch 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780191966729.001.0001)),
  Schwitzgebel & Garza's no-relevant-difference argument
  ([2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/misp.12032)), and the 2024–2026 AI-lab
  statements collected on the [AI hub](ai-consciousness.md).
- **For personal identity:** Locke made consciousness, not substance, the
  bearer of identity: "as far as this consciousness can be extended
  backwards to any past action or thought, so far reaches the identity of
  that person" ([Locke, *Essay* II.xxvii.9](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10615);
  excerpt: `raw/locke-1690-essay-2-27-person-and-consciousness.md`).

## Positions taken

Grouped as the 2020 PhilPapers survey groups them, with its figures for
"accept or lean toward" among all respondents (N = 1020 for this
question; [Bourget & Chalmers 2023](https://doi.org/10.3998/phimp.2109);
excerpt: `raw/bourget-chalmers-2023-philpapers-survey-consciousness-and-amcs-2023-open-letter.md`).
The survey sampled English-language philosophy faculty; the figures
describe them, not the field's history.

| Family | Survey 2020 | Pages | One-line thesis, as its proponents state it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dualism | 21.96 % | [Dualism](../positions/dualism.md) | Mind and body are really distinct (Descartes, *Meditations* VI); or the physical facts do not fix the phenomenal facts (Chalmers 1995). |
| Functionalism | 33.04 % | [Functionalism](../positions/functionalism.md) | What makes a state mental is "solely … its function, or the role it plays" (Putnam 1967, via SEP); Butlin et al.'s "computational functionalism" is its AI-facing form. |
| Identity theory | 13.33 % | [Physicalism](../positions/physicalism.md) | "Sensations are nothing over and above brain processes" (Smart 1959, p. 145); Place 1956. |
| Panpsychism | 7.55 % | [Panpsychism](../positions/panpsychism.md) | "Mentality is fundamental and ubiquitous in the natural world" (SEP, via `raw/goff-2017-panpsychism-via-sep.md`); Strawson's "real physicalism entails panpsychism". |
| Eliminativism | 4.51 % | [Illusionism](../positions/illusionism.md) | "Phenomenal consciousness is an illusion" and the task is "to explain why it seems to exist" (Frankish 2016); Dennett's Multiple Drafts. |
| Other | 29.12 % | — | The survey's residual: agnostic, "no fact of the matter", combinations. |

Scientific theories are positions on the *access* question mostly, and on
the phenomenal question by extension; Seth & Bayne group them into four
families — higher-order, global workspace, re-entry/predictive processing,
integrated information ([Seth & Bayne 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-022-00587-4);
excerpt: `raw/seth-bayne-2022-theories-of-consciousness.md`):

- [Global workspace](../positions/global-workspace.md) — Baars; Dehaene &
  Naccache 2001: consciousness is global availability of information.
- [Higher-order thought](../positions/higher-order-thought.md) —
  Rosenthal: a state is conscious when one is aware of being in it.
- [Integrated information theory](../positions/integrated-information-theory.md)
  — Tononi: consciousness is integrated information (Φ), from axioms
  about experience.
- [Biological naturalism](../positions/biological-naturalism.md) — Seth
  2025: consciousness depends on being a living, self-maintaining system,
  not on computation as such.

The survey's related rows: a hard problem exists, 62.42 % yes / 29.76 %
no; physicalism 51.93 % / non-physicalism 32.08 %; zombies metaphysically
possible 24.41 %, conceivable but not possible 36.52 %, inconceivable
16.40 % (same source).

## Arguments in play

- [The knowledge argument](../arguments/knowledge-argument.md) — Mary the
  colour scientist: "she had all the physical information. Ergo there is
  more to have than that, and Physicalism is false"
  ([Jackson 1982](https://doi.org/10.2307/2960077), p. 130).
- [The zombie argument](../arguments/zombie-argument.md) — "Zombies are
  conceivable. Whatever is conceivable is possible. Therefore zombies are
  possible" (SEP's simplest version; `raw/kripke-1980-naming-and-necessity-via-sep.md`),
  with Kripke's modal argument as its ancestor.
- [The Chinese room](../arguments/chinese-room.md) — "Instantiating a
  computer program is never by itself a sufficient condition of
  intentionality" ([Searle 1980](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00005756),
  abstract, p. 417); the survey: "doesn't understand" 67.12 %.
- [Leibniz's mill](../arguments/leibniz-mill.md) — walk into the enlarged
  thinking machine "as into a mill" and "find only parts which work one
  upon another, and never anything by which to explain a perception"
  (*Monadology* §17; excerpt: `raw/leibniz-1714-monadology-17-mill-argument.md`).
- The **explanatory gap** — Levine's term, as Chalmers credits it (1995,
  §2). Levine's stated purpose is "to transform Kripke's argument from a
  metaphysical one into an epistemological one": psycho-physical identity
  statements "leave a significant explanatory gap", and "one cannot
  conclude from my version of the argument that materialism is false"
  ([Levine 1983](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0114.1983.tb00207.x),
  p. 354; excerpt: `raw/levine-1983-materialism-and-qualia-explanatory-gap.md`).
  Presented on the [knowledge argument](../arguments/knowledge-argument.md)
  page as the epistemic form of that family.
- The **bat** — Nagel's argument that "an objective, physical theory will
  abandon that point of view" (1974, p. 437); presented on the Nagel
  thinker page rather than as a separate argument page.
- **Multiple realizability** — Putnam's argument from the "wide variety
  of terrestrial creatures seemingly capable of experiencing pain"
  against the brain-state theory (SEP, via `raw/putnam-1967-psychological-predicates-via-sep.md`);
  on the [Functionalism](../positions/functionalism.md) page.

## Framings and reframings

Those who hold the question, as usually posed, is malformed, dissolved or
to be replaced — reported:

- **Ryle:** the mind–body problem is a category-mistake; the dualist
  "official doctrine" is "entirely false … not in detail but in
  principle" (1949, ch. I).
- **Turing:** the question "Can machines think?" is replaced, not answered
  (1950, §1).
- **Place:** the appearance of impossibility comes from the
  "'phenomenological fallacy', the mistaken idea that descriptions of the
  appearances of things are descriptions of the actual state of affairs
  in a mysterious internal environment" (1956, p. 44).
- **Dennett:** there is no Cartesian Theater and "no probe-independent
  fact of the matter" about the conscious state (via SEP).
- **Frankish:** the hard problem is replaced by the illusion problem —
  "not easy but not impossibly hard either" (2016, §4).
- **Seth & Bayne:** the "real problem" — explaining the properties of
  experience in terms of mechanism, without first solving the hard one —
  is the working reframing of much consciousness science (2022, Box 1).
- **Dehaene, Lau & Kouider:** bracket "what it is like" as "beyond the
  scope" and define consciousness operationally as C1 + C2 (2017).
- **Nagel** in the other direction: the reframings above "get it
  obviously wrong" by giving consciousness "little attention" (1974,
  p. 435) — the reframing of the reframings.
