# Computational functionalism

## Normative definition (this implant)

**Computational functionalism** is the view that mental states are defined
by their causal-functional roles within a computational system, and that any
system that instantiates the right functional organisation has the
corresponding mental states, regardless of its physical substrate. The term
names the position that makes artificial consciousness a live empirical
question rather than a conceptual impossibility; it also names the position
that Searle's Chinese Room, Block's China brain, and biological naturalism
target.

Why this definition: computational functionalism is the implicit assumption
behind most AI consciousness research (Butlin et al. 2023 explicitly adopt
it as a working assumption, p. 4). It is also the target of the strongest
objections from biological naturalists (Searle), dualists (Chalmers on the
hard problem), and embodied/enactive theorists (Varela, Thompson & Rosch).
The implant uses the term to name the position under debate, not to endorse
it. Pages in [positions](../positions/index.md) state their stance
explicitly.

## Descriptive definitions

- **Putnam 1967:** mental states are functional states of a computational
  system; pain is defined by its causal role, not by its neural realiser
  ("Psychological Predicates", in *Art, Mind, and Religion*, ed. Capitan
  & Merrill, pp. 37–48). Putnam later abandoned this view (*Representation
  and Reality*, 1988).
- **Fodor 1968:** mental states are computational states; the mind is a
  Turing machine realised by the brain (*Psychological Explanation*).
- **Lewis 1972:** functionalism as a reductionist programme; mental terms
  are defined by their causal roles in a folk-psychological theory
  ("Psychophysical and Theoretical Identifications", *Australasian Journal
  of Philosophy* 50(3), pp. 249–258).
- **Block 1978:** distinguishes psychological functionalism (mental states
  are whatever plays the right causal role in cognitive science) from
  commonsense functionalism (mental states are whatever plays the right
  role in folk psychology); argues that neither captures qualia
  ("Troubles with Functionalism", in *Perception and Cognition*, ed.
  Savage, pp. 261–325). See [functionalism](../positions/functionalism.md).
- **Butlin et al. 2023:** adopt computational functionalism as a working
  assumption for the purpose of identifying AI consciousness indicators;
  explicitly note that the assumption is contested and that the indicators
  are conditional on it ("Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence",
  arXiv 2308.08708, p. 4).
- **Searle 1980:** rejects computational functionalism via the Chinese Room
  argument; syntax is not sufficient for semantics, and computation alone
  cannot produce understanding or consciousness ("Minds, Brains, and
  Programs", *BBS* 3(3), pp. 417–457). See
  [Chinese Room](../arguments/chinese-room.md).
- **Chalmers 1996:** functionalism explains the easy problems but not the
  hard problem; phenomenal consciousness is not logically supervenient on
  functional organisation (*The Conscious Mind*, ch. 3). See
  [zombie argument](../arguments/zombie-argument.md).

## Collisions

- **Computational functionalism vs. biological naturalism.** Searle holds
  that consciousness is a biological phenomenon caused by specific
  neurobiological processes; silicon instantiation of the same program
  would not produce consciousness. See
  [biological naturalism](../positions/biological-naturalism.md).
- **Computational functionalism vs. identity theory.** Identity theory holds
  that mental states are identical to neural states, not multiply realisable
  functional states. Place (1956), Smart (1959). See
  [physicalism](../positions/physicalism.md).
- **Computational functionalism vs. embodied/enactive cognition.** Varela,
  Thompson & Rosch argue that cognition is constituted by bodily interaction
  with the environment, not by internal computation alone (*The Embodied
  Mind*, 1991). This challenges the substrate-independence claim.
- **Computational functionalism vs. integrated information theory.** IIT
  holds that consciousness depends on specific causal structures (Φ), not
  on functional organisation; feed-forward systems can be functionally
  identical to conscious systems while having Φ = 0. See
  [IIT](../positions/integrated-information-theory.md).
