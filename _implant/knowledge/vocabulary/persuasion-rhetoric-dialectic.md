# Persuasion, rhetoric, dialectic, argumentation

## Normative definition (this implant)

These four terms name distinct practices that overlap in real discourse but
must be kept separate for the implant's purposes:

- **Persuasion** is any communicative act aimed at changing someone's belief
  or behaviour. It may or may not involve reasons; it may or may not be
  truthful. The implant catalogues persuasion techniques in
  [persuasion](../persuasion/index.md) without judging whether they are
  legitimate.
- **Rhetoric** is the art or study of effective communication. Classical
  rhetoric includes invention, arrangement, style, memory, and delivery;
  modern rhetoric extends to visual and digital media. Rhetoric is neutral
  about truth — a well-crafted speech may be true or false.
- **Dialectic** is structured exchange aimed at testing claims. Socratic
  elenchus, Hegelian dialectic, and pragma-dialectics are distinct species.
  Dialectic aims at truth or at least at exposing error, but its methods
  vary across traditions.
- **Argumentation** is the normative study of how claims are supported,
  challenged, and resolved in discourse. It encompasses formal validity,
  informal strength, dialogical rules, and pragmatic context.

Why this split: the implant's skills treat these as separate domains.
[Analyse-an-argument](../../skills/analyse-an-argument.md) reconstructs
arguments for validity checking;
[spot-persuasion-and-bias](../../skills/spot-persuasion-and-bias.md)
inventories persuasion techniques with their replication records;
[write-persuasively](../../skills/write-persuasively.md) lets the user
choose which techniques to employ and annotates where each was used;
[debate](../../skills/debate.md) structures dialectical exchange. Conflating
them would make it impossible to distinguish a valid argument from an
effective speech, or to let a user choose persuasion techniques audibly.

## Descriptive definitions

### Persuasion

- **Social psychology:** "the process by which a message induces change in
  beliefs, attitudes, or behaviours" (Eagly & Chaiken, *The Psychology of
  Attitudes*, 1993, p. 1). The Elaboration Likelihood Model distinguishes
  central-route (reason-based) from peripheral-route (cue-based) persuasion
  (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986).
- **Aristotle:** persuasion (*peithō*) operates through ethos (character),
  pathos (emotion), and logos (reason) (*Rhetoric* I.2, 1356a). All three
  are legitimate modes; none is privileged as "real" persuasion.
- **Ordinary usage:** often pejorative ("mere persuasion"). The implant uses
  the neutral sense and records pejorative uses when cited.

### Rhetoric

- **Classical:** the art of persuasive speaking, systematised by Aristotle,
  Cicero, and Quintilian. Five canons: invention, arrangement, style,
  memory, delivery.
- **New Rhetoric (Perelman & Olbrechts-Tyteca):** rhetoric is the study of
  argumentation addressed to an audience, aiming at adherence rather than
  demonstration (*The New Rhetoric*, 1958/1969).
- **Burkean:** rhetoric is symbolic action aimed at inducing cooperation
  through identification (*A Rhetoric of Motives*, 1950).
- **Contemporary:** extends to visual, digital, and multimodal
  communication. The implant records specific rhetorical analyses when
  cited but does not maintain a general rhetoric taxonomy.

### Dialectic

- **Socratic / Platonic:** elenchus — cross-examination aimed at exposing
  contradiction in an interlocutor's beliefs. Plato's early dialogues are
  the primary sources.
- **Aristotelian:** reasoning from *generally accepted* opinions (*endoxa*)
  toward first principles (*Topics* I.1, 100a18–b25).
- **Hegelian / Marxist:** thesis–antithesis–synthesis as a dynamic of
  historical or conceptual development. The triad is a later
  simplification; Hegel's own terms were abstract/negative/concrete.
- **Pragma-dialectics:** critical discussion governed by ten rules for
  reasonable discourse (Van Eemeren & Grootendorst, *A Systematic Theory
  of Argumentation*, 2004). Violations are classified as fallacies.

### Argumentation

- **Informal logic:** the study of natural-language arguments, their
  evaluation, and their reconstruction. Johnson & Blair, "Informal Logic
  and the Reconfiguration of Logic" (1987).
- **Pragma-dialectics:** see above under dialectic.
- **Computational argumentation:** formal models of argument structure,
  defeat, and acceptability in AI. Dung, "On the Acceptability of Arguments"
  (1995); Bench-Capon & Dunne, "Argumentation in Artificial Intelligence"
  (2007).

## Collisions

- **Persuasion vs. argument.** A persuasive text may contain no argument; an
  argument may persuade no one. The implant keeps them separate so that
  validity checking and persuasion inventorying are independent operations.
- **Rhetoric vs. manipulation.** Rhetoric is neutral about truth;
  manipulation is persuasion that deceives or coerces. The implant records
  manipulation classifications when attributed but does not classify
  rhetoric as manipulation in its own voice.
- **Dialectic vs. debate.** Debate is a species of dialectic with adversarial
  structure and external adjudication. The implant's
  [debate](../../skills/debate.md) skill implements several dialectical
  formats; not all dialectic is debate.
