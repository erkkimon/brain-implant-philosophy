# Global workspace theory

A scientific theory of consciousness, reported under
[Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md)
and graded by [How claims are graded](../../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md).
Its neuronal form is abbreviated GNWT; its cognitive ancestor GWT. One of
the [positions](index.md) on the [consciousness hub](../problems/consciousness.md).

## Thesis

Dehaene & Naccache's statement, from the abstract of the 2001 paper —
the only part of that paper the implant holds; the body is paywalled and
was not read (excerpt: `raw/dehaene-naccache-2001-global-neuronal-workspace.md`):

> "This framework postulates that, at any given time, many modular
> cerebral networks are active in parallel and process information in an
> unconscious manner. An information becomes conscious, however, if the
> neural population that represents it is mobilized by top-down
> attentional amplification into a brain-scale state of coherent activity
> that involves many neurons distributed throughout the brain."
> ([Dehaene & Naccache 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(00)00123-2),
> abstract)

The identity claim in one sentence: "We postulate that this global
availability of information through the workspace is what we
subjectively experience as a conscious state." (same abstract). Lineage:
the raw file records Bernard J. Baars (1988), *A Cognitive Theory of
Consciousness*, as the origin of the idea (bibliographic only) and
reports that Seth & Bayne 2022 describe Baars 1988 as "the first GWT of
consciousness" framed at a cognitive level.

## Answers the problem

The [consciousness hub](../problems/consciousness.md), and specifically
its *access* question — which information is "available for use in
reasoning and rationally guiding speech and action" (Block 1995,
abstract; excerpt: `raw/block-1995-phenomenal-and-access-consciousness.md`).
Seth & Bayne sort the theory accordingly: global workspace theories
"focus on the functional and behavioural properties associated with
consciousness; although they can be viewed as addressing the hard
problem, this is not the primary goal of their proponents"
([Seth & Bayne 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-022-00587-4), Box 1;
excerpt: `raw/seth-bayne-2022-theories-of-consciousness.md`). The
machine case is on [Could an AI be conscious?](../problems/ai-consciousness.md).

## Canonical statements

- **The three constraints (2001).** "(1) a considerable amount of
  processing is possible without consciousness, (2) attention is a
  prerequisite of consciousness, and (3) consciousness is required for
  some specific cognitive tasks, including those that require durable
  information maintenance, novel combinations of operations, or the
  spontaneous generation of intentional behavior." (abstract)
- **The anatomy (2001).** "Neurophysiological, anatomical, and
  brain-imaging data strongly argue for a major role of prefrontal
  cortex, anterior cingulate, and the areas that connect to them, in
  creating the postulated brain-scale workspace." (abstract) — the
  sentence the 2025 adversarial test bears on.
- **C0 / C1 / C2 (2017).** "the word 'consciousness' conflates two
  different types of information-processing computations in the brain:
  the selection of information for global broadcasting, thus making it
  flexibly available for computation and report (C1, consciousness in
  the first sense), and the self-monitoring of those computations,
  leading to a subjective sense of certainty or error (C2, consciousness
  in the second sense)"; current machines "are still mostly implementing
  computations that reflect unconscious processing (C0)"
  ([Dehaene, Lau & Kouider 2017](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan8871),
  abstract, p. 486; excerpt: `raw/dehaene-lau-kouider-2017-c1-c2-machines.md`).
- **The working hypothesis (2017).** "What we call 'consciousness'
  results from specific types of information-processing computations,
  physically realized by the hardware of the brain." (pp. 491–492)

## Varieties

- **Cognitive GWT** — Baars 1988; no Baars text is held.
- **Global neuronal workspace (GNWT)** — Dehaene & Naccache 2001: the
  idea located in "many neurons distributed throughout the brain" with a
  prefrontal and anterior-cingulate emphasis (abstract).
- **C1 with C2** — Dehaene, Lau & Kouider 2017 pair global broadcasting
  (C1) with self-monitoring (C2); C2 is the point of contact with
  [higher-order thought](higher-order-thought.md).
- **The AI-indicator form** — Butlin, Long et al. derive "GWT-1: Multiple
  specialised systems capable of operating in parallel (modules); GWT-2:
  Limited capacity workspace, entailing a bottleneck in information flow
  and a selective attention mechanism; GWT-3: Global broadcast:
  availability of information in the workspace to all modules; GWT-4:
  State-dependent attention"
  ([Butlin, Long et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708), Table 1,
  p. 5; excerpt: `raw/butlin-long-2023-ai-consciousness-indicators.md`).

## The case for

- **It synthesises the three constraints.** Dehaene & Naccache present the
  workspace as "a theoretical framework that synthesizes those facts"
  (2001, abstract).
- **Loss covaries with loss.** Dehaene, Lau & Kouider close by "noting
  that empirically, in humans the loss of C1 and C2 computations covaries
  with a loss of subjective experience", and hold that "the empirical
  evidence is compatible with the possibility that consciousness arises
  from nothing more than specific computations" (2017, p. 492).
- **It yields computational indicators.** Butlin et al. count it among
  their "best-supported neuroscientific theories" and derive GWT-1 to
  GWT-4 as properties an AI system can be assessed for (2023, abstract
  and Table 1).
- **It is one of Chalmers' six requirements.** Chalmers lists "global
  workspace" among six candidate requirements for consciousness and
  counts it among "perhaps the strongest objections" to consciousness in
  current LLMs ([Chalmers 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07103), §3,
  §4; excerpt: `raw/chalmers-2023-could-an-llm-be-conscious.md`) — a
  case for the theory's relevance on his "mainstream assumptions", not a
  claim that it is true.

## The case against

- **The posterior "hot zone".** Koch, Massimini, Boly & Tononi report
  "recent findings showing that the anatomical neural correlates of
  consciousness are primarily localized to a posterior cortical hot zone
  that includes sensory areas, rather than to a fronto-parietal network
  involved in task monitoring and reporting"
  ([Koch et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2016.22), abstract,
  p. 307; excerpt: `raw/koch-massimini-boly-tononi-2016-ncc-hot-zone.md`),
  and that the P3b wave has "not shown any predictive power" (p. 307).
  This is the rival prediction to the 2001 prefrontal sentence; two of
  the four authors are IIT proponents.
- **The 2025 adversarial test.** The Cogitate Consortium (n = 256; fMRI,
  MEG, intracranial EEG; predictions preregistered with the proponents)
  reports: "GNWT is challenged by the general lack of ignition at
  stimulus offset and limited representation of certain conscious
  dimensions in the prefrontal cortex"
  ([Cogitate 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1), abstract;
  excerpt: `raw/cogitate-2025-adversarial-test-and-fleming-2023-pseudoscience-letter.md`).
  The same abstract says the results "align with some predictions of IIT
  and GNWT, while substantially challenging key tenets of both".
- **It answers access, not experience.** Block: phenomenal consciousness
  "is experience", access consciousness is "availability for use in
  reasoning and rationally guiding speech and action", and "These
  concepts are often partly or totally conflated, with bad results"
  (1995, abstract). Seth & Bayne's sorting (Box 1) locates global
  workspace on the functional side of that line.

## Replies

- **The hard problem is bracketed by design.** Dehaene, Lau & Kouider
  concede that "such a purely functional definition of consciousness may
  leave some readers unsatisfied", ask "Does subjective experience escape
  a computational definition?", and answer that "those philosophical
  questions lie beyond the scope of the present paper" (2017, p. 492).
- **On the adversarial result.** The excerpts hold no reply from the
  GNWT proponents to the 2025 abstract; the paper's body, which reports
  each preregistered prediction's pass/fail status, was not read. The
  raw file says the popular telling that both theories were tested and
  both failed, and the telling that IIT was partially supported, "are
  each partial" (Cogitate raw file, context note).
- **On the hot zone.** No reply to Koch et al. 2016 is held; the 2025
  test was the agreed venue for the front-versus-back dispute
  (preregistration: Melloni, Mudrik, Pitts, Bendtz et al. 2023,
  [PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0268577), bibliographic
  only).

## Who holds it

- **Baars** — *A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness* (1988), bibliographic
  only.
- **Dehaene & Naccache** — *Cognition* 79 (2001), abstract held.
- **Dehaene, Lau & Kouider** — *Science* 358 (2017).
- **Butlin, Long et al.** — as one of the theories behind the 2023
  indicator list, under computational functionalism adopted "as a
  working hypothesis" (2023, p. 3).
- **Survey:** the 2020 PhilPapers "Consciousness" question offers dualism,
  eliminativism, functionalism, identity theory and panpsychism; there is
  no row for global workspace theory (excerpt:
  `raw/bourget-chalmers-2023-philpapers-survey-consciousness-and-amcs-2023-open-letter.md`).
  Functionalism, the nearest family, drew 33.04 % (N = 1020).

## Contrasts

- **[Integrated information theory](integrated-information-theory.md)** —
  the rival in the 2025 test. The divergence is anatomical (prefrontal
  workspace versus posterior hot zone) and, in Seth & Bayne's sorting,
  one of target: IIT "address[es] the hard problem directly", GWT does
  not make that its "primary goal" (2022, Box 1). The 2017 paper's
  surmise that "mere information-theoretic quantities" do not suffice
  is stated, per the raw file's context note, in contrast to IIT.
- **[Higher-order thought](higher-order-thought.md)** — C2 is the
  workspace theory's own higher-order component; the divergence is
  whether broadcast (C1) or the higher-order state makes a state conscious.
- **[Functionalism](functionalism.md)** — the metaphysics the 2017
  "simple hypothesis" assumes (pp. 491–492).
- **[Biological naturalism](biological-naturalism.md)** — Seth's reply to
  that assumption: "The fact that computational functionalism is
  frequently assumed does not mean it is true" (Seth 2025, §3; excerpt:
  `raw/seth-2025-biological-naturalism-and-conscious-ai.md`).

A structural note the implant adds, not a verdict (manifest
[G4](../../vision/manifest.md)): every proponent statement held here
either brackets "what it is like" (2017, p. 492) or is sorted by a
survey as not aiming at it (2022, Box 1); every objection held is either
anatomical (2016, 2025) or the charge that access is not experience
(1995). The two sides meet on the anatomy, not on the phenomenal question.

## Vocabulary

*Consciousness* in Block's access sense (C1) and, in the 2017 paper, a
second sense C2 (self-monitoring); *global availability* / *global
broadcast*; *ignition*; *neural correlate of consciousness* — Koch et
al.'s "minimum neural mechanisms sufficient for any one specific
conscious percept" (2016, p. 307); *computational functionalism* (Butlin
et al.'s sense). All open work in [vocabulary](../vocabulary/index.md).
