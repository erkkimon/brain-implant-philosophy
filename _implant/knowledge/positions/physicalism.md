# Physicalism

A position page of the [positions](index.md) branch, written under
[Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md)
and graded by [How claims are graded](../../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md).

## Thesis

In its narrowest form: "Sensations are nothing over and above brain
processes" ([Smart 1959](https://doi.org/10.2307/2182164), p. 145;
excerpt: `raw/smart-1959-sensations-and-brain-processes.md`). In the
broader form the 2020 PhilPapers survey groups under "physicalism": every
real, concrete phenomenon in the universe is physical — a claim about
which Smart's reason was Occam's razor: "science is increasingly giving
us a viewpoint whereby organisms are able to be seen as physico-chemical
mechanisms" and sensations would otherwise be "'nomological danglers'"
(p. 142).

## Answers the problem

[What is consciousness, and how does it relate to the physical?](../problems/consciousness.md)
— the phenomenal question, answered by saying there is nothing left over
once the physical account is complete. The access question is answered
trivially on this view.

## Canonical statements

- **Place 1956:** "'Consciousness is a process in the brain' … is neither
  self-contradictory nor self-evident; it is a reasonable scientific
  hypothesis, in the way that the statement 'lightning is a motion of
  electric charges' is a reasonable scientific hypothesis"
  ([Place 1956](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0114.1956.tb00207.x), §II,
  p. 45; excerpt: `raw/place-1956-is-consciousness-a-brain-process.md`).
  The "is" is "the 'is' of composition", not definition (§II, p. 45).
- **Smart 1959:** "All it claims is that in so far as a sensation
  statement is a report of something, that something is in fact a brain
  process. Sensations are nothing over and above brain processes" (p. 145).
- **Strawson 2006:** "I take physicalism to be the view that every real,
  concrete phenomenon in the universe is … physical" (p. 3); but "you're
  certainly not a realistic physicalist … if you deny the existence of
  the phenomenon whose existence is more certain than the existence of
  anything else: experience" (p. 3; excerpt:
  `raw/strawson-2006-realistic-monism.md`).

## Varieties

- **Type identity theory** (Place, Smart): each type of mental state is
  identical with a type of brain state.
- **Token identity theory**: each token mental event is identical with a
  token physical event, without type-type commitments.
- **Reductive physicalism**: mental properties are reducible to physical
  ones.
- **Non-reductive physicalism**: mental properties supervene on physical
  ones without being reducible.
- **"Real physicalism"** (Strawson 2006): physicalism that accepts
  experience as real and concludes some ultimates are "intrinsically
  experience-involving" — which Strawson calls micropsychism or
  panpsychism (p. 25). On his account, what is usually called physicalism
  is not.

## The case for

- **Occam's razor.** Smart: "There does seem to be, so far as science is
  concerned, nothing in the world but increasingly complex arrangements of
  physical constituents. All except for one place: in consciousness"
  (p. 142).
- **The phenomenological fallacy.** Place: "the mistaken idea that
  descriptions of the appearances of things are descriptions of the actual
  state of affairs in a mysterious internal environment" (§VI, p. 49).
- **Causal closure.** Not quoted in the excerpts held; the argument is
  standard in the literature but absent from this batch.

## The case against

- **Kripke's modal argument.** Pain cannot stand to a brain state as heat
  stands to molecular motion: "when we think that molecular motion might
  exist in the absence of heat we are confusing this with thinking that
  the molecular motion might have existed without being felt as heat"; no
  such qualitative analogue is available for pain (SEP "Mind/Brain Identity
  Theory" Fall 2024, §8, reporting Kripke 1980 pp. 151–152; excerpt:
  `raw/kripke-1980-naming-and-necessity-via-sep.md`).
- **Levine's explanatory gap.** "Psycho-physical identity statements leave
  a significant explanatory gap … One cannot conclude from my version of
  the argument that materialism is false, which makes my version a weaker
  attack than Kripke's" ([Levine 1983](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0114.1983.tb00207.x),
  p. 354; excerpt: `raw/levine-1983-materialism-and-qualia-explanatory-gap.md`).
  "What is left unexplained by the discovery of C-fiber firing is why pain
  should feel the way it does" (p. 357).
- **Nagel's point of view.** "Every subjective phenomenon is essentially
  connected with a single point of view, and it seems inevitable that an
  objective, physical theory will abandon that point of view"
  ([Nagel 1974](https://doi.org/10.2307/2183914), p. 437; excerpt:
  `raw/nagel-1974-what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat.md`). Nagel adds: "It would
  be a mistake to conclude that physicalism must be false" (p. 445).
- **Jackson's knowledge argument.** Mary learns something on release;
  "Physicalism is false" ([Jackson 1982](https://doi.org/10.2307/2960077),
  p. 130; excerpt: `raw/jackson-1982-epiphenomenal-qualia-mary.md`).
  Jackson later gave the argument up (SEP "Qualia: The Knowledge Argument"
  §5, as recorded in the jackson raw file).
- **Multiple realizability.** Putnam: the "wide variety of terrestrial
  creatures seemingly capable of experiencing pain" argues against any
  single brain-state type (SEP "Functionalism" Fall 2024, §3.1, reporting
  Putnam 1967; excerpt: `raw/putnam-1967-psychological-predicates-via-sep.md`).

## Replies

- **To Kripke:** Smart's identity-theorist replies are named in SEP §8
  (counterpart contextualism; "having a pain") but not quoted in the
  excerpts held.
- **To Levine:** the gap is epistemological, not metaphysical — Levine's
  own framing (p. 354). Whether that saves physicalism is the open
  question.
- **To Jackson:** Jackson himself (2003) endorses representationalism as
  the answer (SEP §5). Dennett denies qualia outright (SEP "Consciousness"
  §9.4; excerpt: `raw/dennett-1991-consciousness-explained-via-sep.md`).
- **To Nagel:** Nagel himself does not conclude physicalism is false
  (p. 445).

## Who holds it

Survey 2020 (N = 1733; [Bourget & Chalmers 2023](https://doi.org/10.3998/phimp.2109)):
physicalism 51.93 % accept or lean toward / non-physicalism 32.08 %.
Identity theory specifically: 13.33 % (N = 1020). Thinkers: Place 1956,
Smart 1959; Armstrong, Lewis, and others are not in the excerpts held.

## Contrasts

- **[Dualism](dualism.md):** denies the identity. Descartes' real
  distinction is the classical form; Chalmers 1995 is the modern one.
- **[Functionalism](functionalism.md):** mental states are defined by
  causal role, not by substrate; Putnam's multiple-realizability is the
  bridge from identity theory to functionalism.
- **[Panpsychism](panpsychism.md):** Strawson's "real physicalism"
  collapses into panpsychism — "physicalism … entails panexperientialism
  or panpsychism" (p. 25).
- **[Illusionism](illusionism.md):** denies that experience has the
  phenomenal properties physicalism struggles to reduce; Frankish 2016.
- **Buddhist not-self (contrast, not identity).** The SEP entry "Mind in
  Indian Buddhist Philosophy" (Fall 2024) reports that Buddhists hold
  human beings "reducible to the physical and psychological constituents
  and processes which comprise them" (preamble), but this is a doctrine of
  not-self (*anātman*), not a physicalist thesis about the nature of
  consciousness; the reduction is to aggregates (*skandhas*), not to
  matter. Excerpt: `raw/sep-mind-indian-buddhism-fall-2024-not-self-and-consciousness.md`.
