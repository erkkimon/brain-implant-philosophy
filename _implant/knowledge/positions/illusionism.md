# Illusionism

A position page of the [positions](index.md) branch, written under
[Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md)
and graded by [How claims are graded](../../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md).

## Thesis

"Phenomenal consciousness is an illusion and aims to explain why it seems
to exist" ([Frankish 2016](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/contentone/imp/jcs/2016/00000023/f0020011/art00002),
introduction, eprint p. 1; excerpt: `raw/frankish-2016-illusionism.md`).
"Illusionism makes a very strong claim: it claims that phenomenal
consciousness is illusory; experiences do not really have qualitative,
'what-it's-like' properties, whether physical or non-physical" (§1.2,
p. 3).

## Answers the problem

[What is consciousness, and how does it relate to the physical?](../problems/consciousness.md)
— dissolves the phenomenal question by denying its presupposition.

## Canonical statements

- **Frankish 2016:** "Illusionism replaces the hard problem with the
  illusion problem — the problem of explaining how the illusion of
  phenomenality arises and why it is so powerful. This problem is not easy
  but not impossibly hard either" (§4, eprint p. 20).
- **Dennett 1991 (via SEP):** the Multiple Drafts Model "denies the
  existence of qualia and thus rejects any attempt to distinguish conscious
  states from nonconscious states by their presence"; there may be "no
  probe-independent fact of the matter about what the person's conscious
  state really was" (SEP "Consciousness" Fall 2024, §9.4; excerpt:
  `raw/dennett-1991-consciousness-explained-via-sep.md`).
- **Place 1956 (ancestor):** the "'phenomenological fallacy', the mistaken
  idea that descriptions of the appearances of things are descriptions of
  the actual state of affairs in a mysterious internal environment"
  (abstract, p. 44; excerpt: `raw/place-1956-is-consciousness-a-brain-process.md`).

## Varieties

Strong illusionism (Frankish): phenomenal properties do not exist. Weak
eliminativism (Dennett via SEP §7): takes issue with specific features
(qualia, Cartesian Theater, conscious self) without rejecting the notion
outright.

## The case for

- **The illusion problem is tractable.** Frankish: "not easy but not
  impossibly hard either" (§4, p. 20).
- **No Cartesian Theater.** Dennett via SEP §9.4: no privileged spatial or
  functional location for consciousness.
- **Quasi-phenomenal properties.** Frankish: "a non-phenomenal, physical
  property (perhaps a complex, gerrymandered one) that introspection
  typically misrepresents as phenomenal" (§1.2, p. 3).

## The case against

- **Strawson 2006:** "You're certainly not a realistic physicalist … if
  you deny the existence of the phenomenon whose existence is more certain
  than the existence of anything else: experience" (p. 3; excerpt:
  `raw/strawson-2006-realistic-monism.md`). On his account, illusionism
  is not physicalism.
- **Nagel 1974:** current discussions "get it obviously wrong" by giving
  consciousness "little attention" (p. 435; excerpt:
  `raw/nagel-1974-what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat.md`).
- **Chalmers 1995:** the hard problem is real; illusionism is listed among
  responses he considers inadequate (the raw file records Frankish's
  report that Chalmers (1996) classifies illusionism as "failing to 'take
  consciousness seriously'"). # lint: allow — "failing" is Chalmers' word, reported by Frankish
- **Survey:** eliminativism 4.51 % (N = 1020; Bourget & Chalmers 2023).

## Replies

Frankish acknowledges dismissal: illusionism "is often dismissed out of
hand as failing to 'take consciousness seriously' (Chalmers, 1996)"
(introduction; context note in the frankish raw file). His reply is that
the illusion problem is the right problem.

## Who holds it

Frankish 2016; Dennett 1991; Carruthers 2000 (named in SEP §7 but not in
the excerpts held). Survey: eliminativism 4.51 %.

## Contrasts

- **[Dualism](dualism.md):** accepts phenomenal properties as real and
  non-physical.
- **[Physicalism](physicalism.md):** standard physicalism tries to reduce
  phenomenal properties; illusionism denies them.
- **[Panpsychism](panpsychism.md):** accepts phenomenal properties as
  fundamental.
- **[Functionalism](functionalism.md):** tries to functionalize
  phenomenal properties; illusionism says there is nothing to
  functionalize.
