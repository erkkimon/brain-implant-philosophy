# Panpsychism

A position page of the [positions](index.md) branch, written under
[Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md)
and graded by [How claims are graded](../../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md).

## Thesis

"Mentality is fundamental and ubiquitous in the natural world" (SEP
"Panpsychism" Fall 2024, §2.1, reporting Goff; excerpt:
`raw/goff-2017-panpsychism-via-sep.md`). Strawson's form: "real
physicalists must accept that at least some ultimates are intrinsically
experience-involving. They must at least embrace micropsychism"
([Strawson 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199267422.003.0003),
p. 25; excerpt: `raw/strawson-2006-realistic-monism.md`).

## Answers the problem

[What is consciousness, and how does it relate to the physical?](../problems/consciousness.md)
— answers the phenomenal question by making experience fundamental rather
than emergent.

## Canonical statements

- **Goff (via SEP):** "For its proponents panpsychism offers an attractive
  middle way between physicalism on the one hand and dualism on the other"
  (preamble). The "simplicity argument": "in the absence of any reason to
  suppose otherwise, the most simple, elegant, parsimonious hypothesis is
  that the matter outside of brains is continuous with the matter of
  brains" (§2.1).
- **Strawson 2006:** "Physicalism, i.e. real physicalism, entails
  panexperientialism or panpsychism. All physical stuff is energy, in one
  form or another, and all energy, I trow, is an experience-involving
  phenomenon" (pp. 25–26).

## Varieties

Micropsychism (ultimates have experience), cosmopsychism (the cosmos as a
whole has experience), Russellian monism (experience is the intrinsic
nature of physical properties). Distinctions from SEP §2 but not fully
quoted in the excerpts held.

## The case for

- **Simplicity.** Goff via SEP §2.1: the simplest hypothesis is continuity
  between brain-matter and non-brain matter.
- **No brute emergence.** Strawson: if physical stuff were "wholly and
  utterly non-experiential" (thesis NE, p. 11), experience would have to
  emerge brutely, which he rejects.
- **Leibniz's mill as ancestor.** Perception "must be sought for" in a
  simple substance, "not in a compound or in a machine" (*Monadology* §17;
  excerpt: `raw/leibniz-1714-monadology-17-mill-argument.md`). Whether
  Leibniz is a panpsychist is disputed; the structural parallel is noted
  here as a structural note the implant adds (manifest
  [G4](../../vision/manifest.md)).

## The case against

- **The combination problem.** How do micro-experiences combine into
  macro-experiences? Named in Goff via SEP as the main objection; details
  not quoted in the excerpts held.
- **Survey:** panpsychism 7.55 % (N = 1020; Bourget & Chalmers 2023).
- **Non-experiential physicalism.** Most physicalists reject premise NE's
  negation; they hold that experience can emerge from non-experiential
  ultimates without brute emergence. Not quoted in the excerpts held.

## Replies

Not in the excerpts held. Goff's book addresses the combination problem;
the raw file holds only the SEP summary.

## Who holds it

Goff 2017; Strawson 2006; historically James, Russell, Whitehead (named
in SEP but not in the excerpts held). Survey: 7.55 %.

## Contrasts

- **[Physicalism](physicalism.md):** standard physicalism denies
  experience-involving ultimates; Strawson says this is not "real"
  physicalism.
- **[Dualism](dualism.md):** panpsychism avoids substance dualism by
  making experience a property of physical ultimates.
- **[Illusionism](illusionism.md):** denies experience exists; panpsychism
  makes it fundamental.
- **[Functionalism](functionalism.md):** substrate-neutral; panpsychism
  ties experience to specific kinds of physical stuff.
