# Biases

Cognitive biases and heuristics: what was claimed, by whom, on what evidence,
and whether it replicated. The heuristics-and-biases literature is uneven —
some effects are among the most replicated in psychology, some did not
replicate, some are contested at the level of interpretation — and the
implant reports that unevenness rather than presenting a tidy list.

Empirical claims here are graded ([How claims are graded](../../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md)):
the canonical study, the effect size, the replication record, each cited to
a DOI. The critique of the programme that named the biases (e.g. the
ecological-rationality school) is content, attributed, on every page it
bears on.

```
## The effect as claimed
The original description, quoted or closely paraphrased, with the canonical
study (DOI).

## Evidence
Effect size and conditions from the canonical study; replication record
(direct replications, meta-analyses, registered reports), each cited; the
evidence grade with the reason for any downgrade.

## Boundary conditions
When the effect appears and when it does not, per the literature.

## Interpretations and critique
Named alternative explanations (e.g. that the "bias" is adaptive, or an
artefact of the task), attributed.

## How to spot it
Markers the literature gives, in reasoning or in text.

## Debiasing
Interventions that have been tested, with their evidence, cited.

## Exploited by
Links to [persuasion](../persuasion/index.md) techniques a source says use
this bias.
```
