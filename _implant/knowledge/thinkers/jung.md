# Carl Gustav Jung

## Facts a claim depends on

- **Dates and places.** Michael Fordham's *Dictionary of Scientific Biography* entry heads: "(b. Kesswil, Switzerland, 26 July 1875; d. Küsnacht, Switzerland, 6 June 1961)"; Jolande Jacobi (*International Encyclopedia of the Social Sciences*) calls him "founder of analytical psychology" (both via Encyclopedia.com; excerpts: `raw/encyclopedia-com-jung-reference-entries.md`).
- **Training and the break with Freud.** MacIntyre (*Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 1967) reports: "He became the first president of the International Psychoanalytic Society in 1911. In 1914 he broke with Freud, founding his own school of analytical psychology." (`raw/macintyre-1967-encyclopedia-of-philosophy-jung.md`). Fordham gives the same year: "in 1914 he formally severed all connections with psychoanalysis to from his own school of analytical psychology." (wording as on the page).
- **Philosophical reading.** Fordham: "He also studied the Greek philosophers, Hegel, and Schopenhauer, whose relation to Kant drew his attention; later, at the University of Basel, he became fascinated by Nietzsche." Jacobi lists among those with "a decisive influence upon him" Heraclitus, Plato, Aristotle, Augustine, Aquinas, Eckhart, Schopenhauer, Nietzsche and Freud.
- **A caution on the sources.** Shamdasani (*New Dictionary of Scientific Biography*) reports that in 1960 "Jung formally stated that the work should not be regarded as an autobiography, but rather as a work by Jaffé to which he had made contributions" — of *Memories, Dreams, Reflections*, which older accounts treat as autobiography. Structural note (G4): this page takes no biographical fact from that book.

## Problems addressed

- **Mind and the unconscious.** Jung: the personal unconscious "rests upon a deeper layer", the collective unconscious, which "is, in other words, identical in all men" (CW 9i, paras. 3–4; `raw/jung-1934-1954-archetypes-of-the-collective-unconscious-cw9i.md`). Jacobi lists his "concept of the psyche as a self-regulating system, in which the conscious and the unconscious realms are compensatorily related." See [archetype and collective unconscious](../vocabulary/archetype-and-collective-unconscious.md). On the mind–matter question Atmanspacher (SEP 2024, §5.2) files the Pauli-Jung conjecture under dual-aspect monism (see Reception); the [consciousness](../problems/consciousness.md) page does not yet cover it.
- **Religion and the God-image.** Jung: "It is psychologically true in as much as it exists." (1938, p. 3, of the idea of the virgin birth) and "there is no question of belief, but of experience. Religious experience is absolute. It is indisputable." (p. 113). MacIntyre reports his view of belief: "Religious beliefs, Jung conceded, cannot be shown to be true; but he held that they cannot be shown to be false, either." Jung traces "archetype" to Philo, "with reference to the Imago Dei (God-image) in man" (CW 9i, para. 5). Fordham: in his last years Jung "linked the Judaic and Christian traditions in a highly original way." The work: [Answer to Job](../works/answer-to-job.md) (CW 11, pp. 355–470, per Jacobi's bibliography).
- **Typology.** Introversion and extraversion and the four functions: [Psychological Types](../works/psychological-types.md) (1921).
- **Synchronicity and causality.** Jung proposes "a hypothetical factor equal in rank to causality as a principle of explanation." (1955, pp. 26–27; `raw/jung-1952-synchronicity-acausal-connecting-principle.md`). See [synchronicity](../vocabulary/synchronicity.md).
- **The self and individuation.** "Individuation, therefore, is a process of differentiation , having for its goal the development of the individual personality." (*Psychological Types*, trans. Baynes 1923, Def. 29, p. 561; spacing as in the scan; `raw/jung-1923-psychological-types-individuation-baynes.md`). Fordham: Jung "developed his theory of the self as a motive force in history". See [individuation](../vocabulary/individuation.md).

## In dialogue with

- **Freud:** collaborator until the 1914 break (MacIntyre; Fordham).
- **Kant, Schopenhauer, Nietzsche:** read in his youth (Fordham); Bishop (2000) treats Kant in relation to synchronicity; Bishop (2016) pairs Nietzsche's superman with Jung's shadow (publishers' descriptions). Jung himself calls "archetype" "an explanatory paraphrase of the Platonic" form (CW 9i, para. 5).
- **William James:** tender- and tough-minded types, discussed in *Psychological Types* (see that page).
- **Wolfgang Pauli:** co-author of *Naturerklärung und Psyche* (1952); the Pauli-Jung conjecture (Atmanspacher, SEP §5.2).
- **Martin Buber:** the 1952 *Merkur* exchange; Zank and Braiterman report that "Jung didn’t read Buber, whereas Buber read Jung extensively." and that Jung said Buber "“was ignorant of psychiatric observation,”" (§7).
- **Anthony de Mello:** Choe (2025) reads de Mello's *Sadhana* through Jung — see [de Mello](de-mello.md) and [Sadhana](../works/sadhana.md).

## Reception

### Philosopher or not: who says what

Whether Jung belongs in a philosophy implant is itself reported here, not
decided ([Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md)).
Admission rests on the thinkers' rule in the [branch index](index.md): read
sources put his claims on problems this implant holds.

**Jung's own statement (against the label).** In the 1937 Terry Lectures:
"Notwithstanding the fact that I have often been called a philosopher, I am an empiricist and adhere to the phenomenological standpoint."
(*Psychology and Religion*, 1938, p. 1); "I approach psychological matters
from a scientific and not from a philosophical standpoint." (p. 2); and
"I restrict myself to the observation of phenomena and I refrain from any application of metaphysical or philosophical considerations."
(p. 2; `raw/jung-1938-psychology-and-religion-empiricist.md`). Fordham
reports the same self-understanding: "He was anxious that his ideas and practices be considered part of the general development of psychological science and that they not be taken dogmatically."

**Readers who treat him in philosophy.**
- MacIntyre wrote the Jung entry of the *Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
  (1967), whose bibliography (updated by Ney, 2005) lists Pauson (ed.),
  *Jung the Philosopher: Essays in Jungian Thought* (1988).
- Bishop (2000), published in the series "Problems in contemporary
  philosophy": "This study examines the filiation of a philosophical concept in relation to its use by the major 20th century thinker C.G. Jung."
  and "It shows how Jung's theory of synchronicity stems from a long and deep preoccupation with such central themes as the mind-body problem."
  (publisher's description; `raw/jung-reception-bishop-noll-campbell-records.md`).
  Bishop (2016) pairs Jung's shadow with Nietzsche's superman (same file).
- Atmanspacher (SEP, "Quantum Approaches to Consciousness", Fall 2024, §5.2)
  places the Pauli-Jung conjecture among dual-aspect positions:
  "The Pauli-Jung conjecture is clearly related to scenario (B) of Sec. 2, combining an epistemically dualistic with an ontically monistic approach."
  (`raw/sep-qt-consciousness-fall-2024-pauli-jung-conjecture.md`).
- Zank and Braiterman (SEP, "Martin Buber", Fall 2026, §6) name Jung beside
  Sartre and Heidegger as a target of Buber's philosophical anthropology
  (`raw/sep-buber-fall-2026-jung-exchange.md`).
- Fordham counts "the philosophy of science" among the fields his influence
  reached (excerpt file above).

**Readers who hold his claims outside philosophy or science.**
- Buber, as Zank and Braiterman report (§7), argues "that Jung’s statements about the psychic origin of religious beliefs exceed the strict confines of empirical psychology"; Jung, they report, dismissed such critics as "“naturally incapable of understanding”" him.
- MacIntyre (1967, "Criticism") judges that "At the root of the problem lies an ambiguous set of ontological claims."
- Shamdasani resituates Jung's work in "networks of debates and discussions in European and American psychology, psychiatry, philosophy, and the human sciences", and holds that a critical edition is needed "before his work can be adequately assessed."

Structural note (G4): the readers above disagree on the *category* of
Jung's claims (empirical, philosophical, or overstepping both); no read
source settles it, and this page does not.

### For and against his claims

**For (published favourable assessments):**
- MacIntyre (1967): "Of all Jung's work his classification of types of personality as extravert or introvert has won the widest acceptance." and other concepts "can also be tested experimentally" (as "it may well be").
- Fordham (DSB): "His theory of types has been subjected to further experimental investigation, with more support for the basic attitude types than for the function types."
- Atmanspacher (SEP 2024, §6): "there is now a huge body of empirically documented (ordinary and exceptional) mind-matter correlations in clinical psychology, psychophysiology, and psychophysics that supports the Pauli-Jung conjecture in particular."
- Neher (1996, abstract): "the critique suggests that Jung's belief in the genetic basis of certain unconscious content holds some promise." ([doi:10.1177/00221678960362008](https://doi.org/10.1177/00221678960362008); `raw/neher-1996-jungs-theory-of-archetypes-critique.md`).

**Against (published critical assessments):**
- MacIntyre (1967): "the question of whether the collective unconscious exists cannot be answered by any possible observation or experiment." and "there are as yet no grounds for believing any of its propositions that go beyond recording empirical data".
- Shields (*Encyclopedia of Science, Technology, and Ethics*): "His belief in synchronicity, a non-causal linkage of mental and physical phenomena, has also been criticized as speculative and without scientific foundation." (the critics are not named).
- Atmanspacher (SEP 2024, §5), of the Pauli-Jung and related lineages: "They are based on speculations that obviously exceed the scope of contemporary quantum theory."
- Neher (1996, abstract): "These notions of Jung's are found to be seriously flawed."
- Frye (1957, pp. 111–112) calls the collective unconscious "an unnecessary hypothesis in literary criticism, so far as I can judge." (`raw/frye-1957-anatomy-of-criticism-archetype.md`).
- Diaconis and Mosteller (1989, §1) report: "Jung argued that meaningful coincidences occur far more frequently than chance allows." and give the "law of truly large numbers" (§7) ([doi:10.1080/01621459.1989.10478847](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1989.10478847); `raw/diaconis-mosteller-1989-coincidences-and-synchronicity.md`).
- Noll (1994), as Anthony Campbell summarises him: "Analytical psychology, the system of treatment founded by Jung, is a cult, not a scientifically based therapy." Campbell in turn judges of Noll that "his presentation of his case is so strongly biased as to be unreliable." (`raw/jung-reception-bishop-noll-campbell-records.md`; Noll's book itself not read).
