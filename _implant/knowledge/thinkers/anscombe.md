## Facts a claim depends on

- **Dates.** "She was born in Limerick, Ireland 18 March 1919" and "Anscombe died in Cambridge on 5 January 2001" (Wiland & Driver, SEP §1);
  IEP §1 gives the same dates.
- **Wittgenstein.** "after his death in 1951, the chief translator of his work into English" (SEP §1); "Wittgenstein died in 1951, having named
  Anscombe as one of three literary executors of his estate" (IEP §1).
- **Post.** "She later had a Teaching Fellowship there until 1970, when she became the Professor of Philosophy at Cambridge University" (IEP §1;
  "there" is Somerville College, Oxford).
- **Foot.** A friendship with [Philippa Foot](foot.md) "her entire life (Mac Cumhaill & Wiseman 2022)" (SEP §1).
- **The 1956 protest**, the occasion of MTD and, per Haldane (SEP §4), of *Intention*: "In 1956 she publicly opposed Oxford University’s
  decision to award an honorary degree to Harry Truman, whom she considered a murderer for intentionally killing the civilians of Hiroshima and
  Nagasaki, Japan" (SEP §1). Richter (IEP §1): Intention was "published the following year, followed by the critical “Modern Moral Philosophy”
  the year after that" — 1957 and 1958.
- **Dating discrepancies in the source.** SEP §2 dates "Mr. Truman's Degree" to 1958 and "War and Murder" to 1972; the SEP bibliography gives
  "War and Murder" in Stein (ed.), *Nuclear Weapons: a Catholic Response*, "London: Merlin, 1961, 43–62". This page uses 1956 for the protest
  (SEP §1) and 1961 for "War and Murder" (SEP bibliography).

## Problems addressed

- **The moral "ought" and the state of moral philosophy.** MMP opens: "I will begin by stating three theses which I present in this paper."
  First, moral philosophy "should be laid aside at any rate until we have an adequate philosophy of psychology, in which we are conspicuously
  lacking"; second, the moral "ought" and moral obligation "ought to be jettisoned if this is psychologically possible; because they are
  survivals, or derivatives from survivals, from an earlier conception of ethics which no longer generally survives, and are only harmful
  without it"; third, "the differences between the well-known English writers on moral philosophy from Sidgwick to the present day are of
  little importance" (MMP, PDF p. 1). Richter summarises her conclusion: "Anscombe’s conclusion is that talk of moral obligation, moral duty, a
  special moral sense of the word ought, and so on, are in fact meaningless" (IEP §6).
- **Consequentialism (the word).** Sidgwick's "denial of any distinction between foreseen and intended consequences, as far as responsibility is
  concerned" explains, she writes, "the difference between old-fashioned Utilitarianism and that consequentialism, as I name it, which marks
  him and every English academic moral philosopher since him" (MMP, PDF p. 10). Wiland & Driver: "the consequentialism (a term she invented) of
  later English philosophers" (SEP §2). Richter: "it was coined by Anscombe (in “Modern Moral Philosophy”) precisely to distinguish a certain
  kind of moral theory from utilitarianism" (IEP §5). Her ground, per Wiland & Driver: "the consequentialist view of responsibility ultimately
  rests on a false view of what it is to act intentionally" (SEP §2).
- **Absolute prohibitions.** The "Hebrew-Christian ethic" teaches "that there are certain things forbidden whatever consequences threaten, such
  as choosing to kill the innocent for any purpose, however good;" (MMP, PDF p. 8). Coady reads MMP as "an early contemporary defence of
  absolutism against the balanced exceptionism of much (she thought all) of the then current moral philosophy" (SEP "The Problem of Dirty
  Hands" §7; `raw/sep-dirty-hands-fall-2024-coady-map.md`). Related: [dirty hands](../problems/dirty-hands.md),
  [moral dilemmas](../problems/moral-dilemmas.md), [Jim and the Indians](../problems/jim-and-the-indians.md),
  [consistency arguments against moral dilemmas](../arguments/consistency-arguments-against-moral-dilemmas.md).
- **[Just war](../problems/just-war.md) and double effect.** "Mr. Truman's Degree": "choosing to kill the innocent as a means to your ends is
  always murder" (MTD, 66, quoted in SEP §2). "War and Murder": she "distinguished choosing to kill the innocent as a means to your end, which
  is always murder, from choosing to do something else which you know as a statistical certainty will kill the innocent", and "the distinction
  between the intended and the merely foreseen is “absolutely essential” to Christian ethics" (SEP §2). Richter: "In her essay on “War and
  Murder,” she defends the so-called Doctrine of Double Effect and rejects pacifism" (IEP §7); on his reading, "Causing some collateral damage
  does not make one a murderer as long as one is engaged in a just war, fighting justly, (intentionally) targeting only legitimate military
  targets, and not launching attacks whose effects are likely to be more bad than good" (IEP §7). The conditions:
  [Double effect: the conditions](../arguments/double-effect-conditions.md).
- **Pacifism.** "But while pacifism is misguided, so too is the view that the state or its officers may do anything to defend itself" (SEP §2,
  reporting WAM). Her words as Fiala quotes them: "pacifism teaches people to make no distinction between the shedding of innocent blood and the
  shedding of any human blood" (Anscombe 1981a, 58, per SEP "Pacifism" §4.1;
  `raw/sep-pacifism-fall-2024-fiala-absolute-contingent-just-war-pacifism.md`). Earlier, "a pamphlet of which she was joint author (with Norman
  Daniel) protesting at the prospect of the Second World War in 1939" (IEP §7).
- **[The trolley problem](../problems/trolley-problem.md).** McIntyre (SEP "Doctrine of Double Effect" §4.5) places her among "those who uphold
  an absolutist version of the principle of double effect and deny that it provides a permission to swerve the trolley (Anscombe, 1982)"
  (`raw/sep-double-effect-and-doing-allowing-fall-2024-trolley.md`).
- **Intention (action theory).** Haldane, quoted in SEP §4: "Perplexed by defenders of Truman she came to the conclusion that they had failed to
  understand the nature of his actions, and it was this that led her to write Intention, in which she pointed out that in doing one thing
  (moving one’s hand) one may intentionally be doing another (directing the death of human beings). (Haldane 2000, 1020)"

## In dialogue with

- **Sidgwick (earlier):** the target of MMP's third thesis and, in her account, the source of the foreseen/intended denial (MMP, PDF p. 10).
- **Truman's defenders (1956):** the occasion of MTD and, per Haldane (SEP §4), of *Intention*.
- **[Philippa Foot](foot.md) and Iris Murdoch (contemporaries):** "Anscombe’s thought here resembled that of her contemporaries Philippa Foot and
  Iris Murdoch" (SEP §2).
- **[Bernard Williams](williams.md) (later):** Chappell & Smyth deny that Williams's Jim must see shooting as under "the ban of some absolute
  prohibition that holds (in Anscombe’s famous phrase) whatever the consequences" (SEP "Bernard Williams" §4;
  `raw/sep-williams-bernard-fall-2024-integrity-objection.md`).
- **Crisp 2004 (later):** "Crisp 2004 suspected that she was surreptitiously arguing for the superiority of a God-based moral philosophy over any
  secular moral philosophy, even one like Aristotle’s" (SEP §2).
- **Glover 2001 (later):** the ferry objection to double effect (IEP §7; Reception).
- **Coady (later):** the absolutism reading (Problems addressed, above).
- **[Judith Jarvis Thomson](thomson.md) and McIntyre (later):** placed beside her in McIntyre's three camps on the trolley problem (§4.5).

## Reception

Every assessment below belongs to the author named.

- **Standing.** Wiland & Driver: "Gertrude Elizabeth Margaret Anscombe was one of the most important philosophers of the twentieth century" (SEP
  preamble). Richter: "an important twentieth century philosopher and one of the most important women philosophers of all time" (IEP intro).
- **MMP and virtue ethics.** Linking them: Richter, "Contemporary interest in virtue theory can be traced directly to this paper" (IEP intro), and
  her rejection of consequentialism and obligation-centered deontology "has been a major reason for the rise of virtue ethics" (IEP §6).
  Qualifying the link: Wiland & Driver, "No doubt Anscombe’s claims in MMP inspired some philosophers to develop modern versions of what’s now
  known as virtue ethics", but "Anscombe herself did not advocate this development" (SEP §2).
- **MMP's manner.** Wiland & Driver: "Each of Anscombe’s three theses is bold; taken together, they are shocking" (SEP §2);
  "MMP touched a nerve with the philosophers she targeted. One reason for this was the rather dismissive tone she took in some of her criticisms." (SEP §2);
  "she brusquely and perhaps uncharitably attacks Butler, Hume, Kant, Bentham, and Mill" (SEP §2).
- **MMP's sincerity.** Crisp 2004's suspicion (above). Wiland & Driver: "In a sense, Crisp is right", followed by two reasons ending "Her call to
  give moral philosophy a rest until we better understand the philosophy of psychology was addressed to us all, not just to a subset of moral
  philosophers" (SEP §2).
- **Double effect, redescription.** Against: "Critics of the doctrine sometimes object that one can make an otherwise forbidden act all right by
  simply redescribing it in one’s mind" (IEP §7). For: Richter answers that an intention is not a private mental act and concludes
  "So this criticism seems to be misguided" (IEP §7). Wiland & Driver quote the target she herself attacked, "The devout Catholic bomber secures
  by a “direction of intention” that any shedding of innocent blood that occurs is “accidental.”" (WAM, 59), and report "This teaching strikes
  Anscombe as quite absurd" (SEP §2).
- **Double effect, the ferry.** Against: "Perhaps more serious is Jonathan Glover’s objection that blowing up a passenger ferry bound for
  Nazi-occupied territory with both innocent people and the means for making an atomic bomb on it would have to count as intentional murder"
  (IEP §7, citing Glover 2001, p. 108). For: "Anscombe might reply, however, that the actual goal in this case is preventing the Nazis from
  acquiring atomic weapons" (IEP §7; Richter's conjecture, not her text).
- **Consequentialism.** Her own: "It is a necessary feature of consequentialism that it is a shallow philosophy" (MMP, PDF p. 10). Richter's
  reconstruction of her second objection ends "Consequentialists then are almost inevitably in bad faith" (IEP §5). No reply by a
  consequentialist to MMP is held in raw/ yet — a recorded gap.
- **Intention.** Wiland & Driver: "Anscombe’s Intention is one of the classics of 20th century philosophy" and "contemporary theories of action
  are often categorized as either Anscombean or Davidsonian" (SEP §4). Richter: "Although not easy to understand, it has been enormously
  influential" (IEP §4).

## Vocabulary

- *Consequentialism.* In MMP, the denial of a distinction "between foreseen and intended consequences, as far as responsibility is concerned"
  (PDF p. 10). Richter: "Consequentialism is the denial that there is any significant moral difference between results of action that are
  brought about intentionally and those that are foreseen but not intended" (IEP §5). Wiland & Driver: "Consequentialism, as she explains, is
  not directly a doctrine about what makes an action right – this meaning came about only later in the recent history of philosophy" (SEP §2).
  No vocabulary entry yet ([vocabulary](../vocabulary/index.md)).
- *Moral "ought".* The sense MMP's second thesis would jettison (PDF p. 1).
- *Murder.* "choosing to kill the innocent as a means to your ends" (MTD, 66, via SEP §2).
