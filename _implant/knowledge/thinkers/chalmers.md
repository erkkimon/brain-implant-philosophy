# David J. Chalmers

## Facts a claim depends on

Born 1966. Australian-American. Standard biographical facts given as
background; not established from the excerpts held.

## Problems addressed

- **[Consciousness](../problems/consciousness.md):** named the hard
  problem (1995); gave credences on LLM consciousness (2023); co-authored
  the 2020 PhilPapers survey (2023). Position: naturalistic dualism or
  panprotopsychism (the raw files do not quote these labels; only the hard
  problem and the credences are grounded here).

## In dialogue with

- **Nagel 1974:** "As Nagel (1974) has put it" (§2 of the 1995 paper).
- **Levine 1983:** credits "explanatory gap" to Levine (§2).
- **Jackson 1982:** the knowledge argument as part of the same family.
- **Dennett 1991 (via SEP):** illusionism as a response to the hard
  problem; Frankish 2016 reports Chalmers 1996 dismisses illusionism as
  failing to "take consciousness seriously".
- **Frankish 2016:** illusionism replaces the hard problem with the
  illusion problem.
- **Seth & Bayne 2022:** the "real problem" as a reframing that brackets
  the hard problem.
- **Butlin et al. 2023:** Chalmers is not an author; the report adopts
  computational functionalism as working hypothesis, which Chalmers' six
  obstacles partially challenge.
- **Anthropic 2025:** describes him as "arguably the best-known and most
  respected living philosopher of mind" ("Exploring model welfare";
  excerpt: `raw/anthropic-2024-2026-model-welfare-statements.md`). This is
  Anthropic's description, attributed.

## Reception

- **For:** the hard problem framing dominates the field; the 2020 survey
  shows 62.42 % accept a hard problem. Anthropic cites his work as
  motivating their welfare programme.
- **Against:** Dennett and Frankish reject the hard problem's presuppositions.
  Seth & Bayne reframe it as the "real problem". The survey shows 29.76 %
  reject the hard problem.
- **Credence note:** Chalmers' "under 10 %" and "25 % or more" figures are
  explicitly conditional on "mainstream assumptions"; his own credences
  are "somewhat higher" (n. 29 of the 2023 paper). Popular summaries that
  give "10 %" as his own view omit this.
