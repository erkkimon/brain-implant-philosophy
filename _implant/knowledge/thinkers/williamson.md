  ([philosophy.ox.ac.uk](https://www.philosophy.ox.ac.uk/people/timothy-williamson),

## Problems addressed

- **[Sorites paradox](../problems/sorites-paradox.md): epistemicism.**
  Hyde & Raffman
  ([SEP Fall 2024 "Sorites Paradox"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/sorites-paradox/),
  §3.2; excerpt `raw/sep-sorites-and-liar-fall-2024-paradox-formulations-and-responses.md`)
  list him among those for whom "vague terms have sharp boundaries whose locations are hidden from us"
  (Williamson 1994a,b, 2000). Three parts of the view, as the encyclopedias report them (S):
  - *Why the boundary is unknown.* Sorensen
    ([SEP Fall 2024 "Vagueness"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/vagueness/)):
    "Timothy Williamson (1994) traces our ignorance of the threshold for childhood to “margin for error” principles."
    Without the margin, "one is right by luck".
  - *What fixes the boundary.* Hyde & Raffman §3.2: "According to Williamson (e.g., 1994b: 184), meaning supervenes on use; in other words, the locations of the sharp boundaries of a vague term are a function of speakers’ dispositions to use it as they do."
  - *Why bivalence.* Hyde & Raffman §3.2 report that retaining classical
    logic and bivalence "is supposed to be a chief advantage of the epistemic approach over other views (e.g., Williamson 1992: 162)";
    §5.2 reports his argument that rejecting bivalence alongside the
    T-schema "is said to lead to absurdity (Williamson 1994a: ch. 7; see Wright 1994 for criticism)."
- **Knowledge ([vocabulary](../vocabulary/knowledge.md)): knowledge first.**
  Ichikawa & Steup
  ([SEP Fall 2024 "The Analysis of Knowledge"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/knowledge-analysis/),
  §11; S): "In recent work, especially his 2000 book Knowledge and Its Limits, Timothy Williamson has argued that the project of analyzing knowledge was a mistake."
  Among the claims they report he accepts: "Knowledge is the most general factive mental state."
  Safety is kept as a necessary condition: "And Williamson (2000: 126) is explicit in endorsement of a safety requirement on knowledge—just not one that serves as part of an analysis."
- **Self-knowledge: anti-luminosity.** Gertler
  ([SEP Fall 2024 "Self-Knowledge"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/self-knowledge/);
  S) reports "Williamson’s (2000) “anti-luminosity” argument, which seeks to establish that no mental states are luminous."
- **[Fitch's paradox of knowability](../problems/fitchs-paradox-of-knowability.md).**
  Brogaard & Salerno
  ([SEP Fall 2024 "Fitch's Paradox of Knowability"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/fitch-paradox/); F)
  report four contributions: "Williamson (1982) argues that Fitch’s proof is not a refutation of anti-realism, but rather a reason for the anti-realist to accept intuitionistic logic." (§3.2);
  "Williamson (1993) and Jago (2010) have shown that versions of the paradox do not require this distributive assumption." (§3.1; "have shown" is the SEP authors' wording);
  "Williamson (2000b) defends Fitch’s reasoning against Kvanvig’s charge." (§4.4);
  and a counter-example to Tennant's restriction: "Williamson (2000a) asks us to consider the following paradox." (§5.3).
  On the status of the result: "Timothy Williamson (2000b) says the knowability paradox is not a paradox; it’s an “embarrassment”––an embarrassment to various brands of antirealism that have long overlooked a simple counterexample." (preamble).
- **[Surprise examination paradox](../problems/surprise-examination-paradox.md):
  KK failure.** Murzi, Eichhorn & Mayr 2021 (excerpt
  `raw/murzi-eichhorn-mayr-2021-surprise-exam-kk-is-innocent.md`): "Williamson (2000, Ch. 6) recommends instead blaming the students' reliance on KK."
  Hemp (IEP, §3; excerpt `raw/hemp-iep-kk-principle-surprise-examination.md`)
  gives the locators "Williamson 1992: 226-32 and 2000:135-146".
- **[Moore's paradox](../problems/moores-paradox.md) and the
  [lottery](../problems/lottery-and-preface-paradoxes.md): the knowledge
  norm of assertion.** Pagin & Marsili (SEP "Assertion", §5.1.4; excerpt
  `raw/sep-assertion-fall-2024-moores-paradox-and-knowledge-norm.md`):
  "Concerning the first, Williamson (2000: 253) claims that an utterance of (26) is just as odd as any ordinary Moorean sentence involving belief, such as (25) above."
  Douven & Williamson 2006 generalise a counter-example to defeater
  accounts of the lottery (excerpt
  `raw/sep-formal-belief-fall-2024-lockean-thesis-and-lottery.md`).

## In dialogue with

- **Sorensen 1988, 2001; Graff Fara 2000,
  2008:** fellow epistemicists in Hyde & Raffman's list (§3.2).
- **Wright 1994:** cited by Hyde & Raffman (§5.2) as a criticism of the
  T-schema argument; **Burgess 2001, Weatherson 2003** debate the symmetry
  reply (§5.1).
- **Weatherson 2004, Duncan 2018:** arguments that some states are
  luminous, described by Gertler as "responses to" anti-luminosity (S).
- **Fitch 1963, Kvanvig 1995 and 2006, Tennant 1997 and 2001a, Edgington
  1985:** the knowability debate (F); Tennant's reply is titled "Is Every
  Truth Knowable? Reply to Williamson", *Ratio* 14(3), 2001, 263–280,
  [doi:10.1111/1467-9329.00162](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9329.00162).
- **Sorensen 1988, Wright & Sudbury 1977, Kripke 2011:** the blindspot /
  knowledge-retention diagnosis of the surprise examination, which Murzi,
  Eichhorn & Mayr report Williamson "dismisses" as "incomplete".
- **Goldman:** Williamson 2009: 305 on safety is "in response to a
  challenge by Alvin Goldman" (Ichikawa & Steup, safety section; S).
- **[Priest](priest.md), [Tarski](tarski.md):** sibling pages on the
  paradoxes of truth; the T-schema argument above is stated with Tarski's

## Reception

For and against, each as its author states it.

- **For epistemicism:** Hyde & Raffman (§3.2, S): "The virtues and the appeal of the epistemic theory are significant, and it has earned its share of supporters."
- **Against epistemicism:** the same authors: "Even its proponents grant that epistemicism is intuitively implausible; and it seems to multiply mysteries."
  and "Nevertheless, epistemicism may have the feel of a “just so” story."
  Sorensen (S) calls the consequence that there is a sharp threshold "astonishing" while reporting that epistemicists accept it.
- **For knowledge first:** Pagin & Marsili list DeRose, Reynolds, Adler,
  Hawthorne, Stanley, Engel, Schaffer and Turri as favouring the knowledge
  norm "Over and above Williamson" (raw assertion excerpt).
- **Against knowledge first:** Ichikawa & Steup (§11, S): "There is no very straightforward argument for this conclusion; its case consists largely in the attempted demonstration of the theoretical success of the knowledge first stance."
- **On Fitch:** Brogaard & Salerno report his "embarrassment" reading and
  add "Others disagree." (F); they report Tennant 2001a's verdict that
  "Williamson has not shown that TKP is an inadequate treatment of Fitch’s paradox."
- **On the surprise examination:** Murzi, Eichhorn & Mayr 2021 argue
  "KK is innocent" against his diagnosis; Hemp (IEP §3) calls the iteration
  suggestion "promising" (Hemp's assessment).
- **Secondary volumes:** the Oxford profile (O) names *Williamson on
  Knowledge* (Greenough & Pritchard, 2009) and *Williamson on Modality*
  (McCullagh & Yli-Vakkuri, 2017) as containing "essays on his work with
  his replies".

## Vocabulary

- *Epistemicism* — vagueness as ignorance of sharp boundaries (Sorensen,
  Hyde & Raffman).
- *Margin for error* — the principle behind inexact knowledge (Sorensen;
  Hemp §4).
- *Knowledge first*, *factive mental state*, *luminous* — see
  [knowledge](../vocabulary/knowledge.md), whose knowledge-first entry
  cites *Knowledge and Its Limits* (2000).
- *KK principle* — knowing that one knows (Hemp, IEP).
