# Thomas Nagel

## Facts a claim depends on

Born 1937. American. The excerpts held do not establish dates from a
primary reference; these are standard biographical facts given as
background.

## Problems addressed

- **[Consciousness](../problems/consciousness.md):** the phenomenal
  question. Position: the paper defines conscious experience by the
  "something it is like" criterion and argues that objective physical
  theory abandons the point of view on which subjective phenomena depend.
  Does not conclude physicalism is false (p. 445).

## In dialogue with

- **Smart 1959 and Place 1956:** the reductive analyses Nagel says are
  "logically compatible with its absence" (p. 436).
- **Jackson 1982:** extends the point-of-view problem into the knowledge
  argument.
- **Levine 1983:** the explanatory gap as the epistemic form of Nagel's
  problem.
- **Chalmers 1995:** "As Nagel (1974) has put it, there is something it is
  like to be a conscious organism" (§2; excerpt:
  `raw/chalmers-1995-facing-up-hard-problem.md`).
- **Block 1995:** "what it is like" as the mark of P-consciousness
  (abstract; excerpt: `raw/block-1995-phenomenal-and-access-consciousness.md`).
- **Frankish 2016:** denies "'what-it's-like' properties" exist (§1.2;
  excerpt: `raw/frankish-2016-illusionism.md`).
- **Dennett 1991 (via SEP):** denies qualia and the Cartesian Theater.

## Reception

- **For:** Chalmers credits Nagel with defining the hard problem's shape.
  Block adopts "what it is like" as the mark of phenomenal consciousness.
- **Against:** Frankish denies the properties Nagel takes as given. Dennett
  rejects the point-of-view framework. Smart and Place offer reductive
  accounts Nagel considers inadequate.
- **The p. 445 hedge:** "It would be a mistake to conclude that physicalism
  must be false" — the paper is not an anti-physicalist proof. This gap
  between popular reception and text is a finding of this implant's reading.
