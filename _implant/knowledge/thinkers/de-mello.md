# Anthony de Mello

This page reports who said what; it does not assert that de Mello was a
philosopher or that his teachings, or the judgements made of them, are true
or false ([Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md);
[How claims are graded](../../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md); [thinkers](index.md)).

## Facts a claim depends on

- **Dates.** The Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith (CDF) gives
  "Father Anthony de Mello (1931-1987)" ([CDF 1998](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19980624_demello_en.html);
  `raw/cdf-1998-notification-writings-de-mello.md`). The Irish Jesuits:
  "Anthony de Mello was born near Mumbai in 1931, into an Indian Catholic family."
  ([Jesuits in Ireland](https://www.jesuit.ie/who-are-the-jesuits/inspirational-jesuits/anthony-de-mello/);
  `raw/jesuits-ireland-de-mello-biography.md`).
- **Formation** (all from the Jesuits in Ireland page unless noted): "At
  the age of sixteen he joined the Jesuits in the seminary of Vinalaya, on
  the outskirts of Mumbai." From 1952 "He studied philosophy in Barcelona
  before undertaking ministry"; he was "ordained a priest there in 1961" at
  De Nobili College, Pune; he took "a master’s degree in pastoral
  counselling at Loyola University" in Chicago; "in 1968 he was made rector
  of the seminary of Vinalaya". The DeMello Spirituality Center adds "a
  postgraduate degree in spiritual theology from Gregorian University in
  Rome" ([Meet Tony](https://www.demellocenter.com/meet-tony);
  `raw/demello-center-and-sadhana-institute-biography.md`).
- **Sadhana Institute.** The sources differ on the year. Jesuits in
  Ireland: "In 1972 de Mello established the Institute of Pastoral
  Counselling at De Nobili College, later renamed the Sadhana Institute."
  The Institute's own history: "Sadhana Institute was founded in Pune by the late Tony de Mello, S.J. in 1973 and was relocated five years later to Lonavla"
  ([Sadhana Institute](https://sadhanainstitute.org/about-us/history/)).
- **Death.** "In 1987, while in New York City where he was giving a
  course, De Mello suffered a heart attack and died." (Jesuits in Ireland).
  Akkara (1998): "who died of a heart attack in 1987 at age 56"
  ([National Catholic Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/indian-jesuits-offer-insight-into-vaticans-censure-of-de-mello);
  `raw/akkara-1998-ncregister-indian-jesuits-de-mello.md`), and as
  "fifty-five" by the DeMello Spirituality Center. Structural note: born
  1931 and dead in 1987, he was 55 or 56 depending on his birthday, which
  the sources read here do not give.
- **Influences reported.** Michael Barnes SJ reports that de Mello told him
  the guiding practice of *Sadhana* "was indeed taken from the Theravada Buddhist tradition of Vipassana or ‘Insight’ meditation."
  and that "Like many post-Vatican II South Asian Christians Tony had done a retreat with S.N.Goenka"
  ([Barnes 2014](https://www.thinkingfaith.org/articles/anthony-de-mello-sj);
  `raw/barnes-2014-thinking-faith-de-mello.md`); the DeMello Center: "His first exposure to the power of awareness came from retreats with the Vipassana teacher S. N. Goenka."

## Problems addressed

No source read places him on a problem page here; they describe his teaching so:

- **Awareness and East–West synthesis.** The DeMello Spirituality Center:
  "Blending Christian mysticism, Eastern philosophy, and psychological insight, his teachings invite people to awaken from conditioned patterns of fear and illusion into a life of presence, freedom, and love."
  Allen (1998): "His many books, tapes and retreats combined traditional
  Christian concepts with insights from Eastern religions."
  ([NCR](https://natcath.org/NCR_Online/archives2/1998c/090498/090498k.htm);
  `raw/allen-1998-ncr-de-mello-censure.md`). The Sadhana Institute: "from
  the first days of Sadhana there was also an emphasis on the integration
  of Christian spirituality with the Indian heritage."
- **Mindfulness as preparation for prayer.** Barnes (2014): "Tony intended the Buddhist mindfulness exercises as no more than an introduction to prayer, a preparation which would build up the right dispositions for listening to the Word of God."
- **Attachment.** Coleman (2010), on de Mello's talks on the Spiritual
  Exercises, reads him as urging people "to move away from inordinate or misplaced attachments (but not from attachment, as such)."
  ([America](https://www.americamagazine.org/content/all-things/anthony-de-mello-be-aware-be-alive-be-love);
  `raw/coleman-2010-america-de-mello-seek-god-everywhere.md`).
- **God and religions (as the CDF reads him):** see Reception.

## Works

- *[Sadhana: A Way to God](../works/sadhana.md)* (1978, per Jesuits in
  Ireland): "outlines spiritual exercises based on those of Saint Ignatius, but blends with them Eastern traditions and practices."
- *[The Song of the Bird](../works/the-song-of-the-bird.md)* (Gujarat
  Sahitya Prakash 1982; `raw/de-mello-1982-song-of-the-bird-preface-and-title-story.md`).
- *[Awareness](../works/awareness.md)* — named by Coleman (2010) with
  *Sadhana* and *The Song of the Bird* among the "earlier writings".
- The nine books the Indian Jesuit publisher called official, as listed by
  Akkara (1998): "Sadhana, The Song of the Bird, Wellsprings, One Minute Wisdom, The Prayer of the Frog Vol. I, The Prayer of the Frog Vol. II, Contact with God, Call to Love and One Minute Nonsense, the last three published posthumously."
- Authorship: the CDF notes "not all of these texts were authorized by him
  for publication".

## In dialogue with

- **Ignatius of Loyola; Vipassana / S. N. Goenka:** see Works and Facts.
- **Christian teachers of Asian meditation:** Barnes (2014) places him on
  "a distinguished list of Christian teachers who have engaged deeply with Asian forms of meditation, from Thomas Merton and Bede Griffiths to William Johnston and Hugo Enomiya Lassalle."
- **Joseph Ratzinger / the CDF** (see Reception): Allen (1998) quotes Ratzinger's 1996 address on Indian and Western
  thought: "“The two philosophies are fundamentally different,” he said in a 1996 address to the doctrinal commissions of Latin American bishops conferences. “Nonetheless, they seem to mutually confirm one another in their metaphysical and religious relativism."
  Barnes (2014): "That process was bound up with the detailed scrutiny of the teaching of Indian theologians that issued in the publication of Dominus Iesus in 2001."
- **[C. G. Jung](jung.md):** Choe (2025) reads *Sadhana* through Jung's
  analytical psychology: "First, based on Jung’s theory, Sadhana pursues wholeness through the union of opposites, such as extraversion and introversion."
  (*Religions* 16(9), 1207, [doi:10.3390/rel16091207](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel16091207);
  abstract; `raw/choe-2025-religions-de-mello-sadhana-jung.md`; see [individuation](../vocabulary/individuation.md)),
  which says Sadhana "integrates psychotherapeutic methods such as Gestalt therapy and the methodology of Vipassana meditation".

## Reception

### The 1998 Notification

Dated "June 24, 1998", signed by Joseph Card. Ratzinger, approved by John Paul II, who "ordered its publication"
(`raw/cdf-1998-notification-de-mello-positions-listed.md`):

- On the early books: "Especially in his early writings, Father de Mello, while revealing the influence of Buddhist and Taoist spiritual currents, remained within the lines of Christian spirituality."
  and they "can be helpful in achieving self-mastery, in breaking the bonds
  and feelings that keep us from being free".
- On the later ones: "But already in certain passages in these early works and to a greater degree in his later publications, one notices a progressive distancing from the essential contents of the Christian faith."
- Positions it attributes to him: "Religions, including Christianity, are one of the major obstacles to the discovery of truth."
  "the personal nature of God is ignored and in practice denied";
  "he considers Jesus as a master alongside others"; "the question of
  destiny after death is declared to be irrelevant"; "since evil is simply ignorance, there are no objective rules of morality."
- Declaration: "With the present Notification, in order to protect the good of the Christian faithful, this Congregation declares that the above-mentioned positions are incompatible with the Catholic faith and can cause grave harm."
- Structural note: the Notification cites no page; its Explanatory Note
  does, e.g. on *Sadhana*: "But already in this work he develops his theory of contemplation as awareness, which seems to be not lacking in ambiguity."
  (`raw/cdf-1998-explanatory-note-de-mello.md`).

### Responses accepting the Notification

- Lisbert d'Souza, Jesuit superior for South Asia (Akkara 1998): "“The Vatican is right because there is the possibility of misunderstandings,”"
  while adding "“Father Tony's books were not doctrinal expositions on theology,”".
- Gispert Sauch SJ (Akkara 1998): "The [Vatican] warning is perfectly justifiable.”" — and he called the books "“basically wisdom literature … his writing [does not offer] doctrine, but advice through stories.”"
- Bishop Oswald Gracias, CBCI, per *Outlook* (1998): "We fully accept the findings of the Church," (`raw/de-mello-books-cautionary-note-and-1998-reception.md`).
- Linus Rego, per *Outlook*: "the writings of Fr Anthony de Mello may work at the level of literature but not as articles of Catholic faith"
  (`raw/outlook-1998-gospel-according-to-de-mello-further-voices.md`).
- Lancy Pereira, per *Outlook*: "There are passages of dubious value in some works officially attributed to him—eg, Call to Love. But these are works that he himself never edited,"

### Responses disputing the Notification

- Francis Stroud SJ (Allen 1998): "“It’s extremely hard for me to believe that anyone would find anything de Mello says to be anything other than orthodox,” said Jesuit Fr. Francis Stroud."
  and "He never denied anything like a personal concept of God."
- Eric Major, Doubleday (Allen 1998): "“It seems rather strange to condemn someone who has no right of reply,”".
- Allwyn D'Silva, per *Outlook*: "Anthony de Mello never denied the
  uniqueness of Christ but simply used stories from the Asian experience."
- Mario Correa, per *Outlook*: "I strongly believe that as in Galileo's case, the Church will live to regret this condemnation."
- John A. Coleman SJ (2010): "No one reading, Seek God Everywhere, would easily understand the strictures or warnings about De Mello by the Congregation of Doctrine and Faith in 1998."
- Martin Fárek (2022), on the "blank" passage of *Sadhana* the CDF quotes: "Instead of interpreting them as a presentation of a void concept of God, we should consider such claims to be practical instructions which try to grasp the experience of silent contemplation."
  ([doi:10.3390/rel13100904](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel13100904); `raw/farek-2022-de-mello-via-negativa.md`).
- Barnes (2014) reports that defenders argued "a man who was a retreat director and not a systematic theologian could hardly be judged by the rigorous standards fit for a university professor."

### Other assessments

- W. Norris Clarke SJ, "a philosopher at Fordham" (Allen 1998): "“That doesn’t have to mean anything unorthodox, although you could read it that way.”"
  of the image of theology as "pointing a finger at the moon".
- The Irish Jesuits' page judges that he "was a hugely important figure in the area of mysticism, melding eastern and western traditions."
  and that the 1998 statement "was poorly received by many for whom his teachings had served to strengthen and deepen their faith."
- Barnes (2014) calls him "a Catholic bodhisattva – literally a ‘being for enlightenment’".
- Choe (2025, abstract): "Anthony de Mello’s Sadhana practice is evaluated as a novel case that presents new possibilities for contemporary Christian spirituality."
- The publisher's 1997 clarification, quoted by Akkara (1998): "“The books of Father Anthony de Mello were written in a multireligious context to help the followers of other religions, agnostics, and atheists in their spiritual search, and they were not intended by the author as manuals of instruction of the Catholic faithful in Christian doctrine or dogma.”"

## Vocabulary

- *Awareness*, *awake*: the CDF reports that for de Mello "Jesus is "awake" and fully free, while others are not"; the DeMello Spirituality Center speaks of "the power of awareness". No source read defines his use; this implant has no normative entry yet ([vocabulary](../vocabulary/index.md)).
- *Attachment*: in Coleman's reading, "inordinate or misplaced attachments", not attachment as such.
- *Apophaticism*: the CDF's term ("This radical apophaticism") for his
  "speaking of God as a pure void" (`raw/cdf-1998-notification-de-mello-positions-listed.md`);
  Fárek (2022) reads the same passages as *via negativa*.
