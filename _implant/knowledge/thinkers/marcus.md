## Facts a claim depends on

- **Born** 2 August 1921, New York: "Marcus was born Ruth Charlotte Barcan in New York on August 2, 1921" (Ballarin, SEP §1). The Chicago Tribune obituary agrees on the year: "Born in New York City in 1921" (`raw/chicago-tribune-2012-ruth-barcan-marcus-obituary.md`).
- **Yale PhD, 1946**: "She started Yale’s graduate program in 1942 and received her PhD in 1946." (SEP §1). SEP's bibliography divides her corpus into "Published as Ruth C. Barcan" and "Published as Ruth Barcan Marcus"; the 1946–1947 *JSL* papers are in the first list. Ballarin: "In this entry Ruth Barcan Marcus is sometimes called Barcan, sometimes Marcus, and sometimes Barcan Marcus, mainly according to the temporal context" (preamble).
- **Posts**: founding chair of philosophy at the University of Illinois at Chicago, 1964; Northwestern; Yale from 1973 to 1992. The obituary: "She went onto to become the founder of the philosophy department at the University of Illinois at Chicago in 1964. After three years at Northwestern University in Evanston, IL, Marcus returned to Yale in 1973, retiring in 1992." SEP §1: "In 1973 Marcus agreed to join and help rebuild the Yale philosophy department"; "Marcus retired from Yale in 1992".
- **Died** 19 February 2012, New Haven: "She died in New Haven on February 19, 2012." (SEP §1); the obituary gives the same date ("Feb. 19, 2012").
- **Source of the biography**: "The main written source of information on Ruth Barcan Marcus’s personal and professional life is her Dewey Lecture “A Philosopher’s Calling” delivered at the Eastern APA meeting on December 29, 2009 (Marcus 2010)." (SEP §1). Marcus 2010 is held bibliographically only (*Proceedings and Addresses of the APA* 84(2): 75–92, per SEP).

## Problems addressed

- **[Can there be genuine moral dilemmas?](../problems/moral-dilemmas.md)**
  Position: dilemmas are real, and their existence does not make a moral code
  inconsistent. In her words: "I WANT to argue that the existence of moral dilemmas, even where the dilemmas arise from a categorical principle or principles, need not and usually does not signify that there is some inconsistency (in a sense to be explained) in the set of principles, duties, and other moral directives under which we define our obligations either individually or socially." (1980, p. 121; `raw/marcus-1980-moral-dilemmas-and-consistency.md`).
  - *Consistency redefined.* "Analogously we can define a set of rules as consistent if there is some possible world in which they are all obeyable in all circumstances in that world." (1980, p. 128). "On the proposed definition, rules are consistent if there are possible circumstances in which no conflict will emerge." (p. 128). Ballarin's gloss: "the consistency of a moral code requires only that there be possible circumstances in which all its demands can be met (1980b: 128)." (SEP §8). McConnell's SEP "Moral Dilemmas" (Fall 2024, §4) quotes the same definition and cites Marcus 1980 for the point that OA and O¬A is "not a contradiction" (`raw/sep-moral-dilemmas-fall-2024-concept-and-consistency-arguments.md`). This is her answer to the [consistency arguments against moral dilemmas](../arguments/consistency-arguments-against-moral-dilemmas.md).
  - *Residue.* Acting on one horn does not cancel the other: "consistency of moral principles or rules does not entail that moral dilemmas are resolvable in the sense that acting with good reasons in accordance with one horn of the dilemma erases the original obligation with respect to the other" (1980, p. 121, within the sentence quoted in the raw file). Ballarin reports the consequence she draws: "Guilt is thus the appropriate moral response, even when the intractability of the circumstances is not our fault." (SEP §8). McConnell names "Williams 1965 and Marcus 1980" as "The best known proponents of arguments for dilemmas that appeal to moral residue" (SEP "Moral Dilemmas" §6; `raw/sep-moral-dilemmas-fall-2024-dirty-hands-and-moral-residue.md`).
  - *Source of dilemmas.* Quoted by Ballarin: "The circumstances of the world conspire against us. However perfect our will, the contingencies are such that situations arise where, if we are to follow one course of action, we will be unable to follow another. (1980b: 127, fn. 6)" (SEP §8).
  - *Second-order principle.* "we ought to conduct our lives and arrange our institutions so as to minimize predicaments of moral conflict." (1980, p. 121). "For dilemmas, when they occur, are data of a kind. They are to be taken into account in the future conduct of our lives." (p. 131).
  - *Deontic logic, 1996.* In "More about Moral Dilemmas", as Ballarin reports: "She now criticizes the axiom \(\neg (OA \land O\neg A)\) as false to the facts of moral dilemmas." and "Deontic operators like epistemic ones are not closed under logical consequence (1996: 29–30)." (SEP §8). The 1996 text is held bibliographically only.
- **[Dirty hands](../problems/dirty-hands.md)** — linked through McConnell's §6 residue sentence above; Ballarin (SEP §8) quotes Marcus 1996: "Such considerations are particularly appropriate to the question of the inevitability of dirty hands in public life." (1996: 33).
- **Quantified modal logic** (no problem page yet). Ballarin's assessment: "Her first 1946 publication contains the first published construction of formal systems of quantified modal logic." (SEP preamble). The Barcan formula, axiom 11 of the 1946 system, ◇(∃α)A ⥽ (∃α)◇A in SEP's notation: "System S2 is not strong enough to prove BF, which is thus assumed as an axiom." (SEP §2.1). The name is Prior's: "the Barcan formula, as Prior will later dub it (1956: 60)." (SEP §2.1).
- **Reference of proper names** (no problem page yet). Marcus 1961, as quoted by Ballarin: "This tag, a proper name, has no meaning. It simply tags. (1961: 310)" (SEP §4.3).

## In dialogue with

- **[Bernard Williams](williams.md)** — the other proponent of the residue argument named by McConnell (§6, above). Marcus 1980 cites him: "Bernard Williams. in Problems of the Self (New York: Cambridge. 1977). chs. II and 12. also recognizes that the source of some apparent inconsistencies in imperatives and rules is to be located in the contingency of their simultaneous inapplicability on a given occasion." (p. 127, fn. 8). Ballarin (SEP §8): "Williams (1973) is a precursor of the view that dilemmas arise from contingent circumstances."
- **Ross, Lemmon, Hare, Rawls, Davidson** — the view she answers, per Ballarin: "Marcus criticizes the dominant view of moral dilemmas which is based on an excessively demanding notion of consistency. She attributes this view in some form or other to W.D. Ross, John Lemmon, Hare, Rawls and Davidson." (SEP §8).
- **[Philippa Foot](foot.md)** — "Foot (1983) is critical of Marcus on guilt." (Ballarin, SEP §8). Foot, "Moral Realism and Moral Dilemma", *J. Phil.* 80(7), 1983, pp. 379–398, DOI [10.2307/2026455](https://doi.org/10.2307/2026455) (verified); held bibliographically only here.
- **W. V. Quine** — on quantified modal logic: "Quine (1946, 1947b and 1947c) had originally reviewed her first three papers, and the friction with Quine was probably triggered by his initial review of her 1947 paper on the identity of individuals in modal systems" (Ballarin, SEP §3).
- **Saul Kripke** — on the necessity of identity: "Burgess (2014) emphasizes the differences between Barcan’s and Kripke’s proofs of the necessity of identity and argues that Kripke’s distinct proof may ultimately derive from Quine’s (1953b, 1953c, 1961) search for a simpler proof than Barcan’s." (SEP §2.3). On names, Marcus in 1978, as quoted by Ballarin: "Kripke provided us with a “picture” which is far more coherent than what had been available." (SEP §4.3).
- **Arthur Prior** — named the Barcan formula (1956: 60, per SEP §2.1).
- **[Timothy Williamson](williamson.md)**, Linsky and Zalta, Simchen — on what the Barcan formula commits one to: "In contrast to Marcus, Linsky and Zalta 1996, and Williamson 1995 and 1998 interpret BF as committed to the existence of possibilia. Simchen 2013 supports Marcus’s actualist reading; Williamson 2013b rejects both interpretations." (SEP §6). Williamson also wrote "In Memoriam: Ruth Barcan Marcus 1921–2012", *Bulletin of Symbolic Logic* 19(1), 2013, pp. 123–126, DOI [10.2178/bsl.1901070](https://doi.org/10.2178/bsl.1901070) (verified; not read).

## Reception

Assessments, each with its owner; for and against are listed in parallel,
unranked.

- **Standing, per Ballarin (SEP 2024).** "Ruth Barcan Marcus (1921–2012) was one of the most influential philosophers of the second half of the twentieth century." (preamble). "In the fifties and sixties, in formidable opposition to Quine, she crucially contributed to establishing the legitimacy of modal logic and thus of modal metaphysics." (preamble). "Her philosophical views—about language, essentialism, belief, and moral dilemmas—are deeply naturalistic and eschew rationalistic idealizations." (preamble). These are the encyclopedia author's assessments.
- **On moral dilemmas — with her.** McConnell (SEP "Moral Dilemmas", Fall 2024, §4) introduces her definition with "Ruth Marcus suggests plausibly that we “define a set of rules as consistent if there is some possible world in which they are all obeyable in all circumstances in that world.”" — "plausibly" is his assessment (`raw/marcus-1980-moral-dilemmas-and-consistency.md`, cross-check section). He groups her with Williams among "The best known proponents of arguments for dilemmas that appeal to moral residue" (§6).
- **On moral dilemmas — against her.** McConnell reports two objections to residue arguments: "The first objection, in effect, suggests that the argument is question-begging (McConnell 1978 and Conee 1982); the second objection challenges the assumption that remorse and guilt are appropriate only when the agent has done wrong." (SEP "Moral Dilemmas" §6). Foot 1983 is reported by Ballarin as "critical of Marcus on guilt" (SEP §8).
- **On direct reference — with the priority reading, and against it.** Ballarin: "One interesting, debated question is whether back in 1961 Marcus was already proposing, though not fully developing, the referential theory of ordinary proper names which will dominate analytic philosophy in the seventies, thanks to the work of Donnellan (1970), Kripke (1972), and Kaplan on direct reference (1989, delivered 1977)." Her own verdict: "The answer to this question seems to be neither an unqualified yes nor a simple no." She records "the bitter controversy that took place on these questions in the nineties (see Humphreys & Fetzer 1998, Holt 1996, and Neale 2001)" (SEP §4.3); those works are not read here.
- **On the Barcan formula.** The interpretations of Linsky and Zalta, Williamson and Simchen are listed under *In dialogue with*. On one sentence of Marcus 1986b criticising Kripke's variable-domain semantics, Ballarin writes: "This is quite a bold claim that is hard to support" (SEP §6).

## Vocabulary

- *Consistent* (of a set of rules) — Marcus 1980, p. 128: obeyable together in some possible world, a rule-set analogue of the consistency of a set of sentences, which she defines on the same page. See [dilemma](../vocabulary/dilemma.md) for the sense of "moral dilemma" this implant uses.
- *Tag* — a proper name that "has no meaning" (Marcus 1961: 310, per SEP §4.3). No vocabulary entry yet.
