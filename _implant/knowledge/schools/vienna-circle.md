# Vienna Circle

All citations on this page are to Thomas Uebel, "Vienna Circle", SEP (Fall
2024). Excerpt: `raw/sep-vienna-circle-fall-2024-members-and-doctrines.md`.

## Facts a claim depends on

- **Who and when.** "a group of scientifically trained philosophers and
  philosophically interested scientists who met under the (nominal)
  leadership of Moritz Schlick for often weekly discussions [...] in the
  years from 1924 to 1936" (§2.1).
- **Names.** Uebel treats "logical empiricism (or logical positivism or
  neopositivism)" as labels for "the Vienna Circle’s early form" and uses
  them "interchangeably" (preamble). This implant files the school under
  the Circle's name. The Berlin group and later logical empiricists are
  not yet separate pages (todo).
- **End.** Members were dispersed by "emigration, forced exile and death".
  In Uebel's list: "1936: Schlick was murdered" (§2.2).
- **Boundaries.** Uebel: "its identity is blurred along several edges"
  (§2.1).

## Defining commitments

Uebel says there "did exist a minimal consensus" despite "the pluralism of
the Vienna Circle’s views" (§2.3). He also says "The Vienna Circle’s
theories were constantly changing" (preamble).

- **An empiricist criterion of meaningfulness**, used against metaphysics.
  Their "radically anti-metaphysical stance was supported by an
  empiricist criterion of meaningfulness" (preamble).
- **Logicism.** "Following the logicism of Frege and Russell, the Circle
  considered logic and mathematics to be analytic in nature" and "both to
  be tautological" (§2.3).
- **No synthetic a priori.** "They denied that any principle or claim was
  synthetic a priori" (preamble).

## Members

Each member is placed in the school by Uebel, §2.1.

- **Members:** Moritz Schlick, Hans Hahn, Philipp Frank, Otto Neurath,
  Olga Hahn-Neurath, Viktor Kraft, Theodor Radacovic, Gustav Bergmann,
  Rudolf Carnap (from 1926), Friedrich Waismann, Herbert Feigl, Marcel
  Natkin, Karl Menger and Kurt Gödel.
- **Later wished to be only "sympathetic associates":** Menger and Kraft.
- **Not a member:** Karl Popper, who "was never a member or associate of
  the Circle". He "discussed its doctrines with Feigl and Carnap".

## Problems characteristic of the school

The meaning criterion (verificationism), the analytic/synthetic
distinction, reductionism and foundationalism, and theoretical terms and
realism. These are Uebel's §3.1–3.5 headings. The problem of induction,
which is on the [MLP plan](../../plans/mlp.md), is a neighbouring topic.

## Contrasts and exchanges

- **Metaphysics.** The Circle's "rejection of metaphysics by means of
  their seemingly devastating criterion of cognitive significance [...]
  attracted immediate opposition" (§3.1).
- **Internal critics.** Uebel: "the most telling criticisms of the
  criterion came from within the Circle or broadly sympathetic
  philosophers". Its weakness, he writes, was that "their own criterion of
  empirical significance seemed to resist an acceptable formal
  characterization" (§3.1).
- **Quine.** The analytic/synthetic distinction "came under sharp
  criticism from Quine in his “Two Dogmas of Empiricism” (1951a)". Uebel
  adds that the criticism "can only be sustained by extending objections of
  a type first published by Tarski" (§3.2).
- **Putnam.** Putnam rejected the anti-metaphysical project on the ground
  of "the alleged self-refutation of the meaning criterion" (§3.1).

## Reception

Uebel: the Circle's program "no longer represents an active research
program". He also reports that "recent history of philosophy of science
has unearthed much previously neglected variety and depth in the doctrines
of the Circle’s protagonists" (preamble). Both statements are his.
