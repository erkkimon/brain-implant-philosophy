# The Chinese room

An argument page of the [arguments](index.md) branch, written under
[Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md).

## Earliest primary source

John Searle, "Minds, Brains, and Programs", *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*
3(3), 1980, pp. 417–424 (the target article; the issue continues to p. 457
with commentaries and reply). Excerpt: `raw/searle-1980-minds-brains-programs-chinese-room.md`.

## Standard reconstruction

Reconstruction by this implant from the quoted text (manifest
[G4](../../vision/manifest.md)).

- P1. "Intentionality in human beings (and animals) is a product of causal
  features of the brain" (abstract, proposition 1, p. 417).
- P2. "Instantiating a computer program is never by itself a sufficient
  condition of intentionality" (proposition 2, p. 417).
- C. "'Could a machine think?' On the argument advanced here only a machine
  could think, and only very special kinds of machines, namely brains and
  machines with internal causal powers equivalent to those of brains"
  (p. 417).

Form: deductive. The Chinese room setup illustrates P2: "Suppose that I'm
locked in a room and given a large batch of Chinese writing … To me,
Chinese writing is just so many meaningless squiggles" (p. 417).

## Supports

[Biological naturalism](../positions/biological-naturalism.md); the case
against [functionalism](../positions/functionalism.md) and strong AI.

## Which premise is disputed, and by whom

- **P2 — the systems reply.** "While it is true that the individual person
  who is locked in the room does not understand the story, the fact is that
  he is merely part of a whole system, and the system does understand the
  story." Searle's response: "let the individual internalize all of these
  elements of the system" (p. 419).
- **P2 — Turing's imitation game.** Turing replaces "Can machines think?"
  with a behavioural test ([Turing 1950](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433),
  §1; excerpt: `raw/turing-1950-computing-machinery-imitation-game-consciousness.md`).
  Searle's argument targets exactly this move.
- **P1 — Bender & Koller 2020 (meaning, not consciousness).** "A system
  trained only on form has a priori no way to learn meaning" (abstract;
  excerpt: `raw/bender-koller-2020-octopus-form-and-meaning.md`). The paper
  is about meaning, not consciousness; the word does not occur. Structural
  parallel noted as a structural note the implant adds (manifest
  [G4](../../vision/manifest.md)).
- **P1 — Agüera y Arcas 2022.** "Statistics do amount to understanding, in
  any falsifiable sense" (abstract; excerpt:
  `raw/aguera-y-arcas-2022-do-llms-understand-us-and-lemoine-lamda.md`).

## Objections

- **The systems reply** (above).
- **The robot reply** — named in Searle's paper but not quoted in the
  excerpts held.
- **Survey:** "doesn't understand" 67.12 % / "understands" 17.85 %
  (N = 1031; Bourget & Chalmers 2023).

## Replies

Searle's replies to the systems reply (internalization) and others are in
the paper; only the systems reply exchange is quoted in the excerpts held.

## Variants

Bender & Koller's octopus (2020) is a meaning-side variant. Butlin et al.
2023 and Seth 2025 represent the modern descendants of the two sides.
