# Leibniz's mill

An argument page of the [arguments](index.md) branch, written under
[Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md).

## Earliest primary source

G. W. Leibniz, *Monadology* §17 (1714; Latta tr. 1898): "if we suppose
that there were a machine so constructed as to think, feel, and have
perception, it might be conceived as increased in size, while keeping the
same proportions, so that one might go into it as into a mill. That being
so, we should, on examining its interior, find only parts which work one
upon another, and never anything by which to explain a perception. Thus it
is in a simple substance, and not in a compound or in a machine, that
perception must be sought for" (excerpt:
`raw/leibniz-1714-monadology-17-mill-argument.md`).

## Standard reconstruction

Reconstruction by this implant from the quoted text (manifest
[G4](../../vision/manifest.md)).

- P1. If perception could be explained mechanically, then inspecting an
  enlarged thinking machine would reveal the explanation.
- P2. Inspecting such a machine reveals "only parts which work one upon
  another".
- C. Perception cannot be explained mechanically; it "must be sought for"
  in a simple substance.

Form: reductio. The conclusion is positive (simple substances) as well as
negative (not machines).

## Supports

[Dualism](../positions/dualism.md) (in Leibniz's own monadological form);
the explanatory gap family shared with the [knowledge argument](knowledge-argument.md)
and the [zombie argument](zombie-argument.md).

## Which premise is disputed, and by whom

- **P1 — Dennett.** The Multiple Drafts Model denies any single place where
  "perception" happens; there is no mill to walk into (SEP "Consciousness"
  §9.4; excerpt: `raw/dennett-1991-consciousness-explained-via-sep.md`).
- **P1 — Frankish.** Illusionism denies that perception has the phenomenal
  properties Leibniz takes as given ([Frankish 2016](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/contentone/imp/jcs/2016/00000023/f0020011/art00002),
  §1.2; excerpt: `raw/frankish-2016-illusionism.md`).
- **C — Huxley 1874 (opposite direction).** Consciousness is "a collateral
  product" of the body's working, "as … the steam-whistle which accompanies
  the work of a locomotive engine is without influence upon its machinery"
  (*Collected Essays* I, p. 240; excerpt:
  `raw/huxley-1874-animals-automata-steam-whistle.md`). Huxley accepts the
  mechanical explanation and makes consciousness epiphenomenal.

## Objections

See above. Also: the argument assumes that inspection at one scale
exhausts explanation; emergent properties may not be visible at the
component level. Not grounded in the excerpts held.

## Replies

Leibniz's reply is the positive thesis: monads. Later forms of the
argument (Levine, Chalmers) drop the positive thesis and keep the gap.

## Variants

- **Levine 1983:** "psycho-physical identity statements leave a significant
  explanatory gap" (p. 354; excerpt:
  `raw/levine-1983-materialism-and-qualia-explanatory-gap.md`). Epistemic,
  not metaphysical.
- **Chalmers 1995:** "Why doesn't all this information-processing go on
  'in the dark'?" (§2; excerpt: `raw/chalmers-1995-facing-up-hard-problem.md`).
  Credits "explanatory gap" to Levine 1983.
- **Nagel 1974:** "Every subjective phenomenon is essentially connected
  with a single point of view, and it seems inevitable that an objective,
  physical theory will abandon that point of view" (p. 437; excerpt:
  `raw/nagel-1974-what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat.md`).
