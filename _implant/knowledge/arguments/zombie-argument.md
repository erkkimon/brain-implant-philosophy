# The zombie (conceivability) argument

An argument page of the [arguments](index.md) branch, bearing on the hub
[What is consciousness?](../problems/consciousness.md) and, through two
word-collisions, on [Could an AI be conscious?](../problems/ai-consciousness.md).
Written under [Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md)
and graded by [How claims are graded](../../conventions/how-claims-are-graded.md).

## Earliest primary source

The excerpts hold no primary text of the argument; both sources below are
the Stanford Encyclopedia's reports (cited as the brief requires:
"SEP <entry> (Fall 2024), §n, reporting <author year>").

- **Kripke 1972/80** is the ancestor SEP names. SEP "Zombies" (Fall 2024,
  Robert Kirk), §2, reporting Kripke 1972/80, p. 153f.: "A metaphor of
  Saul Kripke's helps to show how the zombie idea threatens physicalism.
  Imagine God creating the world and deciding to bring into existence the
  whole of the purely physical universe. Having created this physical
  universe, did he have to do any more work to provide for consciousness?
  Answering yes to this question implies there is more to consciousness
  than the physical facts alone can supply"
  ([SEP Zombies](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/zombies/),
  §2; excerpt: `raw/kripke-1980-naming-and-necessity-via-sep.md`). SEP §3
  adds that "Kripke used a similar argument in his 1972/80". The book is
  *Naming and Necessity* (Harvard University Press, 1980; ISBN
  9780674598461), Lecture III, pp. 144–155 — a standard citation the
  excerpt marks as not verified against the print.
- **Kripke's disanalogy** (SEP "The Mind/Brain Identity Theory", Fall
  2024, J. J. C. Smart, §8, reporting Kripke 1980, pp. 151–152): "when we
  think that molecular motion might exist in the absence of heat we are
  confusing this with thinking that the molecular motion might have
  existed without being felt as heat. He asks whether it is analogously
  possible that if pain is a certain sort of brain process that it has
  existed without being felt as pain. He suggests that the answer is
  'No'"; Kripke "concludes (p. 152) that the apparent contingency of the
  connection between the mental state and the corresponding brain state
  thus cannot be explained by some sort of qualitative analogue as in the
  case of heat" ([SEP Identity Theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/mind-identity/),
  §8). The excerpt's context note records that this SEP entry is written
  by Smart, an identity theorist reporting an opponent.
- **Chalmers 1996** is the systematic form: "The most systematic use of
  the zombie idea against physicalism is by David Chalmers 1996" (SEP
  "Zombies", Fall 2024, §1, reporting Chalmers 1996, pp. 93–171; excerpt:
  `raw/chalmers-1995-facing-up-hard-problem.md`). *The Conscious Mind*
  (Oxford University Press, 1996; ISBN 9780195105537) is held
  bibliographically only; no sentence of the book is in the excerpts.

Whether the word "zombie" or the thought experiment has an earlier source
than Kripke is not in the excerpts held.

## Standard reconstruction

SEP's "simplest version", quoted, not the implant's: "Zombies are
conceivable. Whatever is conceivable is possible. Therefore zombies are
possible" (SEP "Zombies", Fall 2024, §3). The step from possibility to
the falsity of physicalism is supplied by the Kripke metaphor in §2
("Answering yes … implies there is more to consciousness than the
physical facts alone can supply"); the implant joins the two as P3–C
(reconstruction by this implant from the quoted text, manifest
[G4](../../vision/manifest.md)).

- P1. Zombies are conceivable (SEP §3).
- P2. Whatever is conceivable is possible (SEP §3).
- C1. Therefore zombies are possible (SEP §3).
- P3. If zombies are possible, "there is more to consciousness than the
  physical facts alone can supply" (SEP §2, from the God metaphor).
- C. Physicalism is false.

Form: deductive; two chained modus ponens steps. Checked with the
implant's [logic tool](../../skills/analyse-an-argument.md) with C =
conceivable, M = possible, P = physicalism:

```
P1: C    P2: C -> M    P3: M -> ~P    C: ~P
VALID
```

"Conceivable" and "possible" are placeholders here; SEP §3's ellipsis
omits "later versions of the conceivability argument" (excerpt's context
note), which refine both terms, and those refinements are not in the
excerpts held.

## Supports

- [Dualism](../positions/dualism.md) — the conclusion is that the
  physical facts do not fix the phenomenal facts (SEP §2). Chalmers's
  1995 question is the same thought as a question: "Why doesn't all this
  information-processing go on 'in the dark', free of any inner feel?"
  ([Chalmers 1995](https://consc.net/papers/facing.html), §2).
- [Panpsychism](../positions/panpsychism.md) — Strawson's argument does
  not use zombies but shares P3's shape: if physical stuff were "wholly
  and utterly non-experiential" (thesis NE, p. 11), experience would have
  to emerge brutely, which he rejects; hence "real physicalists must give
  up NE" ([Strawson 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199267422.003.0003),
  p. 25; excerpt: `raw/strawson-2006-realistic-monism.md`).

## Which premise is disputed, and by whom

The 2020 PhilPapers Survey question maps onto the premises directly
(N = 1610; [Bourget & Chalmers 2023](https://doi.org/10.3998/phimp.2109);
excerpt: `raw/bourget-chalmers-2023-philpapers-survey-consciousness-and-amcs-2023-open-letter.md`).
The sample is English-language philosophy faculty; the figures are data,
not a ranking.

| Premise | Survey option, "accept or lean towards" | Who disputes it in the excerpts, and on what grounds |
|---|---|---|
| P1 (conceivable) | "inconceivable" 16.40 % | Dennett, as SEP reports him: the Multiple Drafts Model "denies the existence of qualia" and holds there may be "no probe-independent fact of the matter about what the person's conscious state really was" (SEP "Consciousness", Fall 2024, §9.4, reporting Dennett 1991; excerpt: `raw/dennett-1991-consciousness-explained-via-sep.md`) — on which there is nothing for the zombie to lack. The excerpt holds no sentence of Dennett's on zombies as such. |
| P2 (conceivable → possible) | "conceivable but not metaphysically possible" 36.52 % | Smart, in the same SEP §8 in which he reports Kripke, offers identity-theorist replies (counterpart contextualism; "having a pain" rather than "pain"), which the excerpt names but does not quote (`raw/kripke-1980-naming-and-necessity-via-sep.md`, context note). Levine 1983 keeps the intuition and drops the modal step: "I don't believe this intuition supports the metaphysical thesis Kripke defends" ([Levine 1983](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0114.1983.tb00207.x), p. 354; excerpt: `raw/levine-1983-materialism-and-qualia-explanatory-gap.md`). |
| C1 (possible) | "metaphysically possible" 24.41 % | — |
| P3 (possible → physicalism false) | (not a survey option) | Frankish 2016 accepts that experiences lack "qualitative, 'what-it's-like' properties, whether physical or non-physical" ([Frankish 2016](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/contentone/imp/jcs/2016/00000023/f0020011/art00002), §1.2, eprint p. 3; excerpt: `raw/frankish-2016-illusionism.md`) — a position on which, a structural note the implant adds and not a verdict (manifest [G4](../../vision/manifest.md)), we would ourselves be what the zombie is supposed to lack, so P3's antecedent is granted and its consequent denied. |
| Other | 24.16 % | — |

## Objections

- **Qualia denial** (Dennett via SEP §9.4; Frankish 2016 §1.2) — the
  zombie is not missing anything, because introspection "typically
  misrepresents as phenomenal" what is in truth a "quasi-phenomenal
  property … a non-phenomenal, physical property" (Frankish §1.2, p. 3).
  Frankish replaces the hard problem with "the illusion problem — the
  problem of explaining how the illusion of phenomenality arises and why
  it is so powerful" (§4, eprint p. 20).
- **The epistemic reduction** (Levine 1983, p. 354) — "One cannot
  conclude from my version of the argument that materialism is false,
  which makes my version a weaker attack than Kripke's"; the intuition
  supports "an explanatory gap", not a modal conclusion. See
  [Leibniz's mill](leibniz-mill.md) for the explanatory-gap lineage.
- **Smart's identity-theorist replies** (SEP Identity Theory §8) — named
  in the excerpt, not quoted; the excerpts hold no Smart reply from the
  1959 paper, which predates Kripke's lectures.

## Replies

Replies by Chalmers to these objections are not in the excerpts held; the
1996 book is bibliographic only. What the excerpts hold on the proponent
side is Frankish's own report that illusionism "is often dismissed out of
hand as failing to 'take consciousness seriously' (Chalmers, 1996)"
(2016, introduction; excerpt's context note) — the dismissal is
Frankish's report of Chalmers, not a text the raw files quote. Strawson's
p. 3 sentence — experience is "the phenomenon whose existence is more
certain than the existence of anything else" — is the reply of a
panpsychist, not of a dualist, to P1's deniers.

## Variants

Two later uses of the word "zombie" are not the argument and are recorded
here because the collision is itself a finding.

- **IIT's "true zombies" (Oizumi, Albantakis & Tononi 2014).** The
  abstract lists among the theory's consequences: "there can be true
  'zombies' – unconscious feed-forward systems that are functionally
  equivalent to conscious complexes"
  ([doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003588](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003588),
  abstract; excerpt: `raw/oizumi-albantakis-tononi-2014-iit-3-axioms.md`).
  A structural note the implant adds, not a verdict (manifest
  [G4](../../vision/manifest.md)): the philosophers' zombie is
  *physically* identical to a conscious being; IIT's is *functionally*
  equivalent but physically different (feed-forward rather than
  re-entrant), which is a prediction of the theory, not a premise about
  conceivability. See [Integrated information theory](../positions/integrated-information-theory.md).
- **Suleyman's "Seemingly Conscious AI" (2025).** "It shares certain
  aspects of the idea of a 'philosophical zombie' (a technical term!),
  one that simulates all the characteristics of consciousness but
  internally it is blank. My imagined AI system would not actually be
  conscious, but it would imitate consciousness in such a convincing way
  that it would be indistinguishable from a claim that you or I might
  make to one another about our own consciousness"
  ([Suleyman 2025](https://mustafa-suleyman.ai/seemingly-conscious-ai-is-coming),
  Summary; excerpt: `raw/suleyman-2025-seemingly-conscious-ai-and-jang-2025-openai-perceived-consciousness.md`).
  Suleyman himself hedges with "certain aspects"; his system is
  behaviourally, not physically, a duplicate, and his essay argues from
  the risk of users believing it conscious, not from a theory of
  consciousness (excerpt's context note). See
  [Could an AI be conscious?](../problems/ai-consciousness.md).
- **Descartes 1637 as an ancestor?** The *Discourse*, Part V, imagines
  "machines bearing the image of our bodies, and capable of imitating our
  actions as far as it is morally possible" and gives "two most certain
  tests whereby to know that they were not therefore really men" —
  flexible language, and reason as "an universal instrument"
  ([Descartes 1637](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/59), Part V, Veitch
  tr.; excerpt: `raw/descartes-1637-discourse-two-tests-for-machines.md`).
  A structural note the implant adds, not a verdict: Descartes's machines
  are behaviourally *distinguishable* by his two tests, which is the
  opposite of the zombie's stipulation; the excerpt supports him as an
  ancestor of Suleyman's behavioural case and of Turing's test, not of
  the conceivability argument. See [Descartes](../thinkers/descartes.md).
- **The knowledge argument** — Jackson's §II contrasts his argument with
  "the modal argument" (`raw/jackson-1982-epiphenomenal-qualia-mary.md`,
  context note); on its own page: [knowledge argument](knowledge-argument.md).

## Vocabulary

Terms the argument turns on, each open work in
[vocabulary](../vocabulary/index.md): *zombie* (three senses on this
page: physical duplicate, IIT's feed-forward equivalent, Suleyman's
behavioural imitator), *conceivable*, *metaphysically possible*, *rigid
designator* (Kripke via SEP §8), *physicalism*, *quasi-phenomenal*
(Frankish 2016). The survey's own three-way wording — "inconceivable,
conceivable but not metaphysically possible, or metaphysically possible"
— is the contract most respondents answered under.
