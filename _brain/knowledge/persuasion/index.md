# Persuasion

Techniques by which one mind moves another: argumentation, rhetoric,
dialectic and persuasion treated as **one spectrum**, because the same move
(an appeal to emotion, an appeal to authority, a reframing) is called a
fallacy under one theory, a legitimate argumentation scheme under another,
and simply a sales technique under a third. The implant records all three
classifications and adopts none
([Reporting, not endorsing](../../conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md)).

A page never says "this is a fallacy" in the implant's voice; it says who
classifies it so, under which theory, and who classifies it otherwise. The
critique of a technique and the critique of the critique are both content.

The point of this branch for an agent: to spot a technique **systematically
and with a reference**, which a language model alone does fluently but
unsystematically and without receipts. The
[spot-persuasion-and-bias](../../skills/spot-persuasion-and-bias.md) skill
drives it; [write-persuasively](../../skills/write-persuasively.md) and
[debate](../../skills/debate.md) use it in the other direction, with the
user choosing which techniques are allowed.

```
# <title>

## Also known as
Names and near-synonyms across the literatures (Latin name, informal name,
sales-literature name), each cited.

## Mechanism
What the move does — cognitively, socially, rhetorically — as described by
named sources.

## Example
A short, cited or constructed instance, labelled as which.

## Classified as a fallacy by
Who, under which theory of fallacy (e.g. the standard treatment,
pragma-dialectics, Walton's schemes), with the criterion they apply.

## Classified as legitimate by
Who, under which theory (e.g. as an argumentation scheme with its critical
questions; as ethos/pathos/logos in classical rhetoric), cited.

## Critique
Published objections to the technique — and to its classification as a
fallacy — attributed.

## How to spot it
Observable markers, from the sources that give them.

## How to answer it
Counter-moves described in the literature, cited (e.g. the critical
questions attached to the scheme).

## Related biases
Links to the [biases](../biases/index.md) the technique exploits, where a
source says it does.
```
