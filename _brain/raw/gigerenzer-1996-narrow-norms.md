# Gigerenzer 1996 — the "vague heuristics" critique

Source: Gerd Gigerenzer (1996), "On Narrow Norms and Vague Heuristics: A
  Reply to Kahneman and Tversky (1996)", Psychological Review 103(3),
  592–596; abstract and the section "Vague Heuristics".
Original: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.103.3.592
  (copyrighted; author's copy at https://hdl.handle.net/21.11116/0000-0000-BD2F-8;
  excerpts only)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25

> "At issue is the imposition of unnecessarily narrow norms of sound
>  reasoning that are used to diagnose so-called cognitive illusions and the
>  continuing reliance on vague heuristics that explain everything and
>  nothing."  (abstract, p. 592)

> "The heuristics in the heuristics-and-biases program are too vague to
>  count as explanations. They are labels with the virtue of Rorschach
>  inkblots: A researcher can read into them what he or she wishes."
>  (section "Vague Heuristics")

Relied on for: the attributive claim that Gigerenzer criticises the
heuristics-and-biases programme on two counts — the norms it measures
against and the explanatory content of "anchoring", "availability" and
"representativeness" as labels. The companion piece it answers is Kahneman
& Tversky (1996), "On the Reality of Cognitive Illusions", Psychological
Review 103(3), 582–591, https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.103.3.582.
Context: the reply also disputes what Kahneman and Tversky attribute to
Gigerenzer about frequency formats; that exchange is not reproduced.
