# Tversky & Kahneman 1974 — the anchoring passage

Source: Amos Tversky & Daniel Kahneman (1974), "Judgment under Uncertainty:
  Heuristics and Biases", Science 185(4157), 1124–1131; the section
  "Adjustment and Anchoring", p. 1128.
Original: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124
  (copyrighted; excerpts only — three sentences and one worked figure)
Retrieved: 2026-09-25 (bibliographic data verified by DOI content
  negotiation; wording checked against an open teaching copy of the paper)

> "That is, different starting points yield different estimates, which are
>  biased toward the initial values. We call this phenomenon anchoring."
>  (p. 1128)

> "For example, the median estimates of the percentage of African countries
>  in the United Nations were 25 and 45 for groups that received 10 and 65,
>  respectively, as starting points. Payoffs for accuracy did not reduce
>  the anchoring effect."  (p. 1128)

Relied on for: the textual claim of what the original paper calls
anchoring; the empirical claim about the wheel-of-fortune demonstration
(anchors 10 and 65, medians 25 and 45, incentives without effect).
Context: the paragraph headed "Insufficient adjustment" describes a wheel
of fortune spun in the subjects' presence to produce a number between 0 and
100, a higher/lower judgement, then an estimate by adjusting from that
number. The paper's later sections apply anchoring to conjunctive and
disjunctive events and to subjective probability distributions; those are
not reproduced.
